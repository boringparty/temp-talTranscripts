  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.28" data-timestamp="0.28"> From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i></p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Larry King</h4><p begin="00:00:03.06" data-timestamp="3.06"> Mrs. Treanor, can you hear me OK?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kathleen Treanor</h4><p begin="00:00:05.77" data-timestamp="5.77"> Yes, I can.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Larry King</h4><p begin="00:00:06.42" data-timestamp="6.42"> What were your in-laws doing in the building?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:09.00" data-timestamp="9"> This is <i>The</i> <i>Larry</i> <i>King</i> <i>Show</i> the day after the Oklahoma City bombing in 1995. His guests are a young couple, the Treanors, who lost their relatives in the bombing, and a young charismatic preacher.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Larry King</h4><p begin="00:00:19.53" data-timestamp="19.53"> Joining us from Tulsa is Reverend Carlton Pearson, who spoke at yesterday's very moving prayer service. What do you say to people like the Treanors?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Carlton Pearson</h4><p begin="00:00:30.54" data-timestamp="30.54"> Well, Brad already mentioned his faith in God. And I said yesterday that experience is not only what happens to you, but what you do with what happens to you.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:39.32" data-timestamp="39.32"> Carlton Pearson, at the time that he talked to Larry King, was a rising evangelical megastar, a Republican activist who prayed in the Bush Senior White House, a guest on <i>The</i> <i>700</i> <i>Club.</i> Host of a national TV show, he traveled all over the world in chartered jets, lecturing to fundamentalist gatherings. But at the height of his popularity, he became involved in a scandal, though not in the kind of scandal that you usually think of when you hear the word scandal.</p><p begin="00:01:01.86" data-timestamp="61.86">He didn't have an affair, didn't embezzle money. He didn't admit an addiction to prescription painkillers. No, no, none of that. He stopped believing in hell. And what happened to him next was the kind of thing that happens from time to time here in America, even now. He became a heretic, a very prominent heretic in the middle of a religious community, in the middle of our country.</p><p begin="00:01:23.53" data-timestamp="83.53">Every century in our nation, there have been heresy trials and people who've been cast out of their own communities. It didn't end with the Salem witch trials. And things happened to Carlton Pearson. He had an experience that most Americans do not imagine still happens today in modern America. We're devoting our entire show today to his story.</p><p begin="00:01:39.52" data-timestamp="99.52">We first broadcast today's show back in 2005. And we're rerunning it this week because we just made a new movie about Carlton Pearson's life. We're excited about that. It's on Netflix, called <i>Come</i> <i>Sunday.</i></p><p begin="00:01:50.81" data-timestamp="110.81">Our reporter for the radio story is Russell Cobb, who's from Tulsa where most of the story takes place. And when you look at the rise and fall of Carlton Pearson, his rise is almost as remarkable as his fall. And that's where Russell Cobb starts his story.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Rise</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Russell Cobb</h4><p begin="00:02:05.41" data-timestamp="125.41"> Carlton Pearson says there are two kinds of people in his family, preachers and convicts. He grew up in an all-black ghetto in San Diego in a strict Pentecostal denomination-- no smoking, drinking, cursing, or dancing. But there was lots of churchgoing.</p><p begin="00:02:19.93" data-timestamp="139.93">And church is where the really wild stuff happened. People spoke in tongues, got slain by the Holy Spirit. And they definitely believed in hell, to the point where even the faithful could get possessed by demons. Carlton's father and grandfather were ministers. And at an early age, he was following in their footsteps.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Carlton Pearson</h4><p begin="00:02:36.19" data-timestamp="156.19"> Well, the first time I ever cast the devil out of somebody, I was like, 17 years old. 16, maybe. And the girl was my girlfriend.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Russell Cobb</h4><p begin="00:02:42.73" data-timestamp="162.73"> This was a tiny storefront where the church was having a youth revival.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Carlton Pearson</h4><p begin="00:02:46.15" data-timestamp="166.15"> She just let out this scream. And it startled me and everybody else. She fell to the ground. I looked at the pastor, and he just stood there. And nobody else moved. So I started rebuking the devil, and binding the devil in the name of Jesus, and commanding him to come out, and pleading what we call plead the blood. The blood, the blood, the blood, the blood, come out. You lying wonder, in the name of Jesus I command you to cease and desist. Loose her. Come out in Jesus-- Come out. Come out now.</p><p begin="00:03:12.39" data-timestamp="192.39">The things we'd been taught to say. My grandfather used to do that. She kind of thrashed a little bit. And she's, I'm not going out, you know, talking back to me like this. So I was freaking out. But I was the leader of the meeting. It was my revival. So I couldn't-- I had seen people cast devils out before. I never expected that, certainly not from a girl I was dating.</p><p begin="00:03:40.79" data-timestamp="220.79">It took me probably an hour, maybe an hour and a half, before she got through thrashing and talking back to me and screaming. And then it went out of her into another person, supposedly. And they-- the whole pew hit the floor. And there were all these crazy things happening in that little storefront church.</p><p begin="00:03:56.15" data-timestamp="236.15">It was very frightening, very serious. And that kind of thing happened every night for three nights in a row. And I became a hero after that because Carlton Pearson cast the devil out of people three nights in a row.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Russell Cobb</h4><p begin="00:04:10.52" data-timestamp="250.52"> Looking back at this episode right now, I mean as you're telling it, how do you think about it right now?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Carlton Pearson</h4><p begin="00:04:17.67" data-timestamp="257.67"> Well, I expected devils. I expected demons. I saw them everywhere. So that was a part of my life. The devil was as present and as large as God. He had most of the people. He was ultimately going to get most of the people. Demons were all over-- in the church, in the schools, in the neighborhoods. Everything was a devil. So if you believe it, you experience it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Russell Cobb</h4><p begin="00:04:42.03" data-timestamp="282.03"> Carlton was still curious about what went on beyond the world of devils and demons. But intellectual curiosity wasn't really encouraged where he grew up. This wasn't a place where church leaders had PhDs in divinity.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Carlton Pearson</h4><p begin="00:04:52.74" data-timestamp="292.74"> All my pastors but one were janitors. They cleaned banks and restaurants. And I'd sometimes go with them. A lot of them couldn't read. They had no formal education, certainly not seminary. And so we were trying to fit into a big, broad world that we didn't understand, that we felt was basically hellbound, and we were to reach them, but we couldn't relate to them. They couldn't relate to us.</p><p begin="00:05:16.54" data-timestamp="316.54">So my world was getting smaller. And the world was getting larger. And I was smothering. But I had to find a way to get out of that world and still go to heaven. And ORU offered that to me in a sanctified way.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Russell Cobb</h4><p begin="00:05:34.33" data-timestamp="334.33"> ORU was Oral Roberts University. Roberts was one of the few outside influences that made it into the Pearson home. He had a TV show, and Carlton's mother loved it. And I'd like to take a moment here and talk a little bit about Oral Roberts and the school he founded because it forms a backdrop for much of what happens later.</p><p begin="00:05:52.19" data-timestamp="352.19">Oral Roberts was a half-Cherokee charismatic preacher who claimed to heal disease with the touch of a hand, sometimes even through the TV screen. He was one of the first preachers to take TV seriously, the first televangelist the way we think of them now. And his weekly show, <i>The</i> <i>Hour</i> <i>Of</i> <i>Healing,</i> reached tens of millions of viewers. And he tried to change the image of Pentecostals from dusty tent revival Holy Rollers into something respectable and higher class.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Oral Roberts</h4><p begin="00:06:17.21" data-timestamp="377.21"> I grew up in the Christian faith and was taught to give, but was told not to expect anything back. And we listened. And we gave, and received nothing back. We had old rattletrap cars, many times no place to live.</p><p begin="00:06:38.81" data-timestamp="398.81">Our clothes were not fit to wear, and mete out trying to tell people the good news. And people out there saying, yeah, to be like you? No.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Russell Cobb</h4><p begin="00:06:48.47" data-timestamp="408.47"> He also tweaked the Pentecostal message, making it more optimistic. His catchphrases-- expect a miracle, God ain't poor no more, plant a seed and it will grow-- were all about the idea that faith would lead to wealth and happiness on this earth. He called donations to his church investments. And he guaranteed a high rate of return.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Oral Roberts</h4><p begin="00:07:07.96" data-timestamp="427.96"> God said return unto me, and I'll return unto you.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Congregation</h4><p begin="00:07:10.52" data-timestamp="430.52"> That's right.
