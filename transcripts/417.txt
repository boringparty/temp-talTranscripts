  <h3>Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.49" data-timestamp="0.49">During Bill Jerke's years as a state legislator, the Colorado Union of Taxpayers rated him as one of the biggest enemies of taxes in the state.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Jerke</h4><p begin="00:00:08.93" data-timestamp="8.93">I think three years out of eight, I was either the outright winner or tied for most conservative legislator.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:15.93" data-timestamp="15.93">He helped organize the Tea Party chapter in Greeley. And he was proud to let me know--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Jerke</h4><p begin="00:00:20.04" data-timestamp="20.04">I'm a big supporter of Juan Williams. Go on.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:23.98" data-timestamp="23.98">The politics this year are so strange that this anti-tax Republican has found himself spending the fall stumping the state, doing something he's never done.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:33.00" data-timestamp="33">Did you ever picture that you'd be somebody going out, talking to Republican groups, arguing against tax cuts?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Jerke</h4><p begin="00:00:39.32" data-timestamp="39.32">It is hard to picture, frankly, that that's a position I would find myself in.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:45.39" data-timestamp="45.39">Jerke is trying to defeat three ballot initiatives that come before Colorado voters next week. Initiatives 60, 61, and 101 together would strangle most of the ways a government brings in money. They'd cut Colorado's personal income taxes by a fourth, limit the ability of state and local governments to issue bonds, and force the state government to take over $2 billion in education funding that's now covered by local governments, but without the means to pay for that new cost.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Jerke</h4><p begin="00:01:11.87" data-timestamp="71.87">And so you start to add it up, and you figure out pretty quickly that the state will be short $3 to $4 billion out of a $7 billion general fund. And it's hard to understand how we're going to have a prison system, social services system, and higher education would be all but gone. It would have to virtually be all privatized at that point.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:32.93" data-timestamp="92.93">He believes the initiatives would throw Colorado into such an ongoing fiscal crisis that business would leave the state.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:39.39" data-timestamp="99.39">What do you think of the people who are trying to get these three initiatives passed?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Jerke</h4><p begin="00:01:44.56" data-timestamp="104.56">I think that they are desperate patriots. I think that they sincerely believe that government has gotten too big, and I can't disagree with them there. I just think that there are more measured ways of doing it. And they say back to me, &quot;Yeah, but we've heard that for 20 years from you and we're tired of waiting.&quot;</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:01.98" data-timestamp="121.98">Do you think that the politics this year are just very strange, where Republicans who in the past saw eye-to-eye and worked together now are opponents on issues just like this?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Jerke</h4><p begin="00:02:12.03" data-timestamp="132.03">I have good friends that are on the other side. They're a little bit surprised to see me on this side, I think. But when I point out the fatal flaws within these three questions, they don't have much to say to go ahead and defend themselves, either, on it. They kind of get back to just muttering again that something's got to be done to go ahead and slow down government.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:39.95" data-timestamp="159.95">This year we have Republicans impatient with the Republican party, and Democrats frustrated with the Democratic party.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Greg Shropshire</h4><p begin="00:02:46.79" data-timestamp="166.79"> There's a lot of African Americans that are very disheartened with the Democratic party. And they see the abandonment of Barack as the abandonment of black people.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:55.12" data-timestamp="175.12">That's Greg Shropshire of Rome, Georgia, a past president of the local chapter of a service organization called The 100 Black Men of America. He feels that the Democratic party as a whole has not stood by Barack Obama.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Greg Shropshire</h4><p begin="00:03:06.00" data-timestamp="186">The fact that we're still discussing, 20 something months into the presidency, whether Barack is a Muslim or not is just asinine. And the fact that it's allowed, the fact that Democrats even allow people out there to even bring stuff like that up. And it's not we're going to sling mud, but the fact that you're not even speaking up for truth.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:26.87" data-timestamp="206.87">From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i>, distributed by Public Radio International. I'm Ira Glass. Today on our show, on the eve of this mid-term election, people looking at their own political parties and declaring, &quot;This party sucks.&quot; Our show today in two acts. Act one, the conservatives. Act two, the liberals. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Patriot Games</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:58.73" data-timestamp="238.73">Act one, &quot;Patriot Games.&quot; With Tea Parties springing up around the country, so many new people are involved in politics for the first time this election, and they are getting into situations that sometimes they don't know how to handle, it's all so new. Ben Calhoun has this story of one congressional race and two friends.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:04:16.00" data-timestamp="256">The day Congressman Bart Stupak's seat came up for grabs was March 21, 2010, a little after 4:00. Stupak is a Democrat from Michigan. He'd been leading a group of pro-life Democrats who were holding out their votes on the health care bill until there was something in it that prevented federal money from going to abortions. They held out and held out, and then, at 4:06, Stupak announced that President Obama had agreed to issued an executive order, giving in to their demands. And because of that, Stupak's group would provide the rest of the 216 votes needed to pass the health care bill through the House.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bart Stupak</h4><p begin="00:04:50.90" data-timestamp="290.9"> I am please to announce that we have an agreement, and it is with the help of the President and the Speaker, we were able to come with an agreement to protect the sanctity of life in the health care reform.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Journalist</h4><p begin="00:05:01.11" data-timestamp="301.11"> This puts you guys over the top? Are you now past 216?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bart Stupak</h4><p begin="00:05:05.84" data-timestamp="305.84"> We're well past 216, yes.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dan Benishek</h4><p begin="00:05:07.97" data-timestamp="307.97"> People started calling me on the phone, even as Mr. Stupak was at the podium.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:05:14.77" data-timestamp="314.77">That's Dan Benishek, a surgeon, who, just a couple months before all this, had decided to run against Stupak. The day of the health care announcement, the day that Bart Stupak officially became the congressman who gave the nation Obamacare, Benishek was the only Republican challenger on the books. And there was a good reason for that. Because, after 18 years, Stupak was pretty much considered unbeatable. Essentially, you ran against him if you didn't mind losing, or if you were a total political novice like Dan Benishek.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dan Benishek</h4><p begin="00:05:44.06" data-timestamp="344.06">You know, he's still there talking, answering questions and blah, blah, blah, and the phone started to ring. And it didn't stop for, I don't know, days. It rang all day and all night.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:05:56.23" data-timestamp="356.23">And what were people saying to you?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dan Benishek</h4><p begin="00:05:58.53" data-timestamp="358.53">They were saying, like, &quot;How do we send you money?&quot; [LAUGHTER] Because we didn't even have a website out. You know what I mean? I ended up raising a couple hundred thousand dollars in a matter of weeks.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:06:14.83" data-timestamp="374.83">Did that catch you off guard?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dan Benishek</h4><p begin="00:06:16.88" data-timestamp="376.88">Yeah. I'll say. Unbelievably.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:06:20.77" data-timestamp="380.77">Pretty much overnight, Dan Benishek went from being a political nobody to being a political contender. And Bart Stupak went from being the guy who was unbeatable to being the guy who was under 24 hour police protection. Then, 19 days after he announced the health care deal, Stupak announced that he wouldn't be running again. At that moment, Michigan's first district, a conservative place we're both Democrats and Republicans are often pro-life and pro-gun, suddenly became one of the hottest commodities you can get in congressional politics-- an open seat in a swing district.</p><p begin="00:06:53.89" data-timestamp="413.89">On the Democratic side, a state assemblyman named Gary McDowell jumped in to replace Stupak and ran unopposed in the Democratic primary. On the Republican side, Dan Benishek's early good luck had given him a head start, but other people climbed in, and things got nasty and contentious. And on election night, it was close. So there's always an excuse for why they can't push back.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Begala</h4><p begin="00:47:22.59" data-timestamp="2842.59">That's right.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:47:23.55" data-timestamp="2843.55">Then, in early September, Obama and his team seemed to wake up. Obama said that the Republicans were holding, quote, &quot;Middle-class tax cuts hostage to millionaire and billionaire cuts.&quot; The next day, Treasury Secretary Tim Geithner repeated that line almost word for word. A real talking point. I looked forward to one of those Jon Stewart bits where he quotes a half dozen officials robotically reciting the same sound bite before he rests his face in his open palms. Only this time it would be Democrats, not Republicans.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Barack Obama</h4><p begin="00:47:52.74" data-timestamp="2872.74">They want to hold these middle-class tax cuts hostage until they get an additional tax cut for the wealthiest 2% of Americans.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tim Geithner</h4><p begin="00:48:01.99" data-timestamp="2881.99">Hold middle-class tax cuts hostage to make permanent tax cuts that go to just the top 2% of earners in the country.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Barack Obama</h4><p begin="00:48:08.99" data-timestamp="2888.99">$700 billion to give a tax cut worth an average of $100,000 to millionaires and billionaires.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tim Geithner</h4><p begin="00:48:16.71" data-timestamp="2896.71">To have us go out and borrow $700 billion from our children.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Barack Obama</h4><p begin="00:48:20.09" data-timestamp="2900.09">And it's a tax cut economists say would do little to add momentum to our economy.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tim Geithner</h4><p begin="00:48:26.12" data-timestamp="2906.12">Little impact on job creation and years of stagnation in middle-class wages.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:48:31.34" data-timestamp="2911.34">I thought, OK, this election is on. The Democrats would force the issue to the floor and make Republicans vote against a middle-class tax cut weeks before an election. We do have a two party system, and the system is working.</p><p begin="00:48:44.52" data-timestamp="2924.52">Predictably, the Republicans pushed back with classic rapid response. Senator John Kyl distilled this into two words: class warfare. The Democrats' response? They panicked, and went into their usual crouch. A day later it was decided, the issue would be shelved until after the election. They would not run on tax cuts, the most effective political issue in America.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:49:08.04" data-timestamp="2948.04">They shelved it. Explain that to me.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Begala</h4><p begin="00:49:09.88" data-timestamp="2949.88">I can't. I can't defend it, put it that way. Explain it, because they let the senators who are up in this election cycle make the decision on whether they voted on it. And the ones who were up in the cycle said, &quot;You know, I'd just as soon just go home and run my campaign, rather than have the tax issue up.&quot; I think it was a colossal mistake.</p><p begin="00:49:27.57" data-timestamp="2967.57">I cannot imagine the Republicans leaving an issue on the table that they win by two to one, eight weeks before an election. I cannot imagine that. It is a divisive issue, by the way, but it divides two to one for the Democrats. It really infuriated me.</p><p begin="00:49:42.25" data-timestamp="2982.25">But, see, that's what I mean. There are people in my party-- and, believe me, I was involved in meetings with strategists, not politicians, where we were screaming and I was hurling foul language about this. And the view was, Democrats can never win a race on taxes. We can never win if the issue is taxes.</p><p begin="00:50:02.52" data-timestamp="3002.52">I just don't agree. I went to them. I gave them a chart out of <i>The</i> <i>Wall Street</i> <i>Journal</i> that tracked the tax issue during the '08 election. On Labor Day, John McCain and Barack Obama were tied on the question of who do you trust on the issue of taxes. Barack Obama went out every single day after that and said, &quot;If you make less than $250,000, I'm going to cut your taxes. If you make more, I'm not.&quot; Every day. By election day, he was ahead, I think, by 16 points. Maybe 14.</p><p begin="00:50:26.99" data-timestamp="3026.99">So what does that tell you? It tells you, if you wage the fight, you can win the fight. But you can't win it if you don't fight.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:50:33.42" data-timestamp="3033.42">I'm going to back to this question. So why-- do you have any inside information as to why the Obama administration and the Democratic party at large abandoned a sure thing for this campaign, tactically? Was there a secret theory that they had that this would work in their favor?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Begala</h4><p begin="00:50:52.18" data-timestamp="3052.18">They believed that any discussion of taxes would be to their detriment.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:50:56.86" data-timestamp="3056.86">There's always an excuse. I had come to Washington to understand the new media strategy of the revived Democratic party. Instead, I discovered something the dated back almost a century, to when Will Rogers first cracked that joke. &quot;I'm not a member of an organized party. I'm a Democrat.&quot; I couldn't believe it. Again, I turned to Begala.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:51:16.98" data-timestamp="3076.98">It's not even clear that the Democrats have a messaging machine. Is there a phone call? Does a fax go out? Is an email-- is anybody in touch with anybody every day? Is there?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Begala</h4><p begin="00:51:28.08" data-timestamp="3088.08">My guess is it's those three different operations, the House, the Senate, and the White House. And they try to intersect as much as they can. But, no, I don't think the Democrats have this sort of coordination. It's looser. I've been working toward that goal all my career. But I don't think we're there. But it is true, they're never going to be as organized. Now, there is--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:51:46.43" data-timestamp="3106.43">But why is that?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Begala</h4><p begin="00:51:47.13" data-timestamp="3107.13">It's the nature of the culture. It just is. I'm serious. I mean, I've done center-left parties all around the world. And in the main, people who tend to be very corporate, very hierarchical, very obedient, tend to be conservative. We don't just sit waiting for orders on the left.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:52:04.04" data-timestamp="3124.04">So there is no daily phone call or email that goes out from the White House, saying here's what we want to push today. Here's what we want to try to-- Here's what you should be talking about, or alerting the nation to.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Begala</h4><p begin="00:52:17.23" data-timestamp="3137.23">Right. No, there is definitely is an email that goes out every day to talkers. And there are talkers' calls and so forth. I have to say, I don't get on them. And I'm a pretty loyal Democrat. And I don't read their talking points on the air. I don't.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:52:28.70" data-timestamp="3148.7">Maybe that explains what's happening.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Begala</h4><p begin="00:52:30.55" data-timestamp="3150.55">That's what I mean, it's a cultural thing. Here am I, a total Democrat, and I'm an Obama Democrat, and I get stuff from the White House and I delete it. I was like, I&quot;m not going to read that crap on the air. I think for myself.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:52:40.98" data-timestamp="3160.98">Paul, you were just saying how you were trying to build this infrastructure to mimic the Grover Norquist machine. I mean, if you're not paying attention to your own machine, or the one that you're trying to build, why would any Democrat?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Begala</h4><p begin="00:52:54.76" data-timestamp="3174.76">Right. That's a good point. But, also, some of it is, I think, as I said, we like to think for ourselves. Second, you know, you're going to have to bleep this, but my [BLEEP] is better than their [BLEEP], so I don't need their [BLEEP].</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:53:07.29" data-timestamp="3187.29">Did you get that? Even Begala, who's dream has been to create such a machine, admits that he'd rather hit the delete button and fling is own poo. Which explains why the many voices of the Democrats today sound like argle bargle white noise, muttered into a thousand microphones, against which the clarity of the conservative position rings as the only truth anyone can hear.</p><p begin="00:53:28.20" data-timestamp="3208.2">Though Begala told me he actually thinks the Democrats have gotten better in the last few weeks, and that there will be some surprises among the massive losses. Constructive surprises.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Begala</h4><p begin="00:53:36.94" data-timestamp="3216.94">You will find the Democrats who survived are the ones who had some spine. They're not the ones who ran away saying, &quot;Oh, I didn't vote for health care and I didn't--&quot; you know? No. They're going to be the ones who stood up.</p><p begin="00:53:47.19" data-timestamp="3227.19">I'm leaving now, tonight, literally, to go to Ohio to campaign for this guy John Boccieri. Iraq war veteran, he represents a district that had been Republican for 60 years before he was elected. He cast one of the deciding votes for the health care bill. And he was asked about it in his heavily Republican district. And he said, &quot;You know, I used to think I was brave and I used to think I was tough. And I've flown into enemy fire, been on four combat missions. But I'm just not tough enough to look this lady in the eye who's got breast cancer and tell her I'm going to deny her health care. I'm not tough enough to do that. If you want somebody that tough, you've got to vote for a Republican.&quot;</p><p begin="00:54:18.63" data-timestamp="3258.63">I love this guy. I think John Boccieri is going to win. I hope he does, I'm going out to help him. But if he does, people will say, well maybe I ought to be more like that than like some of these, you know, wimpier Democrats.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jack Hitt</h4><p begin="00:54:36.84" data-timestamp="3276.84">Everyone is comparing this year's coming tsunami to the wave election of 1994. That's when the Republicans took control of the House from the Democrats for the first time since 1947. But that was only half the story. The Republicans in those days were led by a lethargic bore of a man named Bob Michael. He was rotund in a 19th century cartoon sort of way and spoke in tones that would silence a colicky baby. The young punk and back-bencher Newt Gingrich had declared war that year, not only on Democrats, but on Bob Michael. Michael's certainty that Democrats would always control the House and that his job was to speak carefully and collect their crumbs was the real corruption Gingrich identified as the rot in the old Republican party. One that would welcome conservative ideas.</p><p begin="00:55:24.74" data-timestamp="3324.74">The current Tea Party rebellion is much the same. They've primaried and overthrown some of the most establishment old bull conservatives in Congress. And next year they'll probably destroy Republican moderates, like Lindsey Graham of South Carolina and Olympia Snow of Maine. Meanwhile, Democrats live one election at a time, one issue at a time, unaware that they've become electoral wallpaper, in front of which daring conservatives delight in performing, and that the Democrats' only hope is to understand that their real enemy is not the Republican party, but their own imaginations.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:56:02.35" data-timestamp="3362.35">Jack Hitt lives in New Haven.</p><p begin="00:56:05.17" data-timestamp="3365.17">[MUSIC - &quot;WHOSE SIDE ARE YOU ON?&quot; BY G.W.
