  <h3>Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.42" data-timestamp="0.42">OK. Here was the idea. They were private investigators, detectives for hire. But they were not grizzled ex-cops in trench coats or old Humphrey Bogart characters in fedoras dragging on their unfiltered cigarettes. They were moms. Soccer moms. Telegenic, sexy soccer moms. Like some kind of MILF <i>Charlie's</i> <i>Angels.</i></p><p begin="00:00:23.99" data-timestamp="23.99">And you could see why that would be a hit. Why people loved that. And why it was really only a matter of time before they would show up as guests on some daytime TV talk show.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Announcer</h4><p begin="00:00:33.46" data-timestamp="33.46">On the next <i>Dr.</i> <i>Phil.</i> They look like your average moms, but they're also--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Phil</h4><p begin="00:00:39.32" data-timestamp="39.32">Gun-packing, hard-hitting private investigators. You would never expect a mom in a minivan to be somebody that's on your tail.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:48.84" data-timestamp="48.84">The brains behind this whole PI Mom thing was a guy named Chris Butler. The way Chris Butler tells it, he'd been running a regular PI firm, when at some point, he hired a mom. According to Chris, this was the Eureka moment. He realized that moms have what it takes to crack cases. Patience, organization, hunches. Plus, they blend in.</p><p begin="00:01:10.72" data-timestamp="70.72">Anyway, that was the shtick. Chris called it his secret formula, and he built a business around it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chris Butler</h4><p begin="00:01:14.97" data-timestamp="74.97">--5 PM. Charmagne to run on point. That photograph in front of you is when we did recon. That's what the layout of the rooms look like.</p><p begin="00:01:24.16" data-timestamp="84.16">Inside this bag, we have the recording units.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:27.19" data-timestamp="87.19">Chris Butler didn't want to talk to us. This is a tape of him running a sting operation on the hour-long episode of <i>Dr.</i> <i>Phil</i> that featured the PI Moms. And that was just one of several big media appearances for Chris and the Moms last year. There was also the <i>Today</i> show, and Fox and a <i>People</i> magazine article, complete with a two page photo spread of three PI Moms in dresses and heels, posed like <i>Charlie's</i> <i>Angels</i>.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pete Crooks</h4><p begin="00:01:48.83" data-timestamp="108.83">I was first approached by his PR agent in Beverly Hills. And his PR agent told me that they had just been on the <i>Today</i> show, and in <i>People</i>, and they were just about to be on <i>Dr.</i> <i>Phil</i>, so I should write about them.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:01.26" data-timestamp="121.26">This is Pete Crooks, a writer and an editor for <i>Diablo</i> magazine. <i>Diablo</i> is one of those glossy monthly magazines that lots of places have, like <i>Chicago</i> magazine, or <i>Columbus</i> <i>Monthly,</i> or <i>Memphis</i> magazine. This particular one serves the East Bay area outside of San Francisco, including the Diablo Valley. The magazine has restaurant reviews, and local events listings, and enthusiastic profiles of celebrities from the area. Pete covers entertainment, so he is the one who got the pitch from the PI Moms public relations person, who told him that Chris Butler and the Moms had just landed a deal for a reality TV show on Lifetime.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pete Crooks</h4><p begin="00:02:36.90" data-timestamp="156.9">I said, you know, we have to do something about this, if this is going to be the <i>Real</i> <i>Housewives</i> meets <i>Magnum</i> <i>PI,</i> and it's all filmed in our backyard with East Bay moms and an East Bay boss. And the PR specialist promised me that I could do more than interview. I could actually ride along on a case and be embedded with the PI Moms.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:58.73" data-timestamp="178.73">Which was perfect. He'd be ahead of the curve on this story that was going to be on national TV and had this strong local angle, and it was a fun story. An easy story. One day reporting, where you ride around with the Moms, photos on a stakeout or whatever, which could be great, a quick writeup. And that is the way it started.</p><p begin="00:03:18.22" data-timestamp="198.22">But Pete ended up spending months on this simple feature story. It unfolded and unfolded, and by the end, he found himself caught up in a very strange world where he was getting mysterious messages from an anonymous informant. For a while, it was unclear who he should believe about anything. And things that were not what they seemed were even less what they seemed than he thought they were when he first discovered they were not what they seemed.</p><p begin="00:03:41.15" data-timestamp="221.15">Today we take you on that same trip that he went on. From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life,</i> distributed by Public Radio International. I'm Ira Glass. Today, The Incredible Case of the PI Moms. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>

<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: A Pretty Dame Walks In</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:01.04" data-timestamp="241.04">Josh Bearman has been talking to all the available witnesses in this case, and he tells the story.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Joshuah Bearman</h4><p begin="00:04:07.58" data-timestamp="247.58">So one Thursday, Pete Crooks goes down to the PI Moms HQ. He introduces himself, gets some background interviews, and meets the Moms and their boss, Chris Butler, the man at the wheel of this minivan full of suburban spy gals.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pete Crooks</h4><p begin="00:04:20.31" data-timestamp="260.31">Chris met me at the office. He was Mr. Cool, like right out of a TV show. In fact, I walked in and I saw there were framed 8 by 10 photos of, like, Colombo, and Cagney and Lacey, and <i>Magnum</i> <i>PI,</i> <i>Charlie's</i> <i>Angels,</i> all the '70s and '80s private eyes from television.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Joshuah Bearman</h4><p begin="00:04:45.82" data-timestamp="285.82">Chris actually was an ex-cop, but he parted ways with official law enforcement because, as he described it on his website, his bosses, quote, &quot;had trouble understanding his tactics and drive,&quot; which were well above the standards of routine performance.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pete Crooks</h4><p begin="00:04:58.39" data-timestamp="298.39">And then was Chris, who's this kind of puffed-up sort of burly guy with a blond goatee and, like, a very tight black sort of motorcycle T-shirt, tight jeans, and very expensive leather boots. He kind of looked like a biker cowboy cop. His voice just didn't ever change octaves. He definitely had this coolest guy in the room attitude.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Joshuah Bearman</h4><p begin="00:05:38.03" data-timestamp="338.03">Chris sat behind his desk, flanked by his posters and two PI Moms, Charmagne Peters and Denise Antoon. Pete had seen them all on the TV segments. And then, to everyone's surprise, a client showed up.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pete Crooks</h4><p begin="00:05:51.23" data-timestamp="351.23">Chris sees a a luxury car pull into the parking lot right outside his office. And this woman comes in who has retained Chris to follow her fiance around to see if he's fooling around. She comes in. Attractive woman in her early 50s. And she explains the situation, and tells me how long she's been engaged to this man, and where she met him.</p><p begin="00:06:16.37" data-timestamp="376.37">And over the past six to eight months, she's just really noticed that he's becoming more distant, and that he goes to the gym on Saturdays. And he used to go for an hour and a half to work out and come home. Now he's gone for seven, eight hours at a time. And on one of these recent trips to the gym, when he came back, she peeked inside his gym bag, and all his workout clothes were clean and neatly folded.</p><p begin="00:06:44.88" data-timestamp="404.88">She was composed, and then she starts crying and saying that she just needs to know. If there's some funny business going on, she needs to know now. Because this is a big point in their relationship</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Joshuah Bearman</h4><p begin="00:06:59.90" data-timestamp="419.9">It's like the opening scene of practically every detective film ever made. Nice-looking lady walks into the PI's office with tears in her eyes. Except this one has a PI Moms twist.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pete Crooks</h4><p begin="00:07:09.07" data-timestamp="429.07">When the client starts to cry, one of the PI Moms jumped up with a box of tissues, and the other one said, &quot;Do you need a hug?&quot; And they had this sort of bonding moment, the two PI Moms and the crying client.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Joshuah Bearman</h4><p begin="00:07:22.65" data-timestamp="442.65">The client suggested the following Saturday for the stakeout. Charmagne and Denise were ready with their surveillance minivan, [UNINTELLIGIBLE] recording devices, and the subject's photo and address. Pete sat in the back, taking notes.</p><p begin="00:07:34.82" data-timestamp="454.82">Sure enough, the fiance quickly appeared. They followed him to a 24 Hour Fitness, where Denise and Charmagne videotaped him going in.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pete Crooks</h4><p begin="00:07:42.21" data-timestamp="462.21">Within ten minutes, he came out of that same front door of the gym, dressed up in this sort of Tommy Bahamas leisure wear. Like khaki slacks and dress shoes and a short-sleeved sort of dressy shirt.
