So I was in.</p><p begin="00:45:09.70" data-timestamp="2709.7">Reading the first one was unlike anything I've ever read. Here's first line. "In the myriadic year of our Lord, the 10,000th year of the king undying, the kindly prince of death, Gideon Nav packed her sword, her shoes, and her dirty magazines. And she escaped from the house of the night." Gideon is a Cavalier, which is kind of like a knight, but she's not like a classic knight. She refers to people as douchebags, and she's constantly saying things like, "That's what she said."</p><p begin="00:45:40.84" data-timestamp="2740.84">Unlike most queer books, there's barely any sex or romance or making out. The words "gay," "lesbian," "queer" never even show up. People are queer, but it's more like their personalities, their humor. It's just understood. And I'll just say, I'm sure these books aren't for everyone, but I love them.</p><p begin="00:45:59.74" data-timestamp="2759.74">I read the first book one summer a few years ago. I was on vacation with a few friends, and we all got pulled into it at the same time. And it was like we all just fell into this kind of imaginary space that Tamsyn had created, kind of youthful and very gay, in a fun rather than oppressive way, which, Tamsyn says, is exactly what she'd hoped for. Tamsyn says she wrote this book for her 17-year-old self, who really could have used books and worlds like this back then.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:46:26.32" data-timestamp="2786.32"> Just ways to be queer that weren't inherently sad. The very mainstream LGBQT stuff, it was miserable. And I was already miserable, because if I wanted to read gay literature at the time, it was always like, my sad, coming-out summer. And yeah, I didn't want to read my sad coming-out summer for the umpty-umpth time. I'd read it. It was good the first 50 times.</p><p begin="00:46:53.44" data-timestamp="2813.44">What I really wanted was a big set piece fight. I wanted to read about girls doing big set piece fights. I mean, that was the era of <i>The</i> <i>Matrix,</i> and I'd also just gotten super into Hong Kong kung fu cinema. And all I did was imagine big kung fu fights.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:47:12.29" data-timestamp="2832.29"> Tamsyn ended up dropping out of high school. She says she wasn't a good student. She'd been put in the class for the, quote, "stupid kids." She said she spent most of high school dyeing her hair, shaving it ways nobody liked, sitting in the back of the class, her eyes glazed over, daydreaming about big martial arts fights. She came out in high school, in a small conservative New Zealand suburb, a different era.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:47:35.96" data-timestamp="2855.96"> And one of my best friends stopped talking to me immediately. And I don't even blame her. I think a lot of people forget how dangerous and grotesque being gay was presented back at the time.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:47:50.15" data-timestamp="2870.15"> She was bullied, beaten up. And to escape, she wrote stories, which is not such a surprise given where she was hanging out online. It's a place she stumbled on when she was about 11.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:48:01.97" data-timestamp="2881.97"> My dad had been on ARPANET, so was that connected with old-school internet. And the internet of 1996 was just a wild frontier. That was cowboy territory. Anything was happening. And I just wanted to look up cool pictures of Sailor Moon or go on ICQ and talk about animals and gargoyles, and I found fanfiction, and I didn't look back.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:48:31.88" data-timestamp="2911.88"> Fanfiction, a whole world of people equally obsessed with stories and sci-fi, to the point they were going online to write their own versions of the stories they loved, <i>X-Files,</i> <i>Lord</i> <i>of</i> <i>the</i> <i>Rings.</i> They fix the things they feel the author got wrong. There's tons of shipping, that is, people deciding which characters should have ended up together and rewriting it so that they do.</p><p begin="00:48:54.38" data-timestamp="2934.38">None of this is for money, for the most part. It's just for fun. Tamsyn was just a kid, but she starts publishing stories there, stuff she never would have taken to school. It's the first place she ever published anything, the first place she ever really showed anyone her work.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:49:09.59" data-timestamp="2949.59"> And I think one of the reasons I got into it initially was because it felt so illegal and faintly naughty. Fanfiction, especially back then, was like the dark web for nerds. You read a story, and the author's note immediately says, please don't sue me. It's like punk for dorks.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:49:30.56" data-timestamp="2970.56"> Punk for dorks, in other words, her people. A lot of people think of fanfic as lowbrow, embarrassing. A lot of big sci-fi fantasy writers slam it. George R. R. Martin, who wrote <i>Game</i> <i>of</i> <i>Thrones,</i> has said it's copyright infringement, a violation of intellectual property. Anne Rice, who wrote <i>Interview</i> <i>With</i> <i>the</i> <i>Vampire,</i> has famously objected to people writing fic about her stories, because who wouldn't want to ship Brad Pitt and Tom Cruise?</p><p begin="00:49:58.94" data-timestamp="2998.94">There are some writers who, like Tamsyn, write fic before going mainstream. But once they make it mainstream, they might delete their accounts, delete all the things they wrote pre-mainstream days. Not Tamsyn. Tamsyn still loves these worlds. When the first book in the series came out, <i>Gideon</i> <i>the</i> <i>Ninth,</i> it was a big deal, nominated for the biggest sci-fi awards.</p><p begin="00:50:22.04" data-timestamp="3022.04">A review in <i>The</i> <i>New</i> <i>York</i> <i>Times</i> called it "meticulous and moody, haunted by difficult and complex feelings in a wasted universe." And in all this excitement about the book, Tamsyn heard about some buzz from the place where she'd always felt so comfortable, one of the main fanfiction sites, AO3, or Archive of Our Own. One day, she saw something she'd never seen before. People had started writing fanfic about her work.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:50:48.05" data-timestamp="3048.05"> And I think I saw there were eight fanfics. And I was like, oh, oh, oh. I'm feeling a lot of different emotions, and I have to run away right now. I cannot look at even what these titles are.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:51:01.06" data-timestamp="3061.06"> Why?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:51:01.51" data-timestamp="3061.51"> You know inherently starting out in fan fiction that you are not allowed to read it if it's for your own creation.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:51:09.91" data-timestamp="3069.91"> What are the reasons?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:51:11.33" data-timestamp="3071.33"> So I think one of the main things is that you worry that if you read fan fiction and then you write anything that mimics anything that somebody has written about your property, you just committed a huge cardinal sin against your own fandom. You just stole candy from the world's most important baby. You ripped off your own fans. You are scum. Even if you hadn't meant to, there is no way that you can prove that you didn't nick off somebody else's ideas.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:51:41.02" data-timestamp="3101.02"> Tamsyn says that writers in general are told pretty firmly to not read fanfic about their work. There needs to be no question of where their ideas come from. Earlier this year, a fanfic writer sued the Tolkien Estate and Amazon Studios for $250 million, alleging they'd ripped off his storyline for <i>The</i> <i>Lord</i> <i>of</i> <i>the</i> <i>Rings</i> prequel. The Tolkien Trust countersued for copyright infringement.</p><p begin="00:52:04.88" data-timestamp="3124.88">If you look up Tamsyn's work on the big fic sites today, there are thousands of entries, people writing, interacting, playing around in the world of <i>The</i> <i>Locked</i> <i>Tomb.</i> Tamsyn can't participate in any of it. And it kills her because, again, these are her people. And now that it's about her own books even more so her people.</p><p begin="00:52:25.58" data-timestamp="3145.58">Not only do they like the same books, but they are devoted to her characters, her humor, the entire fantastical sci-fi universe that lives in her head. So the people who loved it were like a custom-made group of people who could not possibly have interests more tailored to Tamsyn's own brain, all milling around in one spot.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:52:44.42" data-timestamp="3164.42"> Do you have questions about what's going on in--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:52:47.90" data-timestamp="3167.9"> Oh, so many. I want to know everything. Because fandom is so deeply about shipping, whose faces do you want to make mush together? I'm just sitting here, eating popcorn, being like, who do you want to make mush? Come on. Who are you making kiss? I want to figure it out.</p><p begin="00:53:08.19" data-timestamp="3188.19">And then, when I realized that I wasn't going to be able to do that, I'm the kind of person who doesn't do anything by halves. I'm all in or I'm all out. And I realized then that just being a writer, I kind of didn't want to interact with anyone at all. I was all about that hermit lifestyle.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:53:30.23" data-timestamp="3210.23"> Oh, yeah. I mean, it seems like it must be hard to avoid. You're just a couple keystrokes away just to quickly peek. Is it ever hard?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:53:39.20" data-timestamp="3219.2"> It's impossible. That's why most of the internet is banned. I have a beautiful program called Freedom, and I have banned myself from looking pretty much anywhere, because I know I would go look, I would go look at everything. So I had to be sensible, and I've banned myself from fandom.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:53:59.63" data-timestamp="3239.63"> You had to exile yourself.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:54:01.37" data-timestamp="3241.37"> I have exiled myself. I have left. I have gone into the West.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:54:05.54" data-timestamp="3245.54"> That seems like it would have its own kind of melancholy.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:54:08.15" data-timestamp="3248.15"> Yeah. Yeah, it's lonely. You know how I said I wanted no loneliness? I got loneliness.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:54:12.95" data-timestamp="3252.95"> Wow.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:54:13.49" data-timestamp="3253.49"> Yeah. It's a particular kind of melancholy that I didn't think I would ever experience.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:54:26.60" data-timestamp="3266.6"> Talking to her, somehow I couldn't help but tell her what it was like being in that world among her fans. I told her about that summer vacation I spent listening to her book together with friends, how we bought a little boat and named it <i>Ianthe.</i> It was one of the characters in the books, a villain. People online call her a Draco in leather pants.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:54:45.44" data-timestamp="3285.44"> That boat's going to sink. Why would you name your boat <i>Ianthe?</i> Your boat's cursed.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:54:51.59" data-timestamp="3291.59"> We would jump off of it into the lake and yell "Ianthe" as we were jumping into the lake.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:54:56.72" data-timestamp="3296.72"> Sorry. I don't mean to roast you about the boat. It's just, that's the greatest thing I've ever heard.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:55:00.47" data-timestamp="3300.47"> Fandom around her books, speaking from experience, it's really fun. This is what she's missing.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:55:07.81" data-timestamp="3307.81"> It's like you created this whole world that would be perfect for you and perfect for your 17-year-old self.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:55:13.96" data-timestamp="3313.96"> It's your own world, and you can't even post there. You've created the perfect thing for you, and you're on the outside of it. You've been monkey's pawed. You've created the perfect fandom, and you can never walk inside it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:55:26.44" data-timestamp="3326.44"> Yeah, yeah.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tamsyn Muir</h4><p begin="00:55:28.42" data-timestamp="3328.42"> You know how Frodo says that he saved the Shire, but not for himself?
