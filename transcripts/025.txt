  <h3>Act One: Bulls in Our Hearts</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.92" data-timestamp="0.92">The story goes like this. Recently I heard about this couple. He's in his 40s. She's in her mid-20s. And the way it was explained to me, they were having this conversation, and it was one of these conversations that, if you've ever been in a couple with a big age difference, maybe you've taken part in, he was saying basically, what can you see in me? You know, you are so young and energetic and I'm this older person. I'm so much more serious.</p><p begin="00:00:29.32" data-timestamp="29.32">And they talked about this and talked about this. And finally, she brought the conversation to a stop by declaring, no, no, no, don't you understand? I am Dennis Rodman. You are Phil Jackson. You ground me. I am wild. You make everything all right. And that's the way it is right now in Chicago, where I speak to you from right now. Our historic championship basketball team, the Chicago Bulls, is not just the winningest team of all time, with 72 regular season wins, now hurtling through the NBA playoffs. What's happened here in Chicago is that the Bulls are now the lens through which we view all of our experiences. They're the benchmark against we're measuring everything in our lives. They've become our reality.</p><p begin="00:01:32.71" data-timestamp="92.71">Today, in this hour, a dispatch from a city that has left the rest of the American culture and entered into its own little subculture, a basketball subculture. At nights in Chicago, now the Hancock building, which towers over the city, is lit a bright Bulls-red. Bulls posters and souvenirs and caps and T-shirts are for sale in every video storage and corner drugstore, in bars, everywhere. It's like living in China during the cultural revolution. Slogans and propaganda, everywhere, all uniting us as one people. The name United Center was never more appropriate than now. There we are, united behind the vanguard-- Michael, Scottie, Dennis, Phil, Luke, our vanguard, and all the others.</p><p begin="00:02:25.18" data-timestamp="145.18">Well, from WBEZ Chicago, I'm Ira Glass. You're listening to <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i> And I know actually that many people who have never heard our program before around the country are hearing us for the first time today. So a quick word of explanation, the idea of this radio show is to document what's going on around the country, in these United States, using all the different forms of radio storytelling that we can think of, including documentary, monologues, found tapes, just whatever we can think of. Each week, we choose a theme. And our theme for this hour is basketball-- stories of people who have a basketball jones of one sort or another.</p><p begin="00:03:04.34" data-timestamp="184.34">Act One of our show today, The Bulls in Our Hearts. Act Two, Bulls in a Barbershop. Act Three, What Basketball Means, stories of people who love the game. Act Four, Basketball and Commerce, in which, among other things, we visit Bulls star Scottie Pippen's car dealership. And Act Five, the greatest moment in radio-- ever. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act Two: Bulls in a Barber Shop</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:42.97" data-timestamp="222.97">Act One, Bulls in Our Hearts. As a way to measure the place of the Bulls in the conscious and unconscious minds of everyday Chicagoans, consider this case, members of the radio jury. Anaheed was watching a game with her parents. They'd driven in from Detroit, the parents did, for her 26th birthday, and started arguing over Dennis Rodman.</p><p begin="00:04:10.05" data-timestamp="250.05">Just a quick word to public radio listeners who perhaps are not following our national basketball pastime as carefully as they might, Dennis Rodman is the player who you may have seen pictures of with the colored hair, the tattoos, the piercings. He's the one who was involved with Madonna for a while, possibly the greatest rebounder of all time. Anaheed's father did not like him. He's a rebel. He's a terrible man. Well, I'll let Anaheed tell the story.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Anaheed</h4><p begin="00:04:37.22" data-timestamp="277.22">He said, &quot;What kind of role model is he? He's no role model. This man is the Antichrist.&quot; And I said, &quot;I don't think he's the Antichrist, Dad.&quot;</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:47.20" data-timestamp="287.2">What developed between them was the biggest argument Anaheed has had with her parents since she was a child, since she lived at home. It lasted for an hour. And what was remarkable about it is that it got to the most basic issues any family argument can ever get to.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Anaheed</h4><p begin="00:05:06.12" data-timestamp="306.12">He said, &quot;You like this craziness, you are just trying to go against everything of this family. You know that's not what we stand for. You're trying to go against the family. You're trying to divide yourself as much as possible from this family, by liking Dennis Rodman.&quot;</p><p begin="00:05:24.01" data-timestamp="324.01">All of a sudden, I was back in high school all of a sudden. And he hadn't been so completely-- Like I mean, the fight got to the point where he was telling me, like, that I don't love him, and I'm trying to break up our relationship. He wants to have a good relationship with me. He can't even talk to me, and I don't like him, and I don't respect him. And me telling him, I don't think you listen to me, and I don't think you care about the things that I care about, and you don't even know anything about me.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:52.07" data-timestamp="352.07">And it's all because of Dennis Rodman.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Anaheed</h4><p begin="00:05:53.63" data-timestamp="353.63">And it's all because of little Dennis Rodman.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:58.49" data-timestamp="358.49">It is impossible to imagine fighting like this, Anaheed says, over any other public figure.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Anaheed</h4><p begin="00:06:04.73" data-timestamp="364.73">My dad and I are both big basketball fans, and we used to go to Pistons games all the time together when I was in high school, and that was sort of the only thing we had in common. And I think that the NBA to my dad is this really great organization. They're really wonderful, and he really loves the game. And to see somebody like Dennis Rodman sort of flip off the whole organization, I think to him sort of symbolizes me and my role in my family. And everything that I'm doing to him is what Dennis is doing to the NBA.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:47.74" data-timestamp="407.74">Then there are the dreams. I realized a few weeks ago, actually, that even though I had not seen a Bulls game in two years, personally, and even though at the time I really didn't care less about the Bulls-- I wasn't following it at all-- the Bulls are such a presence in everyday life in Chicago-- in conversations, on the street, products, and magazines, and stuff about them everywhere-- that even I was having dreams about them. They had entered my unconscious picture of the world to that extent.</p><p begin="00:07:20.05" data-timestamp="440.05">So anyway, here in Chicago just last week, I went on the radio and I invited Chicagoans to call in and record their dreams about the Bulls. And people responded.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Woman 1</h4><p begin="00:07:30.39" data-timestamp="450.39">Maybe five or six months ago, I was at a really stressed out time at work preparing for an audit. I had a dream during that period that the auditing team showed up at my office as planned, only the chief auditor was Michael Jordan. I was really nervous and stressed out. And there's Michael Jordan in my office. And it somehow developed, while I was having a conversation with Michael Jordan about the audit, and I wasn't completely prepared, but he knew I wasn't ready, and he decided that, in exchange for oral sexual favors, he would make sure we passed the audit. And I had a conversation with my boss about it, and my boss was, like, &quot;Well, I can't really tell you that you should do this, but it's Michael Jordan, how bad can it be?&quot; And we passed the audit. One of the other really funny things about this is that it's Scottie Pippen who I find absolutely just adorable, not Michael.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Woman 2</h4><p begin="00:08:44.48" data-timestamp="524.48">I'm 52 years old. I teach at DePaul University. Last Sunday, I dreamt that I was Michael Jordan.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Woman 3</h4><p begin="00:08:54.64" data-timestamp="534.64">My dream was that my friend was having a bar mitzvah, and he knew Michael Jordan. And so I was going to be there at the bar mitzvah with him.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Woman 4</h4><p begin="00:09:04.98" data-timestamp="544.98">I'm in a hot tub with Michael Jordan. And he says to me, &quot;I love my wife, but you're my best friend.&quot;</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:09:18.37" data-timestamp="558.37">As weird as the Michael Jordan dreams are, the Dennis Rodman dreams are way weirder. One guy called up to describe a dream in which a mob was attacking Dennis Rodman because he was a Communist. In another dream, somebody else called in to say that a woman said that Dennis appeared in a dream of hers in a feather boa in an appliance store to tell the woman that the playoffs are fixed. It's fixed. It's done. It's fixed.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Woman 5</h4><p begin="00:09:46.40" data-timestamp="586.4">I dreamed that Dennis Rodman was swimming in the ocean, and he was way far out, really deep. And everybody was scared that the undertow was going to drag him away.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Man 1</h4><p begin="00:09:58.57" data-timestamp="598.57">I had a dream that Dennis Rodman was a woman. And the two of us worked together at White Castle. Except what was interesting about Dennis was that he had no hands. And on one hand, he had a fork, and on the other hand, he had a spoon. And I felt jilted because he wouldn't talk to me. So the majority of my dream was me feeling dissed by the female Dennis Rodman with a fork and a spoon for hands.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:10:36.39" data-timestamp="636.39">Other people weigh in with their basketball stories, coming up.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act2">
  <h3>Act Three: The Meaning of Basketball</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:11:57.28" data-timestamp="717.28">Act Two, Bulls in a Barber Shop. And no one's going to help him, and I'm sitting there feeling like my pain is becoming less and then lessening. And there was all this pain that Michael Jordan had absorbed for me. And somehow, Michael Jordan, though, had, for this one second, basically neutralized a pain that I had felt my whole life, and this pain with my father, and a pain of just your existence, of being alone.</p><p begin="00:55:43.83" data-timestamp="3343.83">And for this one second, I could feel it. It was the first time in my life that I ever-- and I woke up from the dream-- I ever realized that that's a possibility, that pain is something that you don't necessarily need to feel all the time to understand it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:56:06.58" data-timestamp="3366.58"> Why Michael Jordan? If you're going to have a dream like this, why Michael Jordan?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Brett Grossman</h4><p begin="00:56:12.10" data-timestamp="3372.1">Because there's thing, there's Michael Jordan, and you see it in his eyes. And it happened during the Miami series of this year. I don't know how it is, but his eyes turn inward. He becomes very serious and solemn. And you feel that he is taking everyone on the team and just taking all their faults, all their backaches, all their problems, all their ego trips, and he's cushioning them for them. He's saying, &quot;Look, I'm Michael Jordan, I'm going to win the game, you know that, stop. Just let it all loose and we're going to win the game.&quot;</p><p begin="00:56:46.07" data-timestamp="3406.07">And I mean, I'll be at home watching the game on my chair, with my bottle of water, going crazy. And I see Michael's eyes and suddenly it's like, OK, I can relax, he's going to win it for me. And that feeling, it's crazy, but that feeling is something that I think you get from a god, and you get that from, whether false or not, you get that from a religion and an idol.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:57:11.43" data-timestamp="3431.43">That here's somebody who's going to solve my problems and heal my pain.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Brett Grossman</h4><p begin="00:57:16.26" data-timestamp="3436.26">Heal my pain and take away the indecision. There's nothing to worry about, because he's going to win the game.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:57:34.58" data-timestamp="3454.58">Well, hopefully, we Chicagoans will get to see that look in Michael Jordan's eyes again soon.</p></div>  </div>
