And we all kind of begrudgingly raised our hands, not knowing where he was going. I raised my hand.</p><p begin="00:01:46.27" data-timestamp="106.27">And then he asked us, how many of you know the evidence for that? I couldn't quite figure it out then.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:03:20.58" data-timestamp="200.58"> After the lecture, Diane goes home, newly aware of this vast universe of her personal ignorance, just realizing, so many of the things I think are true, they are just things I've heard from someone who probably heard them from somebody else, probably without evidence either. It is related to morning glories.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:05:22.10" data-timestamp="322.1"> She writes a very detailed answer to the question, even includes some old botanical drawings. Now, I know.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>David  Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:05:31.78" data-timestamp="331.78"> OK, and what's the next one you wrote?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Diane Wu</h4><p begin="00:05:36.61" data-timestamp="336.61"> That's the last one I wrote. I wrote one.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David  Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:05:43.10" data-timestamp="343.1"> I asked Diane, what happened? What about the mission?
