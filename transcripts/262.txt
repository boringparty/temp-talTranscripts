  <h3>Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.37" data-timestamp="0.37">Back in the '60s, when Stephen was a young surgeon in Boston, a guy came in with abdominal pain, which turned out to be his gallbladder. He needed an operation. But the guy told Stephen this story of how, 12 years before, he'd come into that exact same hospital and had had cancer. It started in his stomach and had spread to his belly. The doctors opened him up.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Stephen Rosenberg</h4><p begin="00:00:20.40" data-timestamp="20.4">And the surgeons had seen cancer that had spread into his liver and throughout his abdomen. They could do nothing and they just closed the abdomen and sent him home to die. And yet, here he was 12 years later, seeming quite healthy.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:34.45" data-timestamp="34.45">Stephen was skeptical. Cancer usually does not just go away with no treatment at all. The guy must have gotten the story wrong in some way.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Stephen Rosenberg</h4><p begin="00:00:42.02" data-timestamp="42.02">Then I went back to the patient's chart and I read, in fact, the pathology reports. And I was still skeptical. So then I went back and actually pulled out the slides to be sure in fact he had had the cancer. And we reviewed the slides, and in fact, it was a cancer. And then I made sure it was in fact the same patient. And in fact, it was.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:59.63" data-timestamp="59.63">And then when you opened him up, were you fully expecting you were going to still find some cancer?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Stephen Rosenberg</h4><p begin="00:01:04.06" data-timestamp="64.06">Well, it was 12 years later and I didn't know what to expect. I operated on him, took out his gallbladder, examined the inside of his abdomen, and all of the cancer that had been present 12 years earlier had completely disappeared in the absence of any treatment.</p><p begin="00:01:24.41" data-timestamp="84.41">It appeared that this patient's body had somehow rejected his cancer. This patient set me on a quest for the next 25 years to try to explain how such a thing could happen.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:37.23" data-timestamp="97.23">And in fact, this doctor, Stephen Rosenberg, is now a pioneer in inventing immunotherapies based on that research at The National Cancer Institute, where he's chief of surgery. Now, it's interesting, you can find the story about this early case of his all over the place. It circulates on the internet, it shows up in books about people spontaneously recovering from cancer, and he is not happy about that. He kind of hates it, in fact. Because he says that this story gives people false hope. In his career, he's seen maybe 10,000 cancer patients, and this is the only case like this that he has ever encountered. It is just fantastically rare. And it is so easy, he says, for patients to get their hopes up. How could anybody not get their hopes up? And Dr. Rosenberg has seen lots of patients taken advantage of by people who play on this dream of a spontaneous recovery.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Stephen Rosenberg</h4><p begin="00:02:27.84" data-timestamp="147.84">People who prey on cancer patients, offering quack treatments, often take advantage of cancer patients who are desperate. And I've seen entire families devastated as their entire resources are devoted to treatments of no value.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:53.73" data-timestamp="173.73">Still, hope is important. So what do you do when people place hope in things that probably are not going to work? Well, lots of times his patients come to him asking about vitamins, and herbs, and visualization, and other treatments that have not been proven by science. And if the treatments aren't actually going to hurt them in any way, he says, sure, do it. Studies have shown that patients who are embracing life, feeling more hopeful, feeling more in control of their situation, do better, respond better to medical treatment of all kinds. There are real health effects to hope itself.</p><p begin="00:03:26.17" data-timestamp="206.17">And so, today on our radio show, we have two stories of hope, the power of hope, hope in some things that are not very likely, I have to say. We have stories about the great side of that and the maybe less great side of that. From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i>, distributed by Public Radio International. I'm Ira Glass.</p><p begin="00:03:45.62" data-timestamp="225.62">Our program today in two acts. Act One, a son tries to help his mom in a faraway place defy the laws of medical science. Act Two, a daughter tries to help her dad by going to a faraway place to defy the laws of the United States of America. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>

  <h3>Act One: Changing The Channeler</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:09.11" data-timestamp="249.11">Act One, Changing the Channeler.</p><p begin="00:04:13.03" data-timestamp="253.03">You may remember Davy Rothbart. He's been on our show a few times. At one point, he came onto the show to tell the story of his mother, Barbara, who's a suburban mom in Ann Arbor, Michigan, who seems like lots of funny, smart moms that you have met in your life, except that she channels an ancient being named Aaron. Aaron is a Buddhist monk. Aaron gives advice along Buddhist lines to people who come into the house. He showed up in Barbara's life-- I think that's the right way to say that. He showed up in Barbara's life years ago. Barbara is also deaf. She hasn't been able to hear anything for decades. And a while back, she heard about this healer in Brazil, a man who goes by the name Joao de Deus, John of God, who, people said, could make the deaf hear. And she wanted to go down and see if he could help her out.</p><p begin="00:05:00.45" data-timestamp="300.45">Davy is really close to her and he agreed to along.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Davy Rothbart</h4><p begin="00:05:04.86" data-timestamp="304.86">She was very hopeful about it. She wouldn't say, I'm going to come back, I'm going to be hearing. But I could just tell that, in the months leading up to our trip, she was getting more and more excited about it. I talked to her about this on tape.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Barbara</h4><p begin="00:05:37.62" data-timestamp="337.62">I want to hear Peter singing. Dad's voice, which I have heard, of course, but will not remember.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:51.25" data-timestamp="351.25">Oh, stop the tape for a second, Davy. I want to hear what's happened to music in 40 years.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Davy Rothbart</h4><p begin="00:06:25.43" data-timestamp="385.43">Would you like to hear some 50 Cent?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Barbara</h4><p begin="00:06:29.21" data-timestamp="389.21">Some?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Davy Rothbart</h4><p begin="00:06:29.90" data-timestamp="389.9">50 Cent?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Barbara</h4><p begin="00:06:32.86" data-timestamp="392.86">Want to hear some 50 Cent? Do you mean two quarters clinging together?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Davy Rothbart</h4><p begin="00:06:40.46" data-timestamp="400.46">No, the rapper 50 Cent.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Barbara</h4><p begin="00:06:43.54" data-timestamp="403.54">I've never heard of the rapper 50 Cent. I want to hear everything.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Davy Rothbart</h4><p begin="00:06:50.79" data-timestamp="410.79">Back when my mom could hear, she loved listening to music. Now she still sings sometimes.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:56.89" data-timestamp="416.89">She still sings sometimes?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Davy Rothbart</h4><p begin="00:06:58.36" data-timestamp="418.36">Yeah, it kind of sounds weird. Well, it sounds interesting.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:01.86" data-timestamp="421.86">Because she can't hear herself at all, right?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Davy Rothbart</h4><p begin="00:07:03.76" data-timestamp="423.76">Right, she has 0% hearing.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Barbara</h4><p begin="00:07:06.39" data-timestamp="426.39">A favorite song of mine that I love to hear is &quot;Amazing Grace.&quot; Do you know that? I can't sing it on tune. [SINGING] Amazing grace, how sweet the sound, that saved a soul like me. I once was lost, but now I'm found, was blind but now I see.
