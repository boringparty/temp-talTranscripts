  <h3>Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.53" data-timestamp="0.53">Recently I was talking to this guy who told me how years ago, because of a medical condition, he had to give up beer. He had only been a casual beer drinker before this. He'd never really given beer much thought. But now that he couldn't have a beer, he was thinking of beer all the time.</p><p begin="00:00:18.62" data-timestamp="18.62">He had a fantasy that went like this. He goes into a bar and he orders a beer. That was the whole thing. It's hard giving things up.</p><p begin="00:00:30.28" data-timestamp="30.28">Walter was three, and it was time for him to give up the pacifier. The pacifier was impairing his speech, and so it was time to give it up. And his mom and dad weaned him from it slowly. First, he could only use the pacifier upstairs. Then only upstairs in his room. Then only upstairs in his room, in the bed, though enforcement on the whole bed thing was actually kind of spotty.</p><p begin="00:00:54.07" data-timestamp="54.07">Anyway, finally came the big day. Walter announced he was ready to get rid of the pacifier, and he threw it into the trash himself. And everybody clapped, and everybody hugged him, and then they drove to a toy store and they got him some special presents. A toy tree house, a teddy bear.</p><p begin="00:01:09.65" data-timestamp="69.65">And that first night, when it was time to sleep, Walter was brave. He was stoic. He went right to bed. And maybe-- I don't know-- five minutes passed before he was out of the bedroom and downstairs, crying. &quot;I'm really sad,&quot; he said.</p><p begin="00:01:25.92" data-timestamp="85.92">His mom and dad asked him if he wanted to play with his new toys for a while. And he tried that and then went back to bed. More sobbing. An hour passes. Two hours.</p><p begin="00:01:37.20" data-timestamp="97.2">It breaks his parents' hearts. Frankly, if they still had had the pacifier in the house, they would have caved and given it to him, it was so upsetting.  But he had thrown it into the trash at his grandparents' house. It was gone. And that night, for the first time in his life, Walter slept in his parents' bed. It was the only way that he could actually get to sleep.</p><p begin="00:01:56.62" data-timestamp="116.62">The next night was a little easier. The next a little easier than that.</p><p begin="00:02:00.33" data-timestamp="120.33">Anyway, after a couple of days, Walter announced the special request that he had for all the grown-ups in his life. Grandmas and grandpas, uncles and aunts, the lady next door. He wanted to hear their stories about when they gave up their pacifiers.</p><p begin="00:02:17.04" data-timestamp="137.04">I told this to a friend of mine who gave up drinking a decade ago, how this little boy, Walter, wanted to hear these stories. And she said, man, that's just like AA. Though the problem for Walter, of course, was that no adult really remembers giving up their own pacifier. It's just too long ago, right?</p><p begin="00:02:33.79" data-timestamp="153.79">So everybody either talked about giving up other stuff that they did remember, like giving up their blankets as little kids. One auntie offered to talk to him about how she quit smoking. But she was warned off that. Or they just made up stories, dramatic stories about giving up their pacifiers to tell Walter.</p><p begin="00:02:53.01" data-timestamp="173.01">It's comforting to hear other people's stories of kicking the habit. It's just comforting at any age. It makes it feel like it's possible. And so this week, we bring you four stories of people giving things up, living without, some of them voluntarily, some not.</p><p begin="00:03:09.87" data-timestamp="189.87">From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i>, distributed by Public Radio International. I'm Ira Glass. Act One of our program today, Do You Hear What I Hear? In that act, one man, one teenage daughter, and many, many fish. Act Two, Yerrrrr Out! The baseball story that no baseball player wants to tell. Act Three, The Call of the Great Indoors, in which we hear a defense of living inside from somebody who can speak authoritatively about the pros and cons. Act Four, Tin Man. In that act, life without a heart. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Do You Hear What I Hear?</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:55.24" data-timestamp="235.24"> Act One, Do You Hear What I Hear? This is a story of being forced to give up one thing, and then giving up another by choice. Nubar Alexanian put it together with his daughter Abby.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Nubar Alexanian</h4><p begin="00:04:07.92" data-timestamp="247.92">You know, I didn't talk about it for a long time, because it's so abstract. It's like people can't imagine it.</p><p begin="00:04:19.22" data-timestamp="259.22">[TONE]</p><p begin="00:04:24.18" data-timestamp="264.18">But then I'll be in a restaurant. There's a loud din of people talking and dishes, and all that stuff going on in a restaurant. And somebody will ask me, how loud is it right now? And I'll often say, it's the loudest thing in the room. And people can't-- it's just incomprehensible to people that you could get used to a tone like that in your head all the time. See, like, I'm talking about it right now? It's really loud.</p><p begin="00:04:52.52" data-timestamp="292.52">[TONE]</p><p begin="00:05:00.35" data-timestamp="300.35">The very first doctor I saw was this young ear, nose, and throat guy. And he examined me. But he was examining me as if he were just going through the motions. He already knew what he was going to tell me. So after all the examinations, he said, well, you have tinnitus, and there's no cure for it.  You're just going to have to learn to live with it.</p><p begin="00:05:26.65" data-timestamp="326.65">And I thought, what are you talking about? I can't learn to live with this. And I went through 10 years of five other doctors, going to the high priests in Boston hospitals, to every alternative-- psychics-- you can possibly imagine. I mean, there's almost-- I don't think there's anything-- four or five different acupuncturists-- this one dentist said, well, I'm going to make you a mouth guard. He said, well, I'm sure this will work. It's going to cost $19,000. I did some muscle body work and some homeopathic remedies-- I had my entire jaw realigned by a dentist over a two year period. And then we had things like this guy come in who spread these Petri dishes all over our house. So we had to install all these filtering systems in our house, and fans, and air cleaners, and dehumidifiers-- so I've spent, probably over 10 years, $7,000.</p><p begin="00:06:23.28" data-timestamp="383.28">I'm a photographer, and the first tone appeared around the same time that I started working on this book about musicians called <i>Where Music</i> <i>Comes</i> <i>From.</i> And I travelled around the world with 25 different musicians. And all of a sudden, I had to stop. First tone appeared at the beginning of the project, and then the second tone came, and the whole thing got so bad that my career just came to a stop. I just couldn't travel. I was completely disabled by these tones.</p><p begin="00:06:58.47" data-timestamp="418.47">I'm going to try on the piano. I think what I have on my right ear is a D flat. And in my left ear, a C, one octave lower. The right ear is a D flat, and the left ear is a C.</p><p begin="00:07:21.35" data-timestamp="441.35">The most maddening part of this was when I was with this friend of mine, this jazz musician. And I described it to him. And he threw his head down in his hands on the table. We were having lunch. And I could see in him that he completely understood how maddening it was. Because he understood that the D flat was always trying to resolve itself into the C.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Nubar Alexanian</h4><p begin="00:07:56.14" data-timestamp="476.14">So can you play both of them at the same time?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Woman</h4><p begin="00:08:00.07" data-timestamp="480.07">I might be able to. Let's see. Let me try this for a second.</p><p begin="00:08:11.65" data-timestamp="491.65">[INCREASINGLY LOUD TONE]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Abby</h4><p begin="00:08:32.82" data-timestamp="512.82">Dad?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Nubar Alexanian</h4><p begin="00:08:35.27" data-timestamp="515.27">Test. OK, what were you saying?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Abby</h4><p begin="00:08:38.56" data-timestamp="518.56">That well, as we've been doing this piece, I've been thinking. It must-- I couldn't imagine having a tone in my ear-- especially like a pure tone-- tones that don't stop like that. So I can not even imagine what it must be like to have it all the time.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Nubar Alexanian</h4><p begin="00:09:03.28" data-timestamp="543.28">So what is it about having the tone?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Abby</h4><p begin="00:09:07.45" data-timestamp="547.45">Like you're trapped inside your own head. You're in a room with no doors and no windows, and just a speaker of that sound driving you literally up the walls. I swear, I would cut my head off or something. I'd follow van Gogh's path and cut my ears off.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Nubar Alexanian</h4><p begin="00:09:27.85" data-timestamp="567.85">Is this is an odd thing to say, you think? I'm somewhat grateful for the tone. Of course, I don't feel grateful on bad days. But you know, before you were born, I used to travel constantly on assignment, all over the world. And on good days, when I think about it, I think about the tone being a warning that I needed to slow down. And then you were born, and I thought, not only did I need to slow down, but I wanted to slow down.</p><p begin="00:10:08.30" data-timestamp="608.3">And on bad days, I feel like, oh, OK, I've learned this lesson. I've slowed down. Now the tone can go away. But it doesn't go away.</p><p begin="00:10:21.87" data-timestamp="621.87">[TONE]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Abby</h4><p begin="00:10:29.51" data-timestamp="629.51">I'm reading through this outline of all the tapes that we have.
