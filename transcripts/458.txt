  <h3>Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.52" data-timestamp="0.52">Bill Langworthy makes reality TV shows. He was one of the producers of <i>The</i> <i>Hills</i>. And he says that he's always been surprised, even after doing this for years, at how people end up doing things on television they would never do in real life, throwing themselves into the part so completely they even confuse themselves.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Langworthy</h4><p begin="00:00:15.95" data-timestamp="15.95">I mean, we literally shot scenes before a husband and wife, and midway through the scene, the wife turns to me and says, &quot;Hold on a second, is this for the show, or are we really fighting right now?&quot; And I couldn't answer her.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:34.25" data-timestamp="34.25">So Bill never totally got why so many people would let themselves do so many embarrassing things on camera that they would never do normally.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Langworthy</h4><p begin="00:00:42.14" data-timestamp="42.14">And I would always say, gosh, if I were on one of these shows, if I was in this situation, I would just handle myself so differently.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:49.89" data-timestamp="49.89">And after years of being in jobs like this and thinking all that, he went to a Dodgers game.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Langworthy</h4><p begin="00:00:54.59" data-timestamp="54.59">And I was there with my ex-girlfriend's best friend. And all you need to know-- it's my ex-girlfriend's best friend. This is a girl that I cannot kiss under any circumstances. There are just so many rules against that. And this guy comes around whose job it is to find people to go on the Kiss Cam, which is when they put the camera on you.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:14.25" data-timestamp="74.25">Oh, and it shows up on, like, the jumbotron for the stadium to see.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Langworthy</h4><p begin="00:01:17.88" data-timestamp="77.88">Yeah, exactly. And so we were also there with two of our friends who were married. And so they signed up, that, yeah, we're going to come back later in the game with a camera on you guys. So you guys will kiss and everyone will see it on the jumbotron.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:29.39" data-timestamp="89.39">And they'll cheer.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Langworthy</h4><p begin="00:01:30.68" data-timestamp="90.68">Yeah, and sort of realized now that that guy and I have exactly the same profession, which is we try and find strangers to do embarrassing things on camera. But at the time, none of that's registering with me. And so several innings later and several beer runs later, we've managed to switch seats.</p><p begin="00:01:52.08" data-timestamp="112.08">And of course, that's how they do it, is by what seat you're in. You know, 50,000 people, you don't just say, it's the tall guy with brown hair. So the Kiss Cam comes around, and instead of being pointed at the married couple, it's pointing at me and my ex-girlfriend's best friend.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:06.49" data-timestamp="126.49">Because now you're in those seats.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Langworthy</h4><p begin="00:02:07.92" data-timestamp="127.92">Exactly. And so I have a producer who's looking at me. I expect something of you. The stadium is literally screaming at us. Five seconds earlier, I would have promised you, under no circumstances would I ever kiss this woman. And I leaned over and kissed her on the lips.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:33.11" data-timestamp="153.11">He knew he didn't have to kiss her. There'd be no penalty. There was no contract. No money had changed hands.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Langworthy</h4><p begin="00:02:38.53" data-timestamp="158.53">That didn't even cross my mind. Not doing it was not an option. You couldn't have paid me $1 million to do it, but I was on camera, I had to do it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:48.98" data-timestamp="168.98">Yeah, but that makes no sense. You of all people know that you don't have to do it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bill Langworthy</h4><p begin="00:02:53.68" data-timestamp="173.68">I certainly would have told you that right ahead of time. But yeah, I just got this feeling that I was letting everybody down, I was letting the producer down, that I was letting the audience down.</p><p begin="00:03:05.82" data-timestamp="185.82">50,000 people were looking at the jumbotron. They wanted one thing and one thing only. I realize I guess I'm just not that much different than the people that I put on TV.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:16.91" data-timestamp="196.91">Well, today on our radio program, people who are driven to act very differently than who they really are, flipping their personalities, completely doing the exact opposite of what they normally do. You only go that far if there are very powerful reasons to do it.</p><p begin="00:03:30.12" data-timestamp="210.12">In Act One, it is somebody doing this for money. In Act Two, it's somebody doing it for love. From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i>, distributed by Public Radio International. I'm Ira Glass. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>

<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: The Audacity of Louis Ortiz</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:49.07" data-timestamp="229.07">Act One, The Audacity of Louis Ortiz. Usually when you play a part, the expectations are pretty clear. There are lines. There's a script. There's a storyline you follow.</p><p begin="00:03:59.31" data-timestamp="239.31">So when you're on the Kiss Cam, you kiss. When you're Hamlet, you stress out. You know what you're on stage to do. Our first story today is about a man who found himself in a part with huge expectations and no script whatsoever. He had to make it up as he went along.</p><p begin="00:04:13.96" data-timestamp="253.96">It comes to us from filmmaker, Ryan Murdock. A warning to listeners that this is a story that's partly about race, and a racial slur gets used.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ryan Murdock</h4><p begin="00:04:21.87" data-timestamp="261.87">2008 was a hard year for Louis Ortiz. He was living in the Bronx, but he lost his job as a phone technician for Verizon over a year earlier. So he was collecting workers' comp and playing in pool tournaments for extra spending money. But then things started to change.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louis Ortiz</h4><p begin="00:04:37.77" data-timestamp="277.77">I think it was, like, August '08, people were like, oh, dude, you made the front page. I'm like, what? I don't know what the hell people are talking about. So I look at the page, <i>Daily</i> <i>News</i> I believe it was, and there's this dude with big ears, just smiling. He's running against Hillary, or whatever.</p><p begin="00:05:00.18" data-timestamp="300.18">And I'm like, all right, I don't really get it. I think it's the big ear thing. Three weeks went by of those jokes.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ryan Murdock</h4><p begin="00:05:07.76" data-timestamp="307.76">Louis is a Puerto Rican guy in his late 30s. He describes himself as an occasional voter. And he hadn't really been following the election.</p><p begin="00:05:15.38" data-timestamp="315.38">Then, at some point, Louis went to his usual bar, The Williamsbridge Tavern, to shoot some pool. Pat Tebelis was tending bar that day. Louis sat down and asked for a Corona.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pat</h4><p begin="00:05:25.72" data-timestamp="325.72">I just mentioned it to him. I said, did anybody ever tell you you look like this guy that's running for the president?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louis Ortiz</h4><p begin="00:05:31.40" data-timestamp="331.4">And I said, &quot;Yeah. Who, this dude, Obama?&quot; And he goes, &quot;Yeah. You really look like him.&quot;</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pat</h4><p begin="00:05:37.08" data-timestamp="337.08">And he says, &quot;Yeah. yeah, yeah.&quot; He sort of blew it off.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louis Ortiz</h4><p begin="00:05:40.44" data-timestamp="340.44">I'm like, &quot;I don't see it, dude.&quot;</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pat</h4><p begin="00:05:42.20" data-timestamp="342.2">And I said, &quot;No, no, really. You really do.&quot; And he says, &quot;Nah, I don't know.&quot; I said, &quot;I'm telling you. You go home and you shave off that goatee and mustache.&quot;</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louis Ortiz</h4><p begin="00:05:50.42" data-timestamp="350.42">I'm like, uh-uh. I ain't taking this off.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ryan Murdock</h4><p begin="00:05:53.52" data-timestamp="353.52">Ever since he's been in the Army, Louis had had facial hair. For more than a decade, it had been the same-- full mustache, full goatee. He never would have dreamt of taking it off. But then Pat brought up something no one else had.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pat</h4><p begin="00:06:06.77" data-timestamp="366.77">I told him, I said, &quot;You could make money. If this guy does become the president, it's a tremendous opportunity for you to make money, if you want to go in that direction. You know, first black man to be the president of the United States.&quot; I said, &quot;This is powerful.&quot;</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louis Ortiz</h4><p begin="00:06:19.99" data-timestamp="379.99">And I'm thinking, hey, cha-ching. I need money right now. I'm unemployed for a whole year, so I start listening to the dude, listening to the dude.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Pat</h4><p begin="00:06:29.00" data-timestamp="389">I said, &quot;Look, I'll tell you what.
