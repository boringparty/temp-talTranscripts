And though Beals has this theory on why a campaign like his can beat a Republican in the fall, even some progressives are nervous to go along with him.</p><p begin="00:49:31.60" data-timestamp="2971.6">One progressive group got into a particularly bitter fight over all this. The group's Citizen Action. It's a branch of a national group. There's lots of chapters. Very active chapter in New York. Their volunteers run Faso Fridays.</p><p begin="00:49:44.76" data-timestamp="2984.76">Oriana Mayorga was in charge of Citizen Action's endorsement committee in New York 19. She moved to the district to work for Citizen Action as an organizer. She's a Beals supporter. In January, Mayorga gathered in an endorsement committee of 12 who interviewed the candidates. They discussed, then they voted.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Oriana Mayorga</h4><p begin="00:50:01.63" data-timestamp="3001.63"> There were seven votes for Jeff Beals and five for Antonio Delgado. People wanted Jeff Beals. That was--</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:50:08.92" data-timestamp="3008.92"> Not a huge shocker. Citizen Action is progressive. They hold events and rallies for single payer health care.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Oriana Mayorga</h4><p begin="00:50:15.15" data-timestamp="3015.15"> We're like, Citizen Action is this grassroots organization that fights corporate power. So in no way would I think that they would want to endorse somebody who is from Wall Street, has all these corporate donors backing them and isn't for Medicare for All.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:50:33.73" data-timestamp="3033.73"> Mayorga sent word to the state leadership of Citizen Action, here was our vote. But what happened next surprised her. State leaders said the vote had been too close, closer than they wanted. Essentially, look at this some more and try again. Mayorga says following that first vote, state leaders called members of the committee. They told people they thought Delgado was the stronger candidate.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Oriana Mayorga</h4><p begin="00:50:56.08" data-timestamp="3056.08"> The deputy director spoke very frankly. I mean, it was not-- it wasn't really behind the scenes. She spoke frankly about it with me and while there were volunteers in the office, saying that the organization really felt that Delgado was the most viable candidate. He had the money and he had the support, but that he would have to be for Medicare for All for us to get behind him.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:51:15.70" data-timestamp="3075.7"> They brought Delgado in and they told them that. But he refused to back Medicare for All. What followed was a series of tough meetings, circular arguments, turning over the coin of idealism and pragmatism. The same tortured question from the Sanders versus Clinton race.</p><p begin="00:51:32.53" data-timestamp="3092.53">This time around though it was, should we back Beals? He's more progressive. He's like us. Others were like, Delgado has more money. We worry Beals can't compete.</p><p begin="00:51:41.65" data-timestamp="3101.65">Mayorga says the whole thing was kind of awful. People in charge were like, OK, if that's our endorsement can you Beals folks live with that?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Oriana Mayorga</h4><p begin="00:51:59.86" data-timestamp="3119.86"> And the Beals supporters were in an uproar, right?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:52:01.99" data-timestamp="3121.99"> They were like, we voted with the same split for Beals and were told it was too close.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Oriana Mayorga</h4><p begin="00:52:06.64" data-timestamp="3126.64"> The numbers were now 7-5. We were in the same place, just a different candidate. So technically speaking, both times one would assume that means that it's too close.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:52:16.53" data-timestamp="3136.53"> A few things happened after that meeting. There was another meeting. A bunch of Beals supporters couldn't be there, and one last Beals supporter caved. Then Mayorga told her bosses she was upset about how this whole thing went. The following week she was fired.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Man</h4><p begin="00:52:32.13" data-timestamp="3152.13"> After a careful and deliberative process of our local endorsement committee and our--</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:52:36.46" data-timestamp="3156.46"> In May, Citizen Action officially endorsed Antonio Delgado.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Man</h4><p begin="00:52:40.14" data-timestamp="3160.14"> That candidate is Antonio Delgado.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:52:44.16" data-timestamp="3164.16"> Delgado tweeted out the endorsement calling it quote, "the progressive stamp of approval."</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Oriana Mayorga</h4><p begin="00:52:49.59" data-timestamp="3169.59"> It's like not even-- I mean, it's kind of funny. I mean, Antonio Delgado is Hillary Clinton. I mean, like, that's just the truth. Like he is the Hillary Clinton of the race and Jeff Beals is the Bernie Sanders.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:53:01.92" data-timestamp="3181.92"> When I talk to the DCCC and progressives, one of the things they all said was that they were worried about how Democrats were going to handle the whole influx of enthusiasm this year. I couldn't help but think of that when I was talking to Mayorga.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Oriana Mayorga</h4><p begin="00:53:23.22" data-timestamp="3203.22"> To be fair, after this November I'm no longer a Democrat. So this will be my last Democratic primary I'll be voting in.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:53:32.19" data-timestamp="3212.19"> Oh, wow, you feel-- you're just like, I'm done.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Oriana Mayorga</h4><p begin="00:53:33.94" data-timestamp="3213.94"> Oh, I'm done. Yeah, I'm done. I really am. I think that we have to make the decision. Do we want to support progressive candidates? Or do we want to go with the safe bets, you know, these Hillary Clintons, these Antonio Delgado candidates?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jeff Beals</h4><p begin="00:53:58.02" data-timestamp="3238.02"> Hi, how are you doing? He gets very excited when--</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:54:07.17" data-timestamp="3247.17"> Recently I follow Beals as he canvassed in Oneonta, a beautiful town up in the mountains of New York.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jeff Beals</h4><p begin="00:54:11.89" data-timestamp="3251.89"> I'm running for Congress.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:54:12.84" data-timestamp="3252.84"> He moved quickly from one port to another with a keen eye out for Bernie Sanders stickers on bumpers and in windows.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jeff Beals</h4><p begin="00:54:18.61" data-timestamp="3258.61"> That's a telltale sign.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:54:20.40" data-timestamp="3260.4"> Though Beals says his relationship with the national party went cold, progressive groups have jumped in to help him. He had a Sanders sticker outside.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dana</h4><p begin="00:54:44.19" data-timestamp="3284.19"> I'm a Democrat, I'm a progressive.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jeff Beals</h4><p begin="00:54:47.16" data-timestamp="3287.16"> OK, well me too. You can move on.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:55:15.36" data-timestamp="3315.36"> This happened a number of times that day. As for the army of volunteers Beals knew he would need to win this kind of campaign, did he enlist them? Beals wouldn't say how many he has. But he did say they've knocked on over 40,000 doors. That's the same number Pat Ryan, the veteran, says his campaign has done. Antonio Delgado's campaign says they've knocked on 50,000. So at least by one measure he's keeping up, though Ryan and Delgado are both running TV ads and Beals is not. So far nobody has released an objective poll on the race. With seven candidates, the vote could split in some really weird ways. Everybody agrees someone could win without getting all that much of the vote.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jeff Beals</h4><p begin="00:55:57.24" data-timestamp="3357.24"> Are you registered Democrats? Who's a registered Democrat?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:56:03.63" data-timestamp="3363.63"> One of the things that probably will happen, even if Beals doesn't win, some candidates like him probably will.
