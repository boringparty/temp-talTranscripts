  <h3>Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.37" data-timestamp="0.37"> Adults don't understand anymore what it means to be a nerd. Have you noticed this? Nerd, somehow, has become a badge of honor. You meet all kinds of people who say, proudly, that they were nerds in high school. It's like anybody who had anything that made them feel different now says that they were a nerd.</p><p begin="00:00:18.65" data-timestamp="18.65">And that population-- the population that thinks that it was different-- that's like, everybody who went to high school. You know? People who were chubby, people who were in band, people who liked comic books, people who just didn't drink. I've met people who were actually popular, who actually had a social circle and boyfriends or girlfriends, who now claim they were nerds. That is just wrong.</p><p begin="00:00:41.61" data-timestamp="41.61">I believe that we have forgotten the sweaty, unsexy, cringe-inducing face of hard core nerdom.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Iserson</h4><p begin="00:00:47.95" data-timestamp="47.95">I remember seeing a character on <i>Beverly</i> <i>Hills</i> <i>90210</i> wearing sports jackets to school. And I decided that that was a cool thing to do, and that I would do that. And it was the sort of thing that nobody quite understood. And everyone thought I was dressing up for something I had to go to after school. Everyone was sort of already tired of me at that point.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:09.06" data-timestamp="69.06">That's David Iserson. When he was 13, David got a chance to change schools, leave the tiny school where everybody had known him since he was five, and start all over at a big public school where nobody knew him. And he had a very clear strategy for how he was going to create a glorious new life for himself.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Iserson</h4><p begin="00:01:24.66" data-timestamp="84.66">I bore no illusions that I was going to be cool. So my only goal was to be anonymous. And I had some friends who were a year ahead of me in school. And that was their advice to me. Go in there. Do not say a word. Don't talk to anyone. Don't go up to anyone. Just sit in the back and stay anonymous. And that was my goal.</p><p begin="00:01:48.41" data-timestamp="108.41">That was actually a pretty exciting prospect, of just being somebody that people barely noticed.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:55.08" data-timestamp="115.08">And for a few months it worked. But David didn't just want to be invisible. He also wanted to be an actor. I know. Complete contradiction. He was 13, remember.</p><p begin="00:02:05.76" data-timestamp="125.76">He'd had a small part in <i>Guys</i> <i>and</i> <i>Dolls</i> at summer camp. He played a boy who talked to whales in this tiny community theater. And a couple times a week, his grandmother would drive him to auditions for TV commercials, though he never got cast in anything. Then, his dad came to him with a proposition.</p><p begin="00:02:22.17" data-timestamp="142.17">His dad worked in the family business, this furniture store called Silvert's Furniture. And they'd just added a room full of desks, and beds, and shelving that was designed specifically for teenagers.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Iserson</h4><p begin="00:02:32.71" data-timestamp="152.71">And for a while he had been doing the local television commercials for the store. So he said to me, &quot;You want to be in commercials. I have a great idea. You should do the commercial for our store.&quot; I think he may have even presented it like, you know, I'm only so good at this, but you are a talented young actor. So I think you should promote our room of teen furniture.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:01.65" data-timestamp="181.65">He was flattered. He was thrilled. And they shot the ad. Which was a gas.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Iserson</h4><p begin="00:03:06.06" data-timestamp="186.06">So a few weeks later, my dad brings it home. And he's really, really excited about it. He talked to the guy, the director of it, and they both decided that this was a great commercial. And so he gathered the family into the family room. And he put on the VHS tape. And he started the commercial. And it didn't even have to run for a second before I just had this awful feeling in the pit of my stomach. Like, this was horrible.</p><p begin="00:03:37.13" data-timestamp="217.13">I'm sure all your parents know about the great selection of furniture for them here at Silvert's in Freehold. But they may not know about Silvert's huge selection of teen furniture. After all, we need something that suits us too.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:47.87" data-timestamp="227.87">Could you just describe what your hair cut looks like right here?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Iserson</h4><p begin="00:03:51.07" data-timestamp="231.07">Well, I was sort of fashion-- I guess what could be described as a pompadour.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:56.13" data-timestamp="236.13">I would describe you more as, like-- and I don't want this to sound harsh. I mean this in a -- you look like Big Boy. You know Big Boy? From the hamburger chain? But with braces.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Iserson</h4><p begin="00:04:10.51" data-timestamp="250.51">Yeah. I think that's probably accurate.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:13.35" data-timestamp="253.35">For somebody who wanted to stay invisible, it is hard to imagine how this particular ad could have been worse for him. Everybody saw it. In the commercial David has an air of perfectly cheerful naivete.</p><p begin="00:04:24.20" data-timestamp="264.2">In the first shot, he crosses the screen, careful to never stop facing the camera as he does this. Cut to him putting his school books onto a table. Cut to him lying on a bed on his stomach. In one shot he nods knowingly to the audience.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Iserson</h4><p begin="00:04:38.40" data-timestamp="278.4">And furniture we feel comfortable just hanging out in. Oh yeah, Silvert's has sets for you girls, too. So take your mom and dad--</p><p begin="00:04:46.22" data-timestamp="286.22">To stop it there-- I mean, I think that's the line. That's the line that killed me. &quot;We have sets for you girls, too.&quot; If anyone was to watch that commercial-- and when anyone did watch that commercial, who went to school with me, or anything like that-- that's the big laugh line. That's the thing bullies would say. You know, if I'm walking down the hall, and I hear someone say, &quot;We have furniture for you girls, too,&quot; I know that I'm about to get shoved into a wall of lockers. It was almost like my warning.</p><p begin="00:05:20.55" data-timestamp="320.55">That's the line that ended up following me, basically, all through my adolescence.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:25.53" data-timestamp="325.53">I have to say, when you say it, you give a little jaunty, over the shoulder, one thumb point.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Iserson</h4><p begin="00:05:33.33" data-timestamp="333.33">As I recall, that's how I was directed. And thank God they didn't use the take where I think I actually took a comb, and I combed it through my hair as I said it. Because if it could ever possibly be worse, it would have been worse by doing that.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:50.38" data-timestamp="350.38">And let's just describe what happens in the very last moment of the commercial.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Iserson</h4><p begin="00:05:55.44" data-timestamp="355.44">So I'm saying my last little button on there. And my dad walks in--</p><p begin="00:05:59.80" data-timestamp="359.8">So take your mom and dad to Silvert's Furniture in downtown Freehold for all the furniture we need.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dad</h4><p begin="00:06:05.45" data-timestamp="365.45"> Thanks, Dave. I couldn't have said it better myself.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Iserson</h4><p begin="00:06:08.14" data-timestamp="368.14">I'm smiling. He puts his hand on my shoulder. I mean, it's like holding hands with your mom in the mall. And the &quot;Thanks, Dave. I couldn't have said it better myself,&quot; was another line that got a lot of life out of it when I was walking through the halls of junior high school, and then later, high school.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:40.29" data-timestamp="400.29">He says that a day did not go by for the next year without somebody mentioning the ad. Strangers, his friends, even adults. One teacher tried to get him to quote the commercial in a school play.</p><p begin="00:06:50.69" data-timestamp="410.69">The ad ran a lot during New York Rangers hockey games. If you know anything about Rangers fans, this is maybe the least hospitable possible audience for an ad featuring a pompadour-wearing 13-year-old. These huge guys, men, would stop David and make him perform the ad for their girlfriends.</p><p begin="00:07:06.61" data-timestamp="426.61">David always tried to protect himself by telling people that the stuff that he said in the commercial-- the way the he acted-- the store made him do all that. But, um, that wasn't true. He had written the whole thing himself.</p><p begin="00:07:19.58" data-timestamp="439.58">And when he watched the ad, it was actually a profound thing for him. He thought, this is how I seem to people? This is how it comes across when I talk?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Iserson</h4><p begin="00:07:29.26" data-timestamp="449.26">My big fear was that they were all right, that everything they were making fun of was true. That that was me. Like I'd presented myself, and that was me.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:41.99" data-timestamp="461.99">Do you think it was you? When you shot the commercial, you thought it was good.   <h3>Act Three: Impeachment Day</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:51:08.19" data-timestamp="3068.19">Act Three, Impeachment Day. Well you can't do a spokesman show without hearing from a presidential spokesman. And there is an unusually candid recording of Joe Lockhart, who did that job for President Bill Clinton from 1998 to 2000. Long after Lockhart was out of the White House, he agreed to go on stage as part of The Moth, which is this group that does these great storytelling evenings.</p><p begin="00:51:30.21" data-timestamp="3090.21">Lockhart went on stage and talked about his early days on the job as White House spokesman.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Joe Lockhart</h4><p begin="00:51:34.92" data-timestamp="3094.92">Just after I started, we went on a trip. We went off to Russia. We were going to Ireland, doing big foreign policy stuff. Trip was going pretty. The last night in Moscow, I was coming into the hotel. And I ran into a friend of mine, an old friend of mine, the godfather to my daughter, I hadn't seen in a couple years. He said, come on, we've got to go out. We've got to go out. And he convinced me, you know, we were going to go see the real Moscow. And we went to-- I'll never forget-- a place called The Hungry Duck.</p><p begin="00:51:59.23" data-timestamp="3119.23">And they were doing things there that I couldn't take my eyes off, so I had to stay till 5:00 in the morning, which was OK because we weren't leaving till 6:00.</p><p begin="00:52:08.34" data-timestamp="3128.34">So I got back to my hotel and made one mistake, which was to sit down on the bed. And obviously, I fell asleep. And I'm telling you, you don't know anxiety until you've woken up as the White House press secretary on your first foreign trip at 6:15 in Moscow without a passport, knowing you've missed Air Force One.</p><p begin="00:52:28.72" data-timestamp="3148.72">Now the only good thing that I could think of--</p><p begin="00:52:31.02" data-timestamp="3151.02">[LAUGHTER]</p><p begin="00:52:33.20" data-timestamp="3153.2">The only good thing I think of was the day couldn't get worse. When I finally caught up with the traveling party, I was immediately surrounded by reporters who said, how do you feel about being the first White House press secretary to ever miss Air Force One on a foreign trip? And a strange phrase just caught in my head. And I couldn't lose it.</p><p begin="00:52:49.95" data-timestamp="3169.95">About a week earlier, the president had been at a prayer breakfast talking about his affair with Monica Lewinsky. And he said, you know, I'm really sorry for what I did. And I'm working very hard to make up for it, particularly to those I've hurt the most. So when I got the question of how do you feel, I said, I'm really sorry for what I've done wrong. And I'm working hard to fix it, particularly those who I've hurt the most.</p><p begin="00:53:16.13" data-timestamp="3196.13">Now mocking the president of the United States when you make a mistake isn't always the best idea. But it came in my head, and I said it. So anyway, the day went on. And we finally had a little break where the president had some private meetings. And I went to the back of the Irish Ambassador's residence to go to sleep. And I hadn't been asleep for more than five minutes. And I was actually pretty hungover, which was why I needed the sleep. And the president's personal aide came back and said, the president's in a meeting right now. And he wants you to come into it.</p><p begin="00:53:47.85" data-timestamp="3227.85">Now, I'm not the smartest guy in the world. But this had practical joke written all over it. And I said, get lost. About three minutes later, he came in and said, hey, the president's in. He's in with the band U2, with Bono, and they want to talk to you.</p><p begin="00:54:02.11" data-timestamp="3242.11">I said, well, if the president wants to see me, he can walk his presidential ass right back to this room and ask me himself. Well, about 60 seconds later, the presidential ass showed up and said, what is your problem? These guys want to meet you.</p><p begin="00:54:23.87" data-timestamp="3263.87">So I walked into the meeting. And this guy, this rock star, Bono comes up and gives me this big hug and says, I really want to meet you. And I said, well, that's great Mr. Bono, but why? And he said, anyone who can handle world affairs-- you know, Monica Lewinsky and all that-- and still has time to stay out all night drinking is my kind of guy.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:54:48.39" data-timestamp="3288.39">When he was on stage at The Moth, Joe Lockhart also talked about what it was like to be press secretary the day that Congress voted to impeach the president, December 19, 1998. Also the same day, Bob Livingston, the speaker of the House, declared that he'd had an affair and he was going to resign. And so the White House had to beat back the notion that President Clinton should also resign because of Monica Lewinsky. That was just the early afternoon.</p><p begin="00:55:14.30" data-timestamp="3314.3">Also that day, the Defense Department had bombed Iraq with cruise missiles, because Iraq was not complying with the UN weapons inspections. And Joe Lockhart had to convince the press-- and the country-- that the attacks on Iraq were not a kind of <i>Wag</i> <i>the</i> <i>Dog</i> distraction.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Joe Lockhart</h4><p begin="00:55:29.00" data-timestamp="3329">I then had what we call in the business a communications challenge, because we had two things we had to do in the same time space. And then we had to talk about the war.</p><p begin="00:55:49.37" data-timestamp="3349.37">And I thought, you know what? Sometimes the best thing to do is not worry very much about it, just go out and do it. So out on the south lawn we had the Democrats, very simple message. Partisans, this is politics, Republicans suck. It all went well.</p><p begin="00:56:05.59" data-timestamp="3365.59">Then we went inside-- only 10 minutes apart, the same podium, just a different room, a different set of flags-- and said, there are no Republicans in this country. There are no Democrats. It's just Americans, and we've won the war. And then the president left, leaving me in the room to explain to 50 waiting reporters how the two things fit together.</p><p begin="00:56:27.77" data-timestamp="3387.77">And I think it was so audacious that we even took the breath away from the press. And they seemed to let us get away with it. And I remember at the end of this long day, walking across the hall about 20 feet to my office, going in. And one of my closest friends in the White House was there, one of the president's top aides. And he saw me come in. And I sat down. And he went over to the little bar in the office, and got two beers, opened them up, sat down, put his feet up.</p><p begin="00:56:52.33" data-timestamp="3412.33">And I'll never forget what he said to me, which was, you know, except for getting impeached, we had a pretty good day.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:57:00.30" data-timestamp="3420.3">Joe Lockhart.
