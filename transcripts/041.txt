  <h3>Act One: Life in the White House</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.32" data-timestamp="0.32"> So what was it like after election day this week for the couple who live in the White House? Every morning, they got up, put on their robes, went downstairs, and jumped in the pool for a swim.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Rosemary Eckersley</h4><p begin="00:00:12.47" data-timestamp="12.47"> And we do that year round. And, of course, coffee is very mandatory. [LAUGHS]</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:21.61" data-timestamp="21.61"> And then?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Rosemary Eckersley</h4><p begin="00:00:22.70" data-timestamp="22.7"> Well, the usual. I go fight with the face and Max Factor and my husband goes off to the office.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:30.84" data-timestamp="30.84"> The office in this case is not the Oval Office. No, she spends more time there. He drives into San Francisco where he has an office for his business in international finance. The couple who live in this house are named Norman Eckersley and Rosemary Ashley Eckersley. Neither is a member of the Democratic Party. Both have lots more money than the President of the United States. And the White House-- this one, anyway-- is about 15 miles south of San Francisco in a suburb called Hillsborough.</p><p begin="00:01:04.71" data-timestamp="64.71">From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i>. I'm Ira Glass. Today on our program, surprising stories of American politics. We have campaign diaries from Michael Lewis, a new theory of politics and television from Henry Jenkins, the Mexican-American self-deportation movement, Joan Jett Black, and we start with Act One, Life in the White House.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act Two: Television Man</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:33.75" data-timestamp="93.75"> My tour guide at the Western White House-- this White House-- was the real estate agent who's trying to sell the thing, Ann Riley. And she began the tour with a packet of old photos and press clippings that she'd assembled.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ann Riley</h4><p begin="00:01:46.94" data-timestamp="106.94"> This is really quite a fascinating home, and it has had several lives. It started off this.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:56.05" data-timestamp="116.05"> Originally, this just looks like just a regular big old house.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ann Riley</h4><p begin="00:01:58.82" data-timestamp="118.82"> It was a Victorian. It was an old Victorian. Here it is in the beginning.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:04.39" data-timestamp="124.39"> The house has been around for over half the life of the United States. And it's the kind of huge structure that reflects every prevailing fashion over time. It's been redesigned in the neoclassical style, the French Normandy style, as a massive Swiss chalet, as a Mediterranean home with a tile roof.</p><p begin="00:02:21.97" data-timestamp="141.97">And finally, in 1930, George Hearst, the oldest son of newspaper magnate William Randolph Hearst, bought this place and hired Julia Morgan, the architect who designed Hearst's castle-- San Simeon-- to turn this house into a facsimile of the White House. Why? Ann Riley says the best guess anybody has is this.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ann Riley</h4><p begin="00:02:41.44" data-timestamp="161.44"> Julia Morgan's charge was to deliver a house that he could invite the presidents to stay in when they came to the West Coast. So that's the myth. And I say it's a story.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:54.83" data-timestamp="174.83"> And we actually don't know exactly why he decided. There's no documentation or quotes or anything from him saying, &quot;Here is why I'm going to do this.&quot;</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ann Riley</h4><p begin="00:03:01.83" data-timestamp="181.83"> Well, his father, you know, had all these wonderful Hollywood starlets cavorting all over San Simeon. There was all this great stuff going on. And I think George was just trying to rise to the occasion. So what else would be famous besides a movie starlet? Well, the presidents of the United States might give him a little muscle in the family feud. It just feels so &quot;mmm.&quot;</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:26.10" data-timestamp="206.1"> And once your dad builds Hearst Castle, what are you going to do?</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:03:30.65" data-timestamp="210.65"> It's not an exact duplicate. Yes, there's an oval office. Yes, there's a rose garden. Yes, the columns in the window bays. the fireplace mantles, and the pediments above doors are modeled on those at 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue. But it's more of a kind of tribute to the White House than a copy. From the outside, it reminds you of the White House the way that Madonna resembles Ava Peron. They're shooting for the same effect. There's a strong resemblance. But the copy just doesn't have the gravitas of the original.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ann Riley</h4><p begin="00:04:00.36" data-timestamp="240.36"> Now, this is a fun room. Upstairs play room. Painted bright red slashed with silver paint. Ping pong tables, exercise equipment, massage table.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:15.27" data-timestamp="255.27"> And again, this is, like, a lacquer red room.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ann Riley</h4><p begin="00:04:21.40" data-timestamp="261.4"> Actually, this is a carpet that lights up. It has lights in it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:26.43" data-timestamp="266.43"> What?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ann Riley</h4><p begin="00:04:27.15" data-timestamp="267.15"> This carpet lights up. I don't know how to turn it on. But do you see all those little pinholes?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:32.71" data-timestamp="272.71"> Yeah.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ann Riley</h4><p begin="00:04:33.66" data-timestamp="273.66"> Those actually can be turned on.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:36.36" data-timestamp="276.36"> The owners of this White House reflect the times just as clearly as the occupants of that other White House back East do. During the last century, they included one of the men who founded banks in California and financed the railroad's expansion through the West. And the owner of the shoe company that made Buster Browns. In 1972, it was rumored that John Lennon and Yoko Ono would be moving in.</p><p begin="00:04:55.85" data-timestamp="295.85">As for the current owners, they made their money the way people do these days. He comes from the world of international banking, she from Hollywood. She was the wife of a studio vice president and the wife of a plastic surgeon before this marriage. When I meet her, she descends a staircase telling a slightly off-color and actually very funny joke. Before an hour's up, she's told me why Jean Harlow used to put ice on her breasts, why there are nude calendar girl paintings of herself over the beds in the house, and some story that Jane Russell told her the other night at dinner. I liked her.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Rosemary Eckersley</h4><p begin="00:05:24.44" data-timestamp="324.44"> You know the old song-- Cyndi Lauper-- &quot;Girls Just Want to Have Fun.&quot; That's me. I think that if we can't enjoy our life-- and I tell my friends, I always have something. I have a big calendar on my desk. And I always have something that I can look forward to, even if I have to make that date. If I look down and say, well, I think I'll plan on going to Las Vegas this month. If I have to create something to make that month special, I do it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:58.15" data-timestamp="358.15"> The house itself is a mix of high-brow and low-brow-- incredibly elegant rooms with beautiful furniture and expensive paintings, and here and there, paintings of doe-eyed girls, picked up at sidewalk art sales. The little embroidered pillows that say things like, &quot;Murder, yes. Divorce, never&quot; And &quot;Screw the golden years, there's a safari room.&quot; In short, it's hard to imagine Bill Clinton living here. But it's a place that his mother-- Virginia Kelley-- would probably just love.</p><p begin="00:06:36.02" data-timestamp="396.02">It is impossible to imagine an American building a house today as a duplicate of the White House as a way to lure the president over. People just do not view the presidency with the same respect as they did back in George Hearst's day. Witness Rosemary's response when I ask her this question about her home purchase.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:52.38" data-timestamp="412.38"> Was the fact that it was, in part, a replica of the White House part of what drew you to it?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Rosemary Eckersley</h4><p begin="00:06:57.71" data-timestamp="417.71"> No, it wasn't. It really wasn't.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:10.28" data-timestamp="430.28"> The asking price for this Western White House is $8.5 million. It has 27,000 square feet, 22 rooms. Coldwell Banker real estate agents recently put together an estimate of what the real White House would sell for if it were ever put on the open market. The real White House, by the way, has 43,000 square feet and 132 rooms. It sits on 18 acres of prime downtown real estate.</p><p begin="00:07:34.93" data-timestamp="454.93">Estimate? $64 million. Actually, to qualify for the mortgage, you'd have to be able to make monthly payments $600,000 a month. $64 million. Of course, according to the FEC, candidates Clinton and Dole each spent far more than that for the privilege of just living in the place for four years.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act2">
  <h3>Act Three: Losers</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">

  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:41:58.89" data-timestamp="2518.89"> Act Four, Inspiration. It is easy to feel dispirited by the way things went this election year. Even at 7:30 in the morning, the Mall is nearly deserted, the Lincoln Memorial empty. The Vietnam Veterans Memorial, on the other hand, is teeming with people who appear to have been up for hours, walking slowly along the length of the black marble slab bearing the names of the dead. What matters most to the people who wish to see McCain speak for Dole is the formative experience that the two senators ostensibly share. Both nearly died in a war. Both endured indescribable pain and suffering.</p><p begin="00:43:50.99" data-timestamp="2630.99">Dole's ordeal is at the center of his national campaign. To some extent, it <i>is</i> his campaign. McCain's trials are less known. McCain weighed less than 100 pounds. His hair, flecked with gray since high school, was nearly snow white. Clots of food clung to his face, neck, hair and beard. But the astonishing part of McCain's experience was its voluntary aspect.</p><p begin="00:44:58.67" data-timestamp="2698.67">McCain is the third generation of a distinguished military family.
