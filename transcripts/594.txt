  <h3>Prologue: Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.52" data-timestamp="0.52">My wife told me about this thing that-- honestly, this is news to me. You know when it's like the height of summer and you go out, and a feeling when the sun is just like really hot and hitting your skin and making you all warm? She likes that feeling. She likes the way that feels.</p><p begin="00:00:18.71" data-timestamp="18.71">And I don't know what this says about me, but the notion that anybody would like that had never occurred to me. Totally eye opening because I have never been into that feeling at all. To me, the heat of summer is just something you had to get through. It was like rain, but less wet.</p><p begin="00:00:38.01" data-timestamp="38.01">And since she said this, this summer I've been trying to practice it. I've been trying to reprogram my own experience of the summer. And so when I'm outside, I consciously tell myself, the sun's hitting me. I'm going to enjoy this, get into it. And I can kind of get myself there for a little while. And then I lose it. I can't hold onto it. I just think that some of us really love the summer. And it is not something that you can force.</p><p begin="00:01:06.91" data-timestamp="66.91">A couple weeks ago here on our radio show, we all saw this article about somebody like that. He's a 66-year-old lifeguard who's suing New York State for age discrimination. And I just want to pause on that for a second, a 66-year-old lifeguard.</p><p begin="00:01:20.96" data-timestamp="80.96">All of us here on our staff, we had no idea that could even exist. We all thought lifeguarding is something that you do when you're in high school, maybe a couple years after, into your 20s. Who is still lifeguarding at 66?</p><p begin="00:01:32.14" data-timestamp="92.14">And then it was even more of a question when we realized the lifeguard in the story, he has another job. He's a lawyer. He's a working lawyer.</p><p begin="00:01:39.35" data-timestamp="99.35">His name is Roy Lester. He's a bankruptcy attorney. He's got his own firm in Long Island. And then he lifeguards every weekend in the summer.</p><p begin="00:01:48.09" data-timestamp="108.09">And today on our program, we have stories of people like him, people who love the summer, not people like me. From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i> I'm Ira Glass. We prepared today a program to listen to with the sun beating down on you, the humidity through the roof, a show of people embracing summer and everything about summer. And let's just get right to it with Act One.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Grapes of Wrath</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:10.60" data-timestamp="130.6">Act One, The Grapes of Wrath. So one of our producers, Dana Chivvis, she went out to Long Island and met that guy, Roy Lester, the 66-year-old attorney lifeguard who was suing New York State. Basically the deal is that they tried to make him wear a Speedo. He refused. He lost his job. Here's Dana.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Dana Chivvis</h4><p begin="00:02:28.96" data-timestamp="148.96">If you ask Roy, why are you still lifeguarding at 66, he barely understands the question. It's so self-evident to him. It's been his life since he was 16. He and his buddies were kings of the beach. He lived with other lifeguards. His best man at his wedding was a lifeguard. Their kids grew up playing together on the beach while they were on duty. He never wanted to leave this job. Even when he went to law school in California, he came back to lifeguard every summer.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Dana Chivvis</h4><p begin="00:02:53.49" data-timestamp="173.49">In law school, aren't you supposed to have an internship in a law firm or something like that?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Roy Lester</h4><p begin="00:02:57.86" data-timestamp="177.86">You're supposed to.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Dana Chivvis</h4><p begin="00:02:59.25" data-timestamp="179.25">Did you not do that?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Roy Lester</h4><p begin="00:03:00.31" data-timestamp="180.31">No, I did not do that. I never took it quite that seriously. The idea of giving up the summer was something I just couldn't do.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Dana Chivvis</h4><p begin="00:03:09.24" data-timestamp="189.24">He's not alone. At Jones Beach, where he worked for 40 years, there are dozens of guys-- teachers, firemen, police-- who stayed with it into their 60s. Lifeguarding at Jones Beach is such a thing that a former lifeguard made a film about it. It's called <i>Jones</i> <i>Beach</i> <i>Boys</i>.</p><p begin="00:03:27.39" data-timestamp="207.39">Roy insisted I watch it. I did. It was awesome. Here's my favorite song from it.</p><p begin="00:03:32.46" data-timestamp="212.46">[MUSIC PLAYING]</p><p begin="00:03:34.41" data-timestamp="214.41"></p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:03:38.33" data-timestamp="218.33">"We're going for the rescue and getting to the victim." I never really appreciated how thrilling lifeguarding is until Roy talked about rescues. We were sitting in his law office.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Roy Lester</h4><p begin="00:03:47.91" data-timestamp="227.91">The exhilaration of a good rescue is unlike anything you've ever had. And you don't get that. I sit here, and I shuffle papers. I wouldn't call it exciting. I wouldn't call it rewarding.</p><p begin="00:04:04.04" data-timestamp="244.04">But this is-- you're actually accomplishing something. You're up there, and all of a sudden you're going out in the water, and the rest of the world is behind you. There's nothing else except between you getting from your stand to that victim. That's the only thing. And it's great. It's a great feeling.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Dana Chivvis</h4><p begin="00:04:29.94" data-timestamp="269.94">How many people do you think you've rescued in your career?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Roy Lester</h4><p begin="00:04:33.12" data-timestamp="273.12">Over 1,000.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Dana Chivvis</h4><p begin="00:04:34.46" data-timestamp="274.46">1,000?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Roy Lester</h4><p begin="00:04:35.17" data-timestamp="275.17">Yeah. You have to remember. There were times we would have 40 rescues in an hour.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Dana Chivvis</h4><p begin="00:04:39.89" data-timestamp="279.89">What? Why? What? Who? Why?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Roy Lester</h4><p begin="00:04:42.97" data-timestamp="282.97">Because you have people that come down to Jones Beach who really don't know about swimming, especially when you have a current. And you can get a very strong current at Jones Beach.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Dana Chivvis</h4><p begin="00:04:55.82" data-timestamp="295.82">1,000 rescues-- that's way more rescues than David Hasselhoff did on <i>Baywatch</i>. I figure two rescues per episode, 10 years on the air, Roy would still beat him by 560 rescues.</p><p begin="00:05:08.18" data-timestamp="308.18">Which is to say Roy is one of the lifeguardiest lifeguards there is. He had two wins at the national lifeguard competition. He served as an expert lifeguard witness in court cases. And all was well in his happy lifeguarding world until the Speedo mishegoss began in 2007.</p><p begin="00:05:27.40" data-timestamp="327.4">Here's what happened. If you're a lifeguard at Jones Beach, you have to take a physical fitness test every year to prove that you're still able to do the job. It includes a speed test in a pool. You have to swim 100 yards in 1:20, which is actually pretty fast. A lot of these guys train all year for it.</p><p begin="00:05:45.36" data-timestamp="345.36">For 15 years, Roy took the swim test in his preferred swimsuit, a pair of jammers. They look like bike shorts without the butt cushion. If you're watching the Olympics right now, all of the male swimmers are wearing them. They're tight, and they go down to just above the knee.</p><p begin="00:06:00.34" data-timestamp="360.34">But when Roy showed up for the test in 2007, he was told no jammers. His bosses at the Office of Parks and Recreation said, you can only do the test in one of the official Jones Beach lifeguard swimsuits, which means you have three choices-- board shorts, trunks, or Speedo. Board shorts and trunks are loose, so nobody really takes a swim test in them because they create more drag and slow you down. So, in effect, state officials were saying to Roy, you have to take the test in the Speedo.</p><p begin="00:06:28.41" data-timestamp="388.41">Roy said, no way. I won't do it. And he hasn't been a lifeguard at Jones Beach since.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Roy Lester</h4><p begin="00:06:33.30" data-timestamp="393.3">It was one of those feelings like, am I making the right decision? I'm throwing away a 40-year career over a principle. It was a difficult decision, a very difficult decision.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Dana Chivvis</h4><p begin="00:06:48.92" data-timestamp="408.92">How long did it take you to decide?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Roy Lester</h4><p begin="00:06:51.41" data-timestamp="411.41">A second.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Dana Chivvis</h4><p begin="00:06:58.20" data-timestamp="418.2">I really need to point out he would only have to wear the Speedo for the test, which lasts a minute and 20 seconds.
