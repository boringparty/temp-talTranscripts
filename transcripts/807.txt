  <h3>Prologue: Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:11.86" data-timestamp="11.86"><i></i> <i></i> <i></i> Back in the winter of 2022, in the days and weeks before Russia invaded Ukraine, there was one thing that most Ukrainians and most Russians had in common. They both insisted that war was impossible. War was unbelievable. And one reason it was unbelievable is that many families, possibly most families on either side of the border, have family members in the other country.</p><p begin="00:00:36.93" data-timestamp="36.93">Many Russians have some Ukrainian roots. Many Ukrainians have spent time living in Russia. Even President Zelenskyy, like many Ukrainians, grew up speaking Russian. He had a career in Russia, starred in Russian movies, performed comedy for Russian audiences. Even Putin's seen him perform-- called him a good actor years ago.</p><p begin="00:00:56.54" data-timestamp="56.54">So when Russians did start the war, it wasn't just on the battlefield or in the airspace over Ukrainian cities. It was within families. This is something I had no idea about, the way a conflict like this might take the normal disagreements between people and blow them up. When you think about it, like, of course, right?</p><p begin="00:01:15.61" data-timestamp="75.61">It was the writer Masha Gessen who explained this to me. Masha was born in Moscow, moved to the United States as a teenager with their family, then moved back to Russia as an adult to work as a journalist. They've written several books, now currently a staff writer at <i>The</i> <i>New</i> <i>Yorker.</i> And what Masha has put together today is a very unusual story where they try to capture what this is like by looking at one family and the fights they get into related to the war. So what you're about to hear is one family and eight fights. Here's Masha.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:01:48.07" data-timestamp="108.07"> In normal times, we keep the peace in families by letting things go, by accommodating, by forgetting things or pretending not to notice things. But when war starts, even the quietest, most conflict-avoidant people start fighting. Just before the war began, on February 21, 2022, two people sat at a long dining table in a Moscow apartment.</p><p begin="00:02:14.00" data-timestamp="134">These are my friends Nadia and Karen. They are at the center of this constellation of people I'm going to tell you about. I've known Nadia the longest-- since she was a college student in Moscow about 25 years ago. She was tall, blonde, butch, very cool. She still is. Soon after graduating, in 2003, she moved to Kyiv.</p><p begin="00:02:36.44" data-timestamp="156.44">And there's Karen a gay man, a friend I've known through many relationships. Twelve years ago, Karen and Nadia had a baby together. They lived in different countries, but it didn't seem like a big deal. Karen was in Moscow, Nadia in Ukraine, in Kyiv, so their son Luka was growing up in Kyiv but regularly visited his dad in Moscow. And on this night, all three of them were in Karen's apartment in Moscow. Luka had gone to bed. The adults were watching a speech by Vladimir Putin.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Vladimir Putin</h4><p begin="00:03:07.14" data-timestamp="187.14"> [SPEAKING RUSSIAN]</p><p begin="00:03:10.33" data-timestamp="190.33"></p></div><div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:03:12.15" data-timestamp="192.15"> Putin was declaring war. So Karen and Nadia had a fight.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Fight #1: Where Does Luka Go?</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:03:25.13" data-timestamp="205.13"> Fight number one-- where does Luka go? Even before Putin's speech, Nadia was ready. She didn't think the war would happen, but she'd made a plan for Luka if war broke out-- a plan she hadn't told Karen about until now.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Nadia</h4><p begin="00:03:40.82" data-timestamp="220.82"> By that time, I already knew that we're not going back to Ukraine. We already had tickets to Berlin.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:03:47.18" data-timestamp="227.18"> And who said what? What was the argument?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Nadia</h4><p begin="00:03:50.09" data-timestamp="230.09"> Well, before he was saying nothing is going to happen, and you're exaggerating, and things like this. But then, he was like, OK, maybe something's is going to happen in Ukraine. But you definitely shouldn't, you know, take the kid away to some other country.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Karen</h4><p begin="00:04:08.30" data-timestamp="248.3"> I was like, if there's going to be a war, it is safer to stay in Moscow--</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:04:14.52" data-timestamp="254.52"> This is Karen.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Karen</h4><p begin="00:04:15.50" data-timestamp="255.5"> --because nobody's going to attack Moscow. I live 10 minutes' walk from Kremlin. [CHUCKLES] I can't imagine the war can come into town.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Nadia</h4><p begin="00:04:30.83" data-timestamp="270.83"> He was saying, "no, I want him to stay in Moscow. It's the safest place in the world. I don't want him to go to Berlin." And we actually-- we didn't succeed too much in persuading him.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:04:43.31" data-timestamp="283.31"> And she didn't push too hard because that wasn't her way.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Nadia</h4><p begin="00:04:46.85" data-timestamp="286.85"> I don't know. I just hate fighting. I just hate when it gets ugly. Whenever I even sense that this might be the situation, I would just go in the opposite direction. It would never actually get to far as to have a fight with someone.</p><p begin="00:05:03.57" data-timestamp="303.57">And I think I'm so used to this. It has been for years like this. So we just said, "we're going to go there for two weeks."</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:05:11.24" data-timestamp="311.24"> We, as in Nadia, Luka, and Nadia's girlfriend, Sophie.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Nadia</h4><p begin="00:05:15.23" data-timestamp="315.23"> And only on two weeks he agreed. And everybody was like, yeah, two weeks. Of course. Right. But then I called Sophie anyway, and I said, "Take everything, including the summer clothes."</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:05:28.02" data-timestamp="328.02"> Including the summer clothes.</p><p begin="00:05:34.89" data-timestamp="334.89">Now, Sophie flies to Berlin with as much stuff as she can carry on a plane. Nadia and Luka meet her there.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:05:41.91" data-timestamp="341.91"> Am I understanding this correctly that you're, like, talking him into two weeks because you're not going to have a big fight with him?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Nadia</h4><p begin="00:05:49.35" data-timestamp="349.35"> Yeah, but it's just the story of our relationship. We're just avoiding the conflicts all the time.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:05:56.31" data-timestamp="356.31"> Well, she is. This is how she is with everyone.</p><p begin="00:06:06.48" data-timestamp="366.48">So Nadia and Luka leave. Karen stays. And Russia invades Ukraine. Karen is going to protests, but he's lost, crying every day. After a few days, he goes to a goodbye party at a friend's apartment.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Karen</h4><p begin="00:06:20.98" data-timestamp="380.98"> And there, there were a lot of our common friends-- I don't know, 15 people. And everybody were discussing tickets, when and where everybody's going.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:06:32.74" data-timestamp="392.74"> I can tell you when this was, because I was there. March 4. And it's true that this was all our friends were talking about. There were rumors that Russia would close the border, impose martial law, conscript all the men, and just a general sense that you had to get out now or it will be too late. At the party, Karen started to get upset. Like, wait, you were all just going to leave me here?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Karen</h4><p begin="00:06:57.07" data-timestamp="417.07"> And that was the moment I realized that if everybody is leaving, then I'm going to do it as well.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:07:07.52" data-timestamp="427.52"> So Karen and his boyfriend buy tickets. To give you a sense of just how difficult it was to get out by then, they end up flying to Ulan-Bator, the capital of Mongolia. From there, after a few days, they fly to Seoul, South Korea, then to Dubai, then to Yerevan, the capital of Armenia, where they stay for a bit.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act2">
  <h3>Fight #2: Who Is Russian? And Who Is Ukrainian?</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:07:35.57" data-timestamp="455.57"> Fight number two-- who is Russian, and who is Ukrainian? To understand the roots of this next fight, we have to go back a long time, to New Year's Eve 2010, when Karen and Nadia met.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Karen</h4><p begin="00:07:48.65" data-timestamp="468.65"> My friend Vera, she introduced me to Nadia, saying like, "This is Karen, and he wants to have a child. And this is Nadia, and she wants it too. So please invite me when everything is done and you're going to baptize your kid, and I'll be his or her godmother." And that was it. And we were like, OK, let's talk. And several times, we talked about it. And we decided, yes, let's do it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Masha Gessen</h4><p begin="00:08:21.23" data-timestamp="501.23"> They were 31 and 28. They were both queer. They were both Buddhists.
