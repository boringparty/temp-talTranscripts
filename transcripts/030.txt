  <h3>Act One: Act One</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.49" data-timestamp="0.49"> So Paul, let's begin this week's show this way. I want you to explain to me everything you had to go through in order to see the movie <i>Independence</i> <i>Day</i>.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:00:12.16" data-timestamp="12.16"> I had to get something to eat. I had to get not only a Coke, but also a juice from somewhere outside of the movie theater and not open it up. I didn't want to have it open. I wanted to have it sealed and have a straw with a paper straw cover around it. Had to sit on the aisle. Had to leave the theater during the previews, and then come back in just as the movie was about to start. Had to wash my hands a couple of times, of course, just in case I had to operate or anything.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:45.99" data-timestamp="45.99"> Happens a lot in your modern theatres.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:00:48.54" data-timestamp="48.54"> Who's already scrubbed? [LAUGHS]</p><p begin="00:01:05.89" data-timestamp="65.89">I've sort of known that I had certain little quirks sometimes when I was watching a movie or really doing anything. But for some reason, I just realized it was going a little too far, that these rituals that I'd developed were just a little bit out of control.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:29.44" data-timestamp="89.44">Well, from WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i>. I'm Ira Glass. Each week on our program, we choose a topic, bring you a variety of different kinds of stories, documentaries, radio monologues, short stories, found tape, anything we can think of.</p><p begin="00:01:42.41" data-timestamp="102.41">Today's program, what happens when a little idea becomes a compulsion? What happens when it starts to control you? Our program today in four acts, four different stories.</p><p begin="00:01:55.31" data-timestamp="115.31">And joining me for this hour is Paul Tough, who actually holds a job. Doesn't just go to theaters freaking out his fellow patrons. Paul Tough, an editor at <i>Harper's</i> magazine in New York and a contributing editor to our show as well. Paul has been thinking and talking to a lot of people about this subject of small obsessions and compulsions.</p><p begin="00:02:16.82" data-timestamp="136.82">And can I just say that your story in the movie theatre-- I mean, those are pretty small things. I mean, all those things seem pretty innocent.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:02:24.56" data-timestamp="144.56">They are, they are. I mean, none of them are really out of control. I guess one of the reasons they sort of hit me is just because I've been thinking about this a lot. And what struck me about it was that feeling, where suddenly you think, this could become a lot worse. I mean, there are people-- well, OK, I was washing my hands a couple of times before a movie. The movie theaters are kind of dirty places. But there are people out there who wash their hands hundreds of times a day. And what separates me from that person?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:55.82" data-timestamp="175.82"> A couple hundred times.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:02:56.75" data-timestamp="176.75"> Exactly.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:58.58" data-timestamp="178.58"> There's a numeric number we can assign to that difference.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:03:01.32" data-timestamp="181.32"> But I had this feeling there in the movie theater that it could happen at any point.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:12.47" data-timestamp="192.47"> So Paul, among these cavalcade of stories that you've brought in to play for us over the course of the hour about obsession, I know that some of them are serious and some of them are lighter. What do you want to start with?</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act Two: Further Out</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:03:22.14" data-timestamp="202.14">One of the first people that I talked to is actually someone that I know really well, an ex-girlfriend of mine named Jillian. And what I wanted to talk to her about was her obsession with the number two.</p><p begin="00:03:37.65" data-timestamp="217.65">When she was a kid, just like any kid, she had a favorite number, a favorite color. And her favorite number was two. But it became a lot bigger than that.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jillian</h4><p begin="00:03:48.97" data-timestamp="228.97">So I started being very preoccupied with doing things twice. If I, for example, dropped keys, I would then lean down and pick them up. But before I actually picked them up, I would probably drop them again. Like really close to the floor so no one would quite know what I was doing. But I'd be very aware that I was fulfilling some sort of two obligation.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:04:21.02" data-timestamp="261.02">Her obligations weren't all-consuming, but they were somewhat laborious. At the dinner table or in class, she'd say something, and then she'd have to repeat it under her breath to make it two.</p><p begin="00:04:29.73" data-timestamp="269.73">If she made a phone call and the person that she was calling wasn't there, she'd have to let the phone ring an even number of times. She started to see the whole world as being divided into things that were even, things that were on the side of two, and things that were odd.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jillian</h4><p begin="00:04:43.13" data-timestamp="283.13">My favorite color was blue, and I also had this fantasy that blue was basically an even color. So it all seemed to fit in. But yellow seemed much more odd to me. And red seemed odd to me. And brown seemed even. Black seemed odd. This doesn't resonate at all with you?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:05:02.93" data-timestamp="302.93">Well, I can see blue being even.</p><p begin="00:05:18.31" data-timestamp="318.31">Tell me a little bit more about the other associations with two. Like you talked about-- I think you told me once that days of the week were as well?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jillian</h4><p begin="00:05:28.36" data-timestamp="328.36">Yeah, days. Both Sunday and Monday seem even to me. But I don't like them as days at all. I much prefer Tuesdays and Thursdays, which seem odd. I mean, days are weird. Monday, Wednesday seems pretty even to me. Tuesday, Thursday, Friday all seem odd.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:05:47.85" data-timestamp="347.85">I've got to say, I see it totally differently.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jillian</h4><p begin="00:05:49.68" data-timestamp="349.68">You do?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:05:49.94" data-timestamp="349.94">Yeah. I see Tuesdays and Thursdays and Saturdays as even, and all the others as odd. I mean Tuesday, first of all-- it kind of goes without saying.</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:06:15.76" data-timestamp="375.76">The thing about an obsession like Jillian's is that it can grow to the point where it's not just this game in your head. It can start to really control your behavior. And that's a problem, because that's when people start to notice.</p><p begin="00:06:28.83" data-timestamp="388.83">Jillian, for instance, she not only had a thing with the number two, she also had a deal with symmetry, where things had to be really symmetrical in terms of her body. If she would touch her left ear, then she would have touch her right ear. Things would have to be very even.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jillian</h4><p begin="00:06:45.27" data-timestamp="405.27">The big symmetry thing that I can remember is going to a Broadway show with my family. And I had this sense that it didn't feel quite right to just clap with my hands. And so, I developed this thing about clapping with my elbows, with this idea that somehow that would produce a more symmetrical experience. And so, I would clap with my hands, and then clap with my elbows, and then maybe even clap with the entire hand and elbow.</p><p begin="00:07:18.90" data-timestamp="438.9">And I can remember just sitting there and someone turning to me and saying, what are you doing? What are you doing with your elbows? And thinking like, this is not normal.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:07:39.38" data-timestamp="459.38">What I want to ask you about is the relationship between the two thing and other people in your life. And I just sort of remember that there were times when you and I were going out where I would get really mad about the two thing. I mean, do you remember those times when I would get mad about twos?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jillian</h4><p begin="00:07:55.67" data-timestamp="475.67">Yeah. But I always felt like your irritation about it was just irrational. I mean, for example, it used to come up a lot with the alarm clock, right? Like, I set my alarm clock usually for 8:02. If I need a little more sleep, then it's 8:12 or 8:22. I don't really like 8:32, but I'll do that.</p><p begin="00:08:19.93" data-timestamp="499.93">But so, when I used to want to set the alarm clock for 8:02, and you would object and want to set it-- I mean, first of all, sometimes you would object. You would just want to set it for 8:03, which was just pure malice on your part. Like, obviously, I'm not going to be happy if the alarm clock is set for 8:03. I mean, what would you really care if I set the alarm clock for 8:02?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Paul Tough</h4><p begin="00:08:40.37" data-timestamp="520.37">Well, I actually remember that the minute we started talking about it. I actually remember what made me mad about not being able to set the clock for 8:01. Because it is about unnecessarily cluttering your life with things.
