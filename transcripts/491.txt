    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.86" data-timestamp="0.86">This guy I know, David Rudis, was talking to me on the phone last week. He thinks those organizations need to make themselves more relevant to modern Jews.</p><p begin="00:00:18.18" data-timestamp="18.18">And I was telling him, that's fine for him, but that is just not my thing. Why would I ever join a Jewish organization?</p><p begin="00:00:36.56" data-timestamp="36.56">This, of course, led to the brief obligatory discussion of the Holocaust, which is what we Jews always talk about at a point like this in a conversation like this. I just am a Jew.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Rudis</h4><p begin="00:00:48.88" data-timestamp="48.88"> I think, Ira, you don't even begin to realize how much this identity has affected your life. David thinks that in our big, multicultural world, of course it is just old-fashioned for people to identify strictly with their own ethnic tribe. What parish? What religion?</p><p begin="00:02:06.44" data-timestamp="126.44">It seemed really weird to him at the time. When everybody lived, literally, in a tribe of a few hundred people, or a small band of a few dozen people. And Jared Diamond writes this thing about life then, about what it means to actually live in a tribe, that I had never heard before that really just stuck with me.</p><p begin="00:02:43.16" data-timestamp="163.16">He says that in these societies, because they were so small, you knew everybody. You never encountered strangers. You never ran into them.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jared Diamond</h4><p begin="00:02:51.57" data-timestamp="171.57"> The idea that you could just wander around and meet someone is utterly impossible in New Guinea. If you ran across a strange person on your land, that could only mean that they were there for some bad reason. They were there to scout out your land for a raid, or to steal a woman, or to steal a pig.</p><p begin="00:03:12.77" data-timestamp="192.77">And so if you ran into a strange person in the forest and you couldn't run away from him, you came around a corner and there he was, then the two of you would sit down. To want to stick to our own tribe?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jared Diamond</h4><p begin="00:03:57.51" data-timestamp="237.51"> It's going too far to do that. The term is "freeloader."</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:44.41" data-timestamp="344.41"> [INAUDIBLE] he says that that is the actual scientific term. That's the one that anthropologists use-- "freeloader."</p><p begin="00:05:51.27" data-timestamp="351.27">Well today on our radio show, we hear very different kinds of groups struggle with this question, who's in and who's out. Well, we probably should. It happens all the time.</p><p begin="00:06:07.74" data-timestamp="367.74">Today on our program we have three stories of three groups, including one whose defining characteristic is that they talk very evenly and softly.
