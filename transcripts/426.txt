  <h3>Act Four: Contrails Of My Tears</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:45:04.51" data-timestamp="2704.51"> Act Four, Contrails of My Tears. We end our show today with this story of a place that is the exact opposite of a tough room. Brett Martin says it's happened him.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Brett Martin</h4><p begin="00:45:16.53" data-timestamp="2716.53">A couple of years ago I was on a flight from New York to San Juan, Puerto Rico. The movie was <i>Sweet</i> <i>Home</i> <i>Alabama,</i> which you'll remember. It's about a southern girl played by Reese Witherspoon who moves to New York, joins the fashion industry, and then is forced to return home and come to terms with her white trash roots.</p><p begin="00:45:31.53" data-timestamp="2731.53">At the end, there's a wedding scene when the character has to explain to her big city husband-to-be that she's leaving him for her earthy, down-home high school sweetheart.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Reese Witherspoon</h4><p begin="00:45:39.20" data-timestamp="2739.2"> The truth is I gave my heart away a long time ago, my whole heart. And I never really got it back. And I don't even know what else to say. But I'm sorry. I can't marry you.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Brett Martin</h4><p begin="00:46:00.17" data-timestamp="2760.17">Into the stunned silence that follows walks Candice Bergen as the jilted fiance's dragon lady of a mother, who, coincidentally, also happens to be the mayor of New York.</p><p begin="00:46:08.47" data-timestamp="2768.47">After a volley of insults, Witherspoon decks Bergen. The South has rise again,&quot; that something because to happen to me. My face got hot and constricted. A softball rose in my throat that required a surprisingly loud snort to choke back. My breathing grew rapid. In short, I lost it and started to cry.</p><p begin="00:46:43.19" data-timestamp="2803.19">I should say that <i>Sweet</i> <i>Home</i> <i>Alabama</i> is not a very good movie. It's actually a pretty terrible movie. I have no particular attachment to Reese Witherspoon, and I'm not from the South. Also, this was the fourth time I'd seen it.</p><p begin="00:46:56.94" data-timestamp="2816.94">See, my name is Brett and I cry at movies on airplanes. Not sometimes, always. And not some movies, all movies. Don't believe me? Here's a by no means complete list. <i>Bend</i> <i>It</i> <i>Like</i> <i>Beckham</i>, <i>101</i> <i>Dalmations</i>, <i>What</i> <i>A</i> <i>Girl</i> <i>Wants</i>, <i>Daredevil.</i></p><p begin="00:47:25.33" data-timestamp="2845.33">Let me be clear. I am not afraid of flying. I like flying. And I'm not a crier, at least not on land. Like many men I know, even sensitive ones who know that having a cry can be healthy and good, I passed some invisible line in adolescence where I simply stopped doing it. There have been many times in life that I probably should have cried, actually tried to cry, and wasn't able to. Because, of course, I didn't happen to be at 30,000 feet.</p><p begin="00:47:49.09" data-timestamp="2869.09">Needless to say, this can be embarrassing. I once confessed my problem to a friend, and he thought for a long moment before saying, I'm sorry to hear that. Does it make your mascara run?</p><p begin="00:48:00.07" data-timestamp="2880.07">Earlier this year I was flying from Denver to New York and found myself seated next to a big, burly guy with a cowboy shirt and a western belt buckle. Before takeoff, we talked about football or college basketball or something. Then they announced the movie. It was <i>Under</i> <i>The</i> <i>Tuscan</i> <i>Sun</i>. I glanced at my macho new buddy, thought about watching Diane Lane experience love and loss while rediscovering her inner strength at a farmhouse in the Italian countryside, and read the Sky Mall catalog instead.</p><p begin="00:48:33.53" data-timestamp="2913.53">For a long time, I thought I was alone in this. Then a few months ago, I was at a party and overheard another guest describe how he fell to pieces watching an episode of <i>Everybody</i> <i>Loves</i> <i>Raymond</i> on a flight to California. I started asking around and found I wasn't completely alone.</p><p begin="00:48:47.32" data-timestamp="2927.32">Greg is a 32-year-old guy in jeans and a Mets hat who just finished reading a book about college sports.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Greg</h4><p begin="00:48:52.18" data-timestamp="2932.18">I think it might have been the only movie available was <i>Dirty</i> <i>Dancing</i> <i>2:</i> <i>Havana</i> <i>Nights.</i> The parents watch them dance and they see how special this relationship is. And at that moment, they've gone from angry parents to sort of accepting of Javier. I mean, I got choked up.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Brett Martin</h4><p begin="00:49:19.54" data-timestamp="2959.54">As my fellow weepers will tell you, even not watching the movie is no guarantee of safety. Here's my friend, Lindsay.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Lindsay</h4><p begin="00:49:24.65" data-timestamp="2964.65"> So I was on a flight, I believe California to New York. The specifics don't really stand out. But I do remember that it required a $2 deposit for earphones or something. And I wasn't ready to pay the $2 or I didn't have $2. And I decided I'd read my book. But the movie is playing. And I see it. And I can't take my eyes off of it. So I end up watching the entire duration of the movie without sound. And at various points throughout it I started welling up, thinking wow, I can't believe I'm crying in a movie I can't hear the sound to. And it's <i>Freaky</i> <i>Friday</i>.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Brett Martin</h4><p begin="00:50:03.47" data-timestamp="3003.47">Or take Steven, an avid film festival goer and professional movie critic who can discourse at length on the differences between early and late period Kurosawa. His plane hadn't even taken off.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Steven</h4><p begin="00:50:13.76" data-timestamp="3013.76"> And they were just running this loop of commercials and in-flight programming and stuff. They hadn't started the movie. It was very early on. And there was this AMEX commercial. A man traveling through Europe, and you know, I think it was nighttime. I want to say it was raining or something. And this kind of haggard traveller, this businessman, is walking briskly through the street. And then they close up on a wallet, clearly his, that he had left behind unknowingly.</p><p begin="00:50:43.61" data-timestamp="3043.61">And then you see, cut to the hotel where he's checking in. I think that's when I started choking up.</p><p begin="00:50:59.49" data-timestamp="3059.49">And then he gets American Express on the phone. They explain it'll be OK. It was a pretty tense situation there for about 15 or 20 seconds.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Brett Martin</h4><p begin="00:51:31.67" data-timestamp="3091.67">This is one of the strange features of our problem. For instance, in the movie <i>Larger</i> <i>Than</i> <i>Life</i>, which I saw somewhere over the Atlantic a few years ago, it wasn't the moment where Bill Murray is separated from the elephant that his dead circus clown father has left him as a means to change his life as a down-on-his-luck motivational speaker that had me reaching for the tissues. The camera pans across the faces of the guests just amazed by this unbelievable bounty being wheeled into the room. And the lump because to rise in my throat.</p><p begin="00:52:26.05" data-timestamp="3146.05">I found myself brimming over with joy, with the sense that somewhere in the darkness, miles below, just like on screen, people were laughing, communing, sharing a meal. It was impossibly beautiful. And there was just nothing to do but cry.</p><p begin="00:52:48.06" data-timestamp="3168.06">I've never heard of anyone crying inappropriately on trains, or on buses, or in boats, or cars. What is it about airplanes?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Lindsay</h4><p begin="00:52:54.82" data-timestamp="3174.82">I remember getting off the plane thinking, I should really actually be embarrassed by the fact that I just cried during <i>Freaky</i> <i>Friday</i> and I didn't even hear the sound to it. It's like, what happens in air stays in air, I guess.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Brett Martin</h4><p begin="00:53:10.83" data-timestamp="3190.83">The people I talked to offered a lot of excuses. But I've been on hundreds of planes, including quite a few tiny ones, one sea plane that landed on water, and one blimp. I've taken the controls of a plane. I've jumped out of a plane. I've searched my soul. An honest-to-god, I find no fear of flying. And all the frequent criers I interviewed felt the same.</p><p begin="00:53:40.10" data-timestamp="3220.1">No, something else happens up there, in that weird hanging state between where you're going and where you've left, where there's no phone calls to take, nowhere to go, nothing to do. Some strange overhead compartment of the heart opens up. And critical judgment grabs its flotational seat cushion and follows the lighted pathway to the big, yellow slide.</p><p begin="00:53:58.50" data-timestamp="3238.5">My friend Greg says this actually makes the ride better. Sitting there being a critic, or just simply giving in?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Greg</h4><p begin="00:54:10.47" data-timestamp="3250.47">I mean, I wouldn't have watched <i>Havana</i> <i>Nights</i> in the waiting area waiting to get on the plane. Well, even though I fly all the time, nothing in my little hard-wired brain is capable of understanding-- I mean really understanding-- stepping onto a metal tube, hanging in space for a while, and then stepping off 6,000 miles away in a place with different weather, different stars, different time.</p><p begin="00:55:03.66" data-timestamp="3303.66">It puts you into a kind of sterile, infantilizing travel purgatory. Maybe I cry the tears I should be shedding on Earth.</p><p begin="00:55:53.90" data-timestamp="3353.9">And all of you people who don't cry on airplanes, you're probably the ones I see sobbing on the subway, or on street corners, or at funerals.
