  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.23" data-timestamp="0.23">I came to the whole MacGyver thing kind of late. I never saw the TV show. In fact, I heard of MacGyver because he kept showing up in rap lyrics.</p><p begin="00:00:08.14" data-timestamp="8.14">MacGyver, of course, is the guy who fought bad guys, but never used a gun. He used his scientific knowledge. He used sheer inventiveness to get himself out of scrapes. The show's been off the air for years, but he still shows up in dozens of songs.</p><p begin="00:00:20.25" data-timestamp="20.25">[MUSIC - SAMPLES OF MACGYVER REFERENCES IN HIP HOP SONGS]</p><p begin="00:00:49.96" data-timestamp="49.96">MacGyver would turn a hot water bottle into a tear gas dispenser, or roll up a map to turn it into an impromptu dart gun, or mix an antidote to poison from common ingredients found at his local shopping mall, or escape from evildoers using only a bobby pin or piece of gum, notoriously, or the wrapper from the gum, in some diabolically clever way.</p><p begin="00:01:11.66" data-timestamp="71.66">In real life, most of us never get to be that clever. In real life, even when there's a situation like, for instance, the largest power outage in US history, it's the professionals who have to jury rig new wiring routes, to figure out how to keep hospitals and emergency services going. It's the professionals who have to be brilliantly ingenious.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Sarah Vowell</h4><p begin="00:01:31.22" data-timestamp="91.22">Let's see, ingenuity. I am chewing gum right now just in case I might need to use it on something.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:38.58" data-timestamp="98.58">When I reached our contributing editor, Sarah Vowell, in blacked-out New York this Thursday night, she was reading by candlelight. She'd managed to boil water on a gas stove. But that was about all the MacGyver-like ingenuity her situation seemed to call for.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Sarah Vowell</h4><p begin="00:01:51.43" data-timestamp="111.43">Yeah. I mean, I have the gum, and I have the situation. And so far, everything's fine.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:57.80" data-timestamp="117.8">Well, no, let's just think for a second. There must be something you can do with that gum.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Sarah Vowell</h4><p begin="00:02:02.00" data-timestamp="122">Um-- well, I guess if there was some kind of windstorm, I could latch one of the candles down with it, you know? I'm trying to wrench any little ounce of drama out of this for you that I can, but--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:16.55" data-timestamp="136.55">But there is no drama.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Sarah Vowell</h4><p begin="00:02:17.73" data-timestamp="137.73">I wish I could tell you something, and then you could put that <i>Mission:</i> <i>Impossible</i> music underneath me, and it would sound really super exciting, but--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:25.34" data-timestamp="145.34">No, look. Hold on. I'll put that music under you right now.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Sarah Vowell</h4><p begin="00:02:35.34" data-timestamp="155.34">I am going to take a bath, and light a candle! I might even take a Benadryl!</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:01.74" data-timestamp="181.74">Ingenious problem solving adventures. We almost never get them in our daily lives. And so today on our radio program we have searched, and we have found not one, not two, not three, but four real-life situations, real-life MacGyver solutions. Cases cracked by ordinary people like you and me.</p><p begin="00:03:18.96" data-timestamp="198.96">You know, this is completely the wrong music. Just hold on for a second.</p><p begin="00:03:23.18" data-timestamp="203.18">[MUSIC - THEME FROM "MACGYVER"]</p><p begin="00:03:24.46" data-timestamp="204.46">There we go. The MacGyver theme.</p><p begin="00:03:28.15" data-timestamp="208.15">One story takes place in prison. One involves a very low-budget movie. And one, our first act, is the true story of a potential personal crisis averted in an instant.</p><p begin="00:03:45.23" data-timestamp="225.23">It is <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i> from Public Radio International and WBEZ Chicago. I'm Ira Glass. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>

<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Bolt Of Lightning, Pro And Con</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:57.31" data-timestamp="237.31"> Act One. Bolt of Lightning, Pro and Con.</p><p begin="00:04:01.37" data-timestamp="241.37">When Chuck Klosterman was 20 years old, a junior at the University of North Dakota, when he had his very first girlfriend, she was 29. An adult. He was very young. And Valentine's Day arrived. The day that he came up with a MacGyver-like solution, in an instant, in a flash. Here was the situation.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chuck Klosterman</h4><p begin="00:04:19.25" data-timestamp="259.25">Somehow I was not aware that adults gave presents at Valentine's Day. Like, I only thought children did that, they gave cards to each other, you know?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:30.21" data-timestamp="270.21">Right.  At elementary school, there's swapping cards.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chuck Klosterman</h4><p begin="00:04:32.54" data-timestamp="272.54">Yeah. It didn't seem possible to me that adult people gave presents at Valentine's Day. I'm not sure how I managed to get to be 20 and be unaware of this, but that was sort of the situation.</p><p begin="00:04:46.09" data-timestamp="286.09">And we went to the movie <i>Groundhog</i> <i>Day</i> on Valentine's Day. And I was still living in a dormitory. So we went back to my dorm room, OK? And we're hanging out there, and all of a sudden, she gives me all these presents. She gives me, like, a Soul Asylum album, and a copy of the book <i>Go,</i> <i>Dog.</i> <i>Go!,</i> which I must have mentioned was my favorite book when I was five or something. There were all these sort of really kind of thoughtful gifts, and a whole bunch of them. And she'd obviously put a great deal of effort into the thought behind giving me these things.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:27.01" data-timestamp="327.01">And is your feeling of the noose sort of slowly tightening around your neck rising with each present?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chuck Klosterman</h4><p begin="00:05:34.36" data-timestamp="334.36"> Yeah. I'm sort of kind of paralyzed by fear now, because I realize that she has an expectation that I am going to reciprocate this somehow. And I guess I just didn't feel comfortable saying I didn't know that you were supposed to do this.</p><p begin="00:05:54.05" data-timestamp="354.05">So I tried to remain as cool as possible, and I started walking toward my closet. OK? And as I was walking toward my closet, I kept kind of stalling for time, saying things like, you know, I really didn't know what to get. I didn't want to buy a gift that everybody would buy. I wanted to get you something very creative. I want it to be a reflection of our relationship. I'm just sort of coming up with all these different ways to sort of describe how not only do I have, like, a really wonderful gift-- something really cool-- but it's going to be metaphorical for basically our entire relationship.</p><p begin="00:06:34.73" data-timestamp="394.73">But there's nothing in the closet. I have nothing in there. It's just my clothes. I don't know what I'd like-- and I don't really know what I'm doing now. Because I'm walking toward it, and I'm kind of slowly playing with the handle of the closet, and I'm just giving every indication that something is in there. But there's nothing in there. So I thought, maybe something will evolve in my closet. My shoes will become like a microwave or something, you know?</p><p begin="00:07:03.31" data-timestamp="423.31">But I begin opening this closet, and I look inside it. Of course to see nothing. Remember, nothing is in here. But I just see all my clothes lined up. And what I see is my high school football jacket. OK? And this is not like a letterman's jacket. This is just like a windbreaker-- you know, my name, Chuck, over the heart would be, like where the heart of the jacket is, with my number 18 underneath it. You know? I see this, right? And this thought just suddenly dawned on me, really quickly.</p><p begin="00:07:39.64" data-timestamp="459.64">So I turn to this woman and I say, well, here's the deal. You know, I never had a girlfriend all through high school, and I used to be very envious of all my friends who would give their football jackets to their girlfriends. And I kind of created this whole sort of narrative about how this had been a huge struggle through high school of not being able to give my football jackets to anyone. Which in truth, I never struggled with. I always thought those guys were idiots.</p><p begin="00:08:15.10" data-timestamp="495.1">So then I said, I think it would be really awesome if you would accept this high school football jacket of mine. And she started crying. And she just loved this jacket. I mean, she seemed to think it was really nice, and really sweet, and generally very much unlike me.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:08:36.33" data-timestamp="516.33">Afterwards, what would you feel when you would see her in the jacket? Would you feel good, or would you feel--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chuck Klosterman</h4><p begin="00:08:40.72" data-timestamp="520.72">No, no. I felt-- not really.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:08:44.45" data-timestamp="524.45">Every single time she wore it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chuck Klosterman</h4><p begin="00:08:46.10" data-timestamp="526.1">Yeah. You might want to make it bigger," he said, addressing me directly, "or earlier.</p><p begin="00:54:24.51" data-timestamp="3264.51">"But you wouldn't want to make that too much the point," someone said, "because obviously she was having a really hard time getting over him. I mean, she did feel something for him."</p><p begin="00:54:32.83" data-timestamp="3272.83">"Well, I think that's an interesting question," George said, tipping back in his chair. "What are Katie's true feelings? Is this is a story about social status, or is this a story about true love?" I didn't like the way the question was framed.</p><p begin="00:54:50.81" data-timestamp="3290.81">"Social," the aerobics instructor said. "Definitely social. It seemed like she liked having an older boyfriend." Where was this in my story? But everyone was nodding. Encouraged, the aerobics instructor added, "I mean, if they'd been in love, it would have clicked when they were still together."</p><p begin="00:55:10.19" data-timestamp="3310.19">No! I felt everything slump inside me. As everyone bobbed their heads in agreement, that Shane and I hadn't been in love, I looked very intently at my paper. "Why didn't anything click?" I wrote. Then I couldn't write the rest of it, so I just made a long dash.</p><p begin="00:55:29.65" data-timestamp="3329.65">"Maybe she's going through something more than just Shane," somebody suggested. I perked up a little. This was definitely possible. "But there's not enough in here to give us a sense of what that other thing she's going through might be," said George. "We don't know much about Katie. She went off to college. She enjoys mountain biking. But aside from that, who is she?"</p><p begin="00:55:50.80" data-timestamp="3350.8">She's me, I thought. Look over here! Blonde hair, green skirt, far corner.</p><p begin="00:55:57.18" data-timestamp="3357.18">The class was silent. Then George turned his entire body and looked me straight in the eye. Oh God, I thought. They know.</p><p begin="00:56:06.39" data-timestamp="3366.39">"Susan, you might need to do a little pre-writing about Katie. Get a sense of who she is. Figure her out a little more." Yes. I nodded vigorously, as if I, too, was hoping to unlock the mysteries of this girl I'd created.</p><p begin="00:56:20.98" data-timestamp="3380.98">"OK. Well, we should really move on to the next one," said George. "Susan, I guess my final comment would be that the story needs an end. But it develops well. Nice job," he said.</p><p begin="00:56:31.58" data-timestamp="3391.58">I sat there blankly. The story needs an end? I thought. No duh, it needs an end. We are gathered together today to figure out the end. The story needing an end is the whole reason we are here. And now we just started talking about some other person's work.</p><p begin="00:56:47.21" data-timestamp="3407.21">I sat very still and looked around the bright room, all of us at the desks in a big circle. Nothing had happened. I was still the same. Except for now I'd tried this, and it seemed like I had no more chances.</p><p begin="00:56:59.42" data-timestamp="3419.42">I rode my bike home slowly. It made me feel lonely, thinking of the way they'd talked about my life. It was so in general. None of them knew that they were talking about a real girl in the world.</p><p begin="00:57:17.74" data-timestamp="3437.74">Then, as the days went by, I felt better. The class had acted like my story wasn't so unusual. Just some old thing with an ex-boyfriend. It made the whole thing feel smaller, like less of an emergency. Dozens of girls now riding their mountain bikes through these very foothills had dated boys and been hurt. It was something that people had heard of, something that happened all the time. It was a known thing that made people sad. Now I rode my bike along Arapahoe Road at the speed of a regular girl, wearing regular clothes, like shorts, and pointing my bike light in the regular direction, straight ahead.</p><p begin="00:57:57.24" data-timestamp="3477.24">For a while I still thought about Shane, and then suddenly, I didn't think about him at all. Except on every birthday, when I would think, I'm still not as old as he was. And then I was 23, and that was it. I didn't think about him anymore.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:58:13.99" data-timestamp="3493.99">Susan Burton from New York.</p></div>  </div>
