  <h3>Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.31" data-timestamp="0.31">Remember that big terrorism case back in 2003? The Detroit sleeper cell case?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>News Anchor</h4><p begin="00:00:04.65" data-timestamp="4.65">In Detroit tonight, closing arguments have ended and a jury is set to deliberate the first case against an alleged terror cell inside the US.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:12.69" data-timestamp="12.69">This the first terrorism case after the September 11 attacks to go to trial. There were convictions, it was a big deal. And then something strange happened. The Justice Department-- the very institution that put these guys on trial in the first place-- went out and voluntarily asked the judge to reverse the convictions. They undid their own victory, and they didn't stop there. They turned around and brought criminal charges against the very guy who prosecuted their case, their own colleague.</p><p begin="00:00:39.16" data-timestamp="39.16">You're going to hear a lot about this prosecutor today. his name is Richard G. Convertino. He used to be a rising star at the Justice Department, with commendations from the Attorney General, the head of the FBI. He sent mobsters and drug gangs and white collar criminals to prison.</p><p begin="00:00:53.40" data-timestamp="53.4">Today we try to answer the question, what the hell happened? What could possibly happen that would make the US government overturn its own successful conviction and then go after its own prosecutor. We're devoting our whole show today to this story. It is <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i> from WBEC Chicago, distributed by Public Radio International. I'm Ira Glass.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Conviction</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:11.59" data-timestamp="71.59">Petra Bartosiewicz has been covering terrorism prosecutions for a few years, this one included and she tells her story.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Petra Bartosiewicz</h4><p begin="00:01:18.19" data-timestamp="78.19">So what kind of prosecutor was Rick Convertino? Well, that depends on who you ask. ,</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Man</h4><p begin="00:01:22.86" data-timestamp="82.86">He was relentless. He was just an excellent prosecutor.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Man 1</h4><p begin="00:01:26.51" data-timestamp="86.51">I really think Mr Covertino is fooling everybody, even his wife and he is a devil.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Man 2</h4><p begin="00:01:33.13" data-timestamp="93.13"> Rick was sought after. He got things done.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Man 3</h4><p begin="00:01:36.90" data-timestamp="96.9"> He had the reputation, at least in the legal community, of someone not to be trusted.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Man 4</h4><p begin="00:01:43.50" data-timestamp="103.5">Call me when you hear him admit to a mistake, would you, please?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Petra Bartosiewicz</h4><p begin="00:01:55.50" data-timestamp="115.5">Here's some of the undisputed facts about Richard G. Convertino. For the 16 years he was a federal prosecutor, he loved standing in front of juries and declaring that he represented the United States of America. He sometimes got to the office before dawn. He took on cases other attorneys wouldn't, stayed involved with victims and even sometimes the defendants after a case was over. But his supervisor told me that if you didn't see things Rick's way, you were a bum. And woe to the prosecutor Rick caught checking Michigan football draft picks on the internet, or slacking off in the break room.</p><p begin="00:02:27.10" data-timestamp="147.1">Rick traveled constantly for trials, even after he and his wife Valerie started having kids. He was away most of Val's second pregnancy, and was out working on a bank fraud trial the day she went into labor.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Valerie Convertino</h4><p begin="00:02:37.91" data-timestamp="157.91">I had to drive my oldest daughter to the babysitter, and then I drove myself to the hospital. And he didn't get to see her till she was about two days old.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Petra Bartosiewicz</h4><p begin="00:02:48.14" data-timestamp="168.14">He was gone for most of her third pregnancy too.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Valerie Convertino</h4><p begin="00:02:50.99" data-timestamp="170.99">I thought he was doing the right thing. I kind of viewed his traveling as being similar to a military family, that the sacrifice that I was making was for my country as well, so that he could do his job.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Rick Convertino</h4><p begin="00:03:07.37" data-timestamp="187.37">We thought the sacrifices we were making meant something, meant a lot. But in retrospect now, for me it's impossible not to be bitter.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Petra Bartosiewicz</h4><p begin="00:03:26.10" data-timestamp="206.1">It's conceivable that everything that happened to Rick and to the Detroit sleeper cell case comes down to office politics gone terribly awry. The office politics at the US Department of Justice are like the office politics anywhere else, except the stakes are higher. You know, justice. The story begins just six days after September 11. After the attacks on the World Trade Center and the Pentagon, law enforcement suddenly had this new directive: Stop the next attack. Catch the terrorists before they strike again.</p><p begin="00:03:59.57" data-timestamp="239.57">Outside of Detroit, agents went out looking for number 27 on the FBI's terrorist watch list: Nabil Al-Marabh, a supposed associate of Osama Bin Laden. They ended up at Al-Marabh's last known address, a dumpy two-story house in Dearborn.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Rick Convertino</h4><p begin="00:04:14.16" data-timestamp="254.16">The front door had the the name Nabil Al-Marabh on it. An FBI agent knocked on the door and down came one of the defendants, Karim Koubriti, to answer the door.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Karim Koubriti</h4><p begin="00:04:25.29" data-timestamp="265.29">I just came back from work, and I was taking a shower. Then I'm done with the shower. I heard a knock on the door, and somebody calling Nabil, Nabil, Nabil. So I went down to see, because they were knocking hard. So they asked me for my ID. I was in my bath stuff, so I don't have my ID on, so I was like, I'm going to go upstairs to get it. As soon as I turn, they follow me.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Rick Convertino</h4><p begin="00:04:48.77" data-timestamp="288.77">During the protective sweep, they find two airport identification badges for Detroit Wayne County Airport.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Karim Koubriti</h4><p begin="00:04:56.41" data-timestamp="296.41">I used to work in a company called Sky Shift. This company provided food for airplanes. We were fired because we had a car accident and we didn't go for two days, so they fired us. But nobody told us to turn over the badges, so they were thrown on the table.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Rick Convertino</h4><p begin="00:05:11.60" data-timestamp="311.6">And then they were asked about their work history, and none of them mentioned the airport, which caused concern.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Karim Koubriti</h4><p begin="00:05:20.77" data-timestamp="320.77">And as soon as they see that Metro Airport, everything changed, like we lied to them or something like that. And the FBI guy was real hard. &quot;Why are you lying to me?&quot; I was like, &quot;Lie to you about what?&quot;</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Rick Convertino</h4><p begin="00:05:37.23" data-timestamp="337.23">So they were placed in handcuffs and the agents asked for permission to search the apartment.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Karim Koubriti</h4><p begin="00:05:46.46" data-timestamp="346.46">Because I just came to the country-- it was like 10 months-- I don't even speak the language. I don't know, I think I thought it was like a regular thing. In the beginning, that's how I think about it, you know? It wasn't a big deal. When they pulled their guns out, I was a little upset.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Petra Bartosiewicz</h4><p begin="00:06:03.31" data-timestamp="363.31">During the search of his room, Koubriti told the agents they'd find some fake documents there-- a fake passport, fake social security card, blank birth certificates-- which they did. Then out of a small closet one of the agents pulled down a black suitcase. In its side pocket she found a day planner. Koubriti told the FBI that none of this stuff was his, that it belonged to an ex-roommate. It all looked bad, but the day planner was by far the most disturbing thing the agents found.</p><p begin="00:06:31.18" data-timestamp="391.18">Flipping through the pages, they saw suspicious looking sketches, simplistic drawings of what looked like airplanes flying into some kind of objects, and then what looked to be an explosion. Remember, this was less than a week since the collapse of the World Trade Center.</p><p begin="00:06:45.67" data-timestamp="405.67">So Rick started to put together this very unusual kind of case. He sorted through the evidence from the apartment trying to figure out, not what had happened, but instead, what was maybe going to happen. He says he showed the day planner to intelligence experts.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Rick Convertino</h4><p begin="00:06:59.93" data-timestamp="419.93">This page, as interpreted by the Air Force, was that there were three types of aircraft fighter planes-- the F 16s, the refueling planes--</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Petra Bartosiewicz</h4><p begin="00:07:11.84" data-timestamp="431.84">At first glance, this sketch might look like a drawing your kindergartner would do. But if you stare at it a minute or two, you begin to wonder why your kindergartner would draw the three kinds of American planes enforcing the no fly zone over northern Iraq. It's crude, but you can understand what's so troubling about it. The words American base in Turkey are written at the top. The US does have a single air base in the Middle East, in Turkey, in the town of Incirlik.</p><p begin="00:07:36.20" data-timestamp="456.2">The sketch shows what looks to be three stick figure planes, each drawn slightly differently-- lined up at the end of a runway-- and a squiggly blob on the lower left that some Air Force experts said might have represented a hardened bunker.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Rick Convertino</h4><p begin="00:07:48.96" data-timestamp="468.96"> So when this was seen by the Office of Special Investigations in the United States Air Force, it was taken very seriously.
