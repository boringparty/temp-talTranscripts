  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:00:01.70" data-timestamp="1.7"> It's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i> I'm Chana Joffe-Walt, sitting in for Ira Glass.</p><p begin="00:00:06.53" data-timestamp="6.53">I've been talking to someone here and there over the last couple of months about a situation she's in. And I think she typifies the thing we're going to try to do in this episode, so I'd like you to meet her. Her name is Annika Barber. She's a scientist, a molecular biologist at Rutgers University, a runner. She's got purple hair and a particular way of organizing the world around her-- her books, organized topically and then by height, her lab, color-coded labels.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:00:35.58" data-timestamp="35.58"> I also track how many days of the year my husband eats pizza, and I make a chart of it. He's really into pizza. So sometimes I just like to have data.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:00:42.80" data-timestamp="42.8"> Wait, wait, wait, why? Why do you do that?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:00:45.23" data-timestamp="45.23"> So my husband really can't go 24 hours without talking about pizza. So last year, I accused him of talking about pizza every day, and he swore he didn't. And I was like, I really think you do. And so starting in January of this year, I said that for 2025, I was going to log every day that I heard him say the word pizza, as well as every time he ate pizza for the year.</p><p begin="00:01:05.96" data-timestamp="65.96">He actually eats less pizza than I would expect for the amount he talks about pizza. Like, the consumption to discussion ratio is not what I expected it would be. He's only had pizza 14 times this year.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:01:16.79" data-timestamp="76.79"> Lately, Annika has been trying to get her mind around a new situation that is deeply confusing for her and lots of people. She's tracking scientific meetings that are not happening.</p><p begin="00:01:30.44" data-timestamp="90.44">When President Trump was inaugurated, he put a freeze on research grants. This meant the meetings where scientists like Annika get together at the National Institutes of Health to assess new research-- they're called NIH study sections-- those meetings were off. But then, a judge said the administration couldn't pause all research grants, so the meetings were theoretically back on.</p><p begin="00:01:55.25" data-timestamp="115.25">They were listed on an NIH website as scheduled, but Annika says they did not seem to be happening. The website showed they were, but scientists were saying they weren't. Scientists who had submitted grants literally had no idea if their grant had been reviewed and moved on to the next step of the funding process.</p><p begin="00:02:15.17" data-timestamp="135.17">But Annika learned there was a place you could get a way better picture of whether a meeting was going to happen or had already happened.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:02:23.79" data-timestamp="143.79"> I had never even heard of the federal register in my American tax-paying, voting life.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:02:30.93" data-timestamp="150.93"> What is the federal register?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:02:32.81" data-timestamp="152.81"> The federal register is where all notices of open public meetings that may be happening in the federal government are required to be posted. And for some reason, NIH study sections do have to be posted to the federal register at least 15 days before they meet.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:02:46.83" data-timestamp="166.83"> And if they're not posted to the federal register, they cannot meet.</p><p begin="00:02:52.33" data-timestamp="172.33">It's a strange feeling knowing you're in the middle of an upending, knowing that something fundamental to the way that you live is changing, but you can't understand or see the scale of change as it's happening. You just can't. You have to wait.</p><p begin="00:03:07.63" data-timestamp="187.63">What I like about Annika is she rejects this. She is not waiting. Annika immediately begins scraping data from federal websites, finding all the meetings that were supposed to happen, cross-referencing them with the federal register, reaching out to scientists to see if the supposedly-scheduled meetings did, in fact, occur, compiled all that into a Google spreadsheet, and posted it online.</p><p begin="00:03:32.23" data-timestamp="212.23">Anxious and confused scientists all over America began consulting Annika's spreadsheet and passing it around to see is my study section actually happening? Has my grant been reviewed? Is there any point in submitting my next grant? Will I be able to keep my lab open?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:03:48.47" data-timestamp="228.47"> There are tens of thousands of scientists whose grants depend on this happening who are all wondering what's going on?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:03:56.18" data-timestamp="236.18"> How much science has not happened?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:03:59.38" data-timestamp="239.38"> So let's see. Right now, we are at 182 meetings that have not happened.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:04:09.74" data-timestamp="249.74"> 182 meetings. How many grants is that?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:04:13.75" data-timestamp="253.75"> You know, if we assume roughly 100 grants would have gotten reviewed at each meeting, we're talking about at least 18,000 grants that didn't get reviewed.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:04:24.80" data-timestamp="264.8"> Wow.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:04:26.14" data-timestamp="266.14"> And that's 18,000 grants across all kinds of things-- I mean, very basic science. Just scrolling through here, I've got things like adaptive immunity and bacterial virulence.</p><p begin="00:04:35.99" data-timestamp="275.99">But then we also have things like addiction risk, and learning and memory and decision making, and therapeutic immune regulation. We have training neuroscientists and physician scientists. And we have studies looking at dentistry and eye diseases, and those aren't meeting.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:04:55.21" data-timestamp="295.21"> For five weeks, there were no NIH study sections, till it was 205 study sections that did not meet. All of those proposals sat waiting, and all the ones that came after, stacking up behind them, a long traffic jam of uncertainty and science not happening.</p><p begin="00:05:13.30" data-timestamp="313.3">And then, in March, a meeting appeared on the federal register, an NIH study section, and then another, and another. Now, the meetings to assess new research grants seem to have mostly picked back up. Scientists are doing peer reviews, but it's not clear if the government will actually cough up the money.</p><p begin="00:05:34.54" data-timestamp="334.54">And a few of the grant proposals that were supposed to be reviewed at these meetings, they're being quietly disappeared from the lists. They're not being reviewed at all. They seem to be the grants with DEI words or other thought crimes in them that are no longer allowed. It's unclear if the scientists who submitted these grants even know that their proposals have been dropped.</p><p begin="00:05:57.46" data-timestamp="357.46">Annika went to a meeting herself. It was two months late, pulled together at the very last minute, but it did happen.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:06:04.43" data-timestamp="364.43"> We all made the time, and people were there, and people gave the same quality of review that I have always experienced in these meetings, and the same level of scientific discussion. But it felt so weird to be having these scientific discussions in this climate where you could only wonder, like, yeah, we have all these amazing scientists in this room reviewing the future of the next five years of research in the areas of molecular neurogenetics.</p><p begin="00:06:30.98" data-timestamp="390.98">But is any of this going to be funded? Are any of these studies going to go forward? Is the NIH going to give these people any money?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:06:38.21" data-timestamp="398.21"> Did anybody ask? Did anybody ask?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:06:41.36" data-timestamp="401.36"> No, no. Every grant gets 15 minutes for discussion, and we needed to get through 50-odd grants, so there's no time to think about anything else.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:06:50.30" data-timestamp="410.3"> So will that stuff that you reviewed get funded?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:06:55.93" data-timestamp="415.93"> I don't know.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:07:00.82" data-timestamp="420.82"> Was there a part of you that felt like, are we chumps? Are we just like--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:07:05.93" data-timestamp="425.93"> Yes, absolutely. It feels like we're fiddling while Rome burns, talking about some cool genes, and worms, and mice, and all the interesting genome-wide association studies that we could do if money keeps existing.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:07:18.11" data-timestamp="438.11"> Uh huh.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Annika Barber</h4><p begin="00:07:19.10" data-timestamp="439.1"> And yeah, are we chumps? Are we participating in furthering something or nothing? Are we just wasting our time?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Chana Joffe-Walt</h4><p begin="00:07:30.52" data-timestamp="450.52"> The thing that is killing her is not knowing the shape of the new normal.
