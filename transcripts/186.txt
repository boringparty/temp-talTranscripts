    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.50" data-timestamp="0.5">It was junior prom. David's date had ditched him and he wasn't doing so great. As he walked to get some cigarettes, he explained to me that the prom was the culmination of a three-year campaign on his part to get in with the cool crowd, a mostly white crowd. It began when he arrived at Lincoln Park High School.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David</h4><p begin="00:00:16.65" data-timestamp="16.65">See, there's a story between here, right? When I was a freshman, when I first got to Lincoln Park, I was a total nerd, geeko, right? So of course I see all these glamorous, beautiful girls, and I'm like damn, wish I could-- only dream of having them, right? Or just taking them out on a date, not even doing anything physical with them, right?</p><p begin="00:00:34.53" data-timestamp="34.53">First junior prom at Lincoln Park, the most gorgeous girl in school, I ask her to prom. She says yes. So goddamn! You know, I changed a lot. Since sophomore year, I got into this crowd and stuff, I just changed. I changed my speech, my dress, and all that stuff. I'm still the same person. I changed. This is the popular crowd. I mean, I'm just popular now.</p><p begin="00:00:56.07" data-timestamp="56.07">So I end up going, right? And I know she has a history of being, like, incredibly moody. She's so gorgeous, take her anyway. You know, if I go with her this year, she's so gorgeous, then all these other girls are going to say, damn. I have to go with him now. If he's good enough for her, he's definitely good enough for me. I'm like fine, that's going to boost my rep a little bit.</p><p begin="00:01:13.65" data-timestamp="73.65">And I'll tell you this. I wanted something physical. I wanted to just fool around with her, meaning kiss her, kiss her, feel her up or whatever. Or even more, you know? Go all the way or whatever. But I wasn't counting on that. I was here to have fun. And that would be like a nice feature to it. That would be a bonus and all. I wasn't pushing her or anything.</p><p begin="00:01:28.15" data-timestamp="88.15">Sure enough, her and her like alleged best friend get into a fight. And here I am alone with my rum and Coke.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:40.95" data-timestamp="100.95">The prom. We don't make kids go into the army in this country. We don't make them go to college. We don't make them get married. But if they're still in school when they're 18, they collide with the impenetrable fact that is the prom. If they go to a school with a junior prom, they get it twice.</p><p begin="00:01:57.09" data-timestamp="117.09">And then they have to take a stand. Go. Not go. Have some sort of experience. Use it to try to climb to a new group of friends. Use it to try to get the girl. Just try to get through it. The stakes are weirdly high. Good luck.</p><p begin="00:02:21.49" data-timestamp="141.49">As we get older, I think a lot of people tend to roll their eyes at the idea of prom. I think because of the distance of years, there is something about it that just feels embarrassing. Certainly if you did not go, but even if you did. The clothes and the corsage and the corny music and the whole thing.</p><p begin="00:02:37.59" data-timestamp="157.59">And I think we forget how thrilling it can be. It could be embarrassing to think how thrilled we once were to go out that night. How the whole thing just seems fraught with possibility to a lot of us.</p><p begin="00:02:50.86" data-timestamp="170.86">So today, on the very tail end of prom season, we bring you four stories of the prom. Let us understand it in its fullness, my friends. Now at last, as adults. From WBEZ Chicago and Public Radio International, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i> I'm Ira Glass.</p><p begin="00:03:13.72" data-timestamp="193.72">Act one of our program today. Tornado Prom. What happens when a natural disaster strikes the same tonight as the unnatural disaster that is the prom.</p><p begin="00:03:24.41" data-timestamp="204.41">Act two. Save the Last Dance For Me... Again.   <h3>Act One: Tornado Prom</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:22.30" data-timestamp="262.3"> Act One. Tornado Prom.</p><p begin="00:04:26.23" data-timestamp="266.23">In Hoisington, Kansas, a tornado hit on prom night. Hoisington is a small town of about 3,000. Nobody at the prom was hurt. In fact, nobody at prom knew it happened. But when prom was over, a third of the town was destroyed. One older man was dead. The seniors were left to figure out what to think of the whole thing. Susan Burton tells the story.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Susan Burton</h4><p begin="00:04:46.81" data-timestamp="286.81">On Friday nights, the teenagers in Hoisington drag Main Street. Or they go to a party at someone's house, and the boys shoot baskets in the driveway, and the girls jump on the trampoline. Sometimes the parties are out the country, at ponds. "At six in the sticks," they tell me.</p><p begin="00:05:02.43" data-timestamp="302.43">Since it's Kansas, there's weird weather. High winds that make the power flicker on and off. They get a lot of tornado warnings, but they never thought one would actually touch down here. Hoisington just seemed too boring to be visited by natural disaster.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Sean</h4><p begin="00:05:16.78" data-timestamp="316.78">It's just like, nothing happens in Hoisington. Like everyone says, oh, there ain't nothing to do in Hoisington. Nothing happens here. They're just like, why would a tornado come here?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Susan Burton</h4><p begin="00:05:34.38" data-timestamp="334.38">Prom actually feels grand in Hoisington. The dresses are elaborate, and like all prime finery, extremely difficult to explain.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Girl 1</h4><p begin="00:05:55.08" data-timestamp="355.08">It's like black and it's fitted, but when you look at it from other directions, it's silver and royal blue. Like people would ask me what it looked like, and it was so hard to explain to them, because it's just so unique.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Sean</h4><p begin="00:06:15.36" data-timestamp="375.36">All very long, tight-- not really any of them didn't have like a tight dress, or like, long-- they were just like one sheet, I guess. I really don't know about dresses. But there's just like one cloth.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Boy 1</h4><p begin="00:06:37.32" data-timestamp="397.32">I don't know. I think prom's more of the female. You know. It's her night, you know? Most ladies are like, you know, want prom to be everything. So I think they wanted it to kind of be something, a night they'll never forget. And some of them got it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Girl 1</h4><p begin="00:06:56.26" data-timestamp="416.26">I mean, I went through lots of prom magazines, just looking at themes and stuff. And we had the whole junior class vote on which one they wanted. And they came to be "Lost in the Moment." So it's kind of ironic. We really were lost in what was happening. We didn't know what was going on. So, I mean, after it, it's lost.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Susan Burton</h4><p begin="00:07:27.15" data-timestamp="447.15">What happens when a prom meets a tornado, when two events that often end in disaster collide? It was sprinkling by the time everyone parked their trucks outside the Knights of Columbus Hall on Main Street. They ducked and ran inside to prom.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Sean</h4><p begin="00:07:55.10" data-timestamp="475.1">There was just a crowd of people taking pictures, I mean, like I was at a basketball game or something. Like ch-ch-ch-ch. Hurt my eyes after a little bit.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Susan Burton</h4><p begin="00:08:03.57" data-timestamp="483.57">This is Sean. He's a junior. He videotaped the prom, partly so he could show his mom what she missed. From here, he and his classmates tell the story.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Will</h4><p begin="00:08:12.71" data-timestamp="492.71">It took a minute before people, you know, finally started dancing and stuff.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Girl 1</h4><p begin="00:08:25.18" data-timestamp="505.18">We got there late. So like, as soon as it-- we got there right at eight o'clock. So as soon as we got there, the dance had already started. We just started dancing. And we danced for, like, an hour or so, you know? Yeah, about an hour. And then the lights started flicking off and on. Like, it didn't flicker. It kind of went off, and then come back on, you know? And people just were-- some people, like my friend TyAnn is kind of scared of the dark, you know? So she's freaking out. But some people, it was just fun, you know? So normal blackout thing, you know?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Will</h4><p begin="00:08:56.41" data-timestamp="536.41">There was just a lot of people screaming and yelling, you know? Just acting like fools and stuff. I was too. And I got yelled at one time. Hand me that beer, break out the keg, and stuff like that. And the lights back on. Everybody just kind of looked around and went on their way, you know? Wasn't even anything big.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Girl 2</h4><p begin="00:09:32.17" data-timestamp="572.17">The door kept opening. And we could hear, we knew it was getting windy outside.
