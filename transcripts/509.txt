  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.59" data-timestamp="0.59">A couple years ago, a guy named Lanail Hudson-- Lanail Thaddeus Hudson, actually-- goes to a government office in Jacksonville, Florida to get a new Florida state ID, and they ask him to wait. And then they call out a state trooper, because a man in Miami with the exact same name, Lanail Thaddeus Hudson, had gone to the authorities and flagged the name. Said there had been identity theft. Said, somebody's got a fake driver's license under this name.</p><p begin="00:00:23.93" data-timestamp="23.93">So the trooper shows up, and he's got to figure out what's going on. And the guy walks up to him in the lobby and says, well, maybe we can straighten this out now. Great. So the trooper asks the guy for any proof of who he is. Here's the trooper, Richard Blanco.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:00:36.32" data-timestamp="36.32">And he opens the backpack, and he starts pulling out all these identifications. Just numerous IDs. The passport, social security card, 20 to 30 pieces of identification on him.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:47.69" data-timestamp="47.69">There's a government-issued ID that lets him walk around the port in Jacksonville unescorted. There are two naval IDs that allow him onto Navy vessels. There's a birth certificate.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:00:56.29" data-timestamp="56.29">You know, he's slinging all these identifications on the desk. This is me. This is who I am. These documents, these IDs depicted his photograph. So then I had a little doubt in my mind.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:09.87" data-timestamp="69.87">Your doubt meaning what?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:01:11.30" data-timestamp="71.3">This could be the true Lanail Hudson. He could be the victim.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:21.32" data-timestamp="81.32">So he fingerprints the guy and runs it through the system, and sure enough, the fingerprints match the fingerprints on file for Lanail Hudson. Whoever this is standing in front of them has been arrested all the way back in 1989 as Lanail Hudson. He's actually served in prison as Lanail Hudson.</p><p begin="00:01:37.19" data-timestamp="97.19">But then Trooper Blanco gets the other Lanail Hudson on the phone-- the guy in Miami. That Lanail Hudson turns out to be a corrections officer down in Dade County, and that Lanail Hudson said that ever since his wallet was stolen back in 1989 with his driver's license and his social security card inside, he'd had problems. He tells them--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:01:57.23" data-timestamp="117.23">This guy's taken my identity. He's lived in my identity for 23 years, since 1989. He's caused me all kind of grief. My driver's license is suspended.</p><p begin="00:02:10.47" data-timestamp="130.47">He's cashed checks in my name. He has children in my name. On and on.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:16.70" data-timestamp="136.7">At this point, Trooper Blanco strongly suspected the guy standing in front of him in Jacksonville was probably a fake, living under a stolen name. But vexingly, the guy would not admit this, even after he was arrested. At his job, with his wife and kids-- he had an 8-year-old and a 15-year-old at the time-- everybody knew him as Lanail Hudson. In fact, when this went to trial, the guy from Jacksonville still insisted he was Lanail Hudson.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:02:42.88" data-timestamp="162.88">His version was that he was Lanail Hudson, and that's all he's ever known himself to be is Lanail Hudson.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:49.00" data-timestamp="169">And in his version of the story, the other guy is the criminal.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:02:52.85" data-timestamp="172.85">No, no. He didn't accuse the other guy of being a criminal. He just insisted that he was the real Lanail Hudson. Today, we still don't know who he is, and he's been incarcerated since September 30, 2011.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:14.33" data-timestamp="194.33">Well, today on our radio show, It Says So Right Here. We have stories where what is written on official papers in black and white can determine the rest of your life. From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life,</i> distributed by Public Radio International. I'm Ira Glass.</p></div>  </div>

<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Doe-ppelgangers</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:29.51" data-timestamp="209.51">And this case right here is a good case in point about that. After his trial, the man from Jacksonville-- he could no longer be Lanail Hudson, right? All of his IDs, his birth certificate-- all of that stuff was seized by the government. He became basically a man with no paper trail at all. No documentation.</p><p begin="00:03:46.90" data-timestamp="226.9">He's like a man without a country. He had no identity. So, who was he?</p><p begin="00:03:53.13" data-timestamp="233.13">Well, we found his wife Rosemary, and she told us that she believes him when he says that Lanail Hudson is the only name he's ever known. She's always known him that way, since 1996. Has never met his family, says he rarely talks about his past.</p><p begin="00:04:05.40" data-timestamp="245.4">When they had kids, she said, she suggested that he might take the kids down from Jacksonville to Miami, where he used to live, to meet his people, and he told her, no. It's too much heartache down there. But she didn't know the details. Trooper Blanco, meanwhile, says there is one clue about what could be his real name.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:04:23.50" data-timestamp="263.5">There's a good possibility that it was Leroy Mayer or Meyer.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:27.11" data-timestamp="267.11">Those names show up on police paperwork on September 29, 1989, when he was arrested for four charges, including attempted murder and aggravated battery. He's identified as Lanail Hudson in those police reports, but the paperwork also says that he sometimes goes by the name of Leroy Meyers or Leroy Mayers, and his nickname today is Leroy. That's the name his wife calls him. During his trial, he explained that this way.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:04:51.22" data-timestamp="291.22">He says, well, when I was a young man, I got involved in a fight, and I licked the guy pretty good. I whipped him. And when I got done, all my friends call me Bad, Bad Leroy.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:05.51" data-timestamp="305.51">Like the Jim Croce song? "Bad, Bad Leroy Brown"?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:05:08.08" data-timestamp="308.08">[LAUGHING] Yeah.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:11.01" data-timestamp="311.01">And what's your read on this? Like, what do you think is his real story?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:05:16.72" data-timestamp="316.72">You know, I don't have proof, but I think he's a foreign national. He could be wanted for a crime, could be a fugitive from his country. Even his wife thought he was from another country, but I couldn't prove that he was from another country, so he can't be deported unless you have proof.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:41.94" data-timestamp="341.94">And when you run Leroy Meyer or Leroy Mayer-- when you run down who that might be, do you see someone of his age with a criminal record or anything like that?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:05:51.16" data-timestamp="351.16">Nothing came back to Leroy Meyer or Mayer. We could find nothing. He went to prison as John Doe, so he never told us who he was.</p><p begin="00:06:01.53" data-timestamp="361.53">And the real trick is once he's released from his 10-year sentence, who's he going to be when he gets out of jail or out of prison? That was a concern of the judge. Who will you be when you're released from prison? What will be your identity?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:16.50" data-timestamp="376.5">You think if I ran him down now and asked him, he would still insist that he's Lanail?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Blanco</h4><p begin="00:06:21.85" data-timestamp="381.85">You know what? I'd like to invite you to do that. I'd like to invite you to go and interview him and see if he'd grant an interview, and see what he would say.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John Doe</h4><p begin="00:06:33.96" data-timestamp="393.96">Hello?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:34.98" data-timestamp="394.98">Yes, hi. Is this John Doe?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John Doe</h4><p begin="00:06:38.21" data-timestamp="398.21">Um, yes. I guess according to the state, I am.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:43.54" data-timestamp="403.54">I reached the man that I suppose I'm going to call John Doe at the Federal Correctional Institution in Jesup, Georgia. I asked him what his real name was, the one he was born to.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John Doe</h4><p begin="00:06:52.63" data-timestamp="412.63">Lanail Hudson, and this is the only name I've ever had, the only name I've ever known. I'm dubbed John Doe at this point. I hate when people call me. They don't have the decency to say, well, call me Mr. Doe. John Doe. There are other instances where the recall petitions are being used as a political enemies list-- a blacklist.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Charlie Sykes</h4><p begin="00:45:56.91" data-timestamp="2756.91">OK, Thomas Wolfgram, the judge from Ozaukee County who signed the Walker recall petition, is having now to explain why he signed that.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:46:07.87" data-timestamp="2767.87">During the spring of 2013, the same time Josh was interviewing with the governor's office, another situation was playing out in a judicial election. Wisconsin is one of the states that elects its judges. And at some point, a newspaper had run the names of judges through the recall database. It found 29 signed the recall petition.</p><p begin="00:46:28.37" data-timestamp="2788.37">Among the names, Thomas Wolfgram, a judge from Ozaukee County, one of the most conservative counties in the state. That spring, Wolfgram was up for reelection.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Charlie Sykes</h4><p begin="00:46:38.17" data-timestamp="2798.17">This has now become an issue in that campaign because a young conservative attorney who is an extremely good candidate-- an Iraq war vet, former clerk for the Wisconsin Supreme Court-- is raising the issue.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:46:49.87" data-timestamp="2809.87">What you're hearing is a talk show host in Milwaukee, Charlie Sykes. He was one of the conservatives who turned Wolfgram's signature into an issue. Sykes highlighted the story on air, explaining to listeners that Thomas Wolfgram had been exposed-- how he was this closeted Democrat living among the conservatives of Ozaukee County. And he explained how the judge with the signature had outed himself.</p><p begin="00:47:13.78" data-timestamp="2833.78">On the air, the judge is referred to as a, quote, "flaming liberal" and a "unionista," among many other things. One of these segments also showcased the judge's challenger, a Tea Party backed lawyer named Joe Voiland, who had decided to run against Wolfgram after finding out about the recall signature. In this audio you're about to hear, the host of the show, Charlie Sykes, speaks first.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Charlie Sykes</h4><p begin="00:47:37.44" data-timestamp="2857.44">He's never actually stepped forward and said, I signed it because I wanted to throw Scott Walker out of office. I signed it because I'm a liberal, pro-union Democrat, and I wanted to overturn the 2000 election.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Joe Voiland</h4><p begin="00:47:49.02" data-timestamp="2869.02">Well, he put his John Hancock on that piece of paper, and your signature-- your John Hancock-- is who you are and what you stand for. Now, look. The judge in his private life supported lawlessness. The recalls were about lawlessness and selfishness, and lawlessness and selfishness are two things that a judge should not support.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:48:13.07" data-timestamp="2893.07">In reality, Thomas Wolfgram, the guy being called lawless-- he made a career as a criminal prosecutor, first as an assistant DA, then as a deputy district attorney.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Thomas Wolfgram</h4><p begin="00:48:23.02" data-timestamp="2903.02">And I eventually ran for and was elected district attorney here in Ozaukee County.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:48:29.70" data-timestamp="2909.7">And what were you running as?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Thomas Wolfgram</h4><p begin="00:48:32.28" data-timestamp="2912.28">I ran as a Republican.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:48:36.48" data-timestamp="2916.48">A few things to know about the guy being lampooned as a lawless, pro-union flaming liberal, other than the fact that he's a former prosecutor and a Republican. Wolfgram was originally appointed to the bench by a Republican governor. And once he was on the bench, people liked him. In 2008, Wolfgram was named Judge of the Year by the Wisconsin State Bar Association.</p><p begin="00:48:57.44" data-timestamp="2937.44">Given his reputation and his record, when the signature thing hit, a lot of people rallied to the judge's defense. He was endorsed by every police chief in his county, the county sheriff, the Deputy Sheriffs Association, a number of district attorneys, prominent private attorneys, other state judges, state Supreme Court justices. On talk radio, these people were simply referred to as Wolfgram's, quote, "courthouse cronies."</p><p begin="00:49:22.22" data-timestamp="2962.22">So this campaign went on for months. And remarkably, during that time, the challenger, Voiland-- he actually never criticized any part of Wolfgram's nearly two decades on the bench.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Thomas Wolfgram</h4><p begin="00:49:33.78" data-timestamp="2973.78">To my knowledge, no one ever questioned my criminal sentencing practices, my decision making in civil or family law cases. They never quarreled with my judicial demeanor. It was limited to one issue-- that I had signed a recall petition.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:49:51.12" data-timestamp="2991.12">Joe Voiland, the lawyer who ran against him-- he told me, yeah, that's true. He actually never had any problems with how Wolfgram did his job. But he said Wolfgram's signature permanently compromised his ability to be seen as fair and impartial.</p><p begin="00:50:06.30" data-timestamp="3006.3">Wolfgram, for his part-- he told me he'd signed the petition not as a political statement about Walker's policies, but because he thought the public wasn't given enough time to digest the policies. That's what made it traumatic for the state. This is actually something Walker later said himself.</p><p begin="00:50:23.75" data-timestamp="3023.75">But so for months, Joe Voiland sounded the alarm in speeches, on the internet, on the radio, in mail to voters. Wolfgram's signature had tainted him. Wolfgram went on to lose by a landslide-- by more than 25%.</p><p begin="00:50:46.16" data-timestamp="3046.16">After Wolfgram lost, the head of the Republican Party in his county wrote an editorial for the local paper. The title was, "Calling a Spade a Spade." Quote, "A big thank you is in order to all the Democrats and liberals who signed the Recall Walker petition. As was clearly shown in our election for judge, the signed petitions will be utilized as an effective tool to determine the most viable candidates for future elections."</p><p begin="00:51:11.08" data-timestamp="3071.08">He says Wolfgram's huge defeat is a warning, not just to people who signed the petition, but to people who support anyone like that. It's a not so veiled threat to people who stood up for Wolfgram-- people like the Republican county sheriff and the Republican district attorney. Essentially, he says, look out. We're coming for you next.</p><p begin="00:51:35.02" data-timestamp="3095.02">I talked to one of the bloggers who first outed Josh Inglett for having signed the recall. He's a young conservative named Brian Sikma. He told me, while Governor Walker's office doesn't say whether it checks who signed the recall, he said, frankly, he finds the idea that they don't check-- he called it far-fetched. And if somehow they didn't know before Josh Inglett that bloggers and activists would look at each and every appointment they make and call people out, Brian Sikma says he hopes they learned a lesson-- that outing people like this is now just standard procedure.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Brian Sikma</h4><p begin="00:52:08.35" data-timestamp="3128.35">Sure. Well, there are district attorneys, assistant district attorneys. We found some judges. We also found people that serve on the judicial ethics board. Candidates for school board would probably be about the lowest rung that would be checked and would end up in the public debate, as far as during an election.</p><p begin="00:52:25.47" data-timestamp="3145.47">People in the past may have been left to wonder, well, whose side are they on politically? But now with the recall petition database, we can go in there, enter their name, and we can get some idea, some perspective as to whether or not they would be siding with Governor Walker, or whether they may not be using their position to slow down the governor's agenda, or at least have a certain perspective on an issue being debated in the state right now.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:52:50.26" data-timestamp="3170.26">In terms of the environment this has created within Wisconsin politics, I had a pretty telling conversation with a Republican staffer. The staffer told me Josh Inglett's story in particular made him worried, because, he said, he saw himself in it. He'd also signed the recall petition. Actually, he signed it the day after Josh did.</p><p begin="00:53:11.29" data-timestamp="3191.29">The staffer's mother was a teacher, just like Josh's mom, and the staffer had signed the recall as a Christmas present to her. This person, who we'll keep anonymous for obvious reasons, says he was really afraid that someone would find out about his signature. And he worried it wouldn't just be embarrassing for him, but it could hurt his boss and his boss's standing within the Republican Party-- make it harder for both of them to do their jobs. Describing what this time was like, he said he was seeing people, quote, "exposed and publicly shamed nearly every week." He said, quote, "I lived in fear."</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:53:57.70" data-timestamp="3237.7">What do you feel like this has taught you about politics?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Josh Inglett</h4><p begin="00:54:03.81" data-timestamp="3243.81">I guess there's always a larger game that's at play.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:54:07.02" data-timestamp="3247.02">For his part, Josh Inglett's reaction to all this-- to his name being withdrawn-- wasn't to get mad at all. When reporters came calling, Josh says he was honest, and his honest reaction was to accept what was happening, not to retaliate or point fingers.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Josh Inglett</h4><p begin="00:54:22.59" data-timestamp="3262.59">Not to show any hatred towards the governor, but to show compassion and love, I guess. Not to reject them, but to be supportive. And it was not a time to act childish or to be yelling and kicking and screaming and pouting because of what had happened.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:54:41.13" data-timestamp="3281.13">Was that at all hard to do? Were you feeling that way?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Josh Inglett</h4><p begin="00:54:44.43" data-timestamp="3284.43">No, that was something that I've always been raised to do. When I played quarterback in high school, that was what I had always done. When you play quarterback in Portage, you don't win very many games, but you always take the blame for things.</p><p begin="00:54:59.58" data-timestamp="3299.58">And when you win, it's always the line. The line is the reason that you won the game, and you are the reason that you would lose the game. Credit the line for your wins, and take the blame for your losses.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:55:12.72" data-timestamp="3312.72">Josh says maybe it was advice from his dad, the Lutheran minister, or from his mother, who had always encouraged him to send personal thank you notes. Which that was actually something Josh did in this case, including one to the governor's office, who he told if they ever had any positions in the future, and he could be helpful, he'd still love to serve.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:55:33.22" data-timestamp="3333.22">Given this experience that you've had, if you had the chance to take that signature back, would you do it?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Josh Inglett</h4><p begin="00:55:41.62" data-timestamp="3341.62">No. I wouldn't. I still love my mother, and there's no reason that I should waver away from my love for my mother due to politics like that. That's not something that should interfere there.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ben Calhoun</h4><p begin="00:55:57.25" data-timestamp="3357.25">So for the Walker administration, if you're looking for somebody who agrees with your politics, who's a fan of yours, and who's still excited to serve, but who won't sell out his mom, for your consideration, Joshua Inglett. He says you've got his cell number.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:56:17.54" data-timestamp="3377.54">Ben Calhoun is one of the producers of our show.</p><p begin="00:56:20.64" data-timestamp="3380.64">[MUSIC - "PERMANENT RECORD" BY THE GOLDEN DOGS]</p></div>  </div>
