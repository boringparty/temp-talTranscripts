  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.70" data-timestamp="0.7">When Dave got his job at the homeless shelter, it was one of the lowest level jobs they had. And he didn't think of advancing up the ladder because he really liked his job.</p><p begin="00:00:07.85" data-timestamp="7.85">He liked talking with the residents. He helped make meals, did bed checks, rented movies for movie night, got urine samples for drug testing. One guy had scabies and Dave would put lotion on the scabies. He didn't even mind that.</p><p begin="00:00:20.23" data-timestamp="20.23">On his weekend shift, he'd pick of all kinds of cake mixes and special ingredients that he would blend together into these crazy cakes for the 200 residents.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave Hill</h4><p begin="00:00:26.85" data-timestamp="26.85">That was my rep. When I would walk in, people would get excited because they knew that an incredible cake was just hours away.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:34.55" data-timestamp="34.55">So you were good at the job?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave Hill</h4><p begin="00:00:36.15" data-timestamp="36.15">Yeah. I mean, I thought it was fun. One of my favorite things to do was with the urine samples. Because you'd take like a Dixie cup. The supervisor would ask you to go do this.</p><p begin="00:00:50.88" data-timestamp="50.88">He would say, would you, you know, go get a urine sample, and so he gave me the cup and then the container to put the urine sample in. I would go do it and then this one time he hadn't come back to his desk yet so I had the urine sample and I got another Dixie cup and ran into the kitchen and filled it with apple juice.</p><p begin="00:01:15.71" data-timestamp="75.71">So he said, oh, you got the urine sample? I said, yeah, you know, and I acted like, yeah, I did a great job. I got the urine. Here you go. You know, and I'm like, can you get me another vial because I have left over urine. I couldn't fit all the urine into the vial you gave me so I have this cup of urine still.</p><p begin="00:01:34.72" data-timestamp="94.72">You could see like already the horror was setting in, like, no, that's fine. I just need the one vial. I don't need two vials of urine. And I said, well, I need another vial because I have this urine. What am I going to do with this urine?</p><p begin="00:01:48.67" data-timestamp="108.67">And he was just like, ah, get it out of here. Throw it out. What are you--? And it was really a proud moment for me. I was really happy about it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:25.11" data-timestamp="145.11">And did you then have to explain, no, no, it was apple juice?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave Hill</h4><p begin="00:02:28.94" data-timestamp="148.94">After a while. Eventually I did. I mean, I let it-- I milked the moment for all it was worth. And there were some of the residents standing around watching it happen.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:38.56" data-timestamp="158.56">They must have loved you.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave Hill</h4><p begin="00:02:40.74" data-timestamp="160.74">Yeah, I tried to keep things light.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:43.62" data-timestamp="163.62">So after about a year into this job, Dave was asked if he wanted to fill in as a supervisor for an upcoming Friday night shift.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave Hill</h4><p begin="00:02:50.00" data-timestamp="170">You know, I was excited. I thought I was being rewarded for, you know, the great job that I had been doing all this time. So I was like, oh, wow. Someone believes in me. I can be a supervisor. I felt really good about it. I'd never been supervisor of anything.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:05.10" data-timestamp="185.1">Well, today's radio show, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life,</i> by the way. I'm Ira Glass. Our show is from Chicago Public Radio and Public Radio International. Today's show, on this week when there's a new boss in Washington D.C., is all about what happens when somebody becomes the new boss.</p><p begin="00:03:20.08" data-timestamp="200.08">We've got a story about a guy taking over a town where people dislike him so much, they won't even talk to him.   <h3>Act One: New Guy on the Job</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="subject"><h4>Dave Hill</h4><p begin="00:03:41.91" data-timestamp="221.91">And so I showed up for my shift which was an 11 to 7-- 11 at night to 7 in the morning shift. Like, what do you do as the new boss?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave Hill</h4><p begin="00:03:56.84" data-timestamp="236.84">You have keys to everything, which I was excited about. And you tell people when to do bed checks.</p><p begin="00:04:10.96" data-timestamp="250.96">Everything was going smoothly. I was walking around with my keys. It was sort of a nice feeling. I sort of threw my shoulders back a little bit, you know. You know, I don't think I was getting power mad. I tried to be, you know, as benevolent of a supervisor as I could. But, you know, everything I'm saying, I'm talking about like 45 minutes into it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:36.96" data-timestamp="276.96">Oh, really? 45 minutes into it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave Hill</h4><p begin="00:04:40.19" data-timestamp="280.19">Everything I've just said is really just the first 45 minutes on the job-- all of these things that went through my mind and all these things that happened and didn't happen.</p><p begin="00:04:49.88" data-timestamp="289.88">[LAUGHS]</p><p begin="00:04:56.78" data-timestamp="296.78">So it's about one in the morning on my inaugural supervisor shift. And the weekend cook comes in, and I had worked with him many times before and, you know, was very friendly with him. And so he came in.</p><p begin="00:05:14.62" data-timestamp="314.62">And he said, hey, Dave, can I borrow the keys? He's like, I just want to get some meat out of the freezer. I thought, oh, he's just getting a jump on things, you know. So I said, sure, here's the keys. He disappeared for 10 or 15 minutes and then came back and said, thanks, Dave, here you go. And he gave me the keys back. And I went home and I thought great, like, you know, my first supervisor shift went pretty well.</p><p begin="00:05:54.81" data-timestamp="354.81">And I walked in the next day and the security guard was sitting there. I said, hey, Al, how's it going? And he said, good. Hey, did you hear what happened, you know, with the cook last night? I'm like, what are you talking about? And he said, oh, he stole 300 pounds of beef last night. And, you know, I pretended not to have any idea how that might happen.</p><p begin="00:06:21.54" data-timestamp="381.54">I was like, what? You're kidding. What are you talking about? And he's like, yeah, apparently he got keys to the freezer and went down there and took out 300 pounds of meat. He took it all out and threw it over the fence to his buddy.</p><p begin="00:06:37.35" data-timestamp="397.35">And I was going like, what? Why would anyone have a need for 300 pounds of meat at 1 AM on a Friday? And he's like, well, to sell it. I'm like, why would anyone-- what do you  mean, sell it? He's like, sell it on the street for crack.</p><p begin="00:06:55.53" data-timestamp="415.53">None of this was making any sense to me and he's like, he's a crack head. And I'm like, what? What are you talking about? He's like, well, yeah, he's a crack addict. He was like a recovering crack-- well, now he's, I guess, full on back to being a crack head, but he was working there as a recovering crack addict. And then he starts going down the line and he's like, well, yeah, this guy's a crack addict, this guy's a crack addict. Like program aids, supervisors, security guards.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:26.30" data-timestamp="446.3">People who have your job.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave Hill</h4><p begin="00:07:27.46" data-timestamp="447.46">Yeah. And he was just going down the line. He's like, yeah, he's a crack addict. And then he starts explaining. He's like, don't you remember when he didn't show up for work that one day. Let's put that at $14 trillion.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Adam Davidson</h4><p begin="00:51:48.17" data-timestamp="3108.17">So do you know those numbers on--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Alan Blinder</h4><p begin="00:51:50.14" data-timestamp="3110.14">You don't know any of these numbers. You estimate all of them.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Adam Davidson</h4><p begin="00:51:52.81" data-timestamp="3112.81">So the economy should be producing $15 trillion, but it's only producing $14 trillion. There's $1  trillion shortfall. So the theory says that if the government spends money, or gives us money to spend through tax cuts, that will increase everybody's spending and get the economy back to where it should be.</p><p begin="00:52:10.26" data-timestamp="3130.26">The government doesn't have to spend the whole $1 trillion shortfall, though, because of something called the Keynesian multiplier. Every dollar the government spends produces more than a dollar in spending throughout the economy. If they pay you to build a bridge, you spend your paycheck on rent and food and so on, and then those people have money to spend on other stuff. So, according to Keynes, there's a clear answer to how much Mr. Obama's administration should spend.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Alan Blinder</h4><p begin="00:52:35.94" data-timestamp="3155.94">To raise the GDP by $1 trillion. Suppose your multiplier was around one and a half, so that would lead you to conclude that you needed about $650 billion as a stimulus. Voila. That's the kind of number they're talking about right now. You see it in the newspapers every day, a number in that range.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Adam Davidson</h4><p begin="00:52:58.57" data-timestamp="3178.57">Have you done this more rigorously for yourself?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Alan Blinder</h4><p begin="00:53:01.55" data-timestamp="3181.55">I have not, but I hope they have.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Adam Davidson</h4><p begin="00:53:11.74" data-timestamp="3191.74">Right now, a lot of economists are supporting the idea of a stimulus package. There are people you'd expect, like Paul Krugman, a proud Keynesian at <i>The</i> <i>New</i> <i>York</i> <i>Times,</i> and there are some surprises like President Reagan's Chief Economic Adviser Martin Feldstein. When I talked to economists, I found a lot are in the middle, not convinced it'll work, but in the absence of anything else, they figure let's give it a try.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Alex Blumberg</h4><p begin="00:53:34.04" data-timestamp="3214.04">But the frightening thing about a stimulus package is that no matter which economists you talk to-- the ones who want the Keynesian stimulus and the ones who think it's the worst idea ever-- are looking at exactly the same data. They're just coming to utterly opposite conclusions about it. This, even economists like Alan Blinder will tell you, is the problem with economics.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Alan Blinder</h4><p begin="00:53:51.88" data-timestamp="3231.88">The biggest problem with learning things in economics is the inability to do controlled experiments. So we don't have-- unlike what is the case in many but not all sciences-- the definitive experiment. Right? This experiment they did in the 1920s proved that Einstein was right about the perturbation of mercury. It proved it. We can never do that in economics.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Blumberg</h4><p begin="00:54:18.52" data-timestamp="3258.52">The best you can have is a really good theory.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Alan Blinder</h4><p begin="00:54:20.69" data-timestamp="3260.69">The best you can have is a real good theory. It's not going to work perfectly in a textbook manner all the time.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Alex Blumberg</h4><p begin="00:54:26.93" data-timestamp="3266.93">For Tyler Cowen, though, this is about as good as it gets for testing Keynes's theory. Remember, he like a lot of anti-Keynesians, doesn't think Keynes got us out of the depression. He doesn't support Barack Obama's stimulus plan. He thinks Keynes was probably wrong. But if we do the stimulus and the economy quickly recovers--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tyler Cowen</h4><p begin="00:54:45.30" data-timestamp="3285.3">That would prove that Blinder is right and I am wrong.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Adam Davidson</h4><p begin="00:54:49.09" data-timestamp="3289.09">Definitively?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tyler Cowen</h4><p begin="00:54:50.46" data-timestamp="3290.46">Well, no test is definitive, but it would be strong evidence. If we spend $700 billion and the economy recovers within a year or a year and a half, I would take that as serious evidence that my view is wrong. I don't think it will happen, but I would take it seriously.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Adam Davidson</h4><p begin="00:55:08.13" data-timestamp="3308.13">So, this just happens to be-- just the elements that have come together right now have just accidentally created the perfect conditions for this test.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tyler Cowen</h4><p begin="00:55:18.95" data-timestamp="3318.95">Near perfect. I view this, if we decide to, as the first test of Keynesian fiscal policy as a formula for getting out of a depression. The very first test. It's one reason why I don't want to do it, because in my opinion, the idea's untested. So to spend, say, $1 trillion on an untested idea, in my view, is a mistake.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Adam Davidson</h4><p begin="00:55:52.94" data-timestamp="3352.94">There are lots of economists who think a Keynesian stimulus would be a mistake for all sorts of reasons. Some say you don't need a stimulus to fix the recession because a recession isn't a disease that needs a cure. It is the cure. A recession brings down prices from a falsely inflated high. A recession is what makes houses affordable again.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Alex Blumberg</h4><p begin="00:56:13.27" data-timestamp="3373.27">Others say a stimulus package might end the recession but then we'll have worse problems later on: vicious inflation, a bigger, less efficient government, and $1 trillion more in debt to pay off. The Obama administration is betting that none of this will happen. They're trusting a different theory. They're trusting Keynes.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:56:33.11" data-timestamp="3393.11">Alex Blumberg and Adam Davidson.
