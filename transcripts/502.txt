  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.84" data-timestamp="0.84">When the phone calls started, Meron Estefanos was leading a quiet, busy life. She'd been born in Eritrea in East Africa, moved to Sweden as a teenager. She was living in a suburb of Stockholm.</p><p begin="00:00:19.02" data-timestamp="19.02">She was also a human rights activist, focusing on Eritrea. That's what her radio stories were about, too. But at the end of February 2011 came this phone call--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Meron Estefanos</h4><p begin="00:00:31.59" data-timestamp="31.59">This guy from UK called me and told me that his brother was kidnapped and that he was being asked $20,000 for the release of his brother.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:40.99" data-timestamp="40.99">The man said his brother was with other people who had been kidnapped, all of them Eritrean, and that they were in the desert, far from Eritrea. They were on the Sinai Peninsula in Egypt, close to the border with Israel. He said they were being tortured and held for ransom-- $20,000 each, which seemed absurdly high to Meron. Eritrea's a country where the average person makes less than $1,000 a year.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Meron Estefanos</h4><p begin="00:01:03.16" data-timestamp="63.16">I had a hard time believing that, actually, what he was telling me was true. And she couldn't bring herself to try the numbers. But then that night, she couldn't sleep thinking about it. So--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Man</h4><p begin="00:01:28.12" data-timestamp="88.12">[INAUDIBLE].</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Meron Estefanos</h4><p begin="00:01:30.04" data-timestamp="90.04">Hello?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Man</h4><p begin="00:01:31.49" data-timestamp="91.49">Hello.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Meron Estefanos</h4><p begin="00:01:32.46" data-timestamp="92.46">Hello, salaam. He told her his name was Biniam, he was 22 years old, that he'd fled Eritrea in 2010, had been living in a refugee camp in Sudan, working at a farm, when he was kidnapped and taken to Sinai.</p><p begin="00:02:07.28" data-timestamp="127.28">He said he was there with other people in a group of 28. People had been fleeing Eritrea for years. Its government is one of the most repressive in the world. And sometimes, people got kidnapped, either on their way out on the road or from refugee camps right across the border.</p><p begin="00:03:20.49" data-timestamp="200.49">But it was still this shadowy thing that human rights groups were just starting to report on at that point in 2011. It wasn't clear how many people it was happening to. And the ransom surprised her, like I said-- the size of it, $20,000. Meron had never heard of a ransom that high. This is a story about the severe danger some of them face. A warning to listeners-- this is a story of hostages and people having their lives threatened. There is some content that is violent and probably not right for children to listen to. Here's reporter Yowei Shaw.</p></div>  </div>

  <h3>Act One: Act One</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Yowei Shaw</h4><p begin="00:05:27.74" data-timestamp="327.74">Meron had a lot of questions for these people on the phone. And they torture us.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Biniam</h4><p begin="00:05:58.36" data-timestamp="358.36">[SPEAKING TIGRINYA].</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Translator</h4><p begin="00:06:01.79" data-timestamp="361.79">And towards the evening, after they torture us for so many hours, they give us one bread and they give us very small amounts of water.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Biniam</h4><p begin="00:06:10.79" data-timestamp="370.79">[SPEAKING TIGRINYA].</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Translator</h4><p begin="00:06:12.29" data-timestamp="372.29">We've been tortured so much that we have wounds all over our backs, and we have lost our appetites as a result of that.
