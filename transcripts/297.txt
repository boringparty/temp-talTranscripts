<div class="act" id="act5">
  <h3>Act Five: Water Bed</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:47:48.46" data-timestamp="2868.46">Act Five, Water Bed. Well, we have one last story about homes and houses in New Orleans. Louann Mims is 78 years old, and never paid much attention to the annual hurricane and storm warnings in New Orleans where she lives. But this year, this year was different. The morning of August 29, the day the hurricane hit, she got everything ready to pack her car to go to the Superdome to take refuge from the incoming flood. From a temporary housing facility in Maywood, Illinois, where she is now, she talked to Alex Kotlowitz about that day.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:48:17.59" data-timestamp="2897.59">I got up in the morning and I looked out, and it looked fairly ordinary. I said, well, I'm going to have a bowl of cereal and a banana. I put the cereal in and poured on the milk and had two bites, and I looked, and the water was just coming in from the patio. So I got up, looked to the front part of the house. Water was coming in the front door.</p><p begin="00:48:44.04" data-timestamp="2924.04">Oh, my God, what do I do? But then it came so quickly, and within five to eight minutes there was almost five feet of water in the house. So I'm now, God, what am I going to do? And everything is floating. I sat on the top of the ladder, and I realized then it was getting a little later in the afternoon, and I had better just see if there was anything that I could sleep on that night, because I couldn't get out. I couldn't go walking in all this water.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:49:17.66" data-timestamp="2957.66">Your house is one story?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:49:18.79" data-timestamp="2958.79">Uh-huh. It's a 10-room ranch. So I walked past the spare room, and the mattress is about to go under, so no sleeping on that one. I go to my bedroom, and my mattress was floating. I say, hey, I'll go get my ladder. I  can climb up there. And I did, and that's where I was for eight days and eight nights. And this mattress is a Stearns &amp; Foster, and I had just received it the end of April. I bought the extra firm, so it must have a lot of wood in it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:49:57.60" data-timestamp="2997.6">So you're sitting, you're floating on your mattress. King size or--?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:50:00.99" data-timestamp="3000.99">A queen size.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:50:02.74" data-timestamp="3002.74">Are you lying on your bed all day?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:50:05.20" data-timestamp="3005.2">Well, I had no choice.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:50:07.43" data-timestamp="3007.43">You're just reclining, or sitting on it, or?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:50:10.84" data-timestamp="3010.84">I was just lying. Now, I had two pillows, and they were very wet, but that was all right. I was used to that.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:50:17.05" data-timestamp="3017.05">Up to where on your body, the water?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:50:19.78" data-timestamp="3019.78">Well, there was times when it was about mid-thigh. There was one time the first day, my chest of drawers turned so that the back was up, and I stepped on it in hopes of climbing on the bed. And it just flipped me right into the water, and I went under the water for a second. And I just held my breath a little bit and floated right on up.</p><p begin="00:50:45.61" data-timestamp="3045.61">My refrigerator in the kitchen went belly-up. I couldn't open the door. My refrigerator had that little door outside that has the water and the juice and the milk on the door. So I opened that, and I was able to get some orange juice out. And then I took off my meat cleaver, and I kind of broke one side of that little thing. I got some raisins, some cheese, and each morning I have a few bites because I didn't know how long I'd be there. And I said, I have to have a few bites every day. And then, late in the day when I got hungry, well, I just kind of ignored it.</p><p begin="00:51:23.53" data-timestamp="3083.53">And to my great disappointment, when I came here and I had my physical, I had not lost one ounce. I was really disappointed there, because I was counting on, at least I want to get something out of this. I want to lose a few pounds, but I didn't.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:51:38.56" data-timestamp="3098.56">What did you do about water?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:51:39.86" data-timestamp="3099.86">Well, I had a full gallon. I decided 12 ounces was enough for the day. I'd have 12 ounces of water, and that kept my kidneys healthy enough.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:51:51.24" data-timestamp="3111.24">It sounds like you had survival experience. How would you know?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:51:53.91" data-timestamp="3113.91">Well, the reason-- I was in nursing for 42 years, and I kind of knew how some things would be. People say, weren't you frightened that this was happening to you? Not really. It's just the will to say I will not succumb to this. But I wasn't even thinking like that. I was just thinking of all practical things, because I knew eventually someone would find me.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:52:23.34" data-timestamp="3143.34">Sounds like you were awfully calm. What else would you do to keep your spirits up?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:52:29.03" data-timestamp="3149.03">When I was just lying there, I just kind of said, well, Louann, you know, you have this furniture, some of it about 40 years, and time to get some more anyway. I watch garden and-- what is that HGTV thing? And I watch that a lot. I enjoy that. When I'm bored with something else on TV and there's stupid stuff, I go to that.</p><p begin="00:52:54.09" data-timestamp="3174.09">So they're talking about color all the time, beautiful colors. Oranges-- well, I wouldn't want red or anything like that, but maybe a pale orange or a pale yellow. And then I thought, well, now I have a chance, and I never really liked the Formica they had on my countertops, so I thought, now I'll get marble. And I did not like the floor covering they had on it, now I'll get ceramic tile. The carpet, of course, has got to be thrown out. But I said, now I'll have hardwood floors, because that's what I want anyway. Saying, oh, that looks pretty good.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:53:30.13" data-timestamp="3210.13">So you're on your mattress every day, just lying there, and you're thinking about redecorating your house?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:53:33.98" data-timestamp="3213.98">Well, yeah, I did. That's the way I was motivating myself. If I was going to say, oh poor me, I might die here of this or that, or why don't somebody come and get me? I figured they would eventually make rounds, and they would find me.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:53:50.48" data-timestamp="3230.48">During those eight days, what did you miss the most?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:53:53.71" data-timestamp="3233.71">I have so many TVs in my house, because I love television. I have six. I had one in each bedroom. In the great room was like two in there, because I had a very large one, 53, for sports. I'm an avid sports fan, so I love all the tennis, all the golf, and all the basketball. So I enjoy that on my large screen. But I never missed it one time, and I could not understand why I didn't miss it. I really did not miss it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:54:22.88" data-timestamp="3262.88">And what were you wearing during all this time?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:54:26.29" data-timestamp="3266.29">A bra. That's all, because everything else got so wet. I mean, I didn't have anything anyway. I had that old muumuu that I wore outside. I had that on the first day of the storm, and that was wet. After that, through these eight days, I just had on a bra, nothing more.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:54:43.71" data-timestamp="3283.71">Did you learn anything about yourself that you didn't know before?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:54:48.20" data-timestamp="3288.2">Well, always my upcoming, I was terribly, terribly, terribly poor when I was growing up. And I thought that there was a white world and a black world. I thought white people had things that black people didn't have because of the two worlds, and blacks were not permitted those things. Well, when I grew up, and I left Louisiana when I was 17 years old and ended up in Michigan, I discovered, oh, money was the problem.</p><p begin="00:55:19.51" data-timestamp="3319.51">So when I discovered that, then I said-- I don't know, I can say I set out to prove that I could be equal. And so I educated myself. I set out to improve myself. I was the kind of person, I have so many clothes. No one woman ever needed as many clothes as I've had in my life. I had pajamas that I had never worn: silk, beautiful pajamas. I have two full-length mink coats, and I have a beautiful Persian.</p><p begin="00:55:54.85" data-timestamp="3354.85">It was just ironic that, when I arrived here, I came here in an old muumuu which I didn't notice that was on the wrong side. I had no underclothes on, and I had no shoes. I've had 100 pairs of shoes in my lifetime. So I never want to own all that stuff again.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Alex Kotlowitz</h4><p begin="00:56:14.01" data-timestamp="3374.01">So you realized you just didn't need all of that?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Louann Mims</h4><p begin="00:56:16.53" data-timestamp="3376.53">Right, I didn't need all of that.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:56:19.19" data-timestamp="3379.19">After her rescue, Louann Mims was put on a plane to Chicago wearing only the muumuu she was rescued in and a pair of socks that a relief worker gave her. She's currently at a facility run by the state of Illinois, hoping to get a new apartment somewhere near her daughter. The daughter plans to write a thank you letter to the mattress company, Stearns &amp; Foster, at some point. She talked with Alex Kotlowitz, author of <i>Never</i> <i>a</i> <i>City</i> <i>So</i> <i>Real,</i> <i>There</i> <i>Are</i> <i>No</i> <i>Children</i> <i>Here,</i> and other books.</p></div>  </div>
