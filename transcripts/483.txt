    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.34" data-timestamp="0.34"> I spoke with Julia Lurie the day that she tried something new with her students. She teaches English at an all-girls high school in South Korea, in the city of Gwangju. I reached her on Skype. And she told me that ever since she arrived in South Korea, she'd been really surprised by a bunch of things at her school.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:00:16.79" data-timestamp="16.79">There's a full-length mirror and a scale on every single floor of the school in the main hallway.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:24.28" data-timestamp="24.28">And do girls use them?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:00:25.32" data-timestamp="25.32">Yeah, they use them all the time. A lot of them don't really think twice about it. And then I asked some of the girls about it, and they said, yeah, our principal wants them there because it's an all-girls high school, and he wants us to be skinny. And he made a joke that if we lose a certain amount of weight, then we can get a cafe. And my first response was like,  OK, that's really messed up. My second response was just, that's really mixed messages.</p><p begin="00:00:56.78" data-timestamp="56.78">Plastic surgery is super, super common. Like, one in five women in Seoul have it. A lot of my students want to get it after they finish their college entrance exams, as like a rewards of sorts.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:08.70" data-timestamp="68.7">Is this plastic surgery to look more Western?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:01:12.14" data-timestamp="72.14">Yeah, exactly. So the most common is the-- it's called double eyelid surgery. But it's basically to put a crease in your eyelids so it looks like more Western eyelids. Other common ones are cutting down your jaw bones so that you have more a V-shaped face as opposed to a circular face.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:33.57" data-timestamp="93.57">In class and on the bus, young women carry these hand mirrors, Julia says, big hand mirrors, like the size of a small dinner plate. And they're always pulling them out and checking themselves. Julia says the pressure on them to look a certain way is worse than in the United States.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:01:48.31" data-timestamp="108.31">When you are applying for university or you're applying for a job here, you put a picture of yourself on your resume or your application. And it is sort of taken for granted that how you look will often go into the decision of if you get into that university or if you get that job.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:05.45" data-timestamp="125.45">Really? If you get into university, they'll decide based on how pretty you are?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:02:09.78" data-timestamp="129.78">Well, so I've had it explained to me as, if they have two equal candidates, like otherwise equal candidates, then the prettier person will get the job.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:19.55" data-timestamp="139.55">And her students just see this is as normal, like there's nothing weird or disturbing about that at all. And so she wanted to talk to them about it, though she wanted to be careful how she did this. It would be easy to come off as condescending or preachy, or like, oh, this is so terrible. And she didn't want to do that. She just wanted them to know it's not like this everywhere. You could see all this differently. She recorded the class on her iPhone for us. She started with the basics.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:02:44.79" data-timestamp="164.79">Not ugly. OK, so raise your hand. What else?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Student</h4><p begin="00:02:56.35" data-timestamp="176.35">Me.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Student</h4><p begin="00:02:57.34" data-timestamp="177.34">Big eyes.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:02:58.32" data-timestamp="178.32">Big eyes.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Student</h4><p begin="00:02:59.31" data-timestamp="179.31">It's me.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:02:59.80" data-timestamp="179.8">You!</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:00.30" data-timestamp="180.3">I don't know if you could hear it, one of the girls said "me" when asked what a beautiful woman looks like.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:03:05.35" data-timestamp="185.35">So I got "thin," "tall," "V-shaped face." We had this funny moment where they didn't know the word for "breasts," so I was teaching them  the word for "breast."</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:03:15.21" data-timestamp="195.21">[INAUDIBLE]. No, breast.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Students</h4><p begin="00:03:16.46" data-timestamp="196.46">A breast.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:03:16.72" data-timestamp="196.72">Breast.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Students</h4><p begin="00:03:17.61" data-timestamp="197.61">A breast.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:03:18.27" data-timestamp="198.27">OK, so--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:03:19.13" data-timestamp="199.13">So I'm drawing one on the board.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:03:20.74" data-timestamp="200.74">B cup?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Student</h4><p begin="00:03:21.58" data-timestamp="201.58">C cup.</p><p begin="00:03:22.52" data-timestamp="202.52">[INTERPOSING VOICES]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:03:24.41" data-timestamp="204.41">A cup.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:25.35" data-timestamp="205.35">They talked about how these ideas of beauty are subjective, which definitely was not news to these students. And then Julia explained some of the things that just surprised her as an American living in South Korea. That's how she put it. She wanted to sound as neutral as possible. This is just different than what she'd seen in America. We usually don't have scales and mirrors in high school hallways. We don't use photos on university and job applications the way Koreans do.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:03:48.17" data-timestamp="228.17">Photo, OK. In the USA, it is illegal, against the law--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Student</h4><p begin="00:03:54.15" data-timestamp="234.15">The law.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:03:56.09" data-timestamp="236.09">--to discriminate, to say no to a person because of how they look.</p><p begin="00:04:02.02" data-timestamp="242.02">[INTERPOSING VOICES]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:04:02.86" data-timestamp="242.86">So I cannot say, [INAUDIBLE], you cannot work for me because you are ugly.</p><p begin="00:04:10.21" data-timestamp="250.21">[LAUGHTER]</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:13.41" data-timestamp="253.41">Technically, actually, this isn't true. Ugly people are not a protected group under United States law. But you get her point. Ugliness is not going to keep you out of Harvard. Julia showed them a graph so they could see how much plastic surgery women in South Korea get compared to women in other countries. They are number one, more per capita than anywhere. And they discussed all this. One girl said that that statistic made her feel ashamed as a South Korean.</p><p begin="00:04:38.91" data-timestamp="278.91">And toward the end of the class, Julia asked them to write a couple sentences about self-improvement through plastic surgery, or scales in school hallways, or pictures on resumes, about why they were good or why they were bad. And about half of them thought good and half thought bad. Two of Julia's students surprised her by saying that they'd already had plastic surgery themselves. One of them wrote that it was good for her because it gave her confidence.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:05:00.32" data-timestamp="300.32">And the thing is, this is coming from my smartest class. And it was kind of hard to hear-- like, for scales, for example. Now that they're in high school, they're basically like, I like being able to check my weight and seeing if I should eat less or not. And they made the point-- and it's a really valid one-- that they want to make sure they're on track and not be stressing about it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:30.03" data-timestamp="330.03">It's interesting how much of their argument is, I want to be a success, and this is the way to success.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Julia Lurie</h4><p begin="00:05:35.06" data-timestamp="335.06">Mm-hm.
