  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.49" data-timestamp="0.49"> From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i> I'm Ira Glass. You're powerless.</p><p begin="00:00:27.44" data-timestamp="27.44">The parents in the story today, they thought they were close to their son, who was doing really well in school. Things seemed fine.   <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:00:43.07" data-timestamp="43.07"> It would probably be wrong to say that Cody Treybig loved basketball at the beginning. And you're walking in, and it's like-- it's just really-- it's amazing.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:01:10.03" data-timestamp="70.03"> Cody says he wasn't exactly avoiding physical activity. The game was just super fun. He and his friends would all log on, go on adventures together. As someone who used to play a lot of video games, it felt like bad etiquette not to ask who his online character was.</p><p begin="00:01:23.88" data-timestamp="83.88">Cody told me-- and knowing him a little bit now, this seems entirely appropriate-- he was a paladin, which is a holy knight who can fight. But also, he's nice. He casts healing spells when his co-adventurers get hurt. Cody eventually built them up to level 60, as high as you can go.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Cody Treybig</h4><p begin="00:01:37.98" data-timestamp="97.98"> I was just really proud of having six-eighths of Lightforge gear, which was nothing. But--</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:01:42.84" data-timestamp="102.84"> All of which is just to say that sports were not Cody's top priority. In gym class when they'd have to run laps, he'd always be at the back. Basketball somehow though, he kind of liked. His dad had played when he was growing up. So early on, in third grade, Cody found himself on this local youth league team.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Cody Treybig</h4><p begin="00:01:59.76" data-timestamp="119.76"> I was not very athletic at all. And we'd be talking about <i>World</i> <i>of</i> <i>Warcraft</i> while the game was going on on the bench.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Drew Treybig</h4><p begin="00:02:09.76" data-timestamp="129.76"> OK. Well, from Mom's perspective, I thought he was adorable.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:02:13.70" data-timestamp="133.7"> That's Mom, Drew Treybig. But it bothered him, after a while, that the boys were making fun of him.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:02:45.54" data-timestamp="165.54"> Drew describes herself as a kind of helicopter mom, which seems right. I'm going to get one.</p><p begin="00:02:56.19" data-timestamp="176.19">Drew says the whole family is kind of kooky. Whenever someone was leaving, they'd be sure to say I love you, to the other person. And I don't mean leaving the house, just like leaving the room. Cody's dad, Jimmy, says he had a hard time getting his father to say I love you. So this is their fix.</p><p begin="00:03:11.94" data-timestamp="191.94">The Treybigs live in Texas in the outskirts of Austin. Jimmy was an early tech entrepreneur. There were cows around, but no neighbors you could walk to, which Jimmy told me he regretted.</p><p begin="00:03:28.17" data-timestamp="208.17">As it happened, there was an old basketball court out behind the house. Cody is in third grade at the time.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Cody Treybig</h4><p begin="00:03:43.38" data-timestamp="223.38"> So I remember the first meeting. He came in the front door. He said they used to call him "Springs" was his nickname because he dunked a lot.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:04:01.11" data-timestamp="241.11"></p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Cody Treybig</h4><p begin="00:04:04.80" data-timestamp="244.8"></p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:04:19.78" data-timestamp="259.78"> So that first day, AJ walks in. They all hang out by the front door. Cody is shy, but AJ gets right down on his level. The two of them go out to the basketball court, talking the whole way. When they get there, Cody can't really even do a layup.</p><p begin="00:04:33.09" data-timestamp="273.09">And AJ is like, OK, we can fix this. Let's do some basics. He teaches Cody this drill where you stand just to the side of the basket, try to bank it in off this magic spot. Then you get the rebound, go to the other side, and do it again, back and forth, back and forth.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Cody Treybig</h4><p begin="00:04:46.51" data-timestamp="286.51"> And he would put his hand through the hoop and act like a little monster, a dragon or something, squiggle his fingers around like, feed the monster. Feed the monster. [CHUCKLES] So I thought that was pretty fun.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:05:00.82" data-timestamp="300.82"> AJ works with Cody two days a week, then three days a week. Sometimes other kids come over so they can all train together. And it's great. Mom would bring out snacks for everyone. Or if other kids weren't around, Mom or Dad would get drafted.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Drew Treybig</h4><p begin="00:05:12.75" data-timestamp="312.75"> We would stand there and pretend to be a player on the other team so that they could-- or he told us to try to move into them, or try to steal the ball, or you know. So we could kind of be silly doing that too, right?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:05:25.47" data-timestamp="325.47"> Was that fun?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Drew Treybig</h4><p begin="00:05:26.10" data-timestamp="326.1"> Yeah. Yeah. It was really fun-- but not on the hot days. I'd say, AJ, I don't want to do this. It's too hot. [LAUGHS]</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:05:33.87" data-timestamp="333.87"> Word spreads around town about this great basketball trainer. Other parents hire him. But AJ and Cody, they have something kind of special. They just click, and they have this way of being around each other that seems sweet.</p><p begin="00:05:45.09" data-timestamp="345.09">When AJ was working with Cody, they would trash talk on the court, which I always thought of as involving a lot of swearing. But AJ taught Cody this really hilarious kind of trash talking-- just messing with someone, asking weird, random questions in the middle of the game. Hey, what did you have for breakfast?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Cody Treybig</h4><p begin="00:05:59.38" data-timestamp="359.38"> He would act like my mom sometimes. [LAUGHS] And that would obviously crack me up, and it would be hard to focus.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:06:18.32" data-timestamp="378.32"> Cody played constantly. If the family went on a trip, he would deflate a basketball, stick it in the suitcase, then re-inflate it after they arrived.
