Someone from work, a mutual friend, called Letty with the news.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Aaron Foley</h4><p begin="01:05:19.17" data-timestamp="3919.17"> Was he the first person you knew that had passed from it, or--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Letty Azar</h4><p begin="01:05:23.81" data-timestamp="3923.81"> He was. But interestingly, three hours later, I got a call on a DPD friend who had passed away. That's Jonathan Parnell, who was one of our DPD captains. And I've known his family for some time. So he was a great guy, very well-respected police officer and member of the community.</p><p begin="01:05:46.50" data-timestamp="3946.5">So it was Marlowe and Recon that day, and then the next day two, more. Then, the next day, three more. And that week of March that Marlowe passed away was-- I hope to never have to live anything like that again.</p><p begin="01:06:06.05" data-timestamp="3966.05">But it was just unbelievable, the number of calls, and texts, and people you knew that you worked with, you prayed with, you ate with, you grew up with, that you would see at community meetings, that you realize you're just never going to see again. We lost so many valuable souls in this city. And to come out of this pandemic-- they'd be the first people you'd turn to to say, all right, how do we get everybody-- how do we help rebuild some momentum? And they're not there.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Aaron Foley</h4><p begin="01:06:42.82" data-timestamp="4002.82"> Over the next few hours on that day, tributes started to pour in across social media about Marlowe, about how Detroit lost a giant, a champion, whatever your preferred term may be, and there was now a void that couldn't be filled. Who do you call now when you need advice or when you need mentorship? Who calls you when you need to be checked on? Who among us did he leave, and what would he be doing now in this moment when racial inequities have been laid bare, not just by the virus, but also by the killing of George Floyd?</p><p begin="01:07:14.41" data-timestamp="4034.41">In Detroit, another name you hear at the demonstrations these days is Aiyana Stanley-Jones. She was a seven-year-old girl from the East Side who was killed in a police raid while she slept on a couch back in 2010. Adam Hollier-- my friend, the state rep-- says Marlowe would be jumping in right now to help realize the goals of Black Lives Matter, wherever the biggest need is.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Adam Hollier</h4><p begin="01:07:39.24" data-timestamp="4059.24"> Oh, Marlowe? I mean, Marlowe is the type of person who would have been in the forefront. But he'd also have been someone that white people would have been reaching out to, right? So I have gotten a number of calls and texts from my well-intentioned, caring white friends who were like, hey, just checking in on you. Thinking about you.</p><p begin="01:08:01.74" data-timestamp="4081.74">Marlowe would have been the king of getting those kind of things, right? There would have been a host of people reaching out to him. And they'd say, well, how can I be helping?</p><p begin="01:08:09.83" data-timestamp="4089.83">He was like, well, you can do these three things, right? He'd say, I've got this organization that is doing this work. You can donate. Your business could hire more Black people. You can do these things.</p><p begin="01:08:20.36" data-timestamp="4100.36">And I think people are now willing to say that Black Lives Matter. What they aren't willing to say is how much they value them, right? How much do they matter? He would have been uniquely situated to have those conversations with people.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Aaron Foley</h4><p begin="01:08:34.16" data-timestamp="4114.16"> James Feagin IV is another one of those connectors in Detroit who I know knows everybody. Anytime I see him out, he's in a blazer and a button-up shirt. But now--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>James Feagin Iv</h4><p begin="01:08:43.32" data-timestamp="4123.32"> Do I have to be in "sound bite, I'm on the air" mode? Or can I just talk?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Aaron Foley</h4><p begin="01:08:47.27" data-timestamp="4127.27"> Just talk.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>James Feagin Iv</h4><p begin="01:08:48.71" data-timestamp="4128.71"> All right. Because yeah, I'm in gym shorts on my patio right now. I'm not on right now.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Aaron Foley</h4><p begin="01:08:55.73" data-timestamp="4135.73"> That's fine.</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="01:08:57.20" data-timestamp="4137.2"> Both James and Marlowe are around 40. They both grew up on the East Side during that chaotic time in Detroit when crack started to ravage the city. They both built their careers in Detroit, dedicated to Detroit.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Aaron Foley</h4><p begin="01:09:09.80" data-timestamp="4149.8"> Where is Marlowe missing right now?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>James Feagin Iv</h4><p begin="01:09:17.70" data-timestamp="4157.7"> [EXHALES] You know, this was a question I started thinking about the other day. And it was tough, because I'm still mourning. And a lot of us are. But the sad irony that Marlowe's last act was at a Police and Pancakes breakfast on the Far East Side, trying to foster positive-- I'm sorry. [SOBS] Positive relations with young black males and the police. That's what's missing right now, you know? [SOBS]</p><p begin="01:10:08.92" data-timestamp="4208.92">There's a lot of us who may have had negative experiences with police, but we also got police in our family, friends. If we could create more forums like that, then it would help everybody. Because our goal is that everybody, number one, stays alive, and number two, has a chance to contribute to a better Detroit and a better Black community. And I can just see him right now being that person that would be as angry as we all are about what happened to George Floyd, but also have some real relationships to point to and lean on.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Aaron Foley</h4><p begin="01:10:57.60" data-timestamp="4257.6"> When I talked to Marlowe's wife, Valencia, she didn't want to discuss the final days of her husband's life and says their two children are still adjusting to life without their dad. But she told me this story about an overseas trip they took just this past fall.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Valencia Stoudamire</h4><p begin="01:11:13.32" data-timestamp="4273.32"> We went to Rome in October. And literally, we saw the Colosseum. And he was, like, tearing up. And I'm like, oh, babe, you know, what's wrong? And he's like, you just don't understand. I'm not supposed to be here. This was what I saw in textbooks. This was not real.</p><p begin="01:11:37.33" data-timestamp="4297.33">And here's what had me to believe that it wasn't. (VOICE BREAKING) It wasn't real for me as a Black male. It wouldn't be my reality to see it. And it was overwhelming to him. I'm so glad we got to have that experience.</p><p begin="01:11:59.28" data-timestamp="4319.28">But he just-- it was very important for him that he beat the odds. And it was important to him that not only did he beat them, but all of the young men that looked like him behind him had an opportunity to do the same. And I think that was the most important thing to him.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Aaron Foley</h4><p begin="01:12:37.63" data-timestamp="4357.63"> Last year, Marlowe did this thing for 50 days straight where, each day, he'd write a Facebook post about two Black people in Detroit he saw potential in, which he later turned into a website called <i>Roster</i> <i>Detroit.</i> This came after a Chamber of Commerce official said that Amazon didn't choose Detroit for its new headquarters partly because the city didn't have enough talent. Marlowe's response was this list to say, here's what you missed out on.</p><p begin="01:13:03.52" data-timestamp="4383.52">Right now, the list of people we've lost to COVID is too long. So, too, is the list of people we've lost to police brutality. These lists are about Black death. But at this moment, I like to think about <i>Roster</i> <i>Detroit,</i> Marlowe's list of Black talent-- a list that's about Black people, living.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="01:13:38.59" data-timestamp="4418.59"> Aaron Foley. He's about to become the head of the Black Media Initiative at the Newmark Graduate School of Journalism at the City University of New York.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Marlowe Stoudamire</h4><p begin="01:13:46.14" data-timestamp="4426.14"> I'm looking at this sign in the window. I don't know if y'all can see it. It says, "Nothing stops Detroit." Nothing stops the people of Detroit, is what I would say. And the point that I'm making is, y'all, look-- if not us, then who? You got to make a decision about who you're going to be, right? And the city has to make a decision about what it's going to be and who it's going to be.</p><p begin="01:14:09.18" data-timestamp="4449.18">And I will tell you right now, it's tough. But the last time I checked, this was the D. And so I will just say, stay motivated, stay connected, and help people. Help people, help people, help people. I'll help you. Hit me up. All right, D. Holla.</p><p begin="01:14:29.66" data-timestamp="4469.66"></p></div>  </div>
