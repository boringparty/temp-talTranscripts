You don't think of the word &quot;moxie&quot; much anymore, but when you read this, it is typical. In preparation for writing this book, he read everything he could find on the subject. Then he lists all this stuff that he and his trained researcher read to figure out how the great leaders of all ages had dealt with people.</p><p begin="00:00:36.96" data-timestamp="36.96">He says, &quot;I recalled that we read over 100 biographies of Theodore Roosevelt alone. He says, &quot;I often went fishing up in Maine during the summer. Personally, I am very fond of strawberries and cream, but I have found that for some strange reason, fish prefer worms. So when I went fishing, I didn't think about what I wanted, I thought about what they wanted. I didn't bait the hook with strawberries and cream, rather I dangled a worm or a grasshopper in front of the fish and said, wouldn't you like to have that? Why not use the same common sense when fishing for people?&quot;</p><p begin="00:01:32.92" data-timestamp="92.92">To win friends and influence people, Dale Carnegie says over and over in the book, think about what they want. People, he says, are basically only interested in themselves. And if you can get yourself to the point where you are genuinely interested in them and what they like and what they want, then they will like you, and they will do your bidding.</p><p begin="00:01:52.86" data-timestamp="112.86">So imagine you are 11 years old and you dad notices that you don't bring many friends around the house. And he takes you aside and he gives you a copy of this actual book. This happened. The Dennis the Menace's dad used to wear with this kind of white, slicked back hair. He looked like every guy that was in my dad's Kiwanis with him. And didn't seem to me like a guy who particularly would have that many friends or much influence over people. But he had written the book.</p><p begin="00:02:42.14" data-timestamp="162.14">So my father went onto make this speech basically, about how now that I was getting older-- I was 11 years old-- that it was time for me to kind of get social skills that he assumed I didn't have.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:55.91" data-timestamp="175.91">Now, in fact, in school, Paul had plenty of friends. He was kind of a class cut up. And his way of winning friends was the same as other kids. He talked about what they saw on TV and tried to be funny. It was just around the house that he was quiet and withdrawn. But he figured, he wanted to be more popular. He wanted more friends. So he decided to try out some of the techniques in the book.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Feig</h4><p begin="00:03:17.53" data-timestamp="197.53">And I went through these and principle number one was become genuinely interested in other people. Principle number two was smile. Principle number three was remember that a person's name is to that person the sweetest and most important sound in any language.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:32.88" data-timestamp="212.88">Yeah, I was wondering about that one in particular. If as an 11 year old you tried to employ that one.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Feig</h4><p begin="00:03:38.30" data-timestamp="218.3">Well, the hard thing was when you're 11 years old, the only thing you ever call anybody is by their last name. And so I remember getting to school the next day and my friend Dave Flurry, who I had always called Flurry, I remember going up to him and going, hey, David. How's it going? And he gave me the strangest look.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:10.91" data-timestamp="250.91">Paul used techniques from the book to try to talk to girls. Kids do not talk like that.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Paul Feig</h4><p begin="00:04:24.22" data-timestamp="264.22">And I would sit at lunch with my friends. Then what did your brother do? Oh, he punched you. Really? And what happened then? I was just hating it because I just wanted to get back to my life with my friends.</p><p begin="00:05:48.64" data-timestamp="348.64">Well, the teacher was dissatisfied, and the students were dissatisfied.   <div class="act-inner">
