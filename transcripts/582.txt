<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Beaching and Moaning</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:08.45" data-timestamp="308.45"> Act One, "Beaching and Moaning." And even when an animal is not a pest-- not chewing up homes, not spreading disease, not biting average citizens-- even when an animal is pretty much universally loved, it can still wreak havoc when it arrives in our world. James Spring has this example.</p><p begin="00:05:27.27" data-timestamp="327.27">A community of seals-- harbor seals-- in La Jolla, California near San Diego. Note that the version of this story that we're putting online and as a podcast includes some curse words that we have unbeeped. If you prefer a beeped version of our show, you can find that at our website, thisamericanlife.org. Anyway, here's James.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>James Spring</h4><p begin="00:05:45.71" data-timestamp="345.71"> I feel like it all starts with their little noses, so cute. Soft and rubbery like a pony's. And their stubby flippers. Their plump little bodies like speckled bean bags.</p><p begin="00:05:57.38" data-timestamp="357.38">Visitors come from all over the world to watch the harbor seals of La Jolla wiggle across the white sand of a little beach called Children's Pool. It's called Children's Pool because back in 1931, the city built a seawall along a curved peninsula to make a protected cove for children.</p><p begin="00:06:12.98" data-timestamp="372.98">And it totally worked. I played there when I was a kid. I didn't drown even once.</p><p begin="00:06:18.80" data-timestamp="378.8">There weren't any seals back then. But later, in the mid-'80s, when I was a teenager, they started to come ashore at night. Just a few at first, and then a bunch.</p><p begin="00:06:28.40" data-timestamp="388.4">One night my friend Tom and I ended up at the Children's Pool after a Wang Chung concert. Don't judge me. It was late. I feel like there were maybe 20 seals on the sand.</p><p begin="00:06:37.90" data-timestamp="397.9">And I sat down next to the small one. He looked up at me and I reached out with my hand and then he totally leaned into it with his neck, like a cat, and I thought, oh, rad.
