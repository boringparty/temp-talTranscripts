And so Uncle Vito decided that it would just be easier to keep his own set of food and drink in the garage. I'm Ira Glass.</p><p begin="00:05:54.24" data-timestamp="354.24">Today in our program, family legends. In that act, we hear the greatest Aunt Mary story of all Aunt Mary stories.</p><p begin="00:06:17.78" data-timestamp="377.78">Act Two, We Don't Talk About That. In that story, a mom, two daughters, and the worst acceptance letter in the history of college admissions.</p></div>  </div>

<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Take My Cheese, Please</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:42.55" data-timestamp="402.55"> Act One.</p><p begin="00:06:44.50" data-timestamp="404.5">So, let's pick up again with Eddie Schmidt and his mother and father. Eddie's sister, Lori, figures in the story at a key point.</p><p begin="00:06:51.78" data-timestamp="411.78">The setting-- the whole family, plus Eddie's Uncle John and Aunt Vivian, were at Aunt Mary's for a meal.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Eddie Schmidt</h4><p begin="00:06:57.89" data-timestamp="417.89"> I remember, you know, Aunt Mary setting out the whole spread, and a lot of, you know, cold cuts and salami and pepperoni and cheeses. And I guess there were two different kinds of cheese from which you could choose. Somehow, Uncle Vito's cheese, the nicer cheese, provolone, had found its way onto the table. And maybe something that we didn't always get at home too often. But Aunt Mary was very insistent that we choose one kind of cheese, the American cheese.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Josie Schmidt</h4><p begin="00:07:30.27" data-timestamp="450.27"> Lori was really wanting the different cheese.
