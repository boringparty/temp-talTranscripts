  <h3>Act One: Act One</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Alix Spiegel</h4><p begin="00:03:17.44" data-timestamp="197.44">There are things that we know and there are things that we don't know. We know that Hitler ordered it built in 1936 and that is cost a small fortune, close to $250,000. We know that it was completed in 1939, that each cabin was finished in a different fine wood, and that its sail, over 40 feet long, was made of a rare Egyptian linen.</p><p begin="00:03:39.83" data-timestamp="219.83">We know that its designer was Heinrich Gruber, the most lauded naval architect at the time. And we know that its purpose was to demonstrate the superiority of the master race. But we don't know, we'll never be able to say with absolute certainty whether or not Hitler ever actually set foot on the <i>Ostwind,</i> or if this idea was just some twisted collective fantasy perpetuated for over half a century by celebrity-crazed Americans.</p><p begin="00:04:08.59" data-timestamp="248.59">Of course there were always rumors about the yacht. And some of those rumors were repeated so often that it's difficult now to distinguish them from fact. One of the rumors was that Hitler and his mistress, Eva Braun, used the <i>Ostwind</i> for pleasure cruises. Another is that Hitler had an extraordinary affection for the boat, that it was very important to him, and that when he talked about it, he always referred to it as his special lady.</p><p begin="00:04:34.83" data-timestamp="274.83">But there's no real evidence for any of this, just as there's no real evidence that Hitler's ghost continues to haunt the decks, or that the boat was used as a brothel by high-ranking Nazi officials, or that the yacht itself is cursed, that it will kill, maim, or financially decimate anyone unfortunate enough to own it. Although, on this last point at least, there does seem to be a series of suspiciously consistent anecdotes.</p><p begin="00:05:05.28" data-timestamp="305.28">This is the story of the last days of the <i>Ostwind,</i> one of two yachts built by the Nazi government after a poor German showing in the 1936 Olympic races. Even though its original purpose was to demonstrate the superiority of the German people in Olympic competition, the <i>Ostwind</i> actually spent most of its life in America, war booty transported here by the US Navy in 1947.</p><p begin="00:05:28.92" data-timestamp="328.92">I should tell you that when it arrived in this country, it wasn't known as Hitler's yacht. It became Hitler's yacht. More precisely, we made it Hitler's yacht. The Ostwind's sister ship, the <i>Nordwind,</i> a boat, mind you, with an identical pedigree, built at the same time by the same people for the same purpose, has never been known as Hitler's yacht. To this day, it sails in Britain under the name <i>The</i> <i>White</i> <i>Rose,</i> a charter boat which inspires no controversy, attracts no attention at all.</p><p begin="00:06:01.25" data-timestamp="361.25">But here in America, sometime in the 1950s, we made the <i>Ostwind</i> into Hitler's yacht. We made it into Hitler's yacht and then we sailed it, cursed it, set it on fire, restored it, tried to put it in a museum, stripped it to a skeleton, and finally sailed it into the Miami Harbor, and as a group of Holocaust survivors stood witness on the deck of a boat nearby, sank it to the bottom of the sea.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>J.j. Nelson</h4><p begin="00:06:42.24" data-timestamp="402.24">That road is a back road to the airport. Pecan Park there. It's a modest neighborhood which borders the Trout River, a series of small ranch houses with water views and long docks stretching out behind them.</p><p begin="00:07:04.17" data-timestamp="424.17">J.J. owned one of those docks. He made his living renting boat space to wealthy weekenders who understood the pleasures of a fishing line and a cold beer. J.J., himself, was a fan of fishing lines and cold beer, particularly cold beer. He was a famous Jacksonville drunk.</p><p begin="00:07:19.84" data-timestamp="439.84">Now this is not a past that J.J. is ashamed of, not at all. More than once he's told me, without a note of regret in his voice, that as a young man he had, and I quote, &quot;the worst reputation from Miami to Virginia.&quot;</p><p begin="00:07:35.25" data-timestamp="455.25">So I went down to Jacksonville to visit J.J. I spent two days in his truck listening to his extravagantly entertaining, occasionally appalling, and seemingly endless stories. J.J. is an excellent storyteller and a genius at Jacksonville history. He specializes in two particular kinds of narratives-- tails about crooked politicians thieving the public, and yarns about his own rather intimate experience with debauchery. Like the time J.J. tricked a man who had crossed him into marrying the woman that J.J. had tired of living with.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>J.j. So I said, Johnny, you could come visit, but I said, my wife is a millionaire, she's a nymphomaniac. And she's an alcoholic. And it's all I can do to handle her. I can't put up with you. It don't seem like I hung the phone up, he was there.</p><p begin="00:08:29.16" data-timestamp="509.16">So he came in. And he had been on a ship three years saving his money. So I went down in my office on the side of the house and I'd left her estate book out, which is about this thick of all the properties she owned, and land, and her money. And he had smoked a carton of cigarettes and put the ashtray, reading all of what she was worth. The next night she didn't come upstairs to bed.
