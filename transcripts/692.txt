  <h3>Prologue: Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:01.04" data-timestamp="1.04"> In these dark, and confusing, and combative times, where in just one month-- and it's a month that doesn't feel that atypical-- we have impeachment hearings, and Australia on fire, and a near war with Iran, and a deadly virus spreading around the world. We thought here at our show, we would try the most radical counter-programming possible. So today, we bring you our show about delight. And our story starts with my co-worker Bim Adewunmi, growing up in East London, in Nigeria, learning about America from afar.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:00:35.19" data-timestamp="35.19"> I just remember the feeling of swimming in a lot of American culture. I watched a lot of sitcoms. I'd been watching <i>Roseanne,</i> <i>I</i> <i>Dream</i> <i>of</i> <i>Jeannie,</i> <i>Family</i> <i>Matters.</i></p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:44.41" data-timestamp="44.41"> It was a hodgepodge of Americana. A deep dive on Marilyn Monroe, but also the books of Maya Angelou, and also the Cosby spin-off show, <i>A</i> <i>Different</i> <i>World.</i></p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:00:52.69" data-timestamp="52.69"> And I remember thinking, there are so many different types of black people, doing their own version of black people things, and that was really interesting to me.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:01.48" data-timestamp="61.48"> Bim knew there were problematic things about America, of course. Who didn't know that?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:01:05.30" data-timestamp="65.3"> But at the time, I was like, yay! American pop culture. This is great. And I had such a fixed idea of America. There were a million high school movies and TV shows. I was like, oh, yeah. If I was to land in an American school today, I would know exactly what to do. I know the crowd I would fit in with. It was going to be proms. It was going to be malls. I know where the cafeteria is. I understand that gym is a place of hell. I understood everything around the idea of American school. I had a whole vision of myself and where I would fit in the hierarchy.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:37.02" data-timestamp="97.02"> Seriously?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:01:37.98" data-timestamp="97.98"> Absolutely. I'd be the semi-jock, because I used to run track at school. So I was like, semi-jock, but sensitive, theater child, but also everybody's friend.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:49.73" data-timestamp="109.73"> Yeah.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:01:50.04" data-timestamp="110.04"> And I would be approachable by people who weren't as cool as me. I had a whole strategy planned out.</p><p begin="00:01:55.16" data-timestamp="115.16">[LAUGHTER]</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:55.66" data-timestamp="115.66"> I love that you totally pictured your American high school.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:01:58.80" data-timestamp="118.8"> I was going to be the everyman who was also very cool, incredibly bright, very beautiful, very popular, but wasn't conceited. It seemed a shame to waste all this knowledge that I had about how American society functions by me being in England. It was like, well, what good is all this knowledge here? If you have the knowledge, then you want to be tested. I wanted to be tested. And the only real test is to actually live in America. To live that life.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:34.78" data-timestamp="154.78"> And so, when she was 19, after high school and before college-- they don't call it high school and they don't call it college where she's from-- she decided, I'm going to do it. I'm going to take the test. Live the dream. Go live in America. In fact, not just any old part of America, but a quintessentially American corner of America. And that's summer camp.</p><p begin="00:02:54.58" data-timestamp="174.58">Summer camp's an institution they didn't really have in either of the countries where she'd spent her childhood-- Nigeria and England. She was hired by a company that brings in teenagers from overseas to work as counselors, and she was assigned to a camp in a very un-British location-- just outside Santa Cruz, California.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:03:09.26" data-timestamp="189.26"> We were amongst redwoods, some of the most ancient most majestic things ever on the Earth. They've seen [BLEEP] dinosaurs. So we were between the redwoods and the ocean. And I was like, man! You just don't get vistas like this in East London. It felt so American to be in nature like this. And we were sleeping in cabins, and I was like, yeah, this is a camp, all right.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:29.72" data-timestamp="209.72"> In fact, over and over she found herself seeing and doing things that she'd only encountered in American pop culture. For instance, she ate at an old-fashioned diner, with round stools at a long table, and a waitress in a striped blouse who called her honey. Or there was the day that a guy in a grocery store aisle, a total stranger, hit on her, which she'd only seen on television, which apparently is not a thing British men do very much. And each time these things happened, it was exhilarating and surprising.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:03:55.31" data-timestamp="235.31"> So one of the other counselors at camp, Laurel, invited us all to her parents place on Lake Tahoe. So we drove down in her car, which was a white El Camino truck, and she told us his name was Chester. And I thought, that's perfect. Chester. Chester the El Camino truck. Yes. At that point, I didn't know how to drive. I still don't know how to drive because I lived in London, and there's a fantastic tube, and buses, and whatever, and I walked everywhere--</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:23.93" data-timestamp="263.93"> Whatever. I don't want to hear your British chauvinism of our American way of life.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:04:29.53" data-timestamp="269.53"> It was great. Oh, my god.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:31.52" data-timestamp="271.52"> Mm-hmm.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:04:31.94" data-timestamp="271.94"> Anyway, we drove down to Tahoe. And on the way we had the windows down, we were playing loud music, we're wearing jeans cutoffs, and I remember thinking yet again, this moment of stark clarity, I was like, oh my god! This is America! I'm in a truck! We're on the road! The wind is in our hair! This is perfect! We're a bunch of girls laughing about whatever, and it's great. Like it was a performance, but I knew all the words.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:03.15" data-timestamp="303.15"> So you guys are playing the radio. Are you singing along with the radio?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:05:07.03" data-timestamp="307.03"> Yes, classic road trip-style.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:09.13" data-timestamp="309.13"> And do you remember what songs?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:05:10.81" data-timestamp="310.81"> Oh, I do remember one song. Super girly. That was the year that Norah Jones' album <i>Come</i> <i>Away</i> <i>With</i> <i>Me</i> came out.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:18.49" data-timestamp="318.49"> Mm-hmm.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:05:19.22" data-timestamp="319.22"> I don't care what anyone says. That album is a banger. I love it still. Norah Jones' is just perfect. And I remember the lead song, "I Don't Know Why" was on the radio every single day. And I remember us singing it, the final bit where she sings (SINGING) my heart's in something, something, whatever. And then she says this great line-- (SINGING) and you'll be on my mind.</p><p begin="00:05:45.25" data-timestamp="345.25">And I remember we would sing that, and we'd put our hands to our chests, and we would extend our arms and be like, (SINGING) you'll be-- and we would all sing it.</p><p begin="00:05:52.05" data-timestamp="352.05">[MUSIC - "YOU'LL BE ON MY MIND" BY NORAH JONES]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:06:05.43" data-timestamp="365.43"> And it was just this very romantic, very windswept-- it felt to me, again, like the perfect soundtrack to my American summer.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:15.37" data-timestamp="375.37"> America is real.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:06:16.12" data-timestamp="376.12"> America is real! I was like, oh, my god! American things are happening to me.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:21.08" data-timestamp="381.08"> OK, so like I said at the beginning, our show today is about delight. And it was during that summer, Bim says, because it was so full of moments of delight, she started to really take notice of that feeling and think of it as a thing, a thing that you liked. It wasn't just enjoyable, she thought.
