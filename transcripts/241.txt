  <h3>Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.50" data-timestamp="0.5"> From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i> I'm Ira Glass.</p><p begin="00:00:03.51" data-timestamp="3.51"></p><p begin="00:00:03.82" data-timestamp="3.82">There's this theater company in Chicago that, for years, put out a new show every single week where they performed 30 plays in 60 minutes, called the Neo-Futurists. And when we first broadcast today's program all the way back in 2003, a bunch of us were sitting around talking about that group. And one of us had just seen one of their shows. And we thought, that would be really fun-- 30 stories in 60 minutes? Let's try that.</p><p begin="00:00:29.21" data-timestamp="29.21">Instead of our usual-- each week we choose a theme, bring you three or four stories, blah, blah, blah, public radio, very reflective, what kinds of stories would we end up with if we did 30 in one hour? Or even 20? What would it sound like?</p><p begin="00:00:45.06" data-timestamp="45.06">We honestly had no idea. And so, that's what we're going to do today. Today we're going to find out. And I have to say, time is wasting if we're going to fit everything in.</p><p begin="00:00:57.22" data-timestamp="57.22">How many will it be? How many will we squeeze in? There is but one way to find out, and that is to stick around.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Don't I Know You</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:05.77" data-timestamp="65.77">(HOST) IRA GLASS: And let's begin with this one, Act One-- Don't I Know You? Throughout this hour we're going to be bringing you two-minute documentaries, fantastically short works of fiction, and all kinds of little stories that we ordinarily can't use on the radio show because they are just too short, even though they're really fun stories to listen to and perfect stories for the radio. Our first story is one like that. It's one of those stories-- you know how people you know have their greatest hits stories of things that happened to them? We went on a massive search for stories like that from all over the country, and this is one of the stories like that that we found.</p><p begin="00:01:38.03" data-timestamp="98.03">It happened to this actor named Tate Donovan. He told it to our producer Starlee Kine. He was out one night going to a Broadway play with a friend, being treated in this way he never gets treated.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tate Donovan</h4><p begin="00:01:47.97" data-timestamp="107.97"> We're sitting around waiting for it to start. And I'm not a very-- I'm not a sort of a recognizable actor. I'm an actor who works, but I never get recognized.</p><p begin="00:02:00.04" data-timestamp="120.04">So all of a sudden, the 10 minutes we're sitting there for it to start, three or four people come up to me and recognize me. I mean, they know exactly who I am. And they are quoting lines from a television show I was on. And like, hey, you were Joshua on <i>Friends.</i></p><p begin="00:02:17.47" data-timestamp="137.47">And I've always admired stars who were really gracious. So you always think, that's what I want to be like. I want to be really friendly when I'm famous. So I wanted to be friendly and sweet, and go out to the people. They don't have to come to me all the time.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:02:30.60" data-timestamp="150.6"> [LAUGHS] So for a little window of time, though, you were exactly the kind of celebrity that you wanted to always be. You were gracious and reserved.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tate Donovan</h4><p begin="00:02:38.76" data-timestamp="158.76"> Yeah, and warm, you know what I mean? I wasn't like one of these distant celebrities. I was like, hey, I was genuine. They all left thinking, that guy's a really great guy. He's so sweet.</p><p begin="00:02:49.08" data-timestamp="169.08">I was exactly how I wanted to be. I was doing it. I was doing great.</p><p begin="00:02:53.02" data-timestamp="173.02">And then the kid with the camera came along. This nervous kid-- I don't know, he must have been 16 years old. He's in a rented tuxedo, unbelievably shy and awkward, and he's got acne. And he's got a camera in his hand.</p><p begin="00:03:14.25" data-timestamp="194.25">And underneath the marquee is his date, who is in literally a prom dress, and she's got a corsage. And she's really nervous and sort of clutching her hands. And he sort of comes up to me and he sort of mumbles something about a picture.</p><p begin="00:03:32.88" data-timestamp="212.88">And I just feel for him. So I'm like, absolutely, my gosh. Sure, no problem. My God, you poor thing. And I go up to his girlfriend and I wrap my arms around her.</p><p begin="00:03:43.72" data-timestamp="223.72">And I'm like, hey, where are you from? Fantastic, you going to see the play? That's great.</p><p begin="00:03:48.66" data-timestamp="228.66">And the guy is sort of not taking the photograph very quickly. He's just sort of staring at me. And he's got his camera in his hands and it's down by his chin.</p><p begin="00:03:56.82" data-timestamp="236.82">And she's very stiff and awkward. And I don't know what to do, so I just lean across, and I kiss her on the cheek. And I'm like, all right, come on, take the picture. Hurry up.</p><p begin="00:04:08.43" data-timestamp="248.43">And finally he sort of snaps it. And I'm like, OK, it was really wonderful to meet you. And he just stammered over to me and was like, um, [CLICKS TONGUE] could you take a picture of us?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:04:23.09" data-timestamp="263.09"> [GROANS]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tate Donovan</h4><p begin="00:04:24.58" data-timestamp="264.58"> And the whole time, he just wanted me to take a picture of him and his girlfriend underneath the awning of the play. He didn't want a picture of me. He had no idea who I was. [LAUGHS]</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:04:33.82" data-timestamp="273.82"> [LAUGHS]</p><p begin="00:04:35.25" data-timestamp="275.25"></p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tate Donovan</h4><p begin="00:04:37.63" data-timestamp="277.63"> Oh, God. They were in shock. I don't think they'd ever come across a human being acting this way. I mean, could you imagine-- you ask someone to take a picture, and you just get in it yourself, and kiss them, and hey.</p><p begin="00:04:57.16" data-timestamp="297.16">[LAUGHTER]</p><p begin="00:05:00.08" data-timestamp="300.08"></p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:02.51" data-timestamp="302.51"> Tate Donovan with Starlee Kine.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act2">
  <h3>Act Two: No, Of Course I Know You</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:09.31" data-timestamp="309.31">(HOST) IRA GLASS: Act Two-- No, Of Course I Know You. One of the things that's been really interesting about putting this show together this week is going to some of our regular contributors and commissioning stories that are just two or three minutes long from people who normally write stories that are 10 times that length. This story is an example of that, from Scott Carrier in Salt Lake City.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Scott Carrier</h4><p begin="00:05:28.80" data-timestamp="328.8"> Who was that woman, that woman I just saw while walking out of the restaurant? She was sitting at a table by the door, glasses, a hat, like Lois Lane.</p><p begin="00:05:40.61" data-timestamp="340.61">She looked at me like she knew me. And I said hello because I thought I recognized her. But I couldn't remember her name, so I just kept going out the door.</p><p begin="00:05:49.68" data-timestamp="349.68">Now I can't even remember how I know her. I know I know her, or used to know her. Somehow she was very important to me. She helped me out in a time of trouble.</p><p begin="00:06:01.89" data-timestamp="361.89">She used to roll her eyes. I'd say something dumb and she'd roll her eyes and get me something I needed, even though she didn't have to. Maybe she works in the library, or the county recorder's office, or at the newspaper.</p><p begin="00:06:18.04" data-timestamp="378.04">I think I may have been in love with her. No, she's too young. I was never in love with her, not in that way. It's just that I wanted something, needed something. And she was able to give it to me, almost out of the goodness of her heart. And now I can't even remember who she is.</p><p begin="00:06:36.90" data-timestamp="396.9">I'm sick. I'm old. I should just walk out into traffic and kill myself. At home, at night, I go to sleep, searching for the lost memory.</p><p begin="00:06:47.78" data-timestamp="407.78">Did I meet her down by the river in a canoe, or was it on a ferry in southeastern Alaska, or at the Foreign Correspondents Club in Phnom Penh along the Mekong? She has something to do with water, with life, with mud.</p><p begin="00:07:10.75" data-timestamp="430.75">I sleep poorly, turning and maybe even groaning in anguish. I don't care about the woman anymore. I'm worried for myself. I feel as if there's a black hole in my brain, and slowly but surely, it's swallowing all the memories of my life.</p><p begin="00:07:27.32" data-timestamp="447.32">I get up at 5:30 and drive to work in the dark. I feel terrible. I look like a piece of gum in the gutter.</p><p begin="00:07:35.14" data-timestamp="455.14">I pull in to Java Joe's to get some chemical help, and there she is behind the drive-up window. I want to tell her I love her, but I don't because it would be too weird. All I can say is, wow. And she rolls her eyes and gets me my cup of coffee.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:56.99" data-timestamp="476.99"> Scott Carrier in Salt Lake City. If you haven't heard his podcast yet, it's called <i>Home</i> <i>of</i> <i>the</i> <i>Brave.</i></p></div>  </div>
