    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.33" data-timestamp="0.33"> Eustace grew up in the suburbs in North Carolina. I just decided I would like to try living outside. And so I moved out and started camping in a tepee, hunting and gathering food and making my own tools and clothes.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:36.48" data-timestamp="36.48"> And let me just be sure people understand. In fact, I've lived outside for 20 years now.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:51.55" data-timestamp="51.55"> From the time Eustace moved outside, he was interested in the past and all the things that people knew about how to live with nature, back when they had no choice about living with nature. For years, he lived in the woods, slept in a tepee, hunted his own food with bow and arrow, made his own clothes out of deerskin, his own utensils and tools.</p><p begin="00:01:09.32" data-timestamp="69.32">And then he started to evolve from a life as a hunter-gatherer to basic agriculture. In other words, he personally went to through the major evolutionary stages that took the human species about 10,000 years during the Late Cenozoic. And around the same time he moved out into the forest, he had another idea, to ride a horse across America.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Eustace Conway In Studio</h4><p begin="00:01:34.18" data-timestamp="94.18"> And I decided to do it when I was sitting around a campfire. I just said, I'm going to ride a horse across America someday.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Judson Conway In Studio</h4><p begin="00:01:55.21" data-timestamp="115.21"> And then he asked me if I wanted to go.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:57.05" data-timestamp="117.05"> Eustace's younger brother, Judson.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Judson Conway In Studio</h4><p begin="00:01:59.20" data-timestamp="119.2"> And my first reaction was, well, no, not really.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:03.63" data-timestamp="123.63"> Why?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Judson Conway</h4><p begin="00:02:04.47" data-timestamp="124.47"> It just seemed like a big undertaking, and I was happy in my little life. The ride was partly an experiment in time travel. What happens when you try to live how people lived over a century ago? It was partly a lark, an adventure. Men on horses battling the elements, testing themselves, launched toward a goal they had no idea if they could reach.</p><p begin="00:02:48.39" data-timestamp="168.39">But I should tell you that for Eustace, the main idea behind this trip was not nearly so grand as all that. For Eustace, a big part of it was simply about horses. <div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Act One</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:43.53" data-timestamp="343.53"> Act One, Long Riders. So when they set out, it was Eustace, Judson and Susan, four horses, one was a spare, camping gear, and a truck. And it's kind of a crazy scheme the way that they worked the whole thing. Every day, they would wake up, and then one of the would drive the truck 20 or 30 miles ahead to wherever it was they were going to camp the next night. And then that person would have to hitchhike back the same 20 or 30 miles, which could take hours.</p><p begin="00:06:09.75" data-timestamp="369.75">And then, after that, they would set out on their horses and they would ride all day. A couple months into the trip, they had this guy named Walker Young who joined the team to drive the truck ahead and find places to camp and handle stuff like that. The other crazy 20th century adaptation to their 19th century horse ride is it they did not ride through open country. They trotted along on the shoulders of highways, on the sides of roads. It seemed like the most efficient route.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:34.71" data-timestamp="394.71"> You recorded yourselves as you rode across the country on a cassette machine, about 30 hours of tape, starting with that first day. Can I ask you what it was like setting out?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Eustace Conway In Studio</h4><p begin="00:06:43.81" data-timestamp="403.81"> Oh, it was easy setting out. It was sort of like, OK, the day has come. We're going. I knew that we didn't know what we were doing. That was OK. I was real satisfied with that. I personally knew that there was a big chance that we wouldn't make it. My partners were a little bit younger and had done less traveling, a little bit more idealistic. And so they were Gung ho. Yeah, we're going to do this thing. We're going to make it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Eustace Conway On Tape</h4><p begin="00:07:10.54" data-timestamp="430.54"> That sound in the background is the defroster. This is Eustace Conway on the 25th of December. Sitting beside me-- oh, you hear that little yelp?
