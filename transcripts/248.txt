  <h3>Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:00:01.12" data-timestamp="1.12">From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i>, distributed by Public Radio International. I'm David Rakoff, sitting in for Ira Glass.</p><p begin="00:00:10.55" data-timestamp="10.55">There was an old cartoon, it was either in the <i>New Yorker</i> or <i>National</i> <i>Lampoon</i>, I can't remember, where a man is sitting on the bus. It's pretty empty. An odd stranger boards, and the man in the seat thinks to himself, "Oh, God. Please don't let him sit beside me. Oh God. Please don't let him sit beside me." The guy approaches and says, "God told me to sit beside you."</p><p begin="00:00:34.26" data-timestamp="34.26">In the spring of 1978, I was 13 years old. The local public high school that I would be attending in a year's time was having an evening screening of <i>Casablanca.</i> So I took my friend Jeff and we went off to the high school.</p><p begin="00:00:49.33" data-timestamp="49.33">In addition to my love of old movies and my general cluelessness about popular music and youth culture, I was also a really tiny child. Maybe five feet at time. Probably closer to for four foot eight. So I looked markedly different from other kids my age.</p><p begin="00:01:05.75" data-timestamp="65.75">I was also a little neurotic. I was riddled with phobias. It all served to make me seem a little odd, I'm sure. I didn't lack friends, but I was a strange, round-limbed, feminine little kid. A strange, round-limbed, feminine little kid for whom a screening of <i>Casablanca</i> at the high school, no less, was a fairly important event.</p><p begin="00:01:28.54" data-timestamp="88.54">If I think about it, I guess the fantasy going through my head was that I would meet other old movie buffs who would see past my juvenile exterior and into my very poet's soul. And then I would have, you know, a community of people who didn't care that I was dwarfish or 13. Maybe we would smoke and talk about films.</p><p begin="00:01:59.05" data-timestamp="119.05">Jeff and I were the only ones who showed up. The only two people in the auditorium, aside from two older boys at the back on a ladder who were taping up some banner of some sort or another.</p><p begin="00:02:13.71" data-timestamp="133.71">So the lights are still on and we're waiting for the movie to start. And I hear one of the boys on the ladder call my name. "David! David!"</p><p begin="00:02:24.48" data-timestamp="144.48">If you're a gay kid, or even just a gay-ish kid, you basically spend your life anticipating having the crap beaten out of you, or at the very least, being given what used to be known on <i>Leave</i> <i>It</i> <i>to</i> <i>Beaver</i> as "The Business." I didn't know this guy calling my name. I knew that much. He wasn't a friend of my brother's or my sister's. I knew, actually, two things with complete certainty-- one, that I wasn't the David that he was calling for, and two, that if I did turn around, he would give me "The Business." So I didn't turn around.</p><p begin="00:02:57.29" data-timestamp="177.29">But it just went on and on. Him was calling my name and nobody else being in the auditorium. After about a quarter of an hour, against my better judgment, thinking, perhaps, that there had been some wrinkle in the space-time continuum, and maybe this older guy did somehow know me after all-- there was no one else in the auditorium and he kept on calling "David! David!" So eventually I turned around and I said, "Yes?"</p><p begin="00:03:24.48" data-timestamp="204.48">It was as if I could see the words coming out of his mouth, printed like a speech bubble in a comic, moving through the air towards me as if the entire atmosphere was viscous. Moving as slowly towards me as a bubble traveling through liquid Prell. And I knew, I knew what the words would be before they even reached my ears!</p><p begin="00:03:49.32" data-timestamp="229.32">"David! David!" "Yes?" "Not you, you fag!" Of course, that was what he said.</p><p begin="00:04:00.16" data-timestamp="240.16">I had thought that the unforeseen bonanza of the evening was that I was going to be somehow enfolded, all four feet eight inches of me, into some Bohemian clique, when in fact, what I should have seen was this. If I hadn't turned around, he probably would have just gotten down from the ladder anyway, and walked over to me, tapped me on the shoulder, and called me a fag. "God told me to sit beside you."</p><p begin="00:04:27.12" data-timestamp="267.12">Hope might spring eternal, dread might loom like a dark cloud on the horizon. Sometimes, regardless of your action or inaction, half-empty, half-full, some things in this life are just bound to happen.</p><p begin="00:04:41.91" data-timestamp="281.91">Today on our show-- stories of inevitability. Those things that will happen, like it or not. Our show today in four acts.</p><p begin="00:04:52.26" data-timestamp="292.26">Act One, If It Drives, Go Live. A true crime story about the world's most law-abiding perp ever to receive four hours of continuous live news coverage.</p><p begin="00:05:02.64" data-timestamp="302.64">Act Two, Don't Just Stand There. You can't change the people you love even when your life might depend on it.</p><p begin="00:05:10.57" data-timestamp="310.57">Act Three, Hello, Baby. How to prepare yourself for one of life's greatest inevitabilities, like it and not, with little more than a wobbly movie projector and a breathing chart.</p><p begin="00:05:22.97" data-timestamp="322.97">Act Four, On the Eighth Day, God Created Tartar Sauce. How mother nature, in an act of pure generosity, annually lays out the world's greatest all-you-can-eat raw bar on a small beach in Alabama. Stay with us, won't you?</p></div>  </div>

<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: If It Drives, Go Live</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:43.63" data-timestamp="343.63"> Act One. If It Drives, Go Live.</p><p begin="00:05:47.80" data-timestamp="347.8">If you get into a car chase with the police, you pretty much know how it's going to end. 90% to 95% of the time, you will be caught. Despite, or perhaps because of this, car chases have become an increasingly popular staple of TV news programming. Viewers have watched people try to elude the cops in various ways, like plowing through a crowded pedestrian malls. They've even seen a fleeing suspect attempt a carjacking. Starlee Kine has the story of the latest incarnation of the continuously televised car chase that answers the question, why did they run if they know they're going to get caught? And why do we watch?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:06:26.45" data-timestamp="386.45">In Los Angeles, on August 31, 2001, all other news and KCAL 9 was interrupted in order to bring you this car chase.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Reporter</h4><p begin="00:06:37.94" data-timestamp="397.94">And the pursuit continues. And I believe we're north of the airport now. Larry Welk?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Larry Welk</h4><p begin="00:06:43.53" data-timestamp="403.53">Yeah. Back with you here. This suspect has been going on now for over three hours now, well over three hours. Almost four now. With showing no signs of giving up, no signs of stopping.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:06:56.26" data-timestamp="416.26">This, of course, would have been no big deal, since in LA, there's car chases on TV all the time. And really, this one is no different than your basic high-speed police pursuit-- except for that speed part.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Larry Welk</h4><p begin="00:07:06.10" data-timestamp="426.1">The suspect still driving well within the speed limit. In fact, lower than the speed limit. Driving very reasonably right now through the streets of southern California.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:07:16.49" data-timestamp="436.49">And the police part.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Larry Welk</h4><p begin="00:07:18.16" data-timestamp="438.16">There's nobody else behind him right now. Everybody has pulled off, including the LAPD, Hawthorne, Inglewood. They are not there. Nowhere to be seen.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:07:28.29" data-timestamp="448.29">Oh. And the pursuit part.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Larry Welk</h4><p begin="00:07:30.32" data-timestamp="450.32">You know, at this point, the officers are saying that they have a really good idea of who he is. They have a pretty good idea of where he lives, even. And so at this point, they're going to go ahead and let him drive home, if that's what he wants to do.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:07:44.26" data-timestamp="464.26">On Thursday, August 31, 2001, viewers of KCAL were told they were watching a car chase. But what they were actually watching was a white Geo Prizm driving slowly up and down residential streets, stopping at every stop sign, and signalling every time he wanted to turn, with no police behind him. If you tuned in the day with the sound off, you'd be wondering why it was on TV at all.</p><p begin="00:08:06.03" data-timestamp="486.03">Of course, if you had the sound on, you were probably even more confused, because the image on the screen had very little to do with what was coming out of the news announcer's mouth. It was as though they weren't looking at the screen at all, but were instead reciting their lines for memory, drawing from all the past car chases they covered.</p><p begin="00:08:24.66" data-timestamp="504.66">The car chase began normally enough. At around six o'clock, the car's driver pulled a hit and run. It was a minor fender bender and no one got hurt, but he took off anyway. The LAPD tried to pull him over, but he refused. They chased him for a few hours before finally deciding to stop.</p><p begin="00:08:39.75" data-timestamp="519.75">In the past couple of years, many police departments have begun to rethink their strategies when it comes to chasing.
