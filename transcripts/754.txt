I had no idea how much of bird watching you're actually just listening for the birds. [BIRD CALL] That was the sound of a Steller's jay imitating a red-tailed hawk.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:54.74" data-timestamp="54.74"> Wait, you know the birds well enough you can tell when a bird is imitating another bird badly?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Noah Strycker</h4><p begin="00:01:02.32" data-timestamp="62.32"> Yeah, it's not a real red-tailed hawk. That just blew my mind. I have never listened to birds the same sense. So I think they do make noises just for their own sake a lot of the time.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:21.36" data-timestamp="141.36"> Going out birding with Noah is going out with somebody who is insanely knowledgeable about birds. But he's also still completely excited about birds. Can't get enough.</p><p begin="00:02:42.01" data-timestamp="162.01">He told me this story. He goes to Antarctica for months studying penguins, and sometimes he gives tours to visitors of the penguin colonies. At first he says they love it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Noah Strycker</h4><p begin="00:02:51.36" data-timestamp="171.36"> They go into their colony, they see the baby penguins, which are super cute. And then, like, day five or so, people get up in the morning and they're like, where are we going to go today? And you say, we're going to a penguin colony. And there's this look that I've learned to recognize. It's like, I've just been to eight other penguin colonies in the past four days. How is this one going to be any different? But for me, I have never felt that look in my whole life. I have never got penguined out.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:21.63" data-timestamp="201.63"> He happily goes back to the same colony, or the same woods around his house, every single day. In fifth grade, he first noticed birds for real when his teacher suction-cupped a bird feeder to the classroom window, and he could stare at the birds through the glass just inches away.</p><p begin="00:03:40.29" data-timestamp="220.29">He started reading the field guide, looking for birds on his own, joined a local birding club. Everybody else in the club, by the way, was in their 60s, 70s, and 80s. He was like 12. The one kid. This energetic, birdy Doogie Howser. In person, Noah actually does have kind of a Neil Patrick Harris vibe.</p><p begin="00:03:58.01" data-timestamp="238.01">But the thing he says that changed him from that kind of leisure time birder into somebody who organized his entire life around it happened because of turkey vultures, one particular pack of them, when he was 16. And then 45 minutes later, this turkey vulture comes sailing down through the canopy of that forest and flies unerringly right to the spot where the steak is and digs it up, moves the leaves aside, and gets the meat.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Attenborough</h4><p begin="00:04:55.22" data-timestamp="295.22"> It seems almost unbelievable.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Noah Strycker</h4><p begin="00:04:57.27" data-timestamp="297.27"> When I saw that on <i>The</i> <i>Life</i> <i>of</i> <i>Birds</i> as a high schooler, I just immediately had one thought, which was, I've got to try this at home. That is the like best bird feeding idea I've ever seen in my life.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:15.47" data-timestamp="315.47"> It seemed incredible to him that he might be able to lure these birds that you usually see from so far away. Turkey vultures are shy, hard to get near, and he might be able to see them close up? Then he thought, if David Attenborough got one bird to show up with some old piece of steak, maybe he would improve his odds if his bait was something bigger.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Noah Strycker</h4><p begin="00:05:37.48" data-timestamp="337.48"> Imagine how many turkey vultures I could attract with, like, a dead deer. There are so many deer here in rural Oregon on the side of the highways and freeways. I can now go out and find roadkill, bring a whole carcass home, and see how many turkey vultures we can attract. But it was kind of hard to find a roadkill, as it turned out. It took me, like, a month to track one down.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:04.06" data-timestamp="364.06"> Another way to put that last sentence, if you aren't a 16-year-old very eager to see turkey vultures up close-- it only took a month to find a deer carcass. Noah wrestled the heavy, bloated, gaseous, yellow jacket-covered, putrid-smelling deer into the trunk of his Volvo sedan.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Noah Strycker</h4><p begin="00:06:21.70" data-timestamp="381.7"> Got home, put the deer in a wheelbarrow, wheeled it out to this very spot, where we're standing in this pasture, and dumped it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:30.58" data-timestamp="390.58"> So it's, like, right here where we're standing?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Noah Strycker</h4><p begin="00:06:33.01" data-timestamp="393.01"> This is the spot I chose.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:35.23" data-timestamp="395.23"> At this point in our bird walk, we were standing within eyeshot of the house that he grew up in, but not too close. The cow pasture's overgrown, Douglas firs and white oaks around its edges.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Noah Strycker</h4><p begin="00:06:45.37" data-timestamp="405.37"> I thought this would be a good spot because you can see it from the sky, it has multiple approach paths from the air, so they will be as attracted as possible to this deer carcass.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:57.95" data-timestamp="417.95"> It was dark, so he went to bed, no idea if this was going to work. Gets up the next morning--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Noah Strycker</h4><p begin="00:07:04.04" data-timestamp="424.04"> And there were turkey vultures everywhere. They were sitting on these trees right here. There were vultures sitting on the roof of our house, like 10 of them lined up on our roof. There were vultures circling overhead. There were probably 30 or 40 turkey vultures hanging around the yard. And when I woke up and realized that this had happened, I was so happy.</p><p begin="00:07:31.63" data-timestamp="451.63">I rushed out here. And I had set up a little camouflage tarp next to the deer carcass. The tarp was just kind of in this wet grass. So if I come over here, I was hunkered down right about here. So if I get down, I was sitting under the tarp with a hole in it, and my camera lens sticking out.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:54.25" data-timestamp="474.25"> He's maybe 10 feet from the deer.
