  <div class="act-inner">

  <h3>Act Three: Combo Platter</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:43:24.16" data-timestamp="2604.16">Act Three, Combo Platter. Starting out today we have had a story about a parrot, and we've had a story about a pig. And to end today's show, we have this story from David Sedaris.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David  Sedaris</h4><p begin="00:44:04.47" data-timestamp="2644.47"> When asked why she'd chosen to become a journalist, the parrot was known to cock her head a half an inch to the right, and pause for a moment before repeating the question. "Why did I choose to become a journalist? Well, I guess what really drives me is the money-- that and the free booze." It killed her to follow this with, "I was just joking about the money."</p><p begin="00:44:33.72" data-timestamp="2673.72">The paper she worked at was called The Eagle. And she wrote for the Tempo section, which was later renamed Lifestyles, and was now titled, simply, Living. Most of her stories were little more than puff-pieces. Interview the wealthy tortoise who'd shelled out money for the new speedway. Cover the benefit gala for ringworm, or heartworm or the earthworm or anti-defamation league.</p><p begin="00:44:58.86" data-timestamp="2698.86">She wanted an opportunity to show her chops and finally got her break when a pot-bellied pig took over as director of the local art museum. The Eagle wanted something simple, 300 words tops, but the parrot thought differently. It scheduled a long lunch. Her guest arrived on time, and after ordering they got down to business.</p><p begin="00:45:20.40" data-timestamp="2720.4">"So," the parrot began, "it's a long way from Ho Chi Minh City to the much coveted director's chair of a noted museum. I'd like you to reminisce about the journey a little."</p><p begin="00:45:31.63" data-timestamp="2731.63">"I'm sorry," the pig said. "But I've never been to Ho Chi Minh City."</p><p begin="00:45:36.32" data-timestamp="2736.32">"But you are from that region are you not?"</p><p begin="00:45:39.16" data-timestamp="2739.16">"No," the pig told her, not at all.</p><p begin="00:45:42.75" data-timestamp="2742.75">The parrot ran her fat, black tongue over the ragged edge of her upper beak. "I don't mean to contradict you," she said, "but I have done a little leg work, and it seems that you're officially registered with your health care provider as a Vietnamese pot-bellied pig. So let's turn our thoughts eastward, shall we, and talk about your past."</p><p begin="00:46:03.14" data-timestamp="2763.14">"Technically, yes, I am a Vietnamese pot-bellied pig," the museum director said. "But that's just a silly formality. The fact is that I was born in this country, as were my parents and their parents before them."</p><p begin="00:46:17.74" data-timestamp="2777.74">"I see," the parrot said. And she scratched the words self-hating onto her notepad. "So how will your ethnicity reflect itself in regards to our museum? Can we expect to see more Oriental art, a pricey new Ming wing, perhaps some big Treasures of The Emperor extravaganza?"</p><p begin="00:46:39.03" data-timestamp="2799.03">"Nothing's planned," the pig said.</p><p begin="00:46:41.41" data-timestamp="2801.41">"But you wouldn't rule it out?"</p><p begin="00:46:43.52" data-timestamp="2803.52">"Well, no, not completely, but--"</p><p begin="00:46:46.27" data-timestamp="2806.27">"That's all I wanted to know," the parrot said. And at that moment, their lunch all arrived.</p><p begin="00:46:56.02" data-timestamp="2816.02">It was she who had made the reservation, and, in a moment of inspiration, she decided they'd go to Old Saigon. The fact that it was her idea would not be mentioned in the article. Nor would she add that the pig had never in his life used a pair of chopsticks, and that he gripped them, one in each hoof, as if they were screwdrivers.</p><p begin="00:47:19.37" data-timestamp="2839.37">During the meal, a few blades of lemongrass for him, a Mekong platter for her. They talked about this and that, but she wasn't really engaged, busy as she was dreaming up a headline. Museum Takes on Asian a Slant was good, but she'd have to fight hard to get it past her editor who despised, what she called, word play.</p><p begin="00:47:42.36" data-timestamp="2862.36">When their lunch was over, the pig trotted back to the museum, and the parrot headed down to the VFW Hall, where she hoped to round out her article. There she spoke to a red-shouldered hawk, who hadn't actually fought in Vietnam but might have had the war lasted just a few weeks longer.</p><p begin="00:48:00.45" data-timestamp="2880.45">"I could have practically been killed over there, and now one of them is coming to my museum trying to tell me what art I should look at?"</p><p begin="00:48:08.02" data-timestamp="2888.02">"I know it," the parrot said.</p><p begin="00:48:13.95" data-timestamp="2893.95">The article was due the following morning, and she stayed up all night in order to finish it. Her editor scowled at the bulk of pages but softened after the first read through saying, " Good work, you," and, "Maybe we should send this over to the city desk."</p><p begin="00:48:30.11" data-timestamp="2910.11">The eventual headline was no masterpiece, Pot-bellied Museum Director Stirs Controversy. But the parrot was so relieved to move out of the Living section that they could have called it Mud and she wouldn't have cared. As for the pig, he wasn't nearly as upset as she thought he would be. Rather than threatening a lawsuit or demanding a retraction, he phoned to say that he was disappointed. "Deeply disappointed," were his exact words.</p><p begin="00:48:59.37" data-timestamp="2939.37">The parrot reached for a pen, hoping for quotes that might lead to a second article. "Is that all you have to say?" she asked.</p><p begin="00:49:07.50" data-timestamp="2947.5">"Any response," he sighed. And gently hung up the phone.</p><p begin="00:49:11.85" data-timestamp="2951.85">"Hello," the parrot said, "Hello."</p><p begin="00:49:19.61" data-timestamp="2959.61">The pig would not have admitted it, but what really bothered him was the pot-belly business. He'd been plump all through his youth, and the years of name calling had not just shaped his adult life but deformed it, like some cell made crazy by radiation.</p><p begin="00:49:38.60" data-timestamp="2978.6">He couldn't remember the last time he'd eaten without thinking. Popped a passing canape into his mouth, finished an entire potato chip or dry roasted peanut without calculating the damage. While others prepared for bed, he ran a treadmill. They tucked into their ample breakfasts, and he hung upside down from a bar in his living room, doubling at the waist until he saw stars. Then came the traditional sit-ups, and a half a slice of dry Ry-Vita before examining his silhouette in the hallway mirror and getting ready for work.</p><p begin="00:50:13.04" data-timestamp="3013.04">He did not have a pot-belly. He would never again have a pot-belly. But now, here was this article, essentially, comparing him to Buddha. After hanging up on the reporter, the pig began a three-day fast. Lunchtime came, and as his colleagues shuffled to the museum cafeteria, he sat at his desk and looked out the window at that stupid hawk, marching back and forth with his picket sign.</p><p begin="00:50:49.00" data-timestamp="3049">The veteran had hoped that others might join him, but none of his fellows seemed to care. "The war is over, and it's time to move on," they'd been quoted as saying. "Who cares if some," and there was that word again, "Who cares if some pot-bellied Charlie wants to hang a picture on the wall?"</p><p begin="00:51:07.19" data-timestamp="3067.19">"Damn that parrot from the Eagle." The pig's anger felt vaguely nourishing, but he knew that it was misplaced. The reporter hadn't assigned the animals their names. That was someone else's doing, someone who sat back and ordained, wide-mouth bass, humpback whale, lesser wart-nosed horseshoe bat, not caring whose life was ruined.</p><p begin="00:51:35.01" data-timestamp="3095.01">By the time he next ran into her, the pig had lost close to 10 pounds. They met at a museum benefit, a costume ball which he hosted, and which she hovered on the edges of, guzzling rum punch and gathering quote she'd heard a 1,000 times before. "Wonderful party and, of course, it's for such a good cause."</p><p begin="00:51:57.06" data-timestamp="3117.06">The parrot was, she liked to joke, back with the living, by which I mean section, not the sensation of being alive. She'd assumed that the pig would be in disguise, and was surprised to see him in the same dark suit he worn at the restaurant. He was standing at the bar, nursing a glass of water.</p><p begin="00:52:16.17" data-timestamp="3136.17">And she came from behind and tapped him on the shoulder. "Let me guess," she said, "you're Henry Bacon, right?"</p><p begin="00:52:23.38" data-timestamp="3143.38">"Who's he?" the pig asked.</p><p begin="00:52:25.75" data-timestamp="3145.75">The parrot rolled her eyes. "American architect, designed a little something called the Lincoln Memorial?"</p><p begin="00:52:32.83" data-timestamp="3152.83">"Oh," the the pig said, "that Henry Bacon." He was going to admit that he was no one or, at least, no one special, when the parrot stepped back and examined him again over the rim of her punch class.</p><p begin="00:52:45.09" data-timestamp="3165.09">"I've got it," she said, "you're Luther Ham, took the silver medal for 200 meter freestyle, Helsinki 1952. Little wisp of a thing, but, boy, did he have shoulders."</p><p begin="00:52:56.47" data-timestamp="3176.47">"Right," the pig said. "So who are you supposed to be?"</p><p begin="00:53:01.03" data-timestamp="3181.03">The parrot shrugged and held up her glass for a refill. "I thought I'd go all out and come as a two-bit journalist." For verification, she presented an ink stained claw. "So hey," she added, "I'm sorry about that article."</p><p begin="00:53:17.36" data-timestamp="3197.36">"That's all right," the pig told her.</p><p begin="00:53:19.92" data-timestamp="3199.92">"All right for you," the parrot said, "I'm the one with the god damn hawk calling me every 10 minutes. Now he wants to go after Middle Easterners. Heard of a Persian cat who runs a parking garage down by the civic center and is after me to write an expose."</p><p begin="00:53:35.43" data-timestamp="3215.43">The pig laughed for the first time in months, and immediately thereafter, as was his habit, he began to analyze why. Was it the idea of this parrot bedeviled by a ridiculous hawk, or the image of a long-haired cat making change in one of those sweltering little booths? He pondered this for days but never reached a satisfying conclusion.</p><p begin="00:54:00.02" data-timestamp="3240.02">"Earth to pig, Earth to pig," the parrot said.</p><p begin="00:54:03.21" data-timestamp="3243.21">And he looked down to see her wing resting on his stomach.</p><p begin="00:54:07.37" data-timestamp="3247.37">"Is it my imagination or have you lost some weight?"</p><p begin="00:54:11.19" data-timestamp="3251.19">"No," he told her, "I mean, yes I did. It's not your imagination." He thought of how kind it was for her to mention it. And then he noticed how oddly satisfying it felt to be patted down by a wing.</p><p begin="00:54:25.12" data-timestamp="3265.12">Meanwhile, the parrot was still talking. "Don't get me wrong," she said, "I have seen a cockatoo in my time, but I'm not dating anyone now if that's what you're wondering." She grabbed a passing appetizer, dumped the caviar back onto the tray and ate only the cracker. "A cliche, I know, parrot eats a cracker, but fish eggs make me bloat."</p><p begin="00:54:48.96" data-timestamp="3288.96">"It's the salt," the pig told her . He'd hoped to say something more interesting, but, just then, the band started up. A wolf, in sheep's clothing, called out for a foxtrot. And as if a switch had been thrown, the party came to life. Here was the hare in cat's pajamas dancing with a chameleon whose costume changed with every turn. The Ugly Duckling cut in on a swan. A trio of mice lowered their sunglasses, and as they scouted the floor for partners, the parrot turned to the pig and held out her claw.</p><p begin="00:55:24.15" data-timestamp="3324.15">He accepted it awkwardly in his hoof, this spindly pitchfork, this warm, mottled twig. And so began, what the reporter would later refer to, as her days of swine and roses.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>     Ira  Glass</h4><p begin="00:55:39.30" data-timestamp="3339.3"> David Sedaris is the author of several books, most recently, Dress Your Family in Corduroy and Denim.</p></div>  </div>
