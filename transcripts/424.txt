  <h3>Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.53" data-timestamp="0.53">This is an amazing documentary called <i>Please</i> <i>Vote</i> <i>for</i> <i>Me</i> about an election in a third grade class by a filmmaker named Weijun Chen. It takes place in a city called Wuhan in central China. The eight year olds are told that they are going to vote to decide who is going to be the class monitor. It's the first election in their school ever. None of them has ever witnessed or taken part in anything like this at all.</p><p begin="00:00:20.68" data-timestamp="20.68">[SPEAKING CHINESE]</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:00:23.33" data-timestamp="23.33">&quot;Isn't this new?&quot; their teacher asks them. And one of the three candidates, a little butterball of a kid named Cheng Cheng, turns out to be a natural politician. With no adults prompting him, he immediately starts button-holing his classmates and making promises.</p><p begin="00:00:36.91" data-timestamp="36.91">[SPEAKING CHINESE]</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:00:40.17" data-timestamp="40.17">&quot;Vote for me,&quot; he tells a little boy, &quot;I'll make you steady committee officer.&quot; Quickly the parents get involved, coaching the kids, helping write their speeches. One dad who's with the police department basically wins the election for his son by arranging for a class trip on the city's monorail, which the kids love, and by giving his son little presents to hand out to every kid in the class moments before they vote.</p><p begin="00:01:02.62" data-timestamp="62.62">But the kids own instincts are really interesting to watch too. There's this scene where one of the candidates-- a girl named Xiaofei is about to do a flute recital as part of the competition. Her two friends are going to dance a ballet with red scarves around her as she plays. Before she starts, though, Cheng Cheng tells a friend, &quot;After Xiaofei performs, you must shout, that's terrible, got it?&quot;</p><p begin="00:01:23.85" data-timestamp="83.85">[SPEAKING CHINESE]</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:01:28.96" data-timestamp="88.96">Which results in a whole group of kids, including Cheng Cheng and Luo Lei-- Luo Lei's the other candidate in this race-- taunting her, &quot;Xiaofei, Xiaofei, slowest eater. Xiaofei, Xiaofei, rotten gossip.&quot;</p><p begin="00:01:39.22" data-timestamp="99.22">[SPEAKING CHINESE]</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:01:46.92" data-timestamp="106.92">Xiaofei looks around confused-- nothing like this has ever happened. And she's little, right, her feelings are right on her face. She blinks, she looks over to her teacher for help, she looks down and winces and starts crying. Another little girl in pink, at her desk, she starts crying too, witnessing this. The teacher calls a break, ushers Xiaofei from the room.</p><p begin="00:02:10.42" data-timestamp="130.42">Cheng Cheng then goes to Xiaofei in the hallway and say, &quot;I apologize to you for Luo Lei.&quot; As if Luo Lei was the one who organized the chanting, not him. Then he gets Luo Lei to apologize. And then, not only is Xiaofei crying, both the little boys, they both burst into tears also. Remember, they're all just eight years old.</p><p begin="00:02:31.51" data-timestamp="151.51">[SPEAKING CHINESE]</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:02:35.18" data-timestamp="155.18">The camera moves down the aisle of the classroom, and we see lots of other kids are crying as well. It's like they accidentally invented negative campaigning, on their own, in their own classroom, tried it out, and then realized just how mean and wrong it is. Something adults seem to forget sometimes.</p><p begin="00:02:54.04" data-timestamp="174.04">Today on our show, we have stories of children thrust into the world of politics-- a world that was really built for adults with thicker skins. From WBEZ Chicago, it's This American Life, distributed by Public Radio International. I'm Ira Glass. Our show in three acts, these children take up presidential politics, global warming, and in our last act, they're put in charge of their own school-- make all the rules, including discipline. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>
</div>
<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Trickle Down History</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:29.84" data-timestamp="209.84">Act One, Trickle Down History. Now we move to a group of teachers in California, teachers who are sending their students out to understand a very American kind of politics-- presidential politics. Starlee Kine tells the story.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:03:43.80" data-timestamp="223.8">When I was in high school, my class took a trip to the Richard Nixon Library. I remember putting on a pair of large headphones that whispered snippets of the Watergate recordings into my ears. I remember being shown into this little carpeted room. Our teacher tells we're in luck, there's a very special guest there for us that day. We hope it's someone good. A man with a flattop haircut enters. His name is H.R. Haldeman. He has something important to tell us. We hope he wants to tell us that he is not the real special guest. We have no idea who he is.</p><p begin="00:04:18.81" data-timestamp="258.81">If your history's a little foggy, H.R. Haldeman was President Nixon's Chief of Staff. He was a pal and a confidante. When recordings of Nixon in the White House were released, there were hours and hours of recorded conversation between the two of them on the phone, like a couple of mean girls plotting how to take over the rest of the school. After Watergate broke, it became clear that Haldeman was involved, and he turned in his resignation. It was a big enough deal that Nixon went on TV to announce it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Richard Nixon</h4><p begin="00:04:45.90" data-timestamp="285.9">Today in one of the most difficult decisions of my presidency, I accepted the resignations of two of my closest associates in the White House-- Bob Haldeman, John Erlichman.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:05:00.19" data-timestamp="300.19">Nixon threw his good friend H.R. Haldeman under the bus. Haldeman was convicted of conspiracy, obstruction of justice, and perjury. He served 18 months in prison, passing the time by working as a chemist in the prison's sewage treatment plant. Once he was released, he got involved in various business ventures. He became the owner of eight Sizzler steak houses. He receded into normal life, apparently only to resurface for the occasional public appearance in front of a room full of high school students.</p><p begin="00:05:29.01" data-timestamp="329.01">That day in the library, we didn't know about any of Haldeman's history. I, for one, would have appreciated being told about the Sizzler steak house part up front. I remember clearly that Haldeman seemed like this sad, broken man. He wasn't wearing a suit, he didn't seem like someone with any authority, someone who was a part of history. It seemed clear that Haldeman was there that day to get us to stand in his shoes and see Watergate from his perspective. It was a very freaky Friday kind of moment-- see how hard it is to be an adult for a day.</p><p begin="00:05:58.64" data-timestamp="358.64">And if you'll allow me this tangent, I was reminded of all this recently when I heard about something similar happening now in Simi Valley, California. That's where the library is for California's other Republican president, Ronald Reagan.</p><p begin="00:06:13.88" data-timestamp="373.88">On a sunny Friday morning, I watch a class of fifth graders climb down off their school bus in their especially nice field trip clothes. They're here because the library does these reenactments where the students actually get to play Ronald Reagan and his various staff members-- stand in Reagan's shoes for the day. The kids file inside where a cheerful woman who works for the library stands in front of a large map with countries either red or blue.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tour Guide</h4><p begin="00:06:37.33" data-timestamp="397.33">Who wants to tell me about the map? What do you know about the map? Yes?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Boy 1</h4><p begin="00:06:41.86" data-timestamp="401.86">All of the red countries are the Communist countries.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tour Guide</h4><p begin="00:06:46.55" data-timestamp="406.55">Excellent. Very good. Is this a map of what it looks like today? No? When is this map? What're we looking at? What time period? Yes, sir?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Boy 2</h4><p begin="00:06:57.43" data-timestamp="417.43">The 1980s.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tour Guide</h4><p begin="00:06:58.47" data-timestamp="418.47">Very good. Actually 1983. And in 1983 who was president?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Students</h4><p begin="00:07:03.70" data-timestamp="423.7">Ronald Reagan.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:07:06.19" data-timestamp="426.19">It's obvious the kids have been studying Ronald Reagan all week, and in particular, the invasion of Grenada. One of the great things about tagging around on an elementary school field trip is you re-learn all the history lessons that you'd not only forgotten, but question whether you ever learned in the first place. Do you remember anything at all about the invasion of Grenada? Don't worry, they'll explain to us.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tour Guide</h4><p begin="00:07:27.42" data-timestamp="447.42">In 1983, Grenada is what color? Everyone tell me?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Students</h4><p begin="00:07:32.62" data-timestamp="452.62">Red.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tour Guide</h4><p begin="00:07:32.94" data-timestamp="452.94">Very good, it's red. That means it's a Communist country.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Starlee Kine</h4><p begin="00:07:35.42" data-timestamp="455.42">The kids go on to talk about the rebels who overthrew the Grenadian government in 1983 and about the group of 800 hundred or so American medical students who were stranded there and who became instant pawns in US Cold War politics.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Tour Guide</h4><p begin="00:07:48.03" data-timestamp="468.03">But why do we really care about what's going on in Grenada?
