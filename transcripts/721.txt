And during those rests, they read books, spy thrillers and mysteries, and do the crossword with their neighbors. A trumpet player has taught himself three languages. Another musician ran a woodshop business on his laptop during the show.</p><p begin="00:48:55.89" data-timestamp="2935.89">And socially, it's a bit like middle school. There are the loners, the jocks, and the French horns. They're like the boys in the back of the bus. They bring in fart machines and run the same practical jokes over and over. Sometimes they even mess with the audience. The front row is right up against the pit, so close that their feet sometimes dangle next to the musicians heads. Occasionally, one of the French horn players would take out a bottle of whiteout and write little messages on the soles of the audience's shoes.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Melanie Feld</h4><p begin="00:49:26.04" data-timestamp="2966.04"> Those guys, they're sitting right behind me. They're always chattering and laughing. I, being me, if I play badly, I think, oh, god, they're saying how terrible I am. Oh, god, I don't want to humiliate myself.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jay Caspian Kang</h4><p begin="00:49:40.23" data-timestamp="2980.23"> This, more than anything, Melanie told me, is what makes her want to sound good every night. She's worried the French horn guys will make fun of her.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Melanie Feld</h4><p begin="00:49:49.17" data-timestamp="2989.17"> I'm not playing for the audience because the audience doesn't-- and so I'm playing for those French horn players. I do want to say, one of the compliments I've gotten over the years is, how do you still play so well when you've just been doing <i>Phantom</i> for all those years? I said it's is a choice that I've made. My choice is to play this music like it's any other music that I play and make it beautiful.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jay Caspian Kang</h4><p begin="00:50:11.46" data-timestamp="3011.46"> Can you just play something from <i>Phantom?</i></p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Melanie Feld</h4><p begin="00:50:13.17" data-timestamp="3013.17"> Well, I can play the really hard one. If it's really bad, though, I beg you not to--</p><p begin="00:50:18.07" data-timestamp="3018.07">[PLAYS OBOE]</p><p begin="00:50:21.48" data-timestamp="3021.48"></p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jay Caspian Kang</h4><p begin="00:50:26.85" data-timestamp="3026.85"> Nobody is in the <i>Phantom</i> pit right now. The show temporarily closed in March because of COVID-19. The unstoppable show had finally been put on pause. I recently checked in with Melanie again. She wasn't doing very well. Her mother had died in a nursing home in New Jersey, presumed COVID-positive. She wasn't getting paid by the show anymore. And she missed <i>Phantom.</i></p><p begin="00:50:51.04" data-timestamp="3051.04">This was surprising to me. Melanie and all the other musicians had told me about their fantasies of finally leaving the show. And I had believed them. But now that it had actually happened, she missed the routine.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Melanie Feld</h4><p begin="00:51:03.32" data-timestamp="3063.32"> You know, Phantom, I miss the comradeship, the repetition of the silly jokes and watching everyone eat and-- I don't know-- the routine. I kind of like routine in my life.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jay Caspian Kang</h4><p begin="00:51:16.66" data-timestamp="3076.66"> This, of course, is the opposite of what she'd said in the past, before COVID.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Melanie Feld</h4><p begin="00:51:21.53" data-timestamp="3081.53"> It was always easy to complain that it was boring, and to complain about driving into the city, and wasting all that time in the car, and playing the same music and going home again. And I just thought, I knew I was lucky back then, but it becomes very real now. I mean, what can I say?</p><p begin="00:51:42.00" data-timestamp="3102">Now I really know what it's like not having this job. It's just so much fun to complain about things that don't matter. Oh, the women in the bathroom, they're just always talking about their expensive hair and makeup. And I miss the women in the bathroom. And yeah, I'd be happy to complain about that again.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jay Caspian Kang</h4><p begin="00:52:11.65" data-timestamp="3131.65"> Before the pandemic, every time I talked to Melanie, I would ask how she was doing. Her answer always depended on parking. It's hard to park in midtown Manhattan. A good parking spot was a good day. A bad parking spot was a bad day. This is how she made sense of her life.</p><p begin="00:52:28.75" data-timestamp="3148.75">I think about this all the time. Most of our lives are spent finding parking for the job we don't want to do. Melanie is not alone in that. And after any number of years, those routines accumulate. And that's more or less your life.</p><p begin="00:52:49.19" data-timestamp="3169.19">Of all the people I talked to in the pit, one musician dealt with the mundane and inevitable repetition of life in a way that really stuck with me. For the past two decades in the pit, Kurt, the musician who described himself as a violin operator, had been dreaming up the most elaborate and metaphorically perfect coping mechanism. It's a band made up entirely of automatons. I met these robot musicians in a warehouse in Yonkers.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kurt Coble</h4><p begin="00:53:17.73" data-timestamp="3197.73"> The PAM Band!</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jay Caspian Kang</h4><p begin="00:53:19.58" data-timestamp="3199.58"> The PAM Band stands for Partially Artificial Musicians. Kurt's automatons are made up of scraps of metal and string all wired up to a soundboard that Kurt can program to create whatever sounds he wants. There's Magnus, an electric chord organ, Krieg, the bass guitar, and then there's Rosie, the theremin.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kurt Coble</h4><p begin="00:53:39.19" data-timestamp="3219.19"> This is Jack, a solid body electric violin using the exoskeleton design. This is what helps alleviate the boredom of the redundancy of <i>Phantom,</i> because I'm constantly thinking about this project and how I can improve the automation and the kind of music that I would like to create.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jay Caspian Kang</h4><p begin="00:54:08.68" data-timestamp="3248.68"> Why did you decide to do this?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kurt Coble</h4><p begin="00:54:13.14" data-timestamp="3253.14"> If I ever see a therapist, maybe they will help me understand this.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jay Caspian Kang</h4><p begin="00:54:16.49" data-timestamp="3256.49"> Well, pretend I'm a therapist.</p><p begin="00:54:17.48" data-timestamp="3257.48">[LAUGHTER]</p><p begin="00:54:19.52" data-timestamp="3259.52">Was there part of it where you're like, man, I am playing in this orchestra. It's not the expressiveness that I want. I also kind of feel like an automaton, you know? And maybe I'll just make an automaton as a violinist.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kurt Coble</h4><p begin="00:54:33.57" data-timestamp="3273.57"> Yeah, I mean, I can see exploring that. Am I looking for some kind of soul healing from this dehumanization of being in a violin section? Possibly.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jay Caspian Kang</h4><p begin="00:54:53.03" data-timestamp="3293.03"> I asked Kurt if the PAM Band could play "The Music of the Night," or "All I Ask of You," or any of the <i>Phantom</i> classics. He wasn't into that at all. This band was not designed to play Andrew Lloyd Weber.</p><p begin="00:55:05.60" data-timestamp="3305.6">But something inside him just couldn't get away from <i>Phantom</i> <i>of</i> <i>the</i> <i>Opera.</i> Back when he was sitting in the pit, he'd composed, just in his head, both the prequel and the sequel to <i>Phantom,</i> both which involve <i>Indiana</i> <i>Jones</i> type characters. And years ago, he got a copy of the 1925 silent film version of <i>Phantom</i> and wrote an entire score. He wanted to play it for me. He turned off the lights in the warehouse and projected the film onto the wall. The PAM Band started to play.</p><p begin="00:55:36.88" data-timestamp="3336.88">The score features him, Kurt, as the solo violinist and the star of the show. The automatons all play the same thing, but Kurt always improvises. None of his shows are ever the same.</p><p begin="00:55:49.32" data-timestamp="3349.32">[KURT PLAYING WITH PAM BAND]</p><p begin="00:55:53.30" data-timestamp="3353.3"></p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:56:00.28" data-timestamp="3360.28"> Jay Caspian Kang, he's making a documentary about the <i>Phantom</i> pit and is co-host of the podcast, <i>Time</i> <i>to</i> <i>Say</i> <i>Goodbye.</i></p><p begin="00:56:07.16" data-timestamp="3367.16">[MUSIC - "FOUR WALLS" BY EDDIE HOLMAN]</p><p begin="00:56:10.61" data-timestamp="3370.61"></p></div>  </div>
