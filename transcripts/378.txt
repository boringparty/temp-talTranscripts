I'm Ira Glass, and the theme of today's show is, This I Used to Believe.</p><p begin="00:00:11.48" data-timestamp="11.48">And I have to admit this is just a knockoff of this series that's been running on NPR News called, <i>This</i> <i>I</i> <i>Believe,</i> which in turn, actually is a knockoff of the <i>This</i> <i>I</i> <i>Believe</i> Series that Edward R. Murrow did in the 1950s.</p><p begin="00:00:25.85" data-timestamp="25.85">Maybe you've heard some of these on the radio. These are short essays, three minutes long, by famous people and not so famous people very often, about what life has taught them.</p><p begin="00:00:35.42" data-timestamp="35.42">The NPR series actually invited listeners from all over the world to write in with their own short essays about what they believe in. And 65,000 people responded. 65,000. I was talking to Jay Allison, who heads the team that sorted through the essays and edited the ones that ended up on the radio, and he he told me that while only 200 of the essays actually ended up on the air, all 65,000 went on to this website that they set up.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jay Allison</h4><p begin="00:01:01.32" data-timestamp="61.32">You know, if you go to the website, you can type in any word. I'd started typing in words. I typed like, squid. 11 essays mention squid. Car bomb, 20. Death, 7,000 and some.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:16.80" data-timestamp="76.8">Wait, what were the squid ones? People didn't believe in squid, right? Squid was just an ancillary prop.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jay Allison</h4><p begin="00:01:21.61" data-timestamp="81.61">Squid, squid came in--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:22.11" data-timestamp="82.11">We were having squid and I realized--?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jay Allison</h4><p begin="00:01:24.75" data-timestamp="84.75">Well, one of the ones I read, was the guy was writing it and had recorded it in a deep sea submersible. He was working, I think, for Greenpeace and he was in the Arctic ocean. And he was looking out the window and seeing squid and writing his essay.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:39.13" data-timestamp="99.13">That guy, of course, wrote about how he believed in cocktail sauce. Not really.</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:01:44.65" data-timestamp="104.65">The big things that came up over and over in the essays that people sent in were really exactly what you would expect. People believe in kindness, and the Golden Rule, tolerance, social justice, love, hard work, humility. By the fourth year of the <i>This</i> <i>I</i> <i>Believe</i> series, it got a lot harder to get on the air-- if you believed in kindness.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:03.56" data-timestamp="123.56">Now, I don't want you to get mean about people. And they've written about why they deserve their advantages. And those are somewhat shocking to me. And--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:23.16" data-timestamp="143.16">And who would write this kind of thing?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jay Allison</h4><p begin="00:02:25.51" data-timestamp="145.51">Teenagers with money sometimes write about how they deserve it. I was searching for one actually today, and I remembered that they'd mentioned like a BMW, I think in it. So I just did a search on BMW, but then I started scanning them, and a lot of people wrote about a belief in their car. In the brand of car they had. Those essays take me aback a little bit.</p><p begin="00:02:52.80" data-timestamp="172.8">One of the essays about cars, the person said, &quot;I believe in having a car as cute as I am.&quot; And she believed she was much cuter than her car, her current car. And so she believed that-- I forget whether it was a BMW or an Audi-- but that was the car that she--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:14.12" data-timestamp="194.12">She thought would be as cute as her?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jay Allison</h4><p begin="00:03:15.82" data-timestamp="195.82">Yeah.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:16.43" data-timestamp="196.43">Yeah?</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:03:23.91" data-timestamp="203.91">Jay says that it's become a popular thing to assign <i>This</i> <i>I</i> <i>Believe</i> essays to kids in high school. There's a whole curriculum around it. And it is clear a lot of the kids do not want to do the essays. Some of them just seem to look around the room and just pick something-- &quot;I believe in pencils,&quot;-- and then start writing.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jay Allison</h4><p begin="00:03:41.62" data-timestamp="221.62">There are schools all over this country who are using <i>This</i> <i>I</i> <i>Believe.</i> There is a generation of children who are going to curse our names.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:50.16" data-timestamp="230.16">That's right. Oh, I hate that guy.&quot;</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jay Allison</h4><p begin="00:03:56.30" data-timestamp="236.3">I know. I hate that show, I can't even believe. They just wanted to be critical. And he said-- but secretly he didn't want to be like that. And he said, &quot;And the way I'm going to start stopping to be like that is, I'm going to write this essay. Because it's not cool to do.&quot;</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:31.26" data-timestamp="271.26">NPR News is going to run its last <i>This</i> <i>I</i> <i>Believe</i> later this month. She would keep her voice upbeat. And she would actually try to stay cheerful.</p><p begin="00:05:06.57" data-timestamp="306.57">And even when her own mom, she writes in this essay, was dying in the hospital, she continued that way. How come you haven't done an essay for us?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:41.85" data-timestamp="341.85">Well, actually I mean it's funny. But I'm not sure I believe anything, in that way, that would make for an essay.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jay Allison</h4><p begin="00:05:55.96" data-timestamp="355.96">But did you ever, did you ever sit down-- or do you just sort of ask yourself rhetorically from time to time?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:00.79" data-timestamp="360.79">I ask myself whenever I hear the series. I hear you on the radio and I think, how come I'm not on Jay's series? Like, how come I'm not doing <i>This</i> <i>I</i> <i>Believe?</i></p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jay Allison</h4><p begin="00:06:07.08" data-timestamp="367.08">Are you sure you're not just giving up too easily though?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:10.90" data-timestamp="370.9">I don't know. I think I'm one of those people where like, I had a lot of really strong beliefs about stuff when I was a kid, and I like had a religious phase, and then I had a very strong, like, atheist phase and then I had a very political phase. And I was like politically correct for years. I mean the kind of politically correct where like, when I was in my 20s I went to Nicaragua and I called it Nikh-a-RAH-hua. And you know what I mean? Like, I was horrible. And--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Jay Allison</h4><p begin="00:06:30.77" data-timestamp="390.77">Did you call it Nikh-a-Rah-hua on the radio, too?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:33.53" data-timestamp="393.53">Ah no.
