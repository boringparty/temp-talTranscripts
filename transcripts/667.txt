  <h3>Prologue: Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.57" data-timestamp="0.57"> When Dave was turning his life around and trying different things and figuring out what to do next, one of the places he looked was to the <i>Howard</i> <i>Stern</i> <i>Show.</i> And I think that was a really good choice.</p><p begin="00:00:10.23" data-timestamp="10.23">I know everybody is not a fan of Howard Stern, but I am. And I think that one of the best things about the show is that it's always been a place where people talk with this kind of brutal and funny honesty about themselves. One of Howard's innovations is that he took the strain of crude, dark, confessional comedy that's always been a big part of stand-up, ever since Lenny Bruce-- Howard figured out a way to turn that into radio, daily radio.</p><p begin="00:00:35.16" data-timestamp="35.16">Anyway, to get back to this guy Dave, he loved that about the show. And in 2015, he was a few months sober, in his early 40s, working as a waiter at Katz's Deli in New York City. When he was younger, he'd worked in television, hosting an interview show for college kids but ruined that career with heroin addiction.</p><p begin="00:00:52.83" data-timestamp="52.83">Now sober in recovery, he needed a project. And he decided to a podcast about drugs with the feeling of the <i>Howard</i> <i>Stern</i> <i>Show.</i> Like, he remembered the years when comedian Artie Lange was on Howard Stern-- definitely my favorite period of Howard Stern.</p><p begin="00:01:06.97" data-timestamp="66.97">When Artie would come on every day. He would talk about his own drug problems. He would tell crazy stories. He would talk in this very real way about the mistakes he was making in his life at that time that he was not able to stop, and Dave wanted to shoot for that. He would co-host the show with his friend Chris who-- he had met Chris years before in rehab.</p><p begin="00:01:26.28" data-timestamp="86.28">They would spend hours in rehab smoking cigarettes and talking about the crazy things that they'd done on drugs. That's what they wanted the podcast to be. People'd come on, tell wild drug stories, and-- very important to Dave-- no recovery talk, which Dave found to be sanctimonious and annoying.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave</h4><p begin="00:01:42.66" data-timestamp="102.66"> This show is not necessarily about recovery.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chris</h4><p begin="00:01:46.68" data-timestamp="106.68"> Mm-hmm.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave</h4><p begin="00:01:47.58" data-timestamp="107.58"> It's about drugs.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chris</h4><p begin="00:01:48.42" data-timestamp="108.42"> Maybe eventually it will be who knows.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave</h4><p begin="00:01:50.31" data-timestamp="110.31"> If this show is ever about recovery, you got to get somebody else in this spot. I'm out.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:55.59" data-timestamp="115.59"> OK, in case this isn't clear. In these recordings, Dave is the one with the deeper voice and the loud opinions, Chris is the other guy.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave</h4><p begin="00:02:01.41" data-timestamp="121.41"> This show is about drug stories. It's not about recovery. It's not about doing the next right thing. It's about the last wrong thing.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chris</h4><p begin="00:02:07.92" data-timestamp="127.92"> But maybe with the evolution of your recovery, that's what the show will become.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:12.03" data-timestamp="132.03"> Notice how at the end there, Chris is not agreeing with Dave. Chris is 10 years younger than Dave, but started drugs and drinking at an earlier age-- he was just 11 or 12-- and has way more extreme stories about it, including a year in prison. He's also spent way more time in rehab.</p><p begin="00:02:28.19" data-timestamp="148.19">And at this point, he'd been sober for nearly two years versus four months for Dave. And all of that gave him a different feeling about recovery and sobriety. He was game to talk about it, but Dave was adamant. Sobriety isn't all that entertaining. And first and foremost, he wanted the podcast to be entertaining.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave</h4><p begin="00:02:46.47" data-timestamp="166.47"> If this show was about recovery, it's going to be lame.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:49.35" data-timestamp="169.35"> So they'd tell drug stories. It wasn't meant to be good for you. It was meant to be fun. They listed it in the comedy section of iTunes, not self-help. If you want self-help, Dave had said, go to a meeting.</p><p begin="00:03:04.93" data-timestamp="184.93">They were going to call the show <i>War</i> <i>Stories,</i> but there was already a podcast called <i>War</i> <i>Stories.</i> It was about actual war with tanks and guns and stuff, so they came up with <i>Dopey,</i> which really captures the spirit of the show perfectly. As Chris put it, two dopes talking about dope.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chris</h4><p begin="00:03:22.15" data-timestamp="202.15"> Listen, I am so grateful to be sober. And I'm so grateful to be able to talk about drugs, you know, like in a way where I'm not feeling bad about it. It's not even euphoric recall. It's just funny.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave</h4><p begin="00:03:38.19" data-timestamp="218.19"> And I think that's why we got along, 'cause we felt safe doing that with each other. You know, sometimes you feel a little judged with people when you recount funny stuff that's happened to you.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chris</h4><p begin="00:03:47.55" data-timestamp="227.55"> Yeah.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:48.22" data-timestamp="228.22"> In <i>Dopey,</i> there's going to be no judgment of drug use, past or present. They recorded the first episode on a laptop, no microphone or anything, near a fish tank in Dave's apartment in Manhattan. Maybe you could hear the hum of the tank in that recording. But between this episode, Episode 1 and Episode 143 of <i>Dopey--</i> just 2 and 1/2 years later-- their lives are going to change drastically.</p><p begin="00:04:10.03" data-timestamp="250.03">By Episode 143, <i>Dopey</i> would sound completely different. In the episodes in between, they chronicle this story where so much happens to them and some others, things lots of drug users face. But because podcasts and radio are so intimate, it all gets documented in the most unfiltered way.</p><p begin="00:04:28.66" data-timestamp="268.66">Dave and Chris wanted their show to be just for fun, but it ended up being so much more. Today, we're going to have two stories like this. Stories about a kind of DIY radio-- very different kinds of DIY radio-- but in both stories, it's amateurs inventing how to talk about what it is that they're going through.</p><p begin="00:04:48.49" data-timestamp="288.49">And really, both stories are dispatches from two very different battlefields. I know that sounds a little grand, but really this story-- from the opioid epidemic-- and then the other story is from a small town in Syria, in the middle of the war that's going on there. And we hear about life during that war in this way that is much more personal and close up than we usually get. From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i> I'm Ira Glass. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>

<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Two Dope Kings</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:20.29" data-timestamp="320.29"> Act One, Two Dope Kings. So OK, you remember the premise? Two guys-- Dave and Chris-- they have this podcast. It's called <i>Dopey.</i> Dana Chivvis from our staff, listened to dozens of hours of the podcast, spanning 2 and 1/2 years, and tells the story of what happened.</p><p begin="00:05:37.49" data-timestamp="337.49">Quick warning before we start, since this is the podcast internet version of our show, we have removed the bleeps that we use when we do the show on the radio. If you prefer a bleeped version of the show, it's at our website. Now here's Dana.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Dana Chivvis</h4><p begin="00:05:49.75" data-timestamp="349.75"> We've been talking about this podcast for five minutes already. And the whole point of the podcast is to tell crazy drug stories, so let's get right to one. This is from the very first episode. Chris tells the story.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chris</h4><p begin="00:06:01.12" data-timestamp="361.12"> So I relapsed. I don't even want to say I relapsed. I just started using again after a brief period of abstinence. And I took a lot of Xanax-- about 60 bars-- for those of you that know bars.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave</h4><p begin="00:06:12.58" data-timestamp="372.58"> At once?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chris</h4><p begin="00:06:13.47" data-timestamp="373.47"> No, over a period of about three or four days.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave</h4><p begin="00:06:15.22" data-timestamp="375.22"> So you took 120 milligrams over four days?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chris</h4><p begin="00:06:18.25" data-timestamp="378.25"> Yeah, and I blacked out for about a week. And during that blackout, I robbed a veterinarian. I went into the veterinary clinic. I told them my cat was having seizures and I needed phenobarbital ASAP. They told me something like, you know, we don't just hand out medications for the asking. Please fill out some paperwork and can we see your cat? Obviously, I had no cat with me.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dave</h4><p begin="00:06:39.82" data-timestamp="399.82"> Did you have a cat at home?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Chris</h4><p begin="00:06:41.20" data-timestamp="401.2"> I did have a cat at home, Smegal.</p><p begin="00:06:42.73" data-timestamp="402.73">[LAUGHING]</p><p begin="00:06:44.74" data-timestamp="404.74">So I fill out the paperwork. And while I was filling out the paperwork, I ended up putting it down and storming into the back, knocked over the nurse.
