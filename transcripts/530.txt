    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:01.04" data-timestamp="1.04">Your know who Studs Terkel is? He was an oral historian. But from 1952 to 1997, he also did a radio show on WMFT in Chicago, where he would broadcast the interviews that ended up in his books, and also lots of interviews with famous musicians and actors and artists. Anyway, I was going through some old tapes of his radio show recently for this event they had celebrating his life. And I stumbled across this utterly ordinary, nothing-special-going-on Friday on his radio program. Anyway, here's Studs.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:00:40.79" data-timestamp="40.79">I think it's pretty obvious to readers that Mike Royko's column is not only witty, but I think it's journalism in the true sense, in digging into--</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:48.24" data-timestamp="48.24">It's a Friday in July nearly 50 years ago. It's July 29, 1966. And his guest that day is a good friend of his, newspaper columnist Mike Royko, another iconic Chicago figure. Anyway, on this particular day, they kill two hours very interestingly, I have to say, chatting about stories in the news.</p><p begin="00:01:06.75" data-timestamp="66.75">The medical director of Cook County hospital, for example, had made headlines when he said that he did not need to air condition the operating rooms in the facility. Surgeons, he said, should instead eat salted crackers, which would replace the salt that doctors lose when they sweat. There was some news that week about the prize fighter they still called Cassius Clay. There was a story that was national news at the time that I would be very surprised if you heard of. I had not heard of this.</p><p begin="00:01:33.58" data-timestamp="93.58">Basically, a justice on the Supreme Court, William O. Douglas, a liberal appointed by FDR who'd been on the bench almost three decades at that point-- the news was he had gotten married. Here's Studs talking to Mike Royko about that.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:01:47.79" data-timestamp="107.79">And you did a column, I understand, received quite a response. What happened in Congress, the objections of many-- Pat, I think this is worth reading.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Male Speaker</h4><p begin="00:01:56.07" data-timestamp="116.07"> Mhm.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:01:56.67" data-timestamp="116.67">May I read this one? Because this leads, I know, to all sorts of discussion.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:00.99" data-timestamp="120.99">OK, I'm just going to stop the tape right there. For whatever reason, Royko seems to prefer that Studs read his columns on this show, rather than read them out loud himself. And if it's OK with you, I enjoyed this next bit, and I know that you tuned in just now to hear a radio show made, you know, today, but let's just do a little time travel, OK?</p><p begin="00:02:20.67" data-timestamp="140.67">So your radio or your computer or your smartphone or whatever picks up instead a radio show from 1966 for just a couple minutes. And let's enjoy this together. Here's Studs.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:02:31.63" data-timestamp="151.63">And Mike writes, "a ringing dissent to criticism of Justice Douglas. When Associate Justice William O. Douglas returns from his honeymoon, it would be perfectly proper for him to do the following-- walk with great dignity to his seat on the Supreme Court bench, sit down, draw his black robes about him, slowly raise  is right hand, touch the tip of his thumb to the tip of his nose, wiggle his fingers."</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:54.99" data-timestamp="174.99">OK, just pausing that again. Have you got what he's describing there? OK, good.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:02:59.97" data-timestamp="179.97">"Now, this would provide a fitting message and ensure an explanation for the busybodies of America, who currently are wallowing in indignation, their favorite puddle. It was his fourth marriage, and she is his youngest bride."</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:18.89" data-timestamp="198.89">The issue here, in Royko's opinion, is whether this is really anybody's business at all-- which is, incidentally, the subject of today's radio show, our radio show, when to mind your own business.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:03:28.85" data-timestamp="208.85">"The fact that a man they don't know married a girl they don't know, for reasons that are none of anyone else's business, has a terrible effect on the tempers of many people. Why this is, I don't know."</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:40.98" data-timestamp="220.98">Royko's column then quotes some things that he heard somebody say about the marriage on a Chicago call-in show. And then Royko turns to Congress, where a Democrat from Alabama called for a congressional investigation into Douglas's character because of this marriage, and said that he had heard that Douglas had been cruel to his previous wives. A congressman from Mississippi said that of all the courts troublesome decisions recently, this decision, to head down the quote, "highway of matrimony," has raised the most eyebrows.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:04:09.05" data-timestamp="249.05">"And Representative Paul Finley, Republican of Illinois, said there should be a way to remove a justice from the bench without a trial for crimes or misdemeanors."</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:23.33" data-timestamp="263.33">OK, seriously, hearing this today, with the distance of a half century, this controversy feels so utterly about nothing-- so inconsequential. It makes you wonder, is this what the Anthony Weiner story is going to sound like, a half century from now? And yeah, OK, maybe it's a little icky for a 67-year-old to marry a 23-year-old, but these, you know, are consenting adults. These are people who are doing nothing against the laws of the United States of America, nothing that seems to merit congressional action. Anyway, Royko's column ends with several questions, he says, remain unanswered.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:04:59.73" data-timestamp="299.73">"How many marriages will a justice be allowed before he gets kicked off the bench? What will be an acceptable age difference in the marriage of a justice to a younger woman? Will a justice be permitted to marry an older woman?</p><p begin="00:05:12.45" data-timestamp="312.45">What does the Alabama congressman consider cruelty to a wife? Are tear gas, billy clubs, police dogs, and shotguns acceptable on the highway of matrimony, or only on the highway back home?"</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mike Royko</h4><p begin="00:05:24.63" data-timestamp="324.63">[COUGHS]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:05:25.91" data-timestamp="325.91">Well, naturally, something happened here--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mike Royko</h4><p begin="00:05:29.55" data-timestamp="329.55">[COUGHS] Yeah.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:05:30.41" data-timestamp="330.41">--when you wrote this.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mike Royko</h4><p begin="00:05:31.33" data-timestamp="331.33">Yeah. People got mad. The response was almost unanimously against my position.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:05:38.88" data-timestamp="338.88">Now, what sort of madness?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mike Royko</h4><p begin="00:05:39.72" data-timestamp="339.72">I wasn't surprised. Oh, well, there were some very funny letters. I found out that apparently more people are concerned about the Supreme Court justice's marriage than they are about what he does the rest of the time that he's on the bench.</p><p begin="00:05:57.93" data-timestamp="357.93">One woman wrote me a letter and said that she surely wouldn't vote for him the next time he ran for election.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:06:03.03" data-timestamp="363.03">[LAUGHS]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mike Royko</h4><p begin="00:06:03.98" data-timestamp="363.98">[LAUGHS] I don't think she'd heard of him before. And--</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:06.99" data-timestamp="366.99">This next part of the recording I really just love. And then Mike, perhaps, you could read your comment. You have a little comment at the end of each one.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mike Royko</h4><p begin="00:06:26.38" data-timestamp="386.38">No, you read it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:06:26.87" data-timestamp="386.87">No, you do-- no, the comment.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mike Royko</h4><p begin="00:06:28.09" data-timestamp="388.09">I-- I can't read it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Studs Terkel</h4><p begin="00:06:29.45" data-timestamp="389.45">All right. So I will be Mike Royko. This is Jimmy Jake, an older man. However, you read. This is Jake, here.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Male Staffer</h4><p begin="00:06:35.80" data-timestamp="395.8">"It is hard to believe that you were serious in your column about Justice Douglas. I'm 62. And ever since I read about this bit, I've been trying to figure out how I could tactfully and with justification approach some or even one of the cute young things that populate the building I'm in.
