  <h3>Prologue: Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.81" data-timestamp="0.81">In October 2003, a guy was brought into the psychiatric emergency room at Bellevue Hospital in New York City. Dr. Joel Gold was the chief attending psychiatrist that day and saw him.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Joel Gold</h4><p begin="00:00:10.88" data-timestamp="10.88">He felt that his life was essentially a reality show, that he's been recorded for years, that everyone in his life was an actor reading from a script. And he came to New York essentially to test this hypothesis.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:26.49" data-timestamp="26.49">He thought that maybe 9/11 was faked, just to get a reaction out of him on reality TV.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Joel Gold</h4><p begin="00:00:31.56" data-timestamp="31.56">And if he came to New York, and if the World Trade Centers were still standing, he would know that that was in fact the case. If in fact they had been destroyed, then he would admit that perhaps he was delusional.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:42.98" data-timestamp="42.98">But once he got to New York, instead of visiting the Twin Towers, he walked into the United Nations and asked for asylum-- asylum from a TV show that was filming him without his consent, 24 hours a day, which, you know, is how he ended up in Bellevue.</p><p begin="00:00:57.85" data-timestamp="57.85">Dr. Gold didn't think much of this. People show up at Bellevue with lots of weird delusions all the time. And then a few months later, another guy walks in with the same idea, that he was being filmed 24/7 and broadcast around the world. And the second guy, like the first one, mentioned a film, the 1998 movie <i>The</i> <i>Truman</i> <i>Show</i>.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Joel Gold</h4><p begin="00:01:14.90" data-timestamp="74.9">Both of them name <i>The</i> <i>Truman</i> <i>Show</i> by name. They said, my life is like <i>The</i> <i>Truman</i> <i>Show</i>.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:22.32" data-timestamp="82.32">Truman is played by Jim Carrey. He's filmed all day, every day, on a program that is broadcast to billions of people around the globe. His wife, his best friend, everybody around him is an actor. Everybody knows this is a TV show but him, until one day, he starts to see clues that make him suspicious.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:39.52" data-timestamp="99.52">And just to be clear, you're not saying that the Truman Show necessarily triggered this, like, people watched <i>The</i> <i>Truman</i> <i>Show</i> and suddenly something in their brain snaps.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Joel Gold</h4><p begin="00:01:46.01" data-timestamp="106.01">Yeah, exactly. On the contrary, I think. It's just when people are becoming psychotic, perhaps if you've seen the movie and that's kicking around in your head, you might say, yes, this is what's happening to me.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:58.22" data-timestamp="118.22">If your psychosis includes both paranoia and a sense that you are very, very important-- what psychiatrists called grandiosity-- 30 years ago, you know, you might think that the CIA or the KGB is watching you all the time. These days, you have another possible explanation-- reality TV. A few months later, a third patient showed up with the same delusion, and a few months after that, a fourth. Dr. Gold started calling it <i>The</i> <i>Truman</i> <i>Show</i> delusion. He's written a book about it with his brother Ian called <i>Suspicious</i> <i>Minds</i>. In one case in the book, a patient, a super smart guy, an academic, very altruistic, believed that he was part of an elaborate game show and the world was watching him and betting on everything that he did.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Joel Gold</h4><p begin="00:02:37.56" data-timestamp="157.56">And this was a really fun thing that everyone would be doing online. And the monies collected would go to charities all over the world. And then every single human being on earth would be given some amount of money, and the world would be bettered for it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:54.09" data-timestamp="174.09">One of the things that he included in his delusion, you write in your book, is that he has the thought that he actually was the mastermind who created this game show that he was on, and that he controlled it and he knew the rules when he had originally created the show. But somehow he had forgotten that, and all the rules-- which is so interesting, because, of course, it's true. Like he did invent the game show. And the only fact that he's missing is that it's not real. It's all in his own head.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Joel Gold</h4><p begin="00:03:22.87" data-timestamp="202.87">That's, yeah, an interesting way of putting it. It is kind of fantastical.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:28.10" data-timestamp="208.1">And heartbreaking.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Joel Gold</h4><p begin="00:03:29.42" data-timestamp="209.42">It is.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:30.09" data-timestamp="210.09">Like part of him knows he made it up.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Joel Gold</h4><p begin="00:03:32.08" data-timestamp="212.08">Absolutely.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:32.33" data-timestamp="212.33">But he can't grasp the whole reality.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Joel Gold</h4><p begin="00:03:34.00" data-timestamp="214">He does not remember. At one point, he suggests that he told his best friends, "This is what I'm going to do. You're going to run the show. But you will now hypnotize me, and I will forget what we're talking about now so we can do this really good deed for humanity."</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:56.04" data-timestamp="236.04">Some of these patients respond to treatment, some don't-- same as with other delusions and psychoses. But Dr. Gold says that if they do come back to reality--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Joel Gold</h4><p begin="00:04:04.67" data-timestamp="244.67">Some feel great relief if they've been persecuted. It's quite embarrassing, if you think about it. Every moment of your life, I mean, when you're in the shower, literally everything is filmed, so they feel quite good about it. At the same time, there's a certain sadness that they're not particularly important.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:24.63" data-timestamp="264.63">Do they miss being the most famous person in the world?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dr. Joel Gold</h4><p begin="00:04:29.30" data-timestamp="269.3">No question, there are some who feel that that's a huge loss. At the same time, I think they return to the notion that they're mentally ill, which in and of itself is an unfortunate and sad thing.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:44.78" data-timestamp="284.78">Psychosis aside, I think all this illustrates so clearly there's a downside and an upside to being on stage for the whole world to see, a human spectacle against your will. And today on our program, we have people who became just that. They have an experience so few of us have, that we all get to see from afar. They are on display for everybody, and not because they chose it. What that feels like, the positive parts and the negative side and the real life reality, the whole thing, from the WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i> I'm Ira Glass. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>

<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: I Am the Eggplant</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:24.83" data-timestamp="324.83">Act One, "I Am the Eggplant," koo koo kachoob. In the TV genre that's devoted to pure human spectacle, reality TV, you know, people fight drunkenly in hot tubs. They eat live spiders for money. But none of that can hold a candle to this show, a show that aired in Japan all the way back in 1998. It was called <i>Susunu!</i> <i>Denpa</i> <i>Shonen</i>. And one of its segments in particular got the attention of our producers, Stephanie Foo.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Stephanie Foo</h4><p begin="00:05:53.41" data-timestamp="353.41">The segment is called "Sweepstakes Life." It starts the way a lot of these shows do, with a bunch of people at an audition. One guy beats out everyone else. He's 22 years old, a comedian just starting out in his career. His name is Nasubi.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Announcer</h4><p begin="00:06:09.65" data-timestamp="369.65"> Nasubi.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Stephanie Foo</h4><p begin="00:06:10.60" data-timestamp="370.6">Nasubi means "eggplant" in Japanese, a nickname he got because he has a long face. The producers tell him they have a unique idea for a show, something they've never tried before. It may or may not air. But if it does, he'll be the star. He'll be famous.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Announcer</h4><p begin="00:06:26.26" data-timestamp="386.26"> [SPEAKING JAPANESE]</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Stephanie Foo</h4><p begin="00:06:29.82" data-timestamp="389.82">The producers blindfold him, put him in a car, and take  him to a small apartment.
