Let the fools and cowboys roar on to their wreckage.&quot;</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:55.67" data-timestamp="55.67">You can't that? &quot;Let the fools and cowboys roar on to their wreckage.&quot; In case you're wondering, when Elena got this letter, she had been driving in snow and ice for over a decade.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elena</h4><p begin="00:01:06.22" data-timestamp="66.22">&quot;If you feel yourself slipping, steer gently towards the edge of the highway where the crusty, accumulated snow and ice may keep you from spinning out. When is this going to happen?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:26.43" data-timestamp="146.43">She asked him to stop, he wasn't able to stop. She couldn't understand this behavior. And then she found out from her mom that her dad had been listening to a show on Christian radio, she thinks it was probably James Dobson, and he heard somebody on the show give the advice that people in your family need to be touched a certain number of times per day in order to feel loved. Potassium iodide pills, in the event of a nuclear attack. He was really proud every time he found a new object. Every time he thought of a new thing, he'd say, &quot;I found something new for the box.&quot; And I'd laugh, I guess, because what else can you really do but laugh? Just saying, if you're reading this, something really bad has happened. I just want you to know that your mom and I really, really love you. And everything's going to be fine.</p><p begin="00:06:31.83" data-timestamp="391.83">You know, my dad and I, we talk, and we communicate. Or, like my dad, one of the big ways that he can show his love is by helping me every year with my taxes, which I love. I love my dad.</p><p begin="00:07:22.12" data-timestamp="442.12">Anyway, we have Jonathan Goldstein, we have Michael Ian Black, we have a dad who was not born in this country and has yet to learn the ways of American dads, which are very, very tricky. <div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Astro Boy Meet Robot Dad</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:44.85" data-timestamp="464.85">Act one, &quot;Astro boy meet robot dad.&quot; We start with this story of a dad and kid and the space between them. From Michael Ian Black.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Michael Ian Black</h4><p begin="00:07:54.95" data-timestamp="474.95">My own father wasn't the best father. Not because he didn't love us, but because he simply didn't know how to be around kids. He wasn't socially adept with adults, either, but with children he was hopeless. He didn't wrestle Or make dumb faces. Or play catch. Once he bought me a baseball mitt for my birthday, which I loved, except for the fact that I am a lefty and he bought me a right-handed mitt. It seemed equally possible to me that he was either unaware that baseball mitts came for both righties and lefties, or that he did not know that I am left-handed.</p><p begin="00:08:26.58" data-timestamp="506.58">He was the first person I ever knew who owned his own computer. It was a hulking Tandy TRS-80. It did so much less than my brother and I did, and yet he spent so much more time interacting with the computer than with us. A popular activity with my dad on the weekends, when we visited him, was to hand my brother and I each a plastic bucket and tell us to go outside and pick the rocks out of his lawn. He called the game "go pick up rocks." So much fun.</p><p begin="00:08:55.89" data-timestamp="535.89">Here's how he died. One night, the police find him pulled over in his car on the side of the highway. He's unconscious, with a head injury, the apparent victim of an assault.
