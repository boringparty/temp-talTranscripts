    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:01.73" data-timestamp="1.73">OK, three boys, aged 13, 15, and 16. All three chose to appear with fake names on this radio program. And the fake names they chose, you ready? K-Rad, Mr. Warez, and Fred. Those first two names come from the world of computer hacking and software piracy. Mr. Warez, for example, that's &quot;warez,&quot; as in &quot;wares,&quot; as in &quot;softwares,&quot; as in pirated softwares, illegal softwares. And as for Fred--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Fred</h4><p begin="00:00:40.44" data-timestamp="40.44">Why Fred? For no reason, man. There's got to be someone else named Fred out there.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:46.68" data-timestamp="46.68">You see, anonymity was important given the kinds of things that we were discussing, namely credit card fraud, computer hacking, and the nature of hell. Well, from WBEZ in Chicago, this is <i>Your</i> <i>Radio</i> <i>Playhouse.</i> I'm Ira Glass. Today on our playhouse stage, small-scale stories of small-scale sin. Stay with us, won't you?</p><p begin="00:01:18.19" data-timestamp="78.19">So K-Rad, Mr. Warez, and Fred were trying to be criminals. But they were not too successful at this. And at a McDonald's on Times Square in New York City, they told all. Doesn't that have a nice, <i>Hard</i> <i></i> <i>Copy</i> sound to it? &quot;They told all.&quot;</p><p begin="00:01:39.86" data-timestamp="99.86">This was kind of an unpleasant McDonald's. Because it was in Times Square, they decided to have neon lights everywhere in this place. A crush of sweaty people at the counter, tourists, most of us. It was the middle of summer. Fred took charge.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Fred</h4><p begin="00:01:55.78" data-timestamp="115.78">What do you guys want?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>K-rad</h4><p begin="00:01:57.88" data-timestamp="117.88">I want a number one meal.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Fred</h4><p begin="00:01:59.70" data-timestamp="119.7">OK, I'm paying for this. You're paying for my cab, OK? Whatever I can pay for. OK, we'll have a number one meal.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:06.31" data-timestamp="126.31">They're actually in Manhattan for a computer hackers' convention this summer, and Fred was not used to the downtown prices.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Fred</h4><p begin="00:02:12.75" data-timestamp="132.75">Oh my god. It's $5, man. You guys are chipping in. I can't pay for this.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:18.21" data-timestamp="138.21">I think it's fair to say that one sign that your criminal career is not going so well is if you have to worry about the prices at McDonald's. But K-Rad, Mr. Warez, and Fred were involved in very low-level types of crimes. All of them involve computers. They pirated software. They scammed free CD-ROM games. They cheated one of the big online computer services out of a few hundred dollars in online time.</p><p begin="00:02:43.97" data-timestamp="163.97">All in all, they didn't steal very much. And they didn't steal very effectively. But they did try to steal. Take this phone scam, where they would call people at random and try to get a calling card number or a credit card number out of them.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>K-rad</h4><p begin="00:02:58.25" data-timestamp="178.25">Basically, the way it works is this. You call up somebody. You say, &quot;This is the AT&amp;T operator. I have a priority collect call for--&quot; so-and-so's name. And the whole point is you have to sound like you're not calling somebody up with the intention of getting them. You have to sound like you've been sitting in this chair for 10 hours, and you want to go home.</p><p begin="00:03:17.65" data-timestamp="197.65">So you got to go, &quot;This is the AT&amp;T operator. I have a collect call for Paul. Will you accept charges, sir?&quot; &quot;Uh, yeah. I'll accept charges.&quot; &quot;All right, hold on one second.&quot;</p><p begin="00:03:25.35" data-timestamp="205.35">Then you tap on the keyboard. You say, &quot;I'm sorry. You seem to have a restriction on your phone line. You can't accept collect calls to this line.&quot; And then they yell, and they go, &quot;What do you mean, I can't accept collect calls to my line? I've been getting collect calls to this line for 20 years.&quot;</p><p begin="00:03:37.79" data-timestamp="217.79">And then, you've got to go, &quot;Sir, there's nothing I can do about it. My computer says you can't receive a collect call on this line. Would you like to try an alternate billing method?&quot; In which case they will proceed to either give you a calling card or a major credit card.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:48.42" data-timestamp="228.42">And that, of course, is the idea behind the whole thing, to end up with a calling card number or a credit card number that is not your own, that you can use yourself. Now I should say that before you go out and try this yourselves, if you are thinking about doing this yourself right now, I should tell you one important fact. It doesn't work.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>K-rad</h4><p begin="00:04:06.24" data-timestamp="246.24">The reason it doesn't work is because a good percentage of people, after the point at which they go through the mind-boggling experience of not understanding what it means to have a restriction on your line, because nobody knows what that means, then they'll just say, &quot;Oh, this is too much trouble. Forget it. I won't accept the collect call.&quot;</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:22.00" data-timestamp="262">The other scams these guys run don't do much better. They tried to hack their way into the mainframe system of a big computer retailer. But they were caught and stopped before they got very far. They are such unaccomplished computer hackers that Fred himself was actually the victim of computer hackers twice. Someone broke into Fred's account on one computer bulletin board and used his account and his electronic mail to visit some of the nether regions of the net.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Fred</h4><p begin="00:04:48.44" data-timestamp="288.44">Remember, you talked to the guy on IRC. There's this weird guy. This guy was going on all these sex channels. And he was wrecking my name and stuff. It was awful.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>K-rad</h4><p begin="00:04:57.73" data-timestamp="297.73">You can't get pissed at him. I wouldn't get pissed at him, because that would be entirely hypocritical. I've said that if anybody ever steals one of my credit cards or ever hacks into my account or computer system, I will not care. And I will be perfectly allowable to that.</p><p begin="00:05:12.82" data-timestamp="312.82">To go and say, &quot;I can't believe it. Somebody took my credit card.&quot; Well, so what if I did it to everyone else? I'm perfectly willing to let someone do that to me.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:21.08" data-timestamp="321.08">Over the summer was when they really started to get serious about using other people's credit cards, which they call &quot;carding.&quot; They met a guy who was good at this and who taught them some tricks. K-Rad and Fred ordered $1,600 in computer merchandise on someone else's credit card. They had it delivered to a neutral address. They picked it up. They sold half of it for cash.</p><p begin="00:05:44.46" data-timestamp="344.46">And one thing that's peculiar about this story is that these are rich kids. K-Rad and Fred live in a wealthy upstate community. They attend what is one of the most expensive, prestigious private schools in the country. But they steal.</p><p begin="00:06:00.94" data-timestamp="360.94">Fred believes that he has no choice. He really suffers over money. He's this skinny, young-looking 15-year-old. And he is perpetually strapped for cash. And he said that his parents couldn't help him much with money right now because they had just gone $500,000 into debt.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Fred</h4><p begin="00:06:24.29" data-timestamp="384.29">Because they bought a house right before the depression and stuff. The recession, excuse me. I consider it a depression. I've noticed the effects.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:35.52" data-timestamp="395.52">At one point, to get some cash, he actually sold the computer that his parents had bought for him. And I don't think they knew. His idea is that he wanted to replace the computer as quickly as he could with the help of a stranger's credit card.</p><p begin="00:06:53.15" data-timestamp="413.15">And he also wanted to get all the other things that the other kids at his expensive private school had. He wanted to have a microwave. He wanted to have a stereo. Basically, he just wanted to be like everybody else.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Fred</h4><p begin="00:07:04.61" data-timestamp="424.61">I'm also pretty good at shoplifting. But my whole thing on that is that I will not steal anything if I have the money for it. And a lot of times, like the store at my school, like the grill, I steal from that a lot. But I also give them $10 for no reason if I have the money because I feel bad about what I've stolen. And if I ever get the money so I don't have to do this shit anymore, then I'm not going to do it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:25.30" data-timestamp="445.3">What's striking about this is how these boys want to convince themselves-- I think they really do-- want to convince themselves that they are good, and that what they do does not harm anyone. And if it does harm someone, there's always the reassuring thought that they keep very near at hand that someday they can make it up to the injured party.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>K-rad</h4><p begin="00:07:45.77" data-timestamp="465.77">As noble as this sounds, this is one thing that I absolutely guarantee that sounds like the biggest load of crap anyone could ever say. But if I am ever in a financial--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mr. Warez</h4><p begin="00:07:54.86" data-timestamp="474.86">We believe in you.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>K-rad</h4><p begin="00:07:56.08" data-timestamp="476.08">No, no one should believe in me. Go do your thing on five. Ready? Five, four three, you're in. You're in.&quot; It was just amazing after a while. And we were just so excited we were getting all that stuff. And it was just a rush. It was the flow.</p><p begin="00:45:42.49" data-timestamp="2742.49">Once you start going, you can't stop. You're just steamrolling one after the other. And the flow gets you going, and then you're just like, &quot;Yeah, we rule. We're it.&quot; It breaks down all barriers. Nothing can stop the flow. If you've got the flow, you can conquer everything. That's what people call being in the zone. Once you're in there, you can't stop. It's the juice.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:46:11.95" data-timestamp="2771.95">If anything, it's surprising how little they did with their power over computers. It was mostly pranks, making somebody's phone ring continuously, turning an enemy's home phone line into a pay phone line so when the guy picked up his phone at home, it demanded that he deposit a quarter. Of course, there was no way he could do that because it's his home phone.</p><p begin="00:46:32.86" data-timestamp="2792.86">They did actually call Julia Roberts once. They called Queen Elizabeth, too. But there's an emptiness at the heart of a lot of these stories. Once you've got the Queen on the phone, what do you say?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Eli</h4><p begin="00:46:47.41" data-timestamp="2807.41">She's like, &quot;Hello?&quot; She's talking to us and stuff. And we don't know what to say. &quot;Hi, we're calling from the United States, and this and that.&quot; And she knew what was up. She's like, &quot;OK, hello.&quot; And then she said goodbye, and that was it.</p><p begin="00:46:59.44" data-timestamp="2819.44">We didn't know what to say. What do you say to Queen Elizabeth? &quot;Hi. So you see that movie, <i>True</i> <i>Lies?&quot;</i> You know, what do you say? The fun of it is finding the number.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:47:27.27" data-timestamp="2847.27">Eli was thrown into prison for a relatively minor offense. Some of the members of his crew broke into the computers that list everybody's credit ratings. And they copied some credit reports. And they sold the information to other people. And he was named as a member of this conspiracy.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Eli</h4><p begin="00:47:47.48" data-timestamp="2867.48">They said we abused our power. But we didn't abuse it at all. We did nothing compared to the things that could have been done. What we did was such a small thing in such a larger scheme of things.</p><p begin="00:48:07.04" data-timestamp="2887.04">It's kind of depressing, in a way. There's so many things we could have done. We could have monitored Peter Lynch. What's the next best investment for the day? And we'd make millions of dollars investing or shorting some stock. But we never did. And now we wonder why. We're like, &quot;Damn. There are so many applications for this kind of stuff. What happened?&quot; But then we're like, &quot;Ah, we were just kids.&quot;</p><p begin="00:48:43.99" data-timestamp="2923.99">Then one time, one of us got the <i>Mad</i> magazine owner's phone number. And we called him. And that time, he was going through some rough times or something. And we were calling him for about two weeks. And he was just so goofy. He was kind of crazy. And he was just really stressed or something. And we just kept calling him and calling him.</p><p begin="00:49:10.63" data-timestamp="2950.63">And finally, we started harassing him and harassing him. We'd make fun of him and laugh at him and call him Alfred E. Neuman. And just ridicule him. And then he got so mad at us. And we used to keep calling and screwing around with him.</p><p begin="00:49:23.43" data-timestamp="2963.43">And then one day, we read in a paper he died. The day after we had called him, he passes away. And we're there like, &quot;Yo, did we kill him? I hope not.&quot; And then they said he had some nervous breakdown and this and that. We're like, &quot;Oh my god. Oh my god. I think we contributed to his demise there. We better not tell anybody this.&quot; But then we realized that there was probably other factors that contributed to it, not just a kid calling him on the phone. But since then, we stopped doing things like that because it was like, &quot;Oh my god.&quot;</p><p begin="00:50:04.30" data-timestamp="3004.3">I don't destroy computers. I don't take them down. I don't delete information that shouldn't be deleted. I think there's something morally wrong if you affect a person personally in not only his computer life, but his personal life, his right to make a living. I think that's wrong. It's just a question of morality.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:50:36.55" data-timestamp="3036.55">One thing about the computer world is that it's all so new. And hacking on computers is so new that each person who does it feels like he or she can create his or her own moral code. And Eli's crew had its own particular code of behavior. For example, unlike most hackers, they didn't share what they knew with other hackers.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Eli</h4><p begin="00:51:00.92" data-timestamp="3060.92">We had complete control over certain networks. We could have any system we wanted on that network. Any host was ours. But you don't let it get out to other hacking groups and other hackers. Because if they don't know how to use it, they don't understand the power of it all, you can't trust them. It's too much power for some people.</p><p begin="00:51:32.70" data-timestamp="3092.7">Basically, it's like having a gun. Let's say it's the Wild West. You take it upon yourself to have a gun, you're responsible for it. If you give that to someone, you're responsible for that. So you don't give it out. If you want to shoot somebody's sister, somebody's wife or something, that's upon you. It's all a question of morality in my eyes.</p><p begin="00:51:52.19" data-timestamp="3112.19">And if I know something, and it's of importance to somebody else, who's to tell me I can't sell it if I know it? Like if I knew something about you that somebody would pay a price for, it's up to me and my own morality whether I would sell it to him or not. But nobody could tell me, &quot;You can't do that.&quot;</p><p begin="00:52:12.73" data-timestamp="3132.73">Like if I know about your credit history, and somebody comes up to me and says, &quot;I'm doing background on him. I want to know. Can you get me his credit history?&quot; If I think it's immoral, I'm not going to do it. But if for some reason I think that he's going to get it anyway, and it would benefit me, I'd probably give it to him anyway.</p><p begin="00:52:38.19" data-timestamp="3158.19">We're not evil people. We're good people at heart. There was a time when me and my friend, Nynex Phreak, another guy in the group, we found a system that actually, what it did was you could input a certain series of digits. It would take those digits and see if it was a credit card. So you could basically hack out credit card numbers just by guessing. Is this a credit card? And it would tell you if it was or not. And we found that system.</p><p begin="00:53:03.28" data-timestamp="3183.28">Finally, somebody wrote a program that would automatically do it, scan all night and get thousands of credit cards. And we're like, yeah, this is no good. What if they start selling credit cards and stuff? We're like, this is no good. So we actually called the FBI and told them about it.</p><p begin="00:53:16.39" data-timestamp="3196.39">We wouldn't narc on anybody. We wouldn't say who wrote it or anything. We just said, there's a system, and it isn't right it's open like that. It was just getting ridiculous.</p><p begin="00:53:25.13" data-timestamp="3205.13">So I was like, &quot;We've got to put an end to this.&quot; And we did that. And we were like, &quot;Damn. We can't tell any hacker we did this. They'll be pissed.&quot; And we felt like that was right to do. That's kind of wrong.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:53:37.73" data-timestamp="3217.73">It is because of their own moral code in their group, and also their belief that someday hacking will no longer be seen as a crime, that society will change its ideas about information and computers and breaking into computers, that Eli is able to say something like this next quote about his time in the big house.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Eli</h4><p begin="00:53:59.21" data-timestamp="3239.21">I was a criminal in the sense that Jesus Christ was a criminal, you know? Well, you know how he was thought to be a criminal. And he was sentenced and everything. And now look. Everybody's sporting crosses. And there's churches built around him and everything. Who knows? Maybe one day, it'll be Acid Phreak, the faith. You never know.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:54:24.55" data-timestamp="3264.55">In fact, when I was visiting Eli, he was facing a daily question of right and wrong when it came to his own home confinement. The prison system was keeping track of him during home confinement with this electronic device. It was about the size of a beeper. It was riveted onto an ankle strap.</p><p begin="00:54:40.81" data-timestamp="3280.81">And it would send this signal to a computer that authorities had put into his house. And that computer would call another computer any time that he would leave the house. And as he explained, hacking these computers and the phone line that connected them was pretty easy work.</p><p begin="00:54:59.21" data-timestamp="3299.21">But he did not do it. He said he just didn't want to get in any more trouble. He said he was going straight. And it wasn't because he thought hacking was wrong. It was that he was just tired of it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Eli</h4><p begin="00:55:15.40" data-timestamp="3315.4">We had lists and lists of computers and no time to do it in. It just got to the point where it was such a large burden. It was like, &quot;Aw, man. We got to do this one. Aw, there's another one we got to do.&quot; And then it got to hundreds and hundreds. And finally, it's not even fun anymore.</p><p begin="00:55:30.21" data-timestamp="3330.21">It was such a rush to get it when you initially get it. But then, I don't know. It gets to be boring. I've just burned myself out, I think. I just got to that point where everybody gets burnt out if they have a little too much of everything.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:55:52.73" data-timestamp="3352.73">Now, though he was burned out, it was still a shock when the federal authorities seized all of his computers and he wasn't allowed to hack anymore. This was back when he was first arrested.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Eli</h4><p begin="00:56:06.46" data-timestamp="3366.46">It was like, OK, what do I do? I usually get on the computer now, late at night and stuff. And you'd just go to sleep. And then my lifestyle started changing. I'd be sleeping at night again. So I was like, &quot;Damn. This is like-- I have to fill this void. What should I do?&quot; And I didn't know what to do anymore. It was horrible.</p><p begin="00:56:29.12" data-timestamp="3389.12">It was sad. We would call each other up. And usually, we'd be talking about computers and trading passwords, and then we'd get into this and that. And I remember, the first time we called, it was like, &quot;So, what's up?&quot; &quot;Nothing. I cleaned my room yesterday.&quot; &quot;Yeah, they came over and cleaned my room, too, pretty well.&quot; &quot;Yeah, I know.&quot; &quot;So what do you want to do?&quot; &quot;I don't know.&quot;</p><p begin="00:56:54.98" data-timestamp="3414.98">Because it's such a large part of our lives at that point. You have so much power and to lose it in an instant like that is just such a shock. It's like, bam. You don't have that power anymore. You can't sit on your computer. What are you going to do? Uh-oh, I'm a regular guy now. I'm not Acid Phreak anymore. What's Acid Phreak without a computer? He's just a regular guy. So it was a bummer.</p><p begin="00:57:28.07" data-timestamp="3448.07">At this point, I've already done it. I see it as a teenager thing. OK, I was a hacker when I was a kid and this and that. I got busted. It's a teenager thing to do. And that's that. I move on. I work with computers now, and that's my life.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:57:54.31" data-timestamp="3474.31">Well, Acid Phreak was not the first criminal who told me that he quit being bad because he got bored with it. A few Chicago gang members have told me the same thing, that you end up having the same night over and over again. You just get tired of it.</p><p begin="00:58:10.64" data-timestamp="3490.64">Maybe you remember the book <i>A</i> <i>Clockwork</i> <i></i> <i>Orange,</i> the book and the movie. They're about teenage boys who love ultra-violence, commit murder, do all sorts of horrible things, speak to each other in a very stylized language.</p><p begin="00:58:26.10" data-timestamp="3506.1">At the end of that book-- the movie actually doesn't end this way, but the original British edition of the novel does. At the end of the book, the central character gives up committing acts of violence. And it's not because he thinks it's wrong. And it's not because any of the punishments and treatments that he's received have worked on him.</p><p begin="00:58:46.68" data-timestamp="3526.68">He gives it up because he's bored with it. It's just not that interesting to him. And we will end our program today with a couple of paragraphs from the book.</p><p begin="00:58:58.25" data-timestamp="3538.25">&quot;I knew what was happening, O my brothers. I was like growing up. Yes yes yes, there it is. Youth must go. Ah, yes. But youth is only being in a way like it might be an animal. No, it is not just like being an animal so much as being like one of those malenky toys you viddy being sold in the streets, like little chellovecks made out of tin, with a spring inside and then a winding handle on the outside. And you wind it up, grrr grrr grrr and off it itties, like walking, O my brothers. But it itties in a straight line. And it bangs straight into things, bang bang. And it cannot help what it is doing. Being young is like being one of those malenky machines.&quot;</p><p begin="00:59:33.67" data-timestamp="3573.67">And then at this point, the narrator starts going into this fantasy about having a son someday. And he says, &quot;When I had my son, I would like to explain all that to him, when he was starry enough to like understand. But then I knew he would not understand, or would not want to understand at all, and would do all the veshches I had done, yes perhaps even killing some poor starry forella surrounded with mewing kots and koshkas. And I would not be able to really stop him. And nor would he be able to stop his own son, brothers. And so it would itty on like that to the end of the world, round and round and round, like some bolshy, gigantic like chelloveck, like old Bog Himself, turning and turning and turning, a vonny grahzny orange in his gigantic rookers.&quot;</p></div>  </div>
