  <h3>Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:01.97" data-timestamp="1.97"> Alex was seven. He had a Teddy bear named Tony. Tony had been with him forever. Tony was with him back when he had all sorts of surgery and nobody was sure if he was going to pull through. But Alex did pull through. And afterwards, when the nerves of the family were still jangling, Alex continued to carry around Tony for years, sleep with Tony.</p><p begin="00:00:22.70" data-timestamp="22.7">When he was seven, he took Tony with him to Disneyland. And, when the family got back home to San Francisco from Disneyland, Alex's parents unpacked the bags and looked for Tony. And Tony was not there. Alex's dad, Jon, tells the story.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jon Holtzman</h4><p begin="00:00:37.09" data-timestamp="37.09"> So Alex was in the room and we told him it appears that Tony isn't here. And it took him about a minute to really realize that Tony was gone. And, at that point, he said two things. The first thing is he said that he couldn't imagine life without Tony, and the other is he said, &quot;I feel like I've lost my spirit.&quot;</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:11.85" data-timestamp="71.85"> At that point, what's a parent to do? You know, what do you do? So they call the hotel, and the hotel says over the phone they have not found this Teddy bear. And so Jon flew down there the next morning. He tried to be a good dad, rented a car, retraced the family's path, made his way to the hotel.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jon Holtzman</h4><p begin="00:01:30.00" data-timestamp="90"> After waiting for a few minutes in the lobby, a security guard came and said, &quot;Well, Mr. Holtzman, we have bad news for you. We know what happened to the bear.&quot; And they explained that a maid had seen the bear, apparently, in the garbage and that he'd been thrown away.</p><p begin="00:01:51.36" data-timestamp="111.36">And so my immediate reaction was well, that's great. That's not bad news. That's good news because at least we know where he is. And he said, &quot;Well, no. You've got to see the problem here.&quot; And we went around to the back. And there was this huge sealed dumpster with a compacter on the front, literally, the size of a semi trailer and 25, 30 feet long, at least.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:18.26" data-timestamp="138.26"> They looked at it and there was, literally, no way into this dumpster. Jon started to despair.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jon Holtzman</h4><p begin="00:02:24.94" data-timestamp="144.94"> And I started asking, &quot;Well, when is the next garbage pickup?&quot; And they said, &quot;Well, it's a day from now.&quot; And I'm thinking what am I going to do? And, just as we were having this discussion, the truck arrives to pick up this giant dumpster a day early. And this was kind of an amazing thing because, if I had gotten there 15 minutes later, or 20 minutes later, the garbage truck would've been gone.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:53.96" data-timestamp="173.96"> A squad of hotel employees was deployed to travel with Jon and the garbage truck to the recycling plant to search for the Teddy bear. And, you know, there are only two possible attitudes that adults would have towards this particular mission. One, they would be into it, or, two, they would think that Jon's a nut. And, incredibly, none of them thought that he was a nut. But why? Because almost all of them were parents. And, at some point or another, almost all of them had been in the same or a similar situation.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jon Holtzman</h4><p begin="00:03:29.02" data-timestamp="209.02"> I mean, you couldn't be a parent without having dealt with your kids dealing with loss, you know? And it's so horrible. And, yeah. I mean, I think everybody rallied to exactly that cause.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:46.82" data-timestamp="226.82"> It's interesting because I think that a lot of parents get to points, at different points in their child's lives, where they want to intervene, and they feel like they will not allow fate to hurt their child.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jon Holtzman</h4><p begin="00:04:01.58" data-timestamp="241.58"> Absolutely.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:02.16" data-timestamp="242.16"> And they will simply do whatever they can to stop fate.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jon Holtzman</h4><p begin="00:04:06.47" data-timestamp="246.47"> There's no question. And, you know, if you've ever had a child who almost dies, it means a lot, in a way, to be able to protect your kids from that kind of thing.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:27.75" data-timestamp="267.75"> Well, on this Father's Day, we bring you stories of dads trying to protect their kids and kids trying to protect their dads. From WBEZ Chicago and Public Radio International, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i> I'm Ira Glass.</p><p begin="00:04:40.08" data-timestamp="280.08">Today on our program, the protection racket that is parenthood. Our program today in three acts. Act One, Paddington's Day at the Dump, the story of a man, a small toy bear, and tens of thousands of pounds of soggy garbage. Act Two, A Trip Down Memory Lane. A son takes his father on one, last road trip to jog his dad's failing memory of his own life. Act Three, Age of Enchantment. Writer Lawrence Weschler and his daughter, Sarah, talk about something that he did intending to be kind, intending to protect her from disappointment, that inadvertently broke her heart, and how she saved the day for the two of them. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>

<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Paddington's Day At The Dump</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:29.19" data-timestamp="329.19"> Act One, Paddington's Day at the Dump. So Jon Holtzman and the hotel employees followed the garbage truck that's hauling the dumpster away. In his car, four conscripts. And, at this point, essentially, this story becomes the movie, <i>Saving</i> <i>Private</i> <i>Ryan.</i> And, you know, why don't we have some of the music from the movie just to set the right mood?</p><p begin="00:05:50.98" data-timestamp="350.98">[MUSIC PLAYING - &quot;HYMN TO THE FALLEN&quot; FROM SAVING PRIVATE RYAN]</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:05:59.64" data-timestamp="359.64">Perfect. Somewhere in Southern California there's a little bear named Tony. And these four recruits are going to get him out to assuage the grief of one American family.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jon Holtzman</h4><p begin="00:06:09.62" data-timestamp="369.62"> OK, so we're in the car and there is this kind of silence. I made a deal with everybody in the car. I offered each of them $100 for the person who found the bear. And we were kind of plotting strategy of, well, OK, when they open this dumpster, how are we going to find the bear? Where is he going to be in the dumpster? How many days of garbage is there? And, you know.</p><p begin="00:06:33.92" data-timestamp="393.92">And so we finally arrive at the recycling plant. And, first of all, the scope of this plant is not at all what I'd imagined. I expected it to be just a garbage dump. But, instead, it's what I imagine to be, essentially, a modern recycling plant. And there were these huge caterpillar trailers and tractors running around the plant. And there were these huge steel floors. And the scope, the scale, of the place was just really awesome.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:07.53" data-timestamp="427.53">It's like acres is what you're trying--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jon Holtzman</h4><p begin="00:07:08.78" data-timestamp="428.78">Yeah, it's just huge.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:15.87" data-timestamp="435.87"> It takes a long, long time to talk their way into the plant. If they enter, all work at the recycling center has to stop. But, incredibly, finally, they're allowed in under one condition.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jon Holtzman</h4><p begin="00:07:29.59" data-timestamp="449.59"> We would have 15 minutes to search this pile. But that's all we could have, which put a lot of pressure on us. Although, I'm not sure we understood how much pressure it would put on us until they dumped the garbage out of the trailer.</p><p begin="00:07:50.51" data-timestamp="470.51">So they dumped the garbage out of the trailer. And, even though the trailer was large, I guess I'd kind of underestimated how much garbage can go in a trailer that size. And so they dumped this out on the floor. And, of course, it spread out, so it occupied this really massive space on the floor. And it was full of this brown sludge, and liquid, and just tens of thousands of plastic bags floating, essentially. Not really floating, but oozing in this--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:08:24.30" data-timestamp="504.3"> This soggy sort of--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jon Holtzman</h4><p begin="00:08:25.96" data-timestamp="505.96"> --soggy mess. Exactly. And I took one look at this pile and my heart truly sank because I just thought I don't even know where to begin.</p><p begin="00:08:43.58" data-timestamp="523.58">A number of the people who came with me jumped in and immediately started ripping open bags trying to find the bear. And so, after 30 seconds or so of taking in the enormity of the thing, I just plunged into this pile along with them. And, watching us do it, these garbage men, the folks who were running the recycling plant, at least four or five of them put on their gloves and started doing the same thing with us. So, at this point, there were a group of maybe eight or nine of us pulling apart bags and frantically looking for this bear.</p><p begin="00:09:29.31" data-timestamp="569.31">Seven or eight minutes into it I just despaired of ever finding the bear. And I couldn't believe it, you know? We'd come this far, you know? And it just seemed that it ought to work out, but I didn't see how it would. It was just too big. And I remember saying to myself, &quot;Tony, if you're here, you're really just going to have to appear on the surface because there's just no way.
