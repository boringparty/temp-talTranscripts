  <h3>Prologue: Prologue</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:00:11.40" data-timestamp="11.4"> From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i> I'm Elna Baker, sitting in for Ira Glass. Kids have a stage where they learn to be embarrassed. It begins when they're toddlers, but some of us don't seem to take the lesson to heart, like my friend Jane Marie.</p><p begin="00:00:27.37" data-timestamp="27.37">I've known Jane for years. She used to work at the radio show. And her shamelessness was the quality I most admired about her, especially as a woman. You know, she didn't care what people thought. Am I acting nice? Am I dressed too sexy? Am I being loud? She didn't ask those questions. She was totally opinionated and scrappy.</p><p begin="00:00:46.29" data-timestamp="46.29">Jane was never embarrassed, and for a pretty specific reason. The way she grew up-- she was Methodist-- she was taught you are not the center of the universe, which made her feel free, like no one was watching.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jane Marie</h4><p begin="00:00:59.94" data-timestamp="59.94"> To be embarrassed would be to assume that anyone in this room or anyone who witnessed this actually spends more than 5 seconds thinking about me. And they don't. Why would you think anyone but God gives a shit about anything you're doing, ever? They don't. Like, you are nothing. You mean nothing in the world. It's obnoxious, basically, to think that, like, any of your actions-- that anyone is actually looking in your direction.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:01:31.35" data-timestamp="91.35"> This worldview, that others weren't judging her, helped her not judge herself. Those thoughts just didn't occur to her.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:01:38.86" data-timestamp="98.86"> Did you have an inner critic?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jane Marie</h4><p begin="00:01:42.00" data-timestamp="102"> What do you mean by inner critic?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:01:44.37" data-timestamp="104.37"> Like, a voice that--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jane Marie</h4><p begin="00:01:46.84" data-timestamp="106.84"> (LAUGHING) That's actually funny, sorry. I'm like, [KNOCK KNOCK], come again?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:01:53.14" data-timestamp="113.14"> So I was surprised when she texted me and said she'd heard I was looking for embarrassing stories to put on today's radio show. And that she had one, a big one that she'd never told anyone but her therapist, something that changed who she was. And I'll get to that story in about a minute.</p><p begin="00:02:10.13" data-timestamp="130.13">But first, let me just say, I love embarrassing stories. I always ask for them at parties. If I hear a good one, I'll retell it for years. Some of them, so funny. But that's not the only reason I like them. I think the best ones say so much about us, reveal sides of ourselves maybe we'd rather not see.</p><p begin="00:02:29.41" data-timestamp="149.41">And to prove the point that embarrassing stories have a lot to teach us, I decided to harness the power of the radio program. And we asked you for your embarrassing stories. People sent in over a thousand of them. Today we'll hear some of those and talk about what they reveal.</p><p begin="00:02:44.92" data-timestamp="164.92">But we're going to kick things off with Jane's story because, honestly, of the hundreds of embarrassing stories that I've heard, I've never heard another one like Jane's, where it changed someone so deeply. Her story is our first act. Let's call it Belle of the Gall.</p></div>  </div>

  <h3>Act One: The Belle of the Gall</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:03:01.96" data-timestamp="181.96"> Jane's story starts at a big charity event at a bougie hotel in Beverly Hills. The night is a fundraiser for children with epilepsy. It's held in a grand ballroom with fine china and excessive centerpieces and some of the most powerful producers and executives in Hollywood.</p><p begin="00:03:18.43" data-timestamp="198.43">This is not Jane's scene. She's there because she's interviewing for a job, a job with a big studio. A job that would be a major leap for her and pay a ton more money than she'd ever made.</p><p begin="00:03:28.84" data-timestamp="208.84">They'd met with her several times for interviews, and they'd invited her tonight and paid for her ticket as a kind of fun, looser, let's go do something more social and see how we all hit it off. Knowing where it's held and knowing it's a gala, Jane wears a ball gown, a two-piece dark green dress that shows off her midriff with a giant billowy skirt. She walks into the event and, to her surprise--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jane Marie</h4><p begin="00:03:52.35" data-timestamp="232.35"> People were wearing, like, business casual instead of, like-- it wasn't black tie. Like, I am going into this with full control of myself. Like, I am--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:04:11.35" data-timestamp="251.35"> You're there to make a good impression.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jane Marie</h4><p begin="00:04:13.09" data-timestamp="253.09"> I am there to make a great impression on these people. It doesn't feel great.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:04:39.06" data-timestamp="279.06"> In the ballroom of maybe 500 guests, they're seated at the main table, front and center. There's no moment to jump into the conversation. No one seems curious about her at all.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jane Marie</h4><p begin="00:04:59.41" data-timestamp="299.41"> So they get to the auction portion, or the fundraiser portion, of the evening. And there's a person up onstage who's calling the auction. And they say, all right, let's get things kicked off here.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:05:12.67" data-timestamp="312.67"> Jane has an idea. One time-- the only other time she's been to a gala-- they'd had an auction too. Jane was broke. Her friends at the table knew she couldn't afford it. But she figured, I'm surrounded by rich people. Someone will absolutely outbid me. And they did. The item went for hundreds of thousands of dollars. Everyone thought it was hilarious. So she looked at this table of CEOs and thinks--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jane Marie</h4><p begin="00:05:55.75" data-timestamp="355.75"> I know how to spice up the evening because I've done it before. I'm going to throw my paddle up again. I'm going to throw my paddle up again, get things cranking, have a funny story for our table to laugh about. And so they opened the bidding. And what I hear is, we're going to start the bidding out at $25,000.</p><p begin="00:06:20.14" data-timestamp="380.14">And I shoot my paddle up in the air with the same confidence that it'll get outbid immediately and then we're moving on. And then we're cooking with gas, and everybody's like, you're a blast, you know.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:06:31.26" data-timestamp="391.26"> Jane feels a rush. Ha, that was fun. She waits to be outbid.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jane Marie</h4><p begin="00:06:35.89" data-timestamp="395.89"> And I had somehow not heard that this round of bidding was really just-- it wasn't something that anyone else could bid on.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:06:48.87" data-timestamp="408.87"> It was not an auction at all. It was a pledge.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jane Marie</h4><p begin="00:06:52.42" data-timestamp="412.42"> So it was a $25,000 pledge from the eight people sitting at my table. There was a TV screen, a large TV screen hanging from the ceiling, and the name of the company I was with and our table number and the amount of my pledge.</p><p begin="00:07:23.16" data-timestamp="443.16">And I look over, and the one person I know best at the table, it looks like he's having hives, like an attack. Everyone else is just slack-jawed. Oh my god, Jane, what did you just do? What did you just do? What did you just do? And they're, like, mouthing this. Or even just saying it out loud, depending on how close they are to me. They all just stared at me and were like, how are you going to fix this for our table?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:07:56.25" data-timestamp="476.25"> Sometimes when something embarrassing happens to you, you exaggerate it in your head, think it's a bigger deal than it was. But in this case, no.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Adam Davidson</h4><p begin="00:08:05.51" data-timestamp="485.51"> I wish I could take this away from Jane and tell her her memory's wrong. Her memory's not wrong, because I'm here to tell you, yes, it was that bad. It really was. It was that bad. You're not making that up.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:08:18.82" data-timestamp="498.82"> That's Adam Davidson, Jane's only friend at the table.
