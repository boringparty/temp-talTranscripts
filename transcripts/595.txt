  <h3>Act One: If You Cannot Afford an Attorney, Some Random Dude Will Be Appointed to You</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:39.99" data-timestamp="399.99">Act One, If You Cannot Afford an Attorney, Some Random Dude Will Be Appointed to You.</p><p begin="00:06:45.63" data-timestamp="405.63">So one of the many, many things that we all know from watching television is that when you get arrested, someone is supposed to read you your Miranda rights. And those rights include the right to an attorney. I told him, from now on, you sleep naked on this cold floor. You will not move. You will not make a move.</p><p begin="00:48:02.43" data-timestamp="2882.43">After that, I pissed all over him. Terrible thing to tell  you. His head and everywhere. And he started to move his head and I said to him, don't move. I have my pistol in my hand, I'll shoot you in  the ass. That's how enraged I was.</p><p begin="00:48:16.59" data-timestamp="2896.59">I said, you're just to lie there to get some sense of what you Nazis did to the Jews. This is only a small sense. You will not be burned here. We won't cook you  in an oven. But this German Jew will heap upon you. And don't you move, or else you're a dead man.</p><p begin="00:48:37.49" data-timestamp="2917.49">And I left into my quarters and went to sleep. It's a terrible thing to tell you, but it is the truth. That's what I did. This was an event that gets me very upset, even now.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:48:56.82" data-timestamp="2936.82">For three more days he held Streicher, refused to feed him anything but potato peels that he pissed on too. On the fourth day, Werner handed him off to American officers. Streicher was one of only 11 men sentenced to death at the Nuremberg trials.</p><p begin="00:49:14.60" data-timestamp="2954.6">The specific records about Streicher's capture are spotty and contradictory. Usually someone else is credited with capturing him. But we know Werner was in the area doing this kind of work at the time. And an expert at the National Archives told me he does think Werner could have done this.</p><p begin="00:49:38.44" data-timestamp="2978.44">The man who helped me understand best what it was like to do this work was this last man, also Jewish. He escaped Luxembourg and came here.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Arno Mayer</h4><p begin="00:49:47.29" data-timestamp="2987.29">Well my name is Arno Mayer. And I don't want you to be generous, because it's A-R-N-O, and most people tend to add an L-D to it, which I refuse.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:49:56.48" data-timestamp="2996.48">I met Arno at his apartment in Princeton, New Jersey. He was 88. Small guy, with a full head of white hair, brown eyebrows. He peppered our interview with historical facts and profanity.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Arno Mayer</h4><p begin="00:50:07.78" data-timestamp="3007.78">They invented a name for me. The name was the Intellectual Fuck.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:50:12.69" data-timestamp="3012.69">Arno was assigned to PO Box 1142 at the end of the war. By then, the mission had changed. Instead of interrogating Nazis, they were now asked to wine, dine, and woo them.</p><p begin="00:50:24.97" data-timestamp="3024.97">Many Nazis now at the camp were scientists. They were rounded up because the Americans wanted their scientific expertise. And they wanted to keep that expertise from the Russians.</p><p begin="00:50:35.26" data-timestamp="3035.26">Arno was what they called a morale officer.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:50:38.42" data-timestamp="3038.42">So what were your instructions with these guys?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Arno Mayer</h4><p begin="00:50:41.33" data-timestamp="3041.33">Keep them happy.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:50:42.57" data-timestamp="3042.57">Make the Nazis happy. Not defeat them, not get information out of them. Charm them, get them to join our team.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Arno Mayer</h4><p begin="00:50:49.75" data-timestamp="3049.75">It didn't make any sense. The whole thing didn't make any sense. I mean, the whole thing was so completely surreal, completely surreal.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:50:58.37" data-timestamp="3058.37">Arno was assigned to the most famous Nazi recruit, Wernher von Braun. He designed the V-2 rocket which was targeted at civilians in London. His rocket factory used Jewish slaves. But Arno says they never discussed that. Instead, he and the others entertained the scientists. Some played games with them like volleyball or chess, or brought them liquor and newspapers, took them on outings to DC. Like a concierge, but for Nazis.</p><p begin="00:51:27.44" data-timestamp="3087.44">The scientists told Arno about their wives and their kids, their lives. And after all that, he said it was hard to see the Nazis as just monsters.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Arno Mayer</h4><p begin="00:51:38.28" data-timestamp="3098.28">I don't know. I mean, you can't just then proceed on the assumption well, these are subhumans and to hell with them. And the next time you bring a bottle of wine you put poison in it, you know.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:51:48.80" data-timestamp="3108.8">Really?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Arno Mayer</h4><p begin="00:51:50.09" data-timestamp="3110.09">I don't know. I don't know. It's a difficult question.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:51:55.05" data-timestamp="3115.05">Over time, the indignities of the job piled up. Until a small comment from von Braun set Arno off.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Arno Mayer</h4><p begin="00:52:01.75" data-timestamp="3121.75">And he had already spoken behind my back of me as [GERMAN], a little Jew boy. So I lost it. And I stomped out. I was pretty shaken. Went back to my barrack and I'm summoned to the commander of the camp. Somebody had told him what I had said.</p><p begin="00:52:19.49" data-timestamp="3139.49">And finally he said, if  you do this again you'll be court-martialed. That was for me one of the turning points, really.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:52:29.17" data-timestamp="3149.17">After that, Arno became quietly subversive. Like when they asked to go shopping, Arno, for his own amusement, took them to a Jewish department store.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Arno Mayer</h4><p begin="00:52:38.74" data-timestamp="3158.74">So he came and he said look, I'd like to send packages to our families in Germany, because it's going to be a rough winter. We'd like to go shopping. I was told, make them happy. So what the hell.</p><p begin="00:52:50.07" data-timestamp="3170.07">And I began to be amused by this and decided that I was going to take them to the biggest Jewish department store in Washington, DC. So in    the car I asked them, what do we start with? Well, we want to buy underwear for our wives.</p><p begin="00:53:03.47" data-timestamp="3183.47">In those days, they didn't have bins in which this stuff was in. There was a sales lady. So the four of them, since they were scientists, you had these slide rules. They took them out as the woman asked what size, you see. So the woman came out and she held up a nylon panty. And Wernher von Braun said [GERMAN] but no, made of wool and with long legs.</p><p begin="00:53:28.39" data-timestamp="3208.39">In the meantime, people started looking around. There with these guys in these leather coats, and then there was this little guy next to them in an American uniform. And just as they were telling the lady about the sizes, the military police came and arrested the whole bunch of us.</p><p begin="00:53:43.09" data-timestamp="3223.09">[CHUCKLING]</p><p begin="00:53:45.05" data-timestamp="3225.05"></p></div><div class="host"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:53:49.98" data-timestamp="3229.98">They were conspicuous, this group of German-looking men making a ruckus. Arno told the police he was on a secret military mission. They verified it and let him go. But Arno says he knows his rebellion was small. And his restraint still haunts him.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Arno Mayer</h4><p begin="00:54:05.33" data-timestamp="3245.33">I should have told them to go to hell. But I didn't do it. I was a coward. I mean, I only exploded once. I could have exploded many other times.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:54:12.52" data-timestamp="3252.52">Do you wish you had?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Arno Mayer</h4><p begin="00:54:13.01" data-timestamp="3253.01">Yeah, you're god damn right, god damn right, as I look back.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Karen Duffin</h4><p begin="00:54:17.14" data-timestamp="3257.14">Wernher von Braun stayed in the United States and became something of an American hero. He helped us get to the moon. He was on the cover of <i>Time.</i> He became buddies with JFK.</p><p begin="00:54:28.13" data-timestamp="3268.13">And PO Box 1142 closed down. Its buildings were bulldozed. The files were burned. And the men who worked there went on to become lawyers, a CIA agent, an ambassador, head of the Culinary Institute of America, conductor of the Chicago Chamber Orchestra, the richest man in America in the '80s, John Kluge.</p><p begin="00:54:49.62" data-timestamp="3289.62">Arno became a history professor at Princeton. And the guy who lost it and pissed on the Nazi started his own textile business. And all these men whose job it had been to extract secrets now carried their own. Most never said a word to their wives or kids about the work they had done, about the intimate war they had fought with the Germans in a park in Virginia.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:55:21.85" data-timestamp="3321.85">Karen Duffin is one of the producers of our program.</p><p begin="00:55:30.22" data-timestamp="3330.22">[MUSIC - "WERNHER VON BRAUN" BY TOM LEHRER]</p><p begin="00:55:32.68" data-timestamp="3332.68"></p><p begin="00:55:38.13" data-timestamp="3338.13">(SINGING) Gather 'round while I sing you of Wernher von Braun, a man whose allegiance is ruled by expedience. Call him a Nazi, he won't even for frown, Nazi, schmatzy, says Wernher von Braun.</p><p begin="00:55:55.53" data-timestamp="3355.53">Don't say that he's hypocritical. Say rather that he's apolitical. Once the rockets are up, who cares where they come down. That's not my department, says Wernher von Braun.</p><p begin="00:56:12.61" data-timestamp="3372.61">Some have harsh words for this man of renown. But some think our attitude should be one of gratitude. Like the widows and cripples in old London town, who owe their large pensions to Werner von Braun.</p><p begin="00:56:27.50" data-timestamp="3387.5">You too may be a hero once you've learned to count backwards to zero.
