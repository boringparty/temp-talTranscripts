  <div class="act-inner">

Fundamentally, I am the same person.</p><p begin="00:04:57.02" data-timestamp="297.02">My coworker, Tobin Low, when I told him this story, he was like, well, yeah, I'm sure most people who have these split-second moments of selfishness don't really change. Case closed. She kept the one lonely sneaker, just because hope.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ruby Gans</h4><p begin="00:48:36.61" data-timestamp="2916.61"> It's a new month. I have a new basic pair of shoes that I wear every day. It's a pair of black Doc Martens. And I knew that I had those shoes in the car. I was getting ready for work. I got out to the car, and I could only find one shoe. I was just completely shocked and confused because how could this have happened not once, but twice? I don't know anyone who has ever lost one shoe. And now it's happened to me twice. This just is not possible.</p><p begin="00:49:12.07" data-timestamp="2952.07">And I looked around, I mean, pretty thoroughly, checked everywhere I could think that a shoe would fit. And there was only one shoe in the car. And I was-- I mean, I would say panicked is too strong, but I was worked up about it. This is not a thing that happens in real life. Where is my shoe?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:49:35.74" data-timestamp="2975.74"> I was into Ruby's story. I find losing things to be completely maddening. Objects cannot just disappear. Here's what I want. I want, when you die, for them-- and I don't know who I mean by them-- to tell you where everything you lost over the course of your life actually was when you were looking for it.</p><p begin="00:49:54.25" data-timestamp="2994.25">Anyway, Ruby, who is wired similarly, decides she is not going to work until she finds this shoe. Her hunt begins with deduction. She feels sure it was in the car yesterday, which meant it must have fallen out of the car, which feels kind of unlikely now that I say it. Did the shoe hurl itself out somehow?</p><p begin="00:50:13.44" data-timestamp="3013.44">Anyway, she makes a list of every place she had gone the day before. She'd gone to a city office to file a form. She'd gone to a Trader Joe's. And she'd gone to work. So she checks all these places. The parking lot at the city office-- no shoe.</p><p begin="00:50:28.77" data-timestamp="3028.77">The Trader Joe's-- actually goes into the Trader Joe's and asks the manager, did any workers find a single shoe in the parking lot? No. She texted the facilities guy at work, who texts her back a laughing face emoji and no, no shoe. She also goes to look at her boyfriend's house, where things took a turn.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ruby Gans</h4><p begin="00:50:47.09" data-timestamp="3047.09"> And I went and I looked all around the house, didn't find the shoe. But I did find his dad, who had showed up. And his dad was like, have you considered that someone's messing with you? And I was like, no, I had not considered that. But you're right, I should.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:51:06.26" data-timestamp="3066.26"> He'd just mentioned it in passing, but suddenly, things made a lot more sense. The question was, who was it? It had to be someone who knew about the first shoe and how crazy that had made her, who had then taken the second shoe as a kind of prank. There was a short list of suspects, maybe a friend at work. There are cameras in the parking lot. She could ask the facilities guy to go through them. Or maybe, actually, her boyfriend-- it would have been easy for him to grab her car keys.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ruby Gans</h4><p begin="00:51:32.64" data-timestamp="3092.64"> I texted him saying, be straight with me. Are you messing with me? Do you think someone else is somehow messing with me? Is there a way to tell if someone broke into my car? He's a car guy, so.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:51:48.24" data-timestamp="3108.24"> She waits a minute. He texts her back.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ruby Gans</h4><p begin="00:51:51.25" data-timestamp="3111.25"> "I am not the one hiding your shoes, if that's what you're asking. If someone's doing that, it's not funny to me."</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:51:58.72" data-timestamp="3118.72"> It wasn't funny to her either, but anything was starting to seem possible at this point.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ruby Gans</h4><p begin="00:52:03.48" data-timestamp="3123.48"> I was starting to feel a little bit genuinely concerned. It didn't really process at first, but then once I was thinking about it, I realized that would mean somebody is getting into my car, and that makes me pretty uncomfortable. And I'm starting to feel kind of afraid.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:52:21.56" data-timestamp="3141.56"> Ruby wonders, should I call the police? She drives home from her boyfriend's house, sits on the front steps, when a thought occurs to her. And that thought is, wait, yesterday, didn't I come home for 10 minutes? I did. And didn't I park over there? She gets up, walks over to that spot.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ruby Gans</h4><p begin="00:52:44.42" data-timestamp="3164.42"> I looked in the street. I looked in the gutter. It wasn't there. And I was like, ugh, OK, fine. But then, I looked up.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:52:52.54" data-timestamp="3172.54"> And there, in the tree, was her shoe. I'm kidding. It was a few feet away, on a curb next to someone's yard. It looked like someone had helpfully picked it up and set it off to the side.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ruby Gans</h4><p begin="00:53:03.88" data-timestamp="3183.88"> And I was so relieved. I was relieved beyond words to see the shoe sitting there. I took a picture of it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:53:13.61" data-timestamp="3193.61"> So many things were put right in that moment. It was confirmation that objects do not suddenly stop existing and disappear. And also, the world was suddenly repopulated in her mind with people who were generally kind and helpful.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ruby Gans</h4><p begin="00:53:27.78" data-timestamp="3207.78"> It was really nice to swing from thinking that a stranger is trying to break me psychologically to, oh, a stranger picked this up for me.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:53:38.28" data-timestamp="3218.28"> It was still a little crazy-making that the first missing single shoe was unaccounted for. But then a few days later, she was walking her parents' dog, and it jogged her memory. She'd parked in that very spot she was walking the day she had lost the shoe. It was after the run, but before she went home. She'd just forgotten. And there it was, beside the road, the gray and white Adidas. She was back in the world she had started in.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:54:04.27" data-timestamp="3244.27"> It's interesting how quickly you went to seriously considering the least likely things, you know?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ruby Gans</h4><p begin="00:54:10.09" data-timestamp="3250.09"> Yeah. I immediately started thinking about all the crazy-- all the things that people believe and I think are crazy. I'm like, pfft, that could never happen to me. I'm way too smart and logical.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:54:22.87" data-timestamp="3262.87"> Actually, it was logic, a kind of logic that led you there, right?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Ruby Gans</h4><p begin="00:54:25.91" data-timestamp="3265.91"> Yeah. Maybe I would have thought that I was somehow superior or just-- I don't know. Turns out, I'm not.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Kestenbaum</h4><p begin="00:54:36.25" data-timestamp="3276.25"> If you are listening to this and feeling a little disappointed, like you were promised a plot twist, something exciting and more dramatic than a story about a woman who had just lost two shoes in a row, because that is what you come to this show for, fair enough. But I am just holding up a mirror to the real world, where sometimes a lost shoe is just a lost shoe.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Aviva DeKornfeld</h4><p begin="00:55:05.18" data-timestamp="3305.18"> David Kestenbaum is a senior editor at our show.</p><p begin="00:55:09.21" data-timestamp="3309.21"></p><p begin="00:55:12.21" data-timestamp="3312.21"></p></div>  </div>
