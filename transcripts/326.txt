  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:05.27" data-timestamp="5.27">Bob Harris has won two cars and a lot of money playing <i>Jeopardy</i> on television. But he insists he's not that smart. What he is, he says, is somebody who decided when he was going to be on Jeopardy to take measures.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bob Harris</h4><p begin="00:00:17.48" data-timestamp="17.48">I told my girlfriend at the time, hi. I'm rearranging the furniture now. And I need to make my house look like the <i>Jeopardy</i> studio. I set up low bookcases to be sort of like where the podiums were, about the same height. And I would stand there. I had a little homemade buzzer that I made out of a ballpoint pen and some masking tape.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:39.94" data-timestamp="39.94">He got bright halogen lights. And he pumped up the air conditioning to make it feel like the inside of a TV studio. He placed a big picture of <i>Jeopardy's</i> host, Alex Trebek, in about the spot where the real Alex Trebek stands.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bob Harris</h4><p begin="00:00:50.68" data-timestamp="50.68">I even tried to time my meals to when I would be eating at <i>Jeopardy,</i> so that my whole body clock would be exactly in harmony.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:08.16" data-timestamp="68.16">So he played along with videotapes of the TV show. And he studied for months, memorizing all kinds of stuff, the guys who ran the United Nations in order, the presidents in order, rivers, the elements, British kings, the novels of E. M. Forster-- which include, by the way, <i>Room</i> <i>With</i> a <i>View,</i> <i>Where</i> <i>Angels</i> <i>Fear</i> to <i>Tread,</i> <i>Howards</i> <i>End.</i></p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bob Harris</h4><p begin="00:01:26.19" data-timestamp="86.19">The way to remember a lot of stuff is to build really big, goofy images. OK, so you've got this nice, really big, like-- a friend of mine in Ohio grew up in this big mansion, so it's actually his living room in my head. So OK, it's a room with a beautiful view.</p><p begin="00:01:49.84" data-timestamp="109.84">And <i>Howards</i> <i>End</i> was the next one on the list that I just happened to have in front of me. And so <i>Howards</i> <i>End</i>, and you've got <i>A</i> <i>Room</i> <i>With</i> <i>a</i> <i>View</i>. I mean, how can you not have, like, Howard-- I have a friend named Howard. And so I made a 30-foot buttocks and stuck it in the window. So you have a room with a view of Howard's end where angels fear to tread. So the angels are in this room, and there's this giant 30-foot buttocks in the window.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:11.86" data-timestamp="131.86">That's why the angels fear to tread there.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bob Harris</h4><p begin="00:02:13.79" data-timestamp="133.79">Who really wants to be there, really?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:16.25" data-timestamp="136.25">All I can say is, you do not want to know how he got <i>Passage</i> <i>to</i> <i>India</i> in there. Bob says that a third of the answers that he gave on television-- a third-- came from cramming information into his head with these weird pictures that he would create for himself. And one of his biggest wins came when-- thanks to brute memorization-- he knew the name of an old book called <i>The</i> <i>Compleat</i> <i>Angler,</i> which to this day he has never read, has only the vaguest idea what it's about. And one of his biggest losses came when he could not remember the poem, &quot;Jabberwocky.&quot;</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bob Harris</h4><p begin="00:02:43.84" data-timestamp="163.84">And I was particularly frustrated because this was my father's favorite poem. And so Brillig became my nickname, for some people.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:56.87" data-timestamp="176.87">I wonder if the reason why you didn't remember it was because it wasn't part of your <i>Jeopardy</i> knowledge, but because it was part of your real knowledge.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bob Harris</h4><p begin="00:03:02.77" data-timestamp="182.77">I think that's exactly right. I think there are a lot of times in the game-- most of the big mistakes I ever made were like that, where it wasn't in my <i>Jeopardy</i> notebooks, my study materials, the almanacs.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:15.76" data-timestamp="195.76">It's funny. It's sort of like it was in the wrong section of your brain.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Bob Harris</h4><p begin="00:03:18.37" data-timestamp="198.37">Yeah, exactly. It was in my real brain, the one I walk around with that doesn't have much in it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:22.98" data-timestamp="202.98">Talking to Bob about all this, you realize that a show like <i>Jeopardy,</i> a quiz show, any quiz show on the surface seems like it's about facts, retrieving facts to win prizes. But what's really going on for the players is totally different than just retrieving facts. There's a whole inner world happening, an inner world that is not on TV at all, an inner world that actually may be a lot more interesting than what's in the program that's on TV.</p><p begin="00:03:45.56" data-timestamp="225.56">Well today, we dive in to look at that inner world, at the secret life of quiz shows. From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i>, distributed by Public Radio International. I'm Ira Glass. Our program today in three rounds. Round One, Gamester of Ireland is Fine. The round is about somebody who goes on to <i>Who</i> <i>Wants</i> <i>to</i> <i>Be</i> <i>a</i> <i>Millionaire</i>, and the most amazing thing he comes up with is not any fact, not any answer, it is the sheer courage to appear at all.</p><p begin="00:04:12.44" data-timestamp="252.44">Round Two, Dire Enigmas for Elite Fans, where we visit with some of the best quiz masters anywhere on an all-weekend, all-day, all-night quiz bender.</p><p begin="00:04:23.22" data-timestamp="263.22">Round Three, Girls in Need of a Safer Time. By the way, if you are noticing anything special about these round names, five points for you as you play along at home. Anyway, round three is an attempt to use quiz shows-- yes, quiz shows-- to change teenage girls. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>

  <h3>Act One: Gamester Of Ireland Is Fine</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:47.73" data-timestamp="287.73">Act One, Gamester of Ireland is Fine. So Ronan got onto his bike with his recording gear, and he bicycled over to the guy's house.</p><p begin="00:05:11.90" data-timestamp="311.9">When he got there, he figured that the first thing they would do is watch the video of the guy on the quiz show. What you're about to hear is the story that Ronan put together for Irish radio about this guy. The guy is named Roger Dowds.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ronan Kelly</h4><p begin="00:05:29.30" data-timestamp="329.3">When I got to Roger's house to watch the tape of his appearance, he didn't have a VCR. They made a big issue about waving and smiling and looking as happy as possible.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Gay Byrne</h4><p begin="00:05:48.22" data-timestamp="348.22">[UNINTELLIGIBLE] from Dublin.</p><p begin="00:05:49.41" data-timestamp="349.41">[GAME SHOW MUSIC]</p><p begin="00:05:53.84" data-timestamp="353.84">And it's Fastest Finger first. Stand by your keypads, please.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ronan Kelly</h4><p begin="00:06:03.69" data-timestamp="363.69">He was a contestant on the quiz show, <i>Who</i> <i>Wants</i> <i>to</i> <i>Be</i> <i>a</i> <i>Millionaire</i>. I should have put the word &quot;unlikely&quot; in there. Roger was an unlikely contestant, and you'll find out why as the program unfolds.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Gay Byrne</h4><p begin="00:06:16.87" data-timestamp="376.87">Silence in the studio, please.
