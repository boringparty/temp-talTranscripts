The email was signed by someone calling herself Diana, Hunter of Bus Drivers.</p><p begin="00:44:46.98" data-timestamp="2686.98">This year marks the 20th anniversary of the first disappearances of young women in Juarez, or at least when people started recording these disappearances and considering them as being related, after a 13-year-old girl was kidnapped and then found dead with signs of rape and strangulation. After that, the number of women who were killed just kept growing. It went from dozens of reported murders in a year to hundreds-- so many women, it was hard to count accurately.</p><p begin="00:45:20.58" data-timestamp="2720.58">In 2010, the number peaked. At least 304 women were murdered that year. The circumstances of the crimes were eerily similar. One day, a girl would disappear on the way to work or on the way home. And if there was any news about her, it would be when her body was founded in the desert or an abandoned lot, often with traces of rape and torture. Sometimes they were found together in mass graves.</p><p begin="00:45:52.57" data-timestamp="2752.57">Oscar Maynez, who worked some of these cases as a criminologist, explained to me the way the authorities became complicit in the murders. First, they deny the problem, he said. Then they played it down. And finally they blamed the victims' lifestyle and their families.</p><p begin="00:46:10.87" data-timestamp="2770.87">The criminologist resigned, he says, after realizing his superiors were more interested in covering up investigations and tampering with evidence than in finding justice. So when I heard about the case of this woman, this Diana, who supposedly was trying to do something by herself, I wondered, after all these years without justice, if women in Juarez would look at Diana and say, it's about time. So I went down there to ask them.</p><p begin="00:46:47.51" data-timestamp="2807.51">As you can imagine, most of the women I approached to get their opinions were reluctant to speak to a stranger about the woman who'd murdered bus drivers. "I know nothing about it," said a woman with a small child on the bus, only to fix her eyes on the back of the seat afterwards and not glance in my direction again. "I have no opinion about that," another one said before quickly entering a mall.</p><p begin="00:47:11.82" data-timestamp="2831.82">But also one day I got this answer. "When I heard about what she did, I said, how great that someone's doing what many of us should have done."</p><p begin="00:47:22.95" data-timestamp="2842.95">This was Laura, 25 years old, pregnant with her second child. When I asked her if you were to run into Diana on the street, what would you say to her if you knew it was her? She said, "without a doubt, I would congratulate her."</p><p begin="00:47:39.27" data-timestamp="2859.27">There is a sense among the general population that buses are a bad place to be by yourself. Just this week, a bus driver was arrested for allegedly raping a girl on her way to school.</p><p begin="00:47:51.13" data-timestamp="2871.13">About this Laura said, "I remember when I was in high school, I would hear a lot about it. My friends would say to me, if you're going on the route and no one's there, take a pen with you with the point facing outward, because you never know.</p><p begin="00:48:07.23" data-timestamp="2887.23">That's been happening for years-- years. And it's the same for the ladies working in the maquilas."</p><p begin="00:48:17.57" data-timestamp="2897.57">There's no evidence that the bus drivers who were killed by the blond woman had actually committed a crime at all. But Laura didn't seem concerned about that. In a city where justice rarely brings results, maybe at some point you settle to get the justice available to you.</p><p begin="00:48:35.84" data-timestamp="2915.84">Just a couple of weeks before the Diana the hunter case, Laura's younger sister, Maria Alejandra, 20 years old, suffered a home invasion. A man came in through her bedroom window in the night armed with a gun and tried to rape her.</p><p begin="00:48:51.32" data-timestamp="2931.32">She recognized him. He lived in the neighborhood. After the man left her house, Maria Alejandra told her sister what happened and that she was going to report it to the police. Laura told her not to do that. What for? They knew how the police work.</p><p begin="00:49:08.05" data-timestamp="2948.05">Laura told me, "I told her not to go, not to expose herself, because they would ask questions and check her. I think between the police, they've created a sort of code. So you basically are putting yourself out there to suffer one humiliation after another.</p><p begin="00:49:29.95" data-timestamp="2969.95">"My father was with us. He wanted to go kill him. And my husband too, and me too.</p><p begin="00:49:36.57" data-timestamp="2976.57">"I said, I'll kill him. I said I'll go and I'll kill him. Since we know who it is, we are going to go and do it with our own hands."</p><p begin="00:49:51.73" data-timestamp="2991.73">But despite Laura's pleading, Maria Alejandra, after talking to her friends, decided to go to the police anyway. And things went down exactly as Laura predicted. Even though the doctors had said that there was evidence of sexual assault, Maria Alejandra says the police questioned her as if she was making it all up.</p><p begin="00:50:11.46" data-timestamp="3011.46">Why didn't she have bruises? What took her so long to report the intrusion? "I would have to almost be dying for them to investigate," Maria Alejandra says they told her.</p><p begin="00:50:22.58" data-timestamp="3022.58">Laura was angry. Now the opportunity for revenge was gone. "I said to her, I told you. We should have done something before.</p><p begin="00:50:31.53" data-timestamp="3031.53">"Because now if we took action, they would know who did it. Because that's how justice works here. The victims are the ones that are in jail."</p><p begin="00:50:49.64" data-timestamp="3049.64">In 2001, police did apprehend a bus driver, Victor Garcia Uribe. He was arrested with a colleague and accused of murdering eight women who were left in an old cotton field. He denied any involvement in the killings, but the police kept questioning him.</p><p begin="00:51:08.78" data-timestamp="3068.78">Finally, after long hours of interrogation, he confessed to taking part in those murders and in the murders of three other women. Yes, he said, he enjoyed killing them. Yes, he said, they did it under the influence of marijuana, cocaine, and alcohol. Yes, they loved to rape them and kill them, and then they would throw them afterwards in an empty field.</p><p begin="00:51:33.41" data-timestamp="3093.41">But two days later in court, Garcia Uribe did a complete 180. Again he denied that he was involved in the murders. He said he only confessed because officers had kidnapped him, taken him to a police academy, and tortured him until he did. He said they'd beaten him and burned him with cigarettes.</p><p begin="00:51:53.88" data-timestamp="3113.88">Still, the judge convicted him and sentenced him to 50 years. Garcia Uribe was freed in 2005, after it was proven that the authorities had tortured him into confessing.</p><p begin="00:52:13.44" data-timestamp="3133.44">"What makes me laugh," Laura told me, "is how the bus drivers are so scared. That's what really makes me laugh.</p><p begin="00:52:21.18" data-timestamp="3141.18">I didn't see this myself, but Maria Alejandra says she's seen bus drivers who posted a sketch of Diana near their dashboards. And when a woman who looks like her tries to board the bus, they don't let her. She says they close the doors quickly now. And she's heard that some are carrying knives on them."</p><p begin="00:52:39.29" data-timestamp="3159.29">"You know what it is?" Laura said. "In this country, people don't have balls. That's why my dad has always said," Laura told me, "we don't have balls," he says. "Because if we had a little bit more balls, many things wouldn't be happening.</p><p begin="00:52:56.77" data-timestamp="3176.77">We don't express what we think. We don't express how we feel. And we don't act in accordance with what we think. And that's our problem. That's what we are lacking."</p><p begin="00:53:07.87" data-timestamp="3187.87">So I asked her, but wait, weren't you on the verge of taking things into your own hands with your sister? "Yeah, but I'm not the kind of person who would do it. Sure, in the moment I reacted and I thought about it.</p><p begin="00:53:24.59" data-timestamp="3204.59">But I would never do it. I would never try to kill anyone. It's not how I was brought up. I'm not like that."</p><p begin="00:53:38.73" data-timestamp="3218.73">While I was in Juarez, I spent more time riding the buses on route 4 than doing anything else. These buses, the Juarez public transportation buses, are old school buses imported from the US. They are noisy old buses, reasonably clean, but in a state of decay that everyone accepts.</p><p begin="00:53:59.81" data-timestamp="3239.81">I rode in the back of the bus and in the front of the bus. I rode it at the time of the killings, in the morning, and late at night, when the last bus was about to finish the route. Sitting there, I wondered if the guy driving my bus was scared at all, nervous. I wonder if in moments on his route, he was thinking about Diana the Hunter, and the words she said to the second bus driver on the morning of August the 29th.</p><p begin="00:54:28.01" data-timestamp="3268.01">[SPEAKING SPANISH]. "You all think you are so tough." Right before shooting the driver twice in the head and running away.</p><p begin="00:54:38.66" data-timestamp="3278.66">She hasn't been spotted since. And there's no proof that the email sent by Diana actually came from the woman who did the shootings.</p><p begin="00:54:47.49" data-timestamp="3287.49">Whoever chose Diana's name chose well. Diana the Hunter is the goddess of women and childbirth, who, like many other Roman gods, acts out of basic human feelings, like rage and revenge.</p><p begin="00:55:03.69" data-timestamp="3303.69">There is, on one of Mexico City's most famous streets, a statue of Diana the Hunter. And there's a replica of it outside a restaurant in Juarez. She's muscular, strong, holding a bow with her arms stretched back, about to shoot. Maybe that image was in this one driver's mind on a bus I was riding when he opened the door and a woman came in and he said, "What? Are you Diana the Hunter?"</p><p begin="00:55:35.08" data-timestamp="3335.08">"No, of course not," she replied. "What? Are you afraid of me now?" "Well yeah," he replied. "Shouldn't I be?"</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:55:51.72" data-timestamp="3351.72"> Yuri Herrera. His novel <i>Signs</i> <i>Leading</i> <i>Up to</i> <i>the</i> <i>End of the</i> <i>World</i> is available in Spanish right now. It's going to come out in English next year.</p><p begin="00:55:59.44" data-timestamp="3359.44">We commissioned a piece of artwork of Diana the Hunter on the bus to go with his story from this artist named Alice Leora Briggs. She's been doing these incredible drawings of scenes from Juarez. You can see this piece of artwork at our website. We also have a link to her other drawings at thisamericanlife.org.</p></div>  </div>
