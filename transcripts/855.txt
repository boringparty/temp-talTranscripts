  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:11.78" data-timestamp="11.78"> Kasey is autistic, and she says it's puzzling, neurotypical people and how much they lie. And she's not alone.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:00:18.60" data-timestamp="18.6"> Yeah. In our support groups, that issue comes up a lot. For some people, it's very puzzling, and they just don't understand the concept. And especially because so often, lies are just completely transparent.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:37.55" data-timestamp="37.55"> She gave me this example. When she worked in HR, they caught this guy who was having an inappropriate relationship with his administrative assistant. A naked picture of her was on his work computer. And still, he denied it, kept lying.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:00:52.19" data-timestamp="52.19"> It's just baffling to me. It's just-- it's inexplicable. I don't understand the continuing in the lie. And I don't understand why they haven't learned at an earlier point that it's not productive, that this is not an effective tool for you.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:13.54" data-timestamp="73.54"> Looking around on Reddit, we found a lot of autistic people writing about this exact thing. Here's somebody who posted saying, "I recently realized that a lot of things I had always categorized as lies are not seen that way by NT people--" neurotypical people.</p><p begin="00:01:26.72" data-timestamp="86.72">"Like, they say it, knowing it isn't literally true, but they don't think of it as a lie because they don't expect others to believe it. For example, here are some things that I always thought were weird, inexplicable lies--" and then there's a list. "It was great to see you. Let's do this again soon. I hope you have a great holiday. You are so funny. I love your hairdo. Where did you buy that dress? I need to get one, too. Oh, wow, that's very interesting. See you later."</p><p begin="00:01:51.07" data-timestamp="111.07">They continue-- "I've decided to start translating a lot of NT chatter from its literal meaning into a simple form of, 'Hello, I want you to see me as friendly so I am making friendly noises.'"</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:03.57" data-timestamp="123.57"> Do--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:02:04.14" data-timestamp="124.14"> Yes. [LAUGHS]</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:05.58" data-timestamp="125.58"> Do you relate?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:02:07.02" data-timestamp="127.02"> I do. The white lies and the polite fictions and the pleasantries that go along with small talk, a lot of autistic people really do perceive that as lying. For me, I recognize that it's a cultural structure, rather than an intent to deceive.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:30.37" data-timestamp="150.37"> And does that make it any better?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:02:32.43" data-timestamp="152.43"> Absolutely.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:34.05" data-timestamp="154.05"> She remembers when she realized just how widespread lying is for neurotypical people. She was a teenager, and she says she was heavyset, from a heavyset family.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:02:43.23" data-timestamp="163.23"> That's pretty normal to me. I am not offended by or afraid of the word "fat." But a lot of the people that were my friends were very afraid of that word. And so they would say to me, oh, you're not fat. And for me, that was just baffling.</p><p begin="00:03:09.11" data-timestamp="189.11">I understood that they were trying to be kind, but I couldn't fathom how they thought that would actually be believed or helpful. I mean, it's a demonstrable fact. [LAUGHS] I have a mirror. I know what I look like. It started to make me clue in to this idea of white lies and polite fiction.</p><p begin="00:03:40.94" data-timestamp="220.94">And then with the teenage politics, you start to see people who-- oh, I'm so happy to see you. Awesome. Let's hang out. And then behind the person's back-- oh, my god, I can't stand her. She is just the worst. So I started to catch on that this was not just widespread, but that this was considered appropriate behavior.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:06.60" data-timestamp="246.6"> Now, of course she's used to it. When I talked to her, she was just about to go to a conference where she knew people who barely remember her, who'd be saying, so great to see you, and not mean a word of it. And she's OK with that. She ignores it, moves on. But she tries to keep things more strictly truthful.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:25.32" data-timestamp="265.32"> So you never lie?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:04:29.07" data-timestamp="269.07"> I won't say never. I think of myself as sort of practicing radical honesty with tact. So I do my best to tell the truth in all circumstances.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:46.39" data-timestamp="286.39"> I have to say, if that's your philosophy, I find it so interesting to think about, what are the very few examples where you do let yourself lie, where you feel like that's the right thing to do? What are those?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:04:59.40" data-timestamp="299.4"> So from a moral and scriptural basis, one is justified to lie to protect others from individuals who mean to do them harm. So, for example, there was someone in my life who was in a domestic violence situation, and I helped her to get to a safe place.</p><p begin="00:05:26.61" data-timestamp="326.61">And when her husband called, I said, I have no idea where she is. It's quite simply a lie, but it is a lie that is fully justified because it is information to which he is not entitled for the protection of life and limb of myself or another person.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:46.61" data-timestamp="346.61"> That is obviously a very hard example to argue against. She told me another one where her dog pooped all over her car, and she was late to a meeting. And when she got there, she did not tell the truth about why. She didn't want to gross anybody out. Also, none of their business.</p><p begin="00:06:02.30" data-timestamp="362.3">Otherwise, she almost always picks honesty. When kids picked on her nieces about their weight, they came to her crying and asked, am I fat? And she says, it was really hard not to say the kinds of lies that people said to her when she was their age. But she didn't. She said, let's talk about your body and being fat. There's nothing wrong with being fat. Honesty, she says, is the only way to vulnerability and intimacy, which, of course.</p><p begin="00:06:32.54" data-timestamp="392.54">I was very curious how she does not lie at work. I definitely do most of my lying on the job. Not here, on the air, of course, where everything I say is deeply, thoroughly fact-checked, but just around the office, just white lies. I don't understand how you get by without a little pretending now and then in a workplace. I don't actually understand how you would get things done. Kasey has none of that.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:01.68" data-timestamp="421.68"> OK, let me ask you about a lie that I tell all the time at work. OK? At the end of pretty much any interview I ever do, I thank the person, and I tell them how great they were, even if they were not great, even if they were not good talkers, even if they were not able to describe the thing that we'd hoped that they would describe.</p><p begin="00:07:22.87" data-timestamp="442.87">That is what I say because it seems to me to be such a vulnerable thing to ask people to come and talk in an interview, and they don't know how it's going to go. And it's just kind of a nerve-racking thing that it seems just kind to say, you did a good job.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:07:40.32" data-timestamp="460.32"> I think that most of the time, if the person you're speaking to didn't do well, that they're going to know it. And so the polite fiction is not going to reassure them. So what is the honest thing you could say in that situation? The honest thing is, coming to do this, to have these conversations and be open and vulnerable is a big thing. And I really appreciate that you did it and that you made the effort. Thank you for that. That's honest.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:08:14.26" data-timestamp="494.26"> Hmm. I have to say, that's really good.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:08:22.13" data-timestamp="502.13"> Thank you. It's honest, and it acknowledges them.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:08:26.13" data-timestamp="506.13"> I wasn't expecting you to really say something so actually useful.</p><p begin="00:08:30.66" data-timestamp="510.66">[LAUGHTER]</p><p begin="00:08:33.05" data-timestamp="513.05">I'll do you the favor of being honest about that.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:08:35.97" data-timestamp="515.97"> I appreciate it.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:08:42.59" data-timestamp="522.59"> One of the reasons why I wanted to talk to you today is that we're doing a whole episode of our show about inexplicable lies, lies that you just think, why lie about that? In your experience, what percentage of lies are unnecessary lies?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Kasey</h4><p begin="00:09:02.94" data-timestamp="542.94"> Can I say 100%? I really don't think, except in extreme circumstances, that Anne Frank is hidden in my attic situation, I don't think that lying is necessary. <div class="act" id="act3">
  <h3>Act Three: In Defense of Unnecessary Lies</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:50:59.89" data-timestamp="3059.89"> Act Three, "In Defense of Unnecessary Lies." Well, it's been nearly a whole hour talking about far-fetched lies that do not seem to make the world a better place at all. Our tone, I'll admit, has been skeptical, sometimes incredulous. In this act, we turn that around. I present one of our coworkers here at the radio show, Ike Sriskandaraja.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:51:23.85" data-timestamp="3083.85"> Sometimes in my own life, just for fun, I'll make up something and tell people. Here's one from years ago. You remember Karl Rove, the guy who helped get George W. Bush elected to be governor and then president? I told my friend Charles that this man, well into his 60s, was really only 36. He just looked old.</p><p begin="00:51:49.50" data-timestamp="3109.5">I guess I liked the world where things aren't quite as they appear, and the people running the show are more like children in grown-up suits. And Charles, he went with it, so hard that years later, he came back and told me he'd told dozens of people before catching the lie, which, to be honest, made me feel great and enjoy the lie even more that strangers I never met got to step into this funny little warped reality.</p><p begin="00:52:20.63" data-timestamp="3140.63">But sometimes these lies of amusement don't work out so well. Like, for instance, when my dad came to visit me years ago and was staying over in my small apartment, he noticed a diploma on my desk ordaining me as a minister.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dad</h4><p begin="00:52:37.70" data-timestamp="3157.7"> I still remember that very well. It was a certificate, some kind of a certification.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:52:44.13" data-timestamp="3164.13"> My dad.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dad</h4><p begin="00:52:45.18" data-timestamp="3165.18"> My first impression was you had changed your religion from Hinduism to some other unknown cult.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:52:53.84" data-timestamp="3173.84"> It would have been easy to correct this misunderstanding and tell my dad the real story-- that my friends had asked me to officiate their wedding. And to legally marry them, I had to get ordained in an online church. It just seemed funnier to go along with the story that I was now a minister in a Christian cult.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dad</h4><p begin="00:53:13.43" data-timestamp="3193.43"> It came as a surprise to me because for so many generations, we have been following this religion. So I was a little disappointed. But things happen.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mom</h4><p begin="00:53:26.74" data-timestamp="3206.74"> As soon as he came home, he told me.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:53:29.69" data-timestamp="3209.69"> My mom.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mom</h4><p begin="00:53:30.71" data-timestamp="3210.71"> I thought it was odd because you would have told me about it if you were doing something like this. So it came as a huge surprise to me, too.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:53:40.51" data-timestamp="3220.51"> While my dad met my fake conversion story with resigned disappointment and no follow-up questions, my mom just called me.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mom</h4><p begin="00:53:49.79" data-timestamp="3229.79"> So when we spoke the next time, I brought it up, and I asked you about it. Then you started laughing. Then I knew it was just a joke. Then I had to tell Dad.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dad</h4><p begin="00:54:01.79" data-timestamp="3241.79"> I was relieved. I didn't have to think about it anymore.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:54:06.40" data-timestamp="3246.4"> Did you think it was a good joke?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dad</h4><p begin="00:54:09.23" data-timestamp="3249.23"> Yes. It's an excellent joke.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:54:13.64" data-timestamp="3253.64"> Is that a lie?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dad</h4><p begin="00:54:15.62" data-timestamp="3255.62"> It's a white lie.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:54:18.53" data-timestamp="3258.53"> Honestly, this all surprised me that my dad couldn't tell I was joking, mostly because I learned this type of joke from him.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dad</h4><p begin="00:54:27.36" data-timestamp="3267.36"> What did you say?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:54:28.26" data-timestamp="3268.26"> I've just been doing your joke, the joke you make all the time where you lie about something.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dad</h4><p begin="00:54:35.16" data-timestamp="3275.16"> I didn't know that. [LAUGHS]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mom</h4><p begin="00:54:37.58" data-timestamp="3277.58"> He does lie a lot, yes.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:54:39.77" data-timestamp="3279.77"> My dad tells people he came to America by going through a tunnel under the wall. Other times, it's a boat. When I was a kid, he told me the stretch marks on his shoulders were from a fight he had with a tiger. I told everybody.</p><p begin="00:54:57.44" data-timestamp="3297.44">But my favorite lie he told happened during a math class my dad used to teach at Madison Area Technical College. My good friend Buskus was taking it, and I decided to go with him. Now, before the lesson started, my dad introduced me to the class.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dad</h4><p begin="00:55:13.09" data-timestamp="3313.09"> I do remember that. Although you didn't look like a Nicaraguan.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:55:23.47" data-timestamp="3323.47"> Why did you say that?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Dad</h4><p begin="00:55:25.91" data-timestamp="3325.91"> Because we had students from Central American countries. I think that was the thing that took me to wanting you to stop.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:57:57.35" data-timestamp="3477.35"> Emma compared it to this thing I did when I was 26 and had just moved into my own apartment, when I used to use my kitchen drawers for non-kitchen items, which is not exactly a lie, but felt like waking up on the set of a surrealist play.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Emma</h4><p begin="00:58:14.65" data-timestamp="3494.65"> It's just confusing. [CHUCKLES] And this was when we first started dating or maybe even before. And I was looking for something in your kitchen. You had maybe, like, two forks and two spoons. And I was probably looking for one of these forks or something. And I opened a drawer, like a logical place to have silverware, and instead found either boxers or DVDs, like <i>Seinfeld</i> box set. And it was funny, but it was also maddening.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:58:49.03" data-timestamp="3529.03"> Emma didn't want to live in Wonderland. It's too crazy, too hard to find the forks. If we were going to make plans together for the rest of our lives, she wanted straight answers. So I stopped lying to her. For years, I packed this part of me away, in the dresser I ended up buying for my underwear. But now we have two kids, and I've brought lying back into our house, especially with our oldest.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Emma</h4><p begin="00:59:17.31" data-timestamp="3557.31"> Just recently, like a day ago-- what did you say? You looked at his foot, and you were like-- you were like, huh, where did you get all these toes? Why do you have 10 toes? Most people only have six. And now he's four and wise to you. And he was like, what? What? He's like, you're joking.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ike Sriskandaraja</h4><p begin="00:59:43.29" data-timestamp="3583.29"> Leroy gets it. So far, he hasn't asked me to stop yet. Maybe you would like to try this in your own house. In fact, maybe it's a valuable lesson for your kids. It trains them to determine fact from fiction. It seems useful in our world.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="01:00:08.11" data-timestamp="3608.11"> Ike Sriskandaraja is one of the producers of our show.</p><p begin="01:00:11.50" data-timestamp="3611.5">By the way, his friend Charles, who Ike lied to about Karl Rove, Charles told me recently that he never thought that that was true.
