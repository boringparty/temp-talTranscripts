    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.36" data-timestamp="0.36"> Every Monday, John, and Thax, and Ericka get together at John's apartment to watch <i>Dark</i> <i>Shadows</i>. You know, <i>Dark</i> <i>Shadows</i>, surely you've heard of it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:00:09.03" data-timestamp="9.03"> It was the most popular thing in the whole world, at one point.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:12.12" data-timestamp="12.12"> No.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:00:13.42" data-timestamp="13.42"> It was.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:15.27" data-timestamp="15.27"> John used to watch four or five hours of <i>Dark</i> <i>Shadows</i> every day, back in January when he was unemployed. Now he only watches four hours a week and only with his friends. And, in no way, is as hardcore about it as when he used to watch with his friend Catherine.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:00:28.20" data-timestamp="28.2"> Well, when Catherine was  here she insisted that we watch it. And I'm like, you know, I've got to get up for work tomorrow. She's like, please, just one more. She'd be begging. It got really sad.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:49.45" data-timestamp="49.45"> <i>Dark</i> <i>Shadows</i> is slow. The sets are cheap. The writing is bad. The acting is stilted. It's a Gothic horror soap opera, produced five days a week from 1966 to '71. And, of course, it is not scary. It is never scary. Though when John was a kid, he was so scared of <i>Dark</i> <i>Shadows</i>, he'd get scared of the actresses lipstick, even the lipstick could do it.</p><p begin="00:01:12.84" data-timestamp="72.84">From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i>, and it is the special Halloween edition of our program. Today we bring you stories of things that are supposed to be scary but are not. Act one, <i>Dark</i> <i>Shadows</i>. Act two, Scientist in a Haunted House. Act three, Vampire Girl. Act four, Discovering Evil. Act five, Gang Girl. And Act six, Screams. Those are your screams, your screams as left on our voicemail. I'm your ghost, I mean host, Ira Glass. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>

  <h3>Act One: Dark Shadows</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:52.77" data-timestamp="112.77"> What I learned at John's apartment about <i>Dark</i> <i>Shadows</i> is that the main point of it, the main pleasure of it, is watching things mess up. It's like watching car races for the car crashes. If the car crashes came every 10 minutes and nobody got hurt. <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i> producer Nancy Updike and I were in John's apartment for, I don't know, 10 minutes when a werewolf exited a scene, and through the open door, we could all witness this figure. Not a shadowy, mysterious figure from the year 1896, but instead--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:02:24.20" data-timestamp="144.2"> Oh, did you see the prop guy? Did you see the prop guy? Wait. Here look.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:28.08" data-timestamp="148.08"> Oh yeah.</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:02:30.52" data-timestamp="150.52"> A prop guy. There's a lot of rewinding and replaying this video, which John taped off the Sci-Fi Channel. John, and Thax, and Ericka  have watched actors forget lines, they've seen scripts left on beds, they've seen props fall over and smash. Periodically, there's an offstage cough. And what Thax describes as the sound of a giant zipper, mysterious, inexplicable, and not part of any story. When the vampire, Barnabas Collins, enters the foyer of Collinswood Manner, he closes the door behind him, and a moment later it swings open again.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:03:01.40" data-timestamp="181.4"> Oh, there goes the door.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Thax</h4><p begin="00:03:03.54" data-timestamp="183.54"> Yeah, uh huh.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:03:04.92" data-timestamp="184.92"> There's something wrong with the lock on it. And it's always swinging open after somebody closes it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:11.58" data-timestamp="191.58"> This is the kind of moment they live for. Giant tears in the fabric of the show's fantasy. John says that modern TV shows and movies are way too slick. What he likes are things that are either old, or imperfect, or both. His house is filled with huge sofa-like paintings that he's picked up at garage sales, and recordings where you can hear things fall over in the studio. A fast paced, modern film like <i>Independence</i> <i>Day</i> exhausts him with its relentlessly high production values. <i>Dark</i> <i>Shadows</i> is more human scale.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:03:40.58" data-timestamp="220.58"> Like you notice the littlest things you wouldn't notice in a regular show. We noticed tonight that people have stopped knocking on the door three times. They're now knocking on the door four times.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Child In Scene From Dark Shadows</h4><p begin="00:03:50.73" data-timestamp="230.73"> I'll go out the back way.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Woman In Scene From Dark Shadows</h4><p begin="00:03:53.13" data-timestamp="233.13"> No, no you won't. You'll stay right here.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Child In Scene From Dark Shadows</h4><p begin="00:03:55.05" data-timestamp="235.05"> [UNINTELLIGIBLE] they'll make me go back if they find-- [FOUR KNOCKS] [FOUR KNOCKS]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Thax</h4><p begin="00:04:02.25" data-timestamp="242.25"> So John thinks they had a lengthy meeting deciding that you have to have four knocks instead of three, to spice up the plot a little bit. It's more exciting.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:04:11.91" data-timestamp="251.91"> Like the new director said, we've got to get rid of the old ways. Things are going to change around here. No more of this three knock stuff.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:24.17" data-timestamp="264.17"> John is also sort of obsessed with trying to figure out exactly when the episodes were filmed. He claims that there is a point, in the 1969 episodes when, suddenly, all the characters started saying, groovy and freaky. By his calculations, the episodes that we're watching this night, were probably filmed in February 1969. One clue--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:04:43.82" data-timestamp="283.82"> Because in the summer, when it's summer, you can hear the air conditioner running.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Thax</h4><p begin="00:04:47.37" data-timestamp="287.37"> Yeah, and you see the flies.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:04:48.73" data-timestamp="288.73"> And you see the flies. And I've never--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Thax</h4><p begin="00:04:50.76" data-timestamp="290.76"> There's a lot of flies.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:04:51.82" data-timestamp="291.82"> I mean, it's like the studio is next to an alley that had big garbage dumpsters, because there's always flies on the set. It's like the thing that's so amazing. There are scenes where flies are sitting all over people's faces, and they're pretending that they're not there.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:11.37" data-timestamp="311.37"> It's kind of a character study, which actors decide to shoo the flies away, and which ones ignore them and continue bravely through the scenes.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Thax</h4><p begin="00:05:18.78" data-timestamp="318.78"> But the classic fly scene is like, when somebody was doing an exorcism, and the fly just went right into his mouth. So he just went [BLOWING SOUND] like that to get rid of it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:30.42" data-timestamp="330.42"> What's interesting about all this is that not only does it defy the way you're supposed to watch horror films, it defies the way you're supposed to watch drama. They do not suspend disbelief for one second. Instead, they construct elaborate fantasies, not about the characters, but about the actors who play the characters.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>John</h4><p begin="00:05:46.57" data-timestamp="346.57"> There's like a real vulnerability to all the characters. And even people that you don't like on the show, like the Chris Jennings character, he's this horrible actor, and that Beth character, she's like a horrible actress, but after a while you get really attached to them. Because you feel like you could just see her going off stage and going, oh my god. And people going, oh, you did really well today.
