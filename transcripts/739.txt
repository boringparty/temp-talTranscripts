So she never saw Sybil again.</p><p begin="00:44:36.93" data-timestamp="2676.93">Megan remembers when her mom told her they were going to unplug Sybil, how angry she was, and how she begged them not to.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:44:49.83" data-timestamp="2689.83"> If your sister is your whole world, what happens when she's gone?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:44:54.51" data-timestamp="2694.51"> I was born with Sybil. There was always Sybil. And so when she was gone, I felt so weird. I felt-- that's the word. I just felt like, ugh, this is not right.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:45:17.91" data-timestamp="2717.91"> Without Sybil, Megan really didn't know who she was or who she should be. So she started inventing ways to keep Sibyl around.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:45:26.31" data-timestamp="2726.31"> This is sort of where I was like, I have to do something really exceptional and remarkable with my life. I'm responsible for whatever Sybil didn't get to have, so I'll live that life, and I'll live my life. And I'll combine them, and it's going to be like, ultra-Megatron life. All the pressure was on for me to have the most amazing life of all time. I like, had to be the best.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:45:50.86" data-timestamp="2750.86"> Had you been like that before?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:45:52.36" data-timestamp="2752.36"> I don't think so.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:45:53.73" data-timestamp="2753.73"> [LAUGHS]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:45:55.00" data-timestamp="2755"> No, I was pretty messy. Every teacher, when you'd get like, reviewed for like, how Megan was in class, was like, Megan is exceptionally bright, curious, great kid. Not a perfectionist-- sloppy, very undisciplined.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:46:11.41" data-timestamp="2771.41"> She became disciplined. She also started doing all the things Sybil was no longer doing-- the things Sybil loved, like her ballet classes. Sybil had loved ballet. Megan had quit years earlier-- too strict. The leotards were too girly for her, and it bugged her how the teacher would always be telling her to pull her tush in.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:46:32.11" data-timestamp="2792.11"> But after she died, I started taking ballet lessons, and I danced.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:46:38.69" data-timestamp="2798.69"> Hmm.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:46:39.10" data-timestamp="2799.1"> Ballet and jazz, all of it. And I have to tell you, I didn't like it. [LAUGHS] I didn't like it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:46:52.03" data-timestamp="2812.03"> Sybil had written poetry, so Megan started writing poetry, too. That one, she liked, though-- sitting at her desk in their room, composing in her journal the way Sybil had.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:47:02.89" data-timestamp="2822.89"> In my journal, I would write, we're going to make it. We're making it. We are.</p><p begin="00:47:06.94" data-timestamp="2826.94">I wrote as we-- me and Sybil. We're going to get an A on this test, you know? We're going to ace the SATs. We're-- we have to. We have to make it. Making it. We are making it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:47:26.73" data-timestamp="2846.73"> Making it meant if there was any opportunity to achieve, she owed it to Sybil to excel. So a few months after Sybil died, there was this thing at school-- the Presidential Fitness Award, one of those national fitness tests they made lots of kids do back in the '80s that everyone seems to remember with a sense of shame and failure-- how long could you hold the sitting V, dangling in front of your classmates in gym, trying to do a pull-up?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:47:53.07" data-timestamp="2873.07"> I decided, well, I can be anything, and I'm going to get the Presidential Physical Fitness Award. I have to. I started training for it, basically, with my mom. I'm like, I have to practice the standing broad jump because you have to get a certain amount.</p><p begin="00:48:06.39" data-timestamp="2886.39">And there was like, the nine-minute mile. This was like, so hard for me-- like, running, oh my god, a nine-minute mile. And I remember when I was competing for it, I started feeling so tired in my lungs that I started hyperventilating, because I'd put so much pressure on myself, too. And I was hyperventilating, and I started crying.</p><p begin="00:48:27.36" data-timestamp="2907.36">And I remember these two classmates, these boys were helping me off the track. And I'm crying, and I'm letting myself down, and I'm like, letting Sybil down, and I'm not going to get it. And I-- they're like, helping me off the track, and I pushed them away. And I finished like, crying and hyperventilating.</p><p begin="00:48:47.18" data-timestamp="2927.18">This is literally like, the story of my life. I'm not naturally good at it, I'm pretty close to quitting. Somebody's like, you should quit, and then I'm like, actually, let me finish, crying and hyperventilating, and like, try to get what I want. But my name was up on that wall, and I still have the T-shirt from fifth grade.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:49:12.83" data-timestamp="2952.83"> That first year after Sybil died, Megan doesn't remember letting herself cry about Sybil. She didn't talk about her. She felt like if she started crying, she'd never stop. She'd cry so much that she'd end up in a mental hospital.</p><p begin="00:49:26.33" data-timestamp="2966.33">As she got older, she'd get stuck on certain thoughts-- that she should have been the one who died, that Sybil was the better twin. She often thought about killing herself, but thought, what an insult to Sybil. Her parents weren't much help with any of this.</p><p begin="00:49:42.17" data-timestamp="2982.17">They talked about sending her to a therapist, but Megan didn't want to go, and they didn't force her. So they just never talked about any of it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:49:50.54" data-timestamp="2990.54"> Like, there was no, but what does Megan want? But what about Megan? Like-- which is fine. Truly, like, my parents were living like, a full nightmare.</p><p begin="00:50:05.41" data-timestamp="3005.41">My parents were so sad. Everybody was so sad. And it was like, well, I can't be.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:50:15.24" data-timestamp="3015.24"> She made a point of trying to act like everything was fine, to hold it together. And as she got into high school, she got even more intense, doing this thing she sometimes calls double living-- living a life that's not just the best, but twice as good as a normal life, for Sybil.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:50:32.57" data-timestamp="3032.57"> The destination was like, to go to the best college and to have the best life. Making it-- we were going to make it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:50:46.40" data-timestamp="3046.4"> By the time she's an adult, that voice in her head putting pressure on her, it just kind of became Megan's personality, how she thought about herself.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:50:54.95" data-timestamp="3054.95"> Like, I have to get this right. It has to be perfect. It can't be stupid, it can't just be fun. It has to mean something.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:51:01.89" data-timestamp="3061.89"> So is this something you feel like you wouldn't have done if you weren't doing it for Sybil in some way?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:51:08.55" data-timestamp="3068.55"> That's a really hard question to answer because I don't-- like, who am I? Who am I if Sybil hadn't passed away? I-- that's something I would love to know.</p><p begin="00:51:19.52" data-timestamp="3079.52">But I honestly don't know because I don't know if this ambition would have been here in some ways, anyway. Like, I really don't know.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:51:29.53" data-timestamp="3089.53"> Yeah. What do you think Sybil would have-- would think if she like, were seeing you pushing yourself to do things that were painful for you like that, all those years? What do you think she would say?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:51:44.58" data-timestamp="3104.58"> I understand your question, and I don't know why I always have this like, instant reluctance when people ask me, what do you think Sybil would think or say? And other people have asked. And I think I-- I think I get so just-- it's not your fault, it's that I think I instantly get outraged that I have to answer for her.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:52:08.19" data-timestamp="3128.19"> Oh, yeah.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:52:08.88" data-timestamp="3128.88"> I don't like it. Like, I don't like having to imagine. I'm mad that she can't answer you.</p><p begin="00:52:16.87" data-timestamp="3136.87">And I think maybe my reaction is just a little bit nuts. I think it's a normal question. Like, I think it's a really natural question.</p><p begin="00:52:25.70" data-timestamp="3145.7">But to like, try to imagine her reaction to adult Megan-- in a lot of ways, she's frozen for me, at nine. She is. Sybil's nine. She died at nine. Everything else is an invention.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:52:50.27" data-timestamp="3170.27"> It's only recently that Megan's started trying to sort out who she is outside of Sybil. This began a few years ago, when her mom was dying of cancer. Megan was with her the whole time, through hospice, and all those days when her mom was in and out of consciousness, on morphine, in that middle place between living and dying.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Megan Neuringer</h4><p begin="00:53:10.16" data-timestamp="3190.16"> And she, at one point, was calling me Sybil a lot. And I said, at one point, no, mom, I'm Megan. Sybil died. And she corrected me, and she said, no, Megan died. We lost Megan. Sybil stayed.</p><p begin="00:53:32.97" data-timestamp="3212.97">And-- and I remember, you know, I was kind of shocked, but not because it hurt my feelings, but that maybe she's right. Maybe Megan did die, and Sybil did stay. And this whole time, you know, yeah, I'm the dead one.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Lilly Sullivan</h4><p begin="00:54:00.13" data-timestamp="3240.13"> Megan had thought that if she could do the right things, then she might start feeling less dead, but it wasn't working. So just in the past few years, she's started to let go of this project to live for Sybil. It hasn't been easy, but she feels more like herself.</p><p begin="00:54:18.01" data-timestamp="3258.01">She's put down some of that anger she's always had about the injustice of Sybil's death. And that's cleared the way for her to feel more like she did when they were together. It surprised her, she said. She feels close to Sybil again. She loves her.</p><p begin="00:54:38.98" data-timestamp="3278.98"></p></div><div class="host"><h4>Bim Adewunmi</h4><p begin="00:54:44.87" data-timestamp="3284.87"> Lilly Sullivan is one of the producers of our show. Before we go, there's this passage from <i>Housekeeping,</i> a novel about two sisters written by Marilynne Robinson, published in 1980. It nails something about sisters that I just really identify with-- the kind of inadvertent performance sisters sometimes put on that people notice and are drawn to.</p><p begin="00:55:14.60" data-timestamp="3314.6">She writes, "Having a sister or a friend is like sitting at night in a lighted house. Those outside can watch you if they want, but you need not see them. You simply say, here are the perimeters of our attention. If you prowl around under the windows till the crickets go silent, we will pull the shades.</p><p begin="00:55:34.22" data-timestamp="3334.22">If you wish us to suffer your envious curiosity, you must permit us not to notice it. Anyone with one solid human bond is that smug. And it is the smugness, as much as the comfort and safety, that lonely people covet and admire."</p><p begin="00:55:50.12" data-timestamp="3350.12"></p></div>  </div>
