<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: What's That Smell?</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:23.39" data-timestamp="443.39">What's that smell? The way Steve Poizner sees it, he did something admirable, something daring, something unusual. And when I read his account of what he did, he seemed sincere about it too. He's a bit of a corny writer. Though even that, you can kind of forgive him. He's not a professional author.</p><p begin="00:07:38.41" data-timestamp="458.41">At the age of 45, after starting one Silicon Valley company that he sold for $30 million and a second one that sold for $1 billion, Poizner didn't need to work anymore. Now that's really bad news.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:48:52.53" data-timestamp="2932.53">OK, give me another.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Christopher Ketcham</h4><p begin="00:48:54.23" data-timestamp="2934.23">Well, for example, a researcher biologist named Henry Lai at the University of Washington found that after two hours of exposure to microwave frequencies--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:49:04.55" data-timestamp="2944.55">At the level that comes out of a cell phone.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Christopher Ketcham</h4><p begin="00:49:06.19" data-timestamp="2946.19">At the level that comes out of a cell phone, you can have DNA damage. After two hours, it's double-strand breakage in DNA.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:49:13.71" data-timestamp="2953.71">Now so these are the studies that show that we do have reason to worry. Let me just-- So in other words, if there's no funding from telephone companies or people associated with them, 75% of the studies show, yes, the electromagnetic signals out of cell phones actually do affect our cells. They have a biological effect. OK.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Christopher Ketcham</h4><p begin="00:50:11.79" data-timestamp="3011.79">Yes. Now if you pool the studies that are funded by the industry, you find that only 25% of those studies show a biological effect. So there appears to be-- and the argument has been among the scientists who I interviewed-- there appears to be a skewing, a data skewing, that is related to the source of funding.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:50:33.77" data-timestamp="3033.77">Now the EPA used to look into this to see the environmental effect of this kind of radiation. But you're right that it stopped. Talk about why it stopped.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Christopher Ketcham</h4><p begin="00:50:43.89" data-timestamp="3043.89">Well, what happened is in the 1970s, there was serious laboratory research into the biological effects of microwave radiation.
