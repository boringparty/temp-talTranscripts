  <h3>Act One: Does a Bear Hit in the Woods?</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:13.67" data-timestamp="433.67"> Act One: Does a bear hit in the woods? Some of them don't even take much longer, but they're all to one degree or another, more annoying than they used to be, requiring planning, strategy, and a certain enhanced gracefulness.</p><p begin="00:38:30.13" data-timestamp="2310.13">Oral hygiene. Hold the handle of the toothbrush between your teeth the way FDR or Burgess Meredith playing The Penguin bit down on their cigarette holders. Put the toothpaste on the brush, recap the tube, put it away. You really have to keep things tidy, because if they pile up, you'll just be in the soup. Then reverse the brush and put the bristles in your mouth, proceed.</p><p begin="00:38:56.09" data-timestamp="2336.09">Washing your right arm. Soap up your right thigh in the shower, put your foot up on the edge of the tub, and then move your arm over your soapy lower limb back and forth like an old-timey barbershop razor strop.</p><p begin="00:39:11.10" data-timestamp="2351.1">Grating cheese. Get a pot with a looped handle, the heavier the better. This will anchor the bowl that you want the cheese to go into. Put the bowl into the pot.</p><p begin="00:39:22.91" data-timestamp="2362.91">Now take a wooden spoon and feed it through the handle of the grater and the loop of the pot, and then tuck the end down into the waistband of your jeans. Clean underpants are a good idea. Jam yourself up against the kitchen counter and go to town.</p><p begin="00:39:51.10" data-timestamp="2391.1">Special kitchen note: always, always, always have your bum hand safely out of the way, preferably in a sling since you now have a limb that you could literally-- no joke-- cook on the stove without even knowing it. Which makes me feel not like a freak, exactly, but well actually, like a freak.</p><p begin="00:40:18.33" data-timestamp="2418.33">At dinner with friends recently, the conversation turned to what about yourself was still in need of change? They all seem to feel that they were living half-lives.</p><p begin="00:40:29.89" data-timestamp="2429.89">One fellow hoped that he could be more like the god Pan, unabashedly lusty and embracing experience with gusto. Another wanted to feel less disengaged at key moments, able to feel more fully committedly human, and less like that old science fiction B movie trope: "What is this wetness on Triton 3000's face plate?" "Why, space robot, you're crying!"</p><p begin="00:40:59.67" data-timestamp="2459.67">We were going around the table, so the natural progression of things demanded that I eventually get a turn to weigh in as well. Suppose you're out to dinner with a group of triathletes, all discussing their training regimens. And you have no legs.</p><p begin="00:41:19.81" data-timestamp="2479.81">They can't flat-out ignore you, and they also can't say words to the effect of, "well, we all know what <i>your</i> event is. Getting all that marvelous wonderful parking, you lucky thing!"</p><p begin="00:41:33.46" data-timestamp="2493.46">It was uncomfortable, and I suspect more for me than for them. I have no idea. But thanks to my rapidly dividing cells, I no longer have that feeling-- although I remember it very well-- that if I just buckled down to the great work at hand, lived more authentically, stopped procrastinating, cut out sugar, then my best self was just there right around the corner.</p><p begin="00:42:00.76" data-timestamp="2520.76">Yeah, no. I'm done with all that. I'm done with so many things.</p><p begin="00:42:07.06" data-timestamp="2527.06">Like dancing. I've no idea if I can do it anymore. I've been, frankly, too frightened and too embarrassed to try it, even alone in my apartment.</p><p begin="00:42:15.80" data-timestamp="2535.8">There was a time however-- as recently as about a couple of years ago, when I was already one course of radiation and two surgeries into all this nonsense-- when doing this simple barre exercises while holding onto a kitchen chair achieved what they always used to do. What they're supposed to do.</p><p begin="00:42:33.73" data-timestamp="2553.73">As best as I can describe it, it's the gestures themselves, their repetition, their slowness. It all hollows one out. One becomes a reed or a pipe, and the movement and the air pass through and you become this altered, humming, dare I say, beautiful working instrument of placement and form and concentration. But like I said, that's a long time ago. And a version of myself that has long since ceased to exist. Before I became such an observer-- I'm sorry.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:43:15.49" data-timestamp="2595.49"> So at this point, David Rakoff walks away from the microphone. And just when it seems like he might walk off stage, like he quit, he turns, and turns again. And then raises his right knee, and then places that foot down again, and then traces a half circle on the ground with his left foot. And then he lunges, he arches his back, swings his right arm in an arc from low to high, all totally graceful. And then, he dances.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:44:37.73" data-timestamp="2677.73"> Look, mine is not a unique situation. Everybody loses ability-- everybody loses ability as they age. If you're lucky, this happens over the course of a few decades. If you're not--</p><p begin="00:45:02.34" data-timestamp="2702.34">But the story is essentially the same. You go along the road as time and the elements lay waste to your luggage, scattering the contents into the bushes. Until there you are, standing with a battered and empty suitcase that frankly, no one wants to look at anymore. It's just the way it is. But how lovely those moments were, gone now except occasionally in dreams, when one could still turn to someone and promise them something truly worth their while, just by saying "hey, watch this."</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:45:50.65" data-timestamp="2750.65"> David Rakoff is the author of many fine books, most recently <i>Half</i> <i>Empty</i>.   <h3>Act Four: Turn Around Bright Eyes</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:45:57.64" data-timestamp="2757.64"> We have arrived at Act Four: Turn Around, Bright Eyes.</p><p begin="00:46:04.32" data-timestamp="2764.32">In his writing, David Sedaris can be kind of sharp tongued. But when you talk to him, when you meet him, he's really kind of a sweetheart.</p><p begin="00:46:10.33" data-timestamp="2770.33">When my sister Karen met him at a reading, I remember she said to David, she said, "You're a lot nicer than I thought you would be, based on your books." And I remember David said, "Oh, I'm not nice. Just two-faced."</p><p begin="00:46:26.97" data-timestamp="2786.97">So I'm pleased to say that tonight, you're really gonna see a story. You're gonna see David, how he really is tonight in real life, the real guy.</p><p begin="00:46:35.15" data-timestamp="2795.15">And the story that he's telling is about feelings that often go unexpressed, at least unexpressed at the moment they happen. Welcome David Sedaris, please.</p><p begin="00:46:45.83" data-timestamp="2805.83">OK, an important fact about what the audience is seeing right here at this point in the show. David Sedaris takes the stage in full clown makeup. Red nose, white face, bald wig.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Sedaris</h4><p begin="00:47:00.83" data-timestamp="2820.83"> To those who don't travel very often, the Courtyard Marriott might seem like a decent enough hotel. It's clean, sure. And the staff is polite. I wouldn't give you $0.02 for its pillows though, and the tubs are far too shallow for my taste.</p><p begin="00:47:18.30" data-timestamp="2838.3">In the deserted lobby of the one I stayed at not long ago in New Hampshire, there was a coffee bar. Not a Starbucks, but a place that "proudly served Starbucks" and sold it alongside breakfast cereals and prepackaged sandwiches.</p><p begin="00:47:32.35" data-timestamp="2852.35">I noticed it on my way back from lunch, and just as I decided to get a cup of coffee, someone came around the corner and moved in ahead of me. I'd later learn that her name was Mrs. Dunstan, a towering, doe-colored, pyramid of a woman wearing oversized glasses and a short-sleeved linen blazer. Beside her was a man I guessed to be her husband. And after looking at the menu board, she turned to him.</p><p begin="00:47:56.75" data-timestamp="2876.75">"A latte," she said, "now is that the thing that Barbara likes to get? The one with whipped cream or is that called something else?"</p><p begin="00:48:05.57" data-timestamp="2885.57">Oh [BEEP], I thought.</p><p begin="00:48:10.49" data-timestamp="2890.49">"I can do a latte with whipped cream on top", the young woman behind the counter said. She was fair and wore her shoulder-length hair pushed behind her ears. Tiny moles were scattered like buckshot across her face, which was bare but for a bit of eyeliner. "I can do one with flavors too."</p><p begin="00:48:26.83" data-timestamp="2906.83">"Really?" Mrs. Dunstan said, "what sorts of flavors?"</p><p begin="00:48:31.04" data-timestamp="2911.04">In the end, she settled on caramel. Then her husband squinted up at the board, deciding after a good long while that he'd try one of those mocha something or others, and could he get that iced?</p><p begin="00:48:43.66" data-timestamp="2923.66">As I groaned into my palm, he wandered off. His wife, meanwhile, leaned her bulk against the counter and began her genial interrogation. "Are you from this area?" she asked.</p><p begin="00:48:54.71" data-timestamp="2934.71">"No, from Vermont."</p><p begin="00:48:55.95" data-timestamp="2935.95">"Well, that's interesting. What brought you here?"</p><p begin="00:48:59.60" data-timestamp="2939.6">I learned that the coffee person used to work at the town's other hotel, which had recently closed for remodeling.</p><p begin="00:49:05.35" data-timestamp="2945.35">"So after it's done, will you stay put or go back over there?" Mrs. Dunstan asked. "Me, I have a son at the college, so that's what I'm doing. Just checking in. He's my second boy, actually. The first one went here too.</p><p begin="00:49:16.74" data-timestamp="2956.74">"He's not working in his field yet, but with unemployment as high as it is, he should be lucky to have anything at all. If I've told him that once, I've told him 100 times. But of course, being young, he's impatient, which is natural. Wants to set the world on fire and if it can't happen by tomorrow morning at 9:00 AM, then life's just unfair and hardly worth living. What about you, did you go to college?"</p><p begin="00:49:39.28" data-timestamp="2979.28">After what felt like weeks, the young woman finished with the orders. Two cups the size of waste paper baskets were placed upon the counter. And then Mr. Dunstan reappeared and pointed out the plate glass window toward a cluster of grim buildings on the other side of the parking lot.</p><p begin="00:49:56.10" data-timestamp="2996.1">"What are those?" he asked.</p><p begin="00:49:58.58" data-timestamp="2998.58">The young woman said they used to belong to the college. Of course, that was before they expanded the west side of the campus.</p><p begin="00:50:04.21" data-timestamp="3004.21">"And when was that?" Mr. Dunstan asked. He was a good 10 years older than his wife, mid-70s maybe, and he wore a baseball cap with a tattered brim.</p><p begin="00:50:13.55" data-timestamp="3013.55">"I beg your pardon?" the young woman said.</p><p begin="00:50:15.88" data-timestamp="3015.88">"I said uh, when did they expand the west side of the campus? Was it recent, or did they do it a long time ago?"</p><p begin="00:50:23.04" data-timestamp="3023.04">Who the hell cares? I wanted to shout. What are you, the official historian of Who Gives a Damn College? Do you not notice that there's someone in line behind you? Someone who's been standing here, rocking back and forth on his heel for the last 10 minutes while you and that brontosaurus run your stupid mouths about nothing?</p><p begin="00:50:44.96" data-timestamp="3044.96">I was this close to walking away, to marching off in a huff. But then Mrs. Dunstan would have turned to her husband and the girl behind the counter saying, "Some people!"</p><p begin="00:50:56.30" data-timestamp="3056.3">I'd gotten a similar reaction the previous morning when I'd squeezed past a couple standing side by side on the moving walkway connecting concourses A and B. "In a great big hurry to meet that heart attack!" the man called after me. I wanted to remind him that this was an airport, and that some of us had a tight connection, if that was OK.</p><p begin="00:51:22.99" data-timestamp="3082.99">But of course, I had no connection. I just couldn't bear to see him and his wife standing side by side, blocking the way of someone who <i>might</i> have a tight connection.</p><p begin="00:51:36.70" data-timestamp="3096.7">The Dunstans' bill came to $8, which everyone agreed was a lot to pay for two cups of coffee. But they were large ones, and this was a vacation. Sort of.</p><p begin="00:51:53.96" data-timestamp="3113.96">Not like a trip to Florida, but certainly couldn't do that at the drop of a hat. Especially with gas prices the way they are, and looking to go even higher.</p><p begin="00:52:07.00" data-timestamp="3127">While talking, Mrs. Dunstan rummaged through her tremendous purse. Her wallet was eventually located, but then it seemed that the register was locked, so the best solution was to put the coffees on her bill. That's how I discovered her name. And her room number.</p><p begin="00:52:27.26" data-timestamp="3147.26">My only question then was what time I should arrange her wake-up call for. Let's see how chatty you feel at 4:00 AM, I thought. Then it was all about returning the wallet to the purse and getting that all safely zipped up before picking her drink off the counter and starting in on her long goodbye.</p><p begin="00:52:48.59" data-timestamp="3168.59">When the two of them finally lumbered off toward the elevator, I approached the counter, hoping the woman behind it would roll her eyes, acknowledging that something really needed to be done about people like the Dunstans. She didn't, though. So I decided I would hate her as much as I had hated them. When she told me that her little stand didn't serve regular brew coffee, I hated her even more.</p><p begin="00:53:14.49" data-timestamp="3194.49">"I can do you a nice cappuccino," she said. "Or an iced latte, maybe." This last word was delivered to my back as I stormed out the door.</p><p begin="00:53:23.05" data-timestamp="3203.05">Then it was up the street and around the corner to a real coffee place. He's the author, most recently, of <i>Squirrel</i> <i>Seeks</i> <i>Chipmunk:</i> <i>a</i> <i>Modest</i> <i>Bestiary.</i> Again, OK Go.</p><p begin="00:53:58.81" data-timestamp="3238.81">[MUSIC--"DO WHAT YOU WANT" BY  OK GO]</p></div><div class="host"><p begin="00:55:02.65" data-timestamp="3302.65"> Well, our program was produced today by Seth Lind and myself.
