    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:11.88" data-timestamp="11.88"> It may be a little hard to remember now, but when Donald Trump announced he was officially running for president again this time around, he did not seem inevitable-- far from it.</p><p begin="00:00:20.68" data-timestamp="20.68">It was a week after the midterm elections where the Trumpiest candidates lost some key races. Polls showed Governor Ron DeSantis beating him for the Republican nomination. He had very few endorsements from other Republican politicians. Jonathan Karl covered Trump's announcement for ABC News, where he's Chief Washington correspondent.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:00:37.34" data-timestamp="37.34"> I mean, I was at Mar-a-Lago. It was flat.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Donald Trump</h4><p begin="00:00:55.31" data-timestamp="55.31"> But we will bring our supply chains and manufacturing base back home, as we were strongly doing during the Trump administration. And we will systematically--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:01:04.69" data-timestamp="64.69"> People tried to leave the room. I mean, I was there in Mar-a-Lago. I saw people going to the exits halfway through the speech. And at one point, there were so many people leaving that they actually blocked the exits. They told me it was for security reasons. But I don't think he wanted a half-empty Mar-a-Lago ballroom at the conclusion of his presidential announcement.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:26.57" data-timestamp="86.57"> So, not a great beginning. When did it turn around? Jonathan says that began with the speech that Trump gave a few months later, in March 2023, at the annual CPAC conference of conservatives. It was a very different speech with a very different tone.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:01:41.48" data-timestamp="101.48"> It's really dark, and it is filled with talk about getting, essentially, revenge at those who have wronged him.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Donald Trump</h4><p begin="00:01:51.99" data-timestamp="111.99"> Democrat prosecutors-- oh, they're after me for so many things. Oh, those prosecutors. Some are racists. Some hate our country. They all hate me. They'll get me for anything, anything. "You put a comma in this paragraph. Why did you do that?" I don't really know.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:02:10.07" data-timestamp="130.07"> This is the famous speech where he said, "I am your retribution."</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Donald Trump</h4><p begin="00:02:14.43" data-timestamp="134.43"> In 2016, I declared, I am your voice. I am your retribution.</p><p begin="00:02:29.98" data-timestamp="149.98">[CHEERING]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:02:32.32" data-timestamp="152.32"> And that line, obviously, stuck out in the speech itself. But then speaking to Steve Bannon afterwards--</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:38.89" data-timestamp="158.89"> Steve Bannon, former Trump advisor, still apparently talks to him.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:02:42.21" data-timestamp="162.21"> --he said that was the pivotal moment in the campaign that the campaign got its mojo. And he kept on calling it the Come Retribution speech.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:51.55" data-timestamp="171.55"> Come Retribution, Bannon explained to Jonathan, was reference to a book about a supposed plot by the Confederacy to kidnap and maybe kill Abraham Lincoln. The plan was code named Come Retribution because it was revenge.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:03:04.45" data-timestamp="184.45"> I don't think that Bannon is calling for the assassination of Trump's political opponents or anything like that. The location he chose-- Waco, Texas.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:03:44.51" data-timestamp="224.51"> Now, I've been covering presidential campaigns for a long time. Waco, Texas, of course, is the place where the Branch Davidians, David Koresh, had their standoff with federal agents in 1993.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:09.25" data-timestamp="249.25"> This, of course, ended in disaster. The place went up in flames. 75 people died.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:04:14.56" data-timestamp="254.56"> It is the symbol for the far-right of a government that is out of control and is a threat to its own citizens. So here is the place that Donald Trump goes to have his very first rally. When I asked Bannon about it, I was like, come on, Waco? What is going on here? He said, what do you mean? We are the Trump Davidians.</p><p begin="00:04:42.80" data-timestamp="282.8">We laughed, but there was clearly a message being sent. The federal government was out of control. The deep state was out of control when the Branch Davidians were raided, and the result was a bloodbath. Once again, they are out of control. This time they're going after Donald Trump and his MAGA supporters.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:06.97" data-timestamp="306.97"> And what you saw on stage when the rally started, did it seem appropriate to the setting and to that context?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:05:12.26" data-timestamp="312.26"> It was so remarkable. And a lot of people-- people didn't really note it much because we're early in the presidential campaign cycle, I mean long before people were actually going to be voting.</p><p begin="00:05:24.35" data-timestamp="324.35">But it started with Donald Trump coming out on stage silently, as the speakers started blaring the national anthem performed by a group called the J6 Choir, the J6 Choir being people who are in a DC prison for alleged crimes related to January 6, allegedly assaulting police officers, breaking into the Capitol building.</p><p begin="00:05:55.10" data-timestamp="355.1">And Trump, as this is playing out, is standing on stage, hand over his heart. And the scenes on the screens behind Trump are the images of the assault on the US Capitol on January 6. Again, we're in Waco, Texas.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Donald Trump</h4><p begin="00:06:14.65" data-timestamp="374.65"> One nation under God.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>J6 Choir</h4><p begin="00:06:18.34" data-timestamp="378.34"> (SINGING) O say does that star-spangled--</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:21.69" data-timestamp="381.69"> Donald Trump spoke for an hour and a half, and most of it was the kind of stuff you'd expect-- how Joe Biden's running down the country, how great things were when Trump was president. He talked about inflation and China and the fake news media. But over and over, through the speech, and in his speeches since, he lectured about how the government under Joe Biden is out to get you.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Donald Trump</h4><p begin="00:06:42.69" data-timestamp="402.69"> The Biden regime's weaponization of law enforcement against their political opponent is something straight out of the Stalinist Russia horror show. Let there be no doubt the injustices being done, not only to me but to dozens across our country, will not stand. And we're not going to stand for it.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:07:04.35" data-timestamp="424.35"> It's the heart of his speech. It's the core of his message. The government is out of control. It is out to get you. I'm the guy standing in the way. That's why I'm being prosecuted.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Donald Trump</h4><p begin="00:07:17.56" data-timestamp="437.56"> Prosecutorial misconduct is their new tool, and they are willing to use it at levels never seen before.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Jonathan Karl</h4><p begin="00:07:24.70" data-timestamp="444.7"> The impression that people have is that Trump hasn't changed. Yeah, he's outrageous. Yeah, he does controversial stuff. But that's actually not correct. Trump has changed at least the message. This is a very different campaign. This is not the campaign that got him elected to the White House in 2016. And it's not the campaign that he waged in 2020. This is different.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Donald Trump</h4><p begin="00:07:50.13" data-timestamp="470.13"> For those who have been wronged and betrayed, of which there are many people out there that have been wronged and betrayed, I am your retribution. We will take care of it.</p><p begin="00:07:58.92" data-timestamp="478.92">[CHEERING]</p><p begin="00:08:00.66" data-timestamp="480.66">We will take care of it.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:08:07.10" data-timestamp="487.1"> This is something that Jonathan thinks lots of people might not have noticed until lately, the degree to which Donald Trump's campaign this time around is about getting back at his enemies. Jonathan tries to capture this in his book about Trump, <i>Tired</i> <i>of</i> <i>Winning,</i> which is actually the inspiration for today's show.</p><p begin="00:08:21.79" data-timestamp="501.79">And what exactly might Donald Trump's revenge look like if he got back into office? Well, in some ways, it's still hard to say. Here's the best information out there on this.</p><p begin="00:08:32.32" data-timestamp="512.32">Number one on his agenda, for a long time, seemed to be that he was going to go after other Republicans, specifically people who had been his allies and turned against him.
