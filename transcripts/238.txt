And his Russian teacher thought that he only needed one thing to make his life complete.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Alex Blumberg</h4><p begin="00:00:09.70" data-timestamp="9.7">She thought I needed a Russian girlfriend. I don't know.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:17.12" data-timestamp="17.12">Our American women are just too soft.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Alex Blumberg</h4><p begin="00:00:19.97" data-timestamp="19.97">I don't know what you-- I truly don't know. And we're at the sushi restaurant, and we're all eating, and everybody's telling stories, funny stories. And I'm sitting across from my date. And my Russian teacher is turning to me and saying, you know, tell them this story! Tell them that story! And so then I tell them this story and that story, and everybody's laughing, except for my date.</p><p begin="00:00:50.18" data-timestamp="50.18">And so we leave the sushi restaurant and we go to this club. And we're all dancing. Like, my Russian teacher's dancing, and all of her friends are dancing, and everybody's sort of like, you know, exchanging partners, and it's sort of this fun scene. And everybody's dancing except for my date, who's just sitting at the table and sort of nursing a drink.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:01:09.11" data-timestamp="69.11">And looking--?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Alex Blumberg</h4><p begin="00:01:10.03" data-timestamp="70.03">And looking bored. Really bored.</p><p begin="00:01:13.77" data-timestamp="73.77">And so, you know, I keep on coming back from the dance floor, and saying, are you sure you don't want to dance? You want to dance? And then she will sort of smile bitterly, and just shake her head, and-- and so then somebody else will drag me out.</p><p begin="00:01:28.14" data-timestamp="88.14">So finally I come back and I sit down, and I'm sitting next to her, and I'm trying to strike up a conversation. And you know, something about like, I guess you don't like dancing, or something like that. And she sort of looks out at the floor, and there's people having a great time all around. And she sort of like nods her chin towards the floor and she's like, "This is an American dance number."</p><p begin="00:01:57.50" data-timestamp="117.5">And I drove away thinking, well, that was a horrible date. Like, neither of us enjoyed each other. And that was a disaster, pretty much. Like, we were not going to be seeing each other again.</p><p begin="00:02:08.30" data-timestamp="128.3">I get to Russian class the next week, and my Russian teacher says, "hey, so are you going to call Elena? I was trying to figure out what that could possibly have meant, right?</p><p begin="00:02:28.67" data-timestamp="148.67">And so I was like sort of mulling it over in my head, because it was really fascinating to me. And I realized that I have a very sort of like clear and perhaps culturally informed idea of what a great date is. And it has to do with the idea of, like, what it means to fall in love. And I realized that in my head, what I think about as a great date is what sort of you see in the movies. Like sort of the "falling in love" montage of the movies.</p><p begin="00:02:58.15" data-timestamp="178.15">You know, you go on a great date. It often involves a boardwalk. And like, Americans, I never understand, why do you say, "he makes me laugh?" Why is that so important? "He makes me laugh?" Every American I've ever met, all they say, when you ask them how the relationship is, and they say "he makes me laugh" as if that's the greatest thing in the world? What's so great about "he makes me laugh?"</p><p begin="00:04:50.00" data-timestamp="290">She just, you know, went off on like, something that I have actually said many times. Like, I just want somebody who makes me laugh.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:07.79" data-timestamp="307.79">Some days seems like every story in the world is the story of cultural misunderstanding. And so today, in this era of misunderstandings abroad and at home, we bring you an hour of stories about what gets lost in translation.</p><p begin="00:05:31.20" data-timestamp="331.2">From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i>, distributed by Public Radio International. In that act, two brave people step into that chasm. Drop into that chasm. However you put it.</p><p begin="00:05:51.21" data-timestamp="351.21">Act Two, Star of Bethlehem. We bring you the story of the most important, most respected newsman on television in the town of Bethlehem, who does a program that is unlike any news show that you have ever seen.</p><p begin="00:06:04.44" data-timestamp="364.44">Act Three. Translating For the Very, Very, Very, Very, Very Tall. In which we have, for a change, on our program a story that gets us close to someone so famous that you may have actually heard of him if you follow pro basketball in the tiniest way. Stay with us.</p></div>  </div>

<div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: The Chasm Between Comedy And Music</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:24.38" data-timestamp="384.38"> Act One. The Chasm Between Comedy and Music.</p><p begin="00:06:27.93" data-timestamp="387.93">Well, we start today's program about translation with a story about karaoke. In karaoke, after all, you are the interpreter. You are the translator. You stand there with the words of the song appearing on a little TV monitor and you try to render a life-size performance-- a translation of the original hit, if you will-- rendered in the musical language of you.</p><p begin="00:06:48.30" data-timestamp="408.3">And in a way, it's surprising that the only karaoke we have is for music. Why not karaoke of the "I Have a Dream" speech, or the "You talking to me?" from <i>Taxi</i> <i>Driver.</i> Recently Jonathan Goldstein and Starlee Kine, you hear them both on our program from time to time, heard about a karaoke bar which not only had songs, but comedy routines that you could perform onstage. They decided to find out for themselves how stand up comedy translates to the karaoke stage.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jonathan Goldstein</h4><p begin="00:07:16.15" data-timestamp="436.15">Starlee and I talk quite a bit about chops. Comedic chops. Pacing, placement of the zinger. When to employ whimsy and when to employ wit. Chops.</p><p begin="00:07:26.31" data-timestamp="446.31">Back when we worked in adjoining cubicles, we'd kill time by arguing over what was funny and what wasn't. Blowing cigar smoke in a baby's face? Not funny. A baby smoking a cigar? Funny.</p><p begin="00:07:39.72" data-timestamp="459.72">We were like Borscht Belt comedians without the borscht, the belt, or the audience. But the fact that we weren't knocking them dead at Grossinger's every night didn't keep us from coming up with titles for our showbiz bios. Starlee's book was going to be called <i>The</i> <i>Woman</i> <i>Behind</i> <i>the</i> <i>Chops,</i> while I intended to call mine <i>Sir</i> <i>Chops-a-Lot.</i></p><p begin="00:08:03.48" data-timestamp="483.48">Why, just the other day, I was giving young Starlee a quiz.
