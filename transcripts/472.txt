  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:00.97" data-timestamp="0.97"> One of the first times I met David Rakoff-- this is before he started writing stories for the radio-- he invited me to sit in the window of a department store with him. He was playing Sigmund Freud in a Christmas display window at Barney's, and he was seeing patients in the window. David sat in a chair. I lay on a couch.</p><p begin="00:00:19.27" data-timestamp="19.27">My mom was the therapist. David's dad was a psychiatrist. We'd both been therapy patients ourselves at some point or another. And so it was easy for us to play this simulacrum of psychiatry with each other. I don't remember what we talked about. It was so long ago. But I remember very clearly the feeling of it.</p><p begin="00:00:36.91" data-timestamp="36.91">David, gently asking leading general questions like a real therapist would-- how are things going? Why do you think you said that? It was easy to talk to him. And unlike the real Sigmund Freud, he offered advice. And it was surprisingly cozy.</p><p begin="00:00:55.29" data-timestamp="55.29">Though one wall of the room that we were in was this plate glass window that faced Madison Avenue, the space was narrow and cocoon-like. The muffled street noise that leaked through the window made it feel more womb-like if anything. People on the street couldn't hear what we said to each other. So it was both public and intimate. I left feeling close to him. I left wishing we could do it again.</p><p begin="00:01:21.88" data-timestamp="81.88">And we did, in a way. Nearly everything he ever made for our radio show was a personal act, made in close collaboration for public consumption, performed behind a plate glass window of one kind or another. This week marks the fifth anniversary of his death. He'd been on <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life</i> 25 times. The first time was in 1996, just two months after we went on the air. The last was just three weeks before he died.</p><p begin="00:01:48.97" data-timestamp="108.97">He travelled with the show when we took it on the road and on tour. When the staff and some favorite contributors all had our testosterone levels tested as a stunt for an episode in 2002, David, to everybody's surprise including his, was the one with the highest testosterone. When my mom died in 2003, and for the first time we had a guest host fill in for me, David was the guest host. Because of that, I have a recording of him handling this next bit of business I need to do.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:02:15.03" data-timestamp="135.03"> From WBEZ Chicago, it's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life.</i> I'm David Rakoff. Stay with us, won't you?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:27.88" data-timestamp="147.88"> Today, those of us who make this radio show have the pleasure of bringing you an hour of stories by our friend, David Rakoff. This is the show we made and broadcast back when he died to remember him by. David helped invent and define what our radio show is. And he was just a great writer-- lucid and funny and mean and big-hearted.</p><p begin="00:02:50.47" data-timestamp="170.47">He was that way in conversation, too. In thinking about what stories to put on today's program, I realized that David has been on the program so often that we have stories covering most of the different phases in his life. We could actually line up the essays in an order and tell his life story. And we're going to do that, at least for a bit here.</p><p begin="00:03:07.81" data-timestamp="187.81">As you'll hear, it is not a story with big dramatic turns and surprises. But like the old saying goes, great stories happen to those who can tell them. These are very, very fun to listen to.</p><p begin="00:03:26.58" data-timestamp="206.58">So, David was born in Canada. He talked about Canada and his Canadianness with me in an interview back in 1997, the third time he appeared on our radio program. He said that, actually, from the day that he arrived in New York City for college as a teenager, he didn't want to seem Canadian. He wanted to pass as a New Yorker.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:03:46.38" data-timestamp="226.38"> My tactics were to adopt a certain kind of world-weary, jaded, anxious neuroticism. And it was taken on as a cosmetic mantle at the beginning until such time as, you know, you simply can't pull the mask off your face. Oh my god, it's stuck! But there you are, years later, a jaded, affectless, neurotic, disenchanted, sad person.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:20.43" data-timestamp="260.43"> I'm told that Canadians tend to know who else is Canadian, you know, who's famous.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:04:25.59" data-timestamp="265.59"> All the time, everything. And that, to me, is chemical. Live and learn, huh?</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:43.70" data-timestamp="283.7"> Who could be more American than the host of <i>Let's</i> <i>Make</i> <i>a</i> <i>Deal?</i> Even the name, <i>Let's</i> <i>Make</i> <i>a</i> <i>Deal.</i></p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:04:47.43" data-timestamp="287.43"> And yet, remember Monty only facilitated the deals.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:51.64" data-timestamp="291.64"> [LAUGHS]</p><p begin="00:04:54.05" data-timestamp="294.05"></p><p begin="00:05:00.83" data-timestamp="300.83">Who else?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:05:02.62" data-timestamp="302.62"> Glenn Ford, John Kenneth Galbraith. But here's the thing about knowing who's Canadian. I mean, there is a woman named Shania Twain. She is Canadian. I know that she's Canadian. I do not know who the hell Shania Twain is. I don't know what she does. And yet, for some reason, I know that she's famous in America and that she's Canadian.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:25.34" data-timestamp="325.34"> How did this come up? And of course, you know, the arm on your space shuttle--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:58.01" data-timestamp="358.01"> I'm sorry?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:05:58.76" data-timestamp="358.76"> The arm on your space shuttle, for making interstellar repairs.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:03.20" data-timestamp="363.2"> In Canada, the space shuttle was referred to as--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:06:05.57" data-timestamp="365.57"> The American space shuttle with its Canadian-built arm.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:08.48" data-timestamp="368.48"> [LAUGHS]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:06:09.74" data-timestamp="369.74"> Never any other way, as in the American space shuttle with its Canadian-built arm blew up today.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:16.64" data-timestamp="376.64"> No, that didn't really happen.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:06:18.14" data-timestamp="378.14"> No, well, I was actually here at the time, but the Canadian-built arm gets a lot of air play. Not down here, huh? In fact, if you were to ask most Canadians, what do you think the space shuttle is for, they'd say, oh, you know, to go up and move stuff around in space-- with an arm.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:33.91" data-timestamp="393.91"> [LAUGHS]</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:06:35.34" data-timestamp="395.34"> You know.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:42.46" data-timestamp="402.46"> And so when a Canadian finds out that some figure is Canadian, what happens in their heart?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>David Rakoff</h4><p begin="00:06:48.87" data-timestamp="408.87"> Oh, well, your heart does a little bit of-- a certain special Canadians chamber opens up and enfolds that name, and you keep it.
