He was married, had three kids. And he traveled in high society, which is unusual for a pirate.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mark Hanna</h4><p begin="00:00:51.15" data-timestamp="51.15"> Yeah. The vast majority of people who turned to piracy did so out of desperation, and this man didn't.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:00:55.41" data-timestamp="55.41"> So who's this?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:00:57.36" data-timestamp="57.36"> I've told this story so many times to so many people that I just wanted to make sure I was getting the facts right, so I decided to talk to a pirate historian. That's Mark Hanna. He's also a professor at UC San Diego.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mark Hanna</h4><p begin="00:01:09.54" data-timestamp="69.54"> The vast majority of pirates start their lives by mutinying, or they gather a crew together and they steal a ship from someone. I'll have my ship built according to specifications that I think are best.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:01:24.45" data-timestamp="84.45"> And then he hired a crew and he paid them wages, like regular normal-people wages, which was a big no-no.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mark Hanna</h4><p begin="00:01:31.83" data-timestamp="91.83"> The vast majority of pirates, if you don't capture a ship, you don't make any money. I know I'll be paid this week, you're going to be a little less aggressive about capturing a ship. What is he saying?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:02:30.51" data-timestamp="150.51"> He says-- and other books say the same thing-- that what led him to piracy was that he had a nagging wife.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:37.05" data-timestamp="157.05"> No way.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:02:37.71" data-timestamp="157.71"> Mhm.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:02:38.04" data-timestamp="158.04"> They're blaming the wife?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:02:39.18" data-timestamp="159.18"> They blame the nagging wife. And it seems like the reality set in very soon after he had started off on his voyage.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:03:24.13" data-timestamp="204.13"> And this is what I love about this story is that, instead of just dreaming of this alternate life where he's a bad guy, he goes for it. You want to be seen as decisive, fast, aggressive.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:03:57.57" data-timestamp="237.57"> But they have a few successes. They take a few ships. He even gets a reputation. This is when they start calling him the Gentleman Pirate.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:05.62" data-timestamp="245.62"> Does he kill people?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:04:06.99" data-timestamp="246.99"> No, actually. He's kind of a nice pirate. He captures people. He takes their goods. And then he lets them go.</p><p begin="00:04:14.74" data-timestamp="254.74">But then he attacks a big ship, a Spanish man-of-war, and over 30 of his men are killed or wounded. And it's a huge disaster. Bonnet is gravely injured. And they have to escape and flee to the Bahamas.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Mark Hanna</h4><p begin="00:04:27.73" data-timestamp="267.73"> And there he met one of the most iconic pirates in world history, Edward Thatch, who's better known as Blackbeard.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:04:33.82" data-timestamp="273.82"> Yeah. When he would first appear on your ship, he would have prepared for that moment by tying these pieces of twine called slow matches into his long, big beard, and he would light them on fire just as he appeared so that there was this cloud of smoke. And in the smoke, you would just see the eyes of Blackbeard. So he looked like he was appearing out of hell.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:33.62" data-timestamp="333.62"> I don't understand that at all. It just seems like the fire would catch fire to his beard.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:05:38.99" data-timestamp="338.99"> He had a method, I'm sure.</p><p begin="00:05:49.09" data-timestamp="349.09">And this was actually a strategy. Their whole thing was to inspire as much terror as possible because, if they made you incredibly afraid, you would give up the ship without a fight.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:00.65" data-timestamp="360.65"> Oh, this never occurred to me until this second. So the skull and crossbones and all that, it's all like a brand to make people scared.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Elna Baker</h4><p begin="00:06:07.42" data-timestamp="367.42"> Yeah. Their brand was death. They arrive with a skull and crossbones. If you think death is coming for you, you're going to surrender. But in fact, some pirates, like Blackbeard, they didn't even kill many people. In fact, there's no evidence Blackbird ever killed anyone till the very end. So it was very theatrical. They're putting on a show, and they understood that. And I like imagining these pirates scheming and plotting it out, being like, OK, no, here, I have a really good idea.
