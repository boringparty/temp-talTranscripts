<div class="act" id="credits">
  <h3>Act One: Mother Knows Best</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:09:35.84" data-timestamp="575.84"> It's <i>This</i> <i>American</i> <i>Life,</i> Act One, "Mother Knows Best."</p><p begin="00:09:40.51" data-timestamp="580.51">Fela Kuti is at a special level of fame where, yes, lots of people have no idea who he is. But for people who do, there could not be a bigger musical star. He was a worldwide musical phenomenon by the 1980s, iconic, from Nigeria. He ushered in an entire new genre of music, which he called afrobeat.</p><p begin="00:10:12.74" data-timestamp="612.74">Fela was also a profoundly political figure. Nigeria was still a newly independent country in the 1970s and '80s, still very much trying to answer questions about what kind of nation it was going to be. Fela's songs criticized colonialism in all of its forms, openly challenged Nigeria's ruling military government. He took on apartheid South Africa and the United States.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Fela Kuti</h4><p begin="00:10:33.10" data-timestamp="633.1"> All dey talk of Black man power</p><p begin="00:10:36.00" data-timestamp="636">Water, him not get enemy!</p><p begin="00:10:38.58" data-timestamp="638.58">I dey talk of Black power, I say</p><p begin="00:10:40.84" data-timestamp="640.84">Water!</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:10:42.45" data-timestamp="642.45"> Fela called for the complete rejection of most things European and Western and tried to live that way. He went so far as to found his own commune and declared it to be its own republic, free of government control.</p><p begin="00:10:53.41" data-timestamp="653.41">His politics were radical, but also messy. His version of being an Africanist led him to pretty ugly views about gay people and women. They make him a complicated person to explain.</p><p begin="00:11:04.33" data-timestamp="664.33">Recently, Jad Abumrad embarked on a journey to do just that. Jad is the creator and longtime co-host of <i>Radiolab,</i> and he did a big series on Dolly Parton that won all kinds of awards and lots of people heard. Now, he's put out a whole series about Fela, and a big chunk of it is about how Fela's music and his politics spoke to each other.</p><p begin="00:11:24.13" data-timestamp="684.13">And one of the stories that Jad tells, a story that we're going to excerpt here today, is about where Fela's anti-colonial politics came from. Jad says that some of those beliefs can be traced back to his mother, Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti. The story of her political accomplishments is not that well known outside of Nigeria, and it's kind of an amazing story on its own. What she did in her small town helped transform the entire country. Here's Jad with the story.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jad Abumrad</h4><p begin="00:11:51.28" data-timestamp="711.28"> The story starts in the 1940s, Abeokuta, Nigeria, a town that is about 50 miles north of Lagos.
