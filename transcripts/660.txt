  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:03:34.95" data-timestamp="214.95"> Act One, The Sun Never Sets-- On the Moosewood Restaurant. Yeah.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Sean Cole</h4><p begin="00:04:27.58" data-timestamp="267.58"> OK. I'm going to take a sip of water here. Well, Ira, I had a salad. I don't really like the peach.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:58.65" data-timestamp="298.65"> Joel Lovell's story began when he left the working class town where he grew up in upstate New York. And then completely unplanned, I suddenly said-- as a slice of pizza was passed to me, this pizza with sausage on top of it, I said, you know, I can't take that because my parents are vegetarians.</p><p begin="00:06:32.04" data-timestamp="392.04">And everybody in the room sort of turned and looked at me because it wasn't even as if I said, well, I'm a vegetarian. And I'm not entirely-- well, I mean, I have some ideas now about why I said that.</p><p begin="00:06:51.34" data-timestamp="411.34">But at the time, I had no idea what I was saying. Motty giggled, and others followed. Rabbi Breyer slowly rose to his feet, his thick fists pushing themselves into the desktop. It may have been a loophole, but technically speaking, I was correct. Rabbi Breyer himself had told us that our Sages tell us that the Torah tells us that there were three situations in which one is absolutely prohibited from reciting a blessing.</p><p begin="00:52:10.36" data-timestamp="3130.36">One, while facing a male over the age of nine years old whose genitals are showing; two, while facing a female over the age of three years old whose genitals are showing; and three, in the presence of feces. For a big man, Rabbi Breyer moved pretty quickly.</p><p begin="00:52:35.16" data-timestamp="3155.16">It's true, I said, as he barreled toward me.
