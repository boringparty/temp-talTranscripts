This is money in limbo.</p><p begin="00:02:26.10" data-timestamp="146.1">And thinking about all this, Chris Sewell realized how he could pay for his doctors. He also found 100 checks that were owed to the hospital itself. <div class="act" id="act1">
  <h3>Act One: Losing It</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:07:06.67" data-timestamp="426.67"> Act One. Losing It.</p><p begin="00:07:10.48" data-timestamp="430.48">Well, if you're going to do a program about people who are lost, you pretty much have to do a story about adolescence. Jonathan Goldstein is a regular contributor to our show. A quick warning before we begin, for parents listening with kids, he talks about sex in this story. Please welcome Jonathan Goldstein.</p><p begin="00:07:27.06" data-timestamp="447.06">[AUDIENCE CHEERING]</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Jonathan Goldstein</h4><p begin="00:07:37.04" data-timestamp="457.04"> Dying a virgin happened to people. Hans Christian Andersen, Queen Elizabeth the First. My grandmother's prophetically named brother, Uncle Hymen.</p><p begin="00:07:54.44" data-timestamp="474.44">And I knew it was the kind of thing that could happen to me. Days would turn into months, months into years, and eventually I would drop dead in a men's hotel and be buried sexless in a single cemetery plot squeezed in on my side between my parents. And so I began, at an early age, what I thought of as my life's work, the work of not dying a virgin.</p><p begin="00:08:21.28" data-timestamp="501.28">At 10 I met Varid, my first crush. She was in from Israel with her family, and was staying with our neighbors. Since this was the summer <i>Grease</i> came out, I invited Varid upstairs to our living room, where I played the soundtrack on our stereo. While it played, I danced for her. My feeling was that you had to give girls everything you had-- your showmanship, your best moves, even your dignity.</p><p begin="00:08:49.00" data-timestamp="529">As the music played, Varid sat there with quiet indifference. At one point, I tried to bring her soul to life by lying down on our coffee table and spinning around on my stomach like a spastic Lazy Susan. When I did the splits, I fell to the ground with a ferocity that almost tore my scrotal sac in two.</p><p begin="00:09:12.20" data-timestamp="552.2">Before Varid went back to Israel, I handed her all 10 <i>Archie</i> digests that I owned. I stacked them so all the spines lined up perfectly. I wanted them to look solemn and substantial, like a package wrapped in rope from the old country. Act Three: I Found Your Letter.</p><p begin="00:42:27.78" data-timestamp="2547.78">You know when you're walking down the street and you spot some random piece of paper floating around-- a page from a letter, a shopping list with a note on it, some kid's paper from class-- do you pick it up? Do you read it? Me? Very nosy, yes.</p><p begin="00:42:43.43" data-timestamp="2563.43">As writing goes, these pieces of paper, they're little writing orphans, cut off from their original life, cut off from everything that gave them meaning. But still there with a lot on their minds and a lot to say.</p><p begin="00:42:53.61" data-timestamp="2573.61">A few years back, Davy Rothbart decided that somebody, somewhere should do a magazine that was made up entirely of this kind of writing. And then through sheer force of will, he became that somebody and created <i>Found</i> <i>Magazine.</i> Now, people all over the country send him this stuff. He's going to come out and read some of this writing that has been discarded and misplaced, lost by its original owners.</p><p begin="00:43:15.43" data-timestamp="2595.43">Please welcome Davy Rothbart.</p><p begin="00:43:17.82" data-timestamp="2597.82">[AUDIENCE APPLAUSE]</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Davy Rothbart</h4><p begin="00:43:25.59" data-timestamp="2605.59"> Everyone can find stuff. And I want to give you just a sense of some of the stuff that people are finding and sending in to us. This right here is a note that was found in LA.</p><p begin="00:43:38.76" data-timestamp="2618.76">And it says, "Jesse, I did not take anything. I know there's no convincing you once you've made up your mind. And although I cannot offer you any other explanation as to what happened to it, that doesn't mean I did it. How could I have? You say your car was locked and Katie had the keys.</p><p begin="00:44:01.80" data-timestamp="2641.8">Anyway, I don't need to take something of yours when I could get my own. It doesn't make sense. But here's a replacement, because I can't stand when you think I've wronged you. Mom."</p><p begin="00:44:17.40" data-timestamp="2657.4">[AUDIENCE LAUGHING]</p><p begin="00:44:21.53" data-timestamp="2661.53">The authors of some of these notes, they don't always paint themselves in the best light, you know? Sometimes they're going fine, fine, and they just skewer themselves at the end. And this is one that was found in Tacoma, Washington.</p><p begin="00:44:33.20" data-timestamp="2673.2">And at the top, OK, it just says, "what kind of relationship do I want? How do I see myself in it? I'm nurturing, I'm relied upon, I'm getting my ya-yas." But then down here it says, "what I hate about women: they're hard, impossible to please and satisfy. They talk a lot without saying much or being intimate. And they're self-involved and self-absorbed. I want them to be absorbed with me."</p><p begin="00:45:05.12" data-timestamp="2705.12">This one was found in Berkeley, California, actually on the front lawn of a fraternity house. And it was a whole book of letters back and forth from the elders of the house to the pledges, the young kids who are trying to get into the fraternity. So this is one of the notes that's from one of the elders to one of the young pledges named A.J.</p><p begin="00:45:22.23" data-timestamp="2722.23">And he says, "A.J., tonight's event was just a tradition of the house. It was not gay. Even though you had to pull down your pants, at least you didn't have to show it to all the actives, just your pledge master. Its meaning: to prove your manhood and that you are not a boy. But none of us are gay. Aside from all this, I want to know that I've seen some improvement out of you since your first pledge. Keep up the effort and have a positive attitude of things. I tell ya, it's all worth it once you've crossed. Take my word for it. Just hang in there, OK?</p><p begin="00:46:12.12" data-timestamp="2772.12">Listen, I want you to have dinner with me when you're free someday. It's a great chance for you to get to know me better and for me to answer questions you may have, all right? Aaron."</p><p begin="00:46:26.28" data-timestamp="2786.28">And so many of the notes, as I read through them when they come in the mail every day that people are finding, they deal with love and relationships, you know? And one of the favorite notes that I've ever found myself is this note I found in Chicago on my windshield. I came out of my friend's apartment late one night. It had snowed a little bit. And you know, with like a thin layer of snow, every car looks a little bit the same. And my name's Davy, but there's a note on my windshield that was addressed to Marco.</p><p begin="00:46:53.45" data-timestamp="2813.45">And it said, "Marco, I freakin' hate you. You said you had to work? Then why is your car here at her place? You're a freakin' liar. I hate you, I freakin' hate you. Crystal. P.S. Page me later?"</p><p begin="00:47:19.69" data-timestamp="2839.69">And then this one just came in last week from-- it was found in North Carolina. And it says, "Dear Will, the longer I think about what I'm doing, the sicker I feel. Will, I'm sorry, but I don't think we should continue to have a relationship together. At least not as a couple. I love you, but things have not been the same since we found out that we were related."</p><p begin="00:47:42.84" data-timestamp="2862.84">[AUDIENCE LAUGHING]</p><p begin="00:47:50.21" data-timestamp="2870.21">My little brother, Peter, he is the best finder I know. He amazes me with what he brings home. And this is one he found when he was visiting me in Chicago. And it's written in like a kid's handwriting. It looks like maybe a 12-year-old kid. It says, "To cashier, will you please sell my son one pack of Newport Lights." Ah, that trick. "Thank you for your help." On there's just this scrawled, adult-looking signature.</p><p begin="00:48:20.41" data-timestamp="2900.41">And then this one my brother found pinned under the back tire of his bicycle. And it's this handsome looking sign. Someone spent a good deal of time putting this thing together.</p><p begin="00:48:31.99" data-timestamp="2911.99">It says, "After leaving the building please lock this door. It will prevent unauthorized people from entering the building and defecating in the washing machine. Many thanks."</p><p begin="00:48:53.03" data-timestamp="2933.03">And when you send your finds in, I ask that you name your finds. And also that you write one line, you know, a sense of response or interpretation to it. About this one, my brother wrote, he says, "Note that those who are authorized to defecate in the washing machine will be given a key for entry."</p><p begin="00:49:13.67" data-timestamp="2953.67">And yeah, I applaud for whoever made that sign, but also for my brother, who just picked it up off the ground. And I asked him, you know, "how do you do it? What's your secret?" My brother says that it's been scientifically calculated-- by him-- that one in five, 20%, of all finds are gems of finds. So that means if you pick one thing up a day, that every week you'll have a real jewel of a find.</p><p begin="00:49:36.21" data-timestamp="2976.21">Let's read this one here. This is from Erie, Pennsylvania. And it's a note, a kid writing a letter to his dad.</p><p begin="00:49:46.77" data-timestamp="2986.77">It says, "Hi, Dad. What's up? Not much here. Just working to save money. I'm working at Old Country Buffet starting at $6.50 an hour cooking. Dad, are there any cooking jobs down there? Let me know when you write me back. I gave Cleav your letter, OK? Without Mom knowing, OK?</p><p begin="00:50:06.47" data-timestamp="3006.47">I can't wait to come down there. Erie sucks bad. There ain't nothing to do. Too cold. The weather sucks, too. Dad, I drink, too. Beer. Budweiser. But I don't drink a lot. And I do a little drugs, too. I smoke joints. And I got a bowl that's cool. I'll let you see it, OK? I talked to Grandma. She said she's going to send you a letter and some money.</p><p begin="00:50:24.95" data-timestamp="3024.95">Dad, I don't know if Cleav or Beth is coming down, but I'll talk to them and see. If not, it will be just me only.</p><p begin="00:50:31.60" data-timestamp="3031.6">Dad, you're going to rent a mobile home. That's cheap. $100 a week. That's cheap. Dad, try and see if there's any apartments for rent and let me know how much, OK? Dad, I'm going to take a Greyhound because it's cheap-- only $49-- but I'll call you ahead of time. Dad, all the money I'm saving from working at my job and my income tax check, I should have a lot, OK? With the money, we should be able to get a place for us, OK?</p><p begin="00:50:54.47" data-timestamp="3054.47">Dad, I can't wait to come down there pretty soon."</p><p begin="00:50:56.97" data-timestamp="3056.97">Turn over.</p><p begin="00:50:59.12" data-timestamp="3059.12">"Dad, I'm a cook, and I can cook us some [BLEEP] up. Cook some meals up for us, get a grill and cook on that. Also, I got a lot of CDs, like AC/DC, Led Zeppelin, Metallica, Pink Floyd, The Doors, Jimi Hendrix, Judas Priest, Black Sabbath, and all that. We can jam out and have lots of fun.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:54:56.72" data-timestamp="3296.72"> Davy Rothbart. You can go to his magazine's website, foundmagazine.com.</p></div>  </div>
