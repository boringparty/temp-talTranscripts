  <h3>Act One: Life in the Middle Ages</h3>
  <div class="act-inner">
    <div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:04:45.26" data-timestamp="285.26">Act One, Life in the Middle Ages. So we wanted to understand what is going on exactly physically and emotionally with middle school kids, and we talked to all kinds of people about that these last few weeks. And one of the producers on our staff had a very strong opinion about all this, Alex Blumberg. Before he got into radio, he taught in a middle school in Chicago for four years. He was a science teacher. And he concluded from that experience--</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Alex Blumberg</h4><p begin="00:05:09.08" data-timestamp="309.08">I don't know if they learned anything. They are so consumed with learning all these other lessons about where they fit in, in the social order, and how their bodies are now working and--</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:22.38" data-timestamp="322.38">And who they're going to be.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Alex Blumberg</h4><p begin="00:05:22.79" data-timestamp="322.79">--who they're attracted to, and who they're going to be, that facts and figures and geography, and all the other stuff that you teach in school, it just doesn't even penetrate.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:31.04" data-timestamp="331.04">Wait, are you saying we shouldn't even bother to have them in school? We should just basically put them to work in the factory for two or three years?</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Alex Blumberg</h4><p begin="00:05:38.22" data-timestamp="338.22">Yeah. I basically came away thinking you're sort of wasting your time trying to teach middle school students anything.</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:05:44.69" data-timestamp="344.69">If that seems just insane, Alex told me that Maria Montessori, who's the creator of the Montessori schools who looked at what kids could learn at each developmental stage, at one point in her career actually tossed out a similar suggestion. Perhaps, she said, young adolescents would be better off at a farm school, what she called an Erdkinder, where they would work with the land.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Marian Strok</h4><p begin="00:06:05.11" data-timestamp="365.11">[LAUGHING] Has he just got a great sense of humor or what?</p></div><div class="host"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:10.51" data-timestamp="370.51">I ran Alex's thoughts by Marian Strok, the principle of Evergreen Academy Middle School in Chicago, which was listed as one of the best middle schools in the country, a school to watch, by the National Forum to Accelerate Middle Grades Reform.</p></div><div class="subject"><h4>Marian Strok</h4><p begin="00:06:22.99" data-timestamp="382.99"> I have to say, well, I obviously don't agree, OK? I think that we definitely have a way to stick knowledge in their heads. It's being creative about how we do it, and taking into consideration all of those other things. I'm not going to lie. It's not easy.</p></div><div class="interviewer"><h4>Ira Glass</h4><p begin="00:06:38.37" data-timestamp="398.37">Can I ask you how much of the stereotype around students this age is true?
